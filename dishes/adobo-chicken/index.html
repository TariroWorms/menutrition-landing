🍗 Chicken adobo

Chicken adobo is the best-known dish of the Philippines: chicken braised in vinegar and soy sauce with lots of garlic, bay leaves and whole black peppercorns until tender, then often browned and glazed in the reduced sauce. The taste is tangy, salty and garlicky, and the sauce is dark and glossy. It is served with plenty of steamed rice to soak up the sauce.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • 120 ml cane or white vinegar
  • 80 ml soy sauce
  • 1 whole head of garlic, cloves crushed
  • 4 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp whole black peppercorns
  • 250 ml water
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Put the chicken, vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, bay leaves, peppercorns and water in a pot and leave for 30 minutes.
  2. Bring to the boil without stirring, then cover and simmer for 30–35 minutes until the chicken is tender.
  3. Lift out the chicken and boil the sauce for 5–10 minutes until reduced and slightly thick.
  4. Brown the chicken in the oil in a hot pan, then return it to the sauce and coat well.
  5. Serve hot with steamed rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken thighs or breast and skip the browning step to save oil and fat.
  • Skim the fat from the sauce before reducing it.
  • Serve with a smaller scoop of rice and plenty of steamed greens such as pak choi or green beans.

History

Cooking meat in vinegar and salt to keep it is generally believed to predate Spanish rule in the Philippines; the name adobo comes from the Spanish word for a marinade. Soy sauce, usually linked to Chinese influence, became a common ingredient over time. Today adobo has countless regional and family versions and is often called the unofficial national dish.
