🌶️ Pad kra pao (pork)

Origin: Thailand · Bangkok and central Thailand

Pad kra pao is a fiery Thai stir-fry of minced pork with holy basil, chilli and garlic, seasoned with soy sauce and fish sauce. It is cooked fast in a very hot wok so the basil wilts and perfumes the meat. It is usually served over jasmine rice and topped with a crisp-edged fried egg whose runny yolk mixes into the spicy, savoury pork.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g minced pork
  • 1 large handful of holy basil leaves
  • 4 garlic cloves and 3–5 bird's eye chillies, pounded
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Cooked jasmine rice to serve

Method

  1. Fry the eggs in hot oil until the edges are crisp and set them aside.
  2. In the same wok stir-fry the garlic and chilli for 20 seconds.
  3. Add the pork and stir-fry over high heat until cooked through.
  4. Add the soy sauce, fish sauce, sugar and a splash of water, then toss in the basil until just wilted.
  5. Serve over rice with the fried egg on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced pork or swap half of it for chopped green beans.
  • Poach or soft-boil the egg instead of deep-frying it in oil.
  • Keep the rice to a small scoop and add cucumber slices on the side.

History

Pad kra pao is one of the most popular street and canteen dishes in Thailand, often ordered as a quick one-plate meal. Its exact origin is not well documented, but it is widely seen as a modern classic of central Thai street food.
