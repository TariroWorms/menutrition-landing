🧀 Chami (Emirati fresh cheese)

Origin: United Arab Emirates · Gulf

Chami is a soft, crumbly fresh cheese from the Emirates, made by straining salted yogurt or soured milk until thick. It tastes mild and tangy, a little like a dry labneh or cottage cheese. It is eaten at breakfast with dates, rolled into thin regag bread or drizzled with olive oil.

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg plain full-fat yogurt
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Stir the salt and lemon juice into the yogurt.
  2. Spoon it into a cloth-lined sieve over a bowl and leave in the fridge for 12–24 hours to drain.
  3. Squeeze out the remaining liquid, crumble the curd and keep refrigerated for up to 4 days.

How to make it lighter

  • Make it from low-fat yogurt for a lighter, still tangy chami.
  • Pair it with one or two dates rather than a full plate to keep sugar in check.
  • Measure the olive oil drizzle — 1 tbsp is about 120 kcal.

History

Chami is a traditional dairy food in the UAE and neighbouring Gulf countries, long made at home as a way to use and keep milk; today it is still found at Emirati breakfasts and heritage markets.
