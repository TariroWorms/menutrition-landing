🫑 Peperonata

Peperonata is an Italian dish of sweet bell peppers slowly stewed with onion, tomato and garlic in olive oil until soft and silky. Red and yellow peppers give it a bright colour and natural sweetness, balanced by the gentle acidity of tomato. It is served warm or at room temperature as a side dish, a topping for grilled meat or simply with bread.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 4 bell peppers (red and yellow), cut into strips
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 300 g ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and pepper
  • Basil leaves to finish

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in a wide pan and soften the onion for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic and peppers, cover and cook for 15 minutes, stirring now and then.
  3. Add the tomatoes, season and simmer uncovered for 15–20 minutes until the peppers are very soft.
  4. Finish with basil and serve warm or at room temperature.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1–2 tbsp olive oil and a lid to steam the peppers: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve it with grilled chicken for a light, filling plate.
  • A larger portion of peperonata in place of starchy sides keeps the meal low in calories.

History

Peperonata is a home-cooking staple across Italy, especially in the south and in Sicily, with regional versions that add vinegar, capers or olives. It has no single documented origin and is today a common contorno, or vegetable side, on Italian tables.
