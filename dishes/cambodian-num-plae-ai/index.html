Num plae ai (Cambodian palm sugar rice balls)

Origin: Cambodia
258kcal

Num plae ai are small balls of glutinous rice dough with a nugget of palm sugar hidden inside. Once boiled they turn soft and chewy, and the sugar melts into a warm syrup that bursts in the mouth. They are rolled in grated coconut with a pinch of salt and sesame for balance.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g glutinous rice flour
  • 160 ml warm water
  • 80 g palm sugar, chopped
  • 100 g freshly grated coconut
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
  • 1 pinch salt

Method

  1. Mix the flour with warm water into a smooth, soft dough.
  2. Pinch off walnut-sized pieces and flatten each one.
  3. Press a small piece of palm sugar into the centre and seal into a ball.
  4. Boil in plenty of water until the balls float, then 1 more minute.
  5. Lift out, drain, and roll in coconut mixed with salt and sesame.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less palm sugar per ball to cut sugar without losing the surprise.
  • Roll in coconut rather than coating with extra syrup.
  • Serve 2-3 balls as a dessert portion and share the rest.

History

The sweet is a familiar treat at Khmer festivals and street stalls. Its name is often explained as meaning something like 'eat it and be surprised', a nod to the molten centre.
