Jachnun

Origin: Israel · Yemenite Jewish

Jachnun is a Yemenite Jewish Sabbath pastry made of thin dough brushed with fat, rolled up and baked very slowly overnight. The result is soft inside, deeply caramelised outside and a little sweet. It is served in the morning with a hard-boiled egg, grated tomato and zhug.

Recipe

6 servings600 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 g butter or margarine, softened
  • 1 tsp salt, 1 tbsp sugar
  • 240 ml water
  • 6 hard-boiled eggs
  • 3 tomatoes, grated

Method

  1. Knead flour, water, salt and sugar into a smooth dough and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Divide into 6 balls and stretch each one very thin on a greased surface.
  3. Spread generously with butter and roll into a tight log.
  4. Place in a covered pot in a low oven at about 100 °C.
  5. Bake 10 to 12 hours until deep brown.
  6. Serve warm with egg, grated tomato and zhug.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less butter and brush thinly for a lighter version.
  • Half wholemeal flour adds fibre.
  • Add plenty of tomato and skip the extra spread.

History

It is a traditional Shabbat breakfast of Yemenite Jews, cooked overnight because no cooking is allowed on the day of rest. Today it is sold in Israel in cafes and markets.
