Dodo (fried plantain)

Origin: Nigeria

Dodo is ripe plantain cut into slices or chunks and fried in oil until the edges are dark and caramelized and the inside is soft and sweet. A pinch of salt balances the sweetness. It is served beside jollof rice, beans or stew, or eaten alone as a snack.

What it is made of

plantainoilsalt

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 3 ripe plantains (about 600 g)
  • 200 ml oil for frying
  • 1/2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Peel the plantains and slice diagonally about 1 cm thick.
  2. Sprinkle lightly with salt.
  3. Heat oil to medium heat.
  4. Fry slices in batches until golden brown on both sides.
  5. Drain on paper.
  6. Serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use an air fryer or oven to cut the oil.
  • Choose just-ripe plantains for less sugar.
  • Pair with beans and vegetables to balance the plate.

History

Dodo is a Yoruba name for fried plantain, and the dish is eaten in homes, restaurants and at parties across Nigeria. It uses very ripe plantains that have turned yellow with black spots.
