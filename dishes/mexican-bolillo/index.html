Bolillo (Mexican crusty roll)

Origin: Mexico

The bolillo is Mexico’s everyday white roll: oval, pointed at both ends, with a thin crackling crust and a soft, fluffy interior. It is split for tortas, dunked in soups, served with beans or eaten plain with coffee. A softer flat cousin, the telera, is used for many tortas too.

Recipe

10 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g bread flour
  • 10 g salt
  • 8 g sugar
  • 7 g dried yeast
  • 320 ml warm water
  • 15 g butter or lard

Method

  1. Mix flour, yeast, sugar and water, then add salt and butter and knead for 10 minutes.
  2. Let rise about 1 hour until doubled.
  3. Divide into 10 pieces, shape into ovals with tapered ends and lay on a tray.
  4. Rise 45 minutes, then slash each roll lengthwise.
  5. Bake at 220 °C with steam (a tray of water in the oven) for 18–20 minutes until golden and crisp.

How to make it lighter

  • Scoop out some of the soft crumb before filling a torta to save calories.
  • Replace a third of the flour with whole-wheat flour.
  • Eat it with beans and salsa rather than with butter and jam.

History

The roll is commonly linked to French bakers in nineteenth-century Mexico and the influence of French bread at that time. It is now baked in panaderías across the country and sold by the piece every morning.
