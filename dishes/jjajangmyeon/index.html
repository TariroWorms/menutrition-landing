🍜 Jjajangmyeon

Jjajangmyeon is a Korean-Chinese dish of thick wheat noodles topped with a glossy, dark sauce of black bean paste stir-fried with diced pork, onion and zucchini. The sauce is savoury, slightly sweet and earthy, and coats every strand. It is mixed at the table and often eaten with yellow pickled radish and raw onion.

Recipe

2 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g fresh thick wheat noodles
  • 200 g pork shoulder, diced
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 1 zucchini, diced
  • 4 tbsp Korean black bean paste (chunjang)
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp potato starch mixed with 3 tbsp water
  • 300 ml water

Method

  1. Fry the black bean paste in 1 tbsp oil over low heat for 2 minutes, stirring, then set aside.
  2. In the remaining oil stir-fry the pork until browned, add onion and zucchini and cook 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in the fried paste, sugar and water and simmer 5 minutes.
  4. Thicken with the starch mixture until glossy.
  5. Boil the noodles, drain and divide between bowls; pour the sauce over and mix before eating.

How to make it lighter

  • Noodles carry most of the calories: use a smaller nest and add extra zucchini and onion to the sauce.
  • Choose lean pork loin instead of shoulder and trim visible fat.
  • Frying the paste needs only a little oil; each tablespoon is about 120 kcal.

History

Jjajangmyeon was developed by Chinese immigrants in Korea, and its early history is usually linked to the port city of Incheon in the early 20th century. It adapted a northern Chinese noodle dish to Korean tastes with a sweeter black bean paste. Today it is one of the most popular delivery meals in South Korea.
