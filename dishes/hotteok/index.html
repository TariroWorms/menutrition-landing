🥞 Hotteok (Korean Sweet Pancake)

Origin: South Korea

Hotteok is a Korean street-food pancake made from a soft yeasted wheat dough filled with brown sugar, cinnamon and chopped peanuts. It is pressed flat in an oiled pan until the outside is golden and crisp and the filling melts into a hot, gooey syrup. It is sold from street stalls, especially in winter, and eaten warm from a paper cup.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g plain flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tbsp sugar plus 80 g brown sugar for the filling
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 180 ml warm water
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 40 g roasted peanuts, chopped
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil for frying

Method

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, 1 tbsp sugar, salt and warm water into a sticky dough; cover and let it rise for 1 hour.
  2. Mix the brown sugar, cinnamon and peanuts for the filling.
  3. With oiled hands, divide the dough into 6 pieces, flatten each, add 1 tbsp filling and pinch closed.
  4. Fry seam-side down in a lightly oiled pan over medium heat for 1 minute, flip and press flat.
  5. Cook 2–3 minutes per side until golden and the filling has melted; serve warm, carefully.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a lightly oiled non-stick pan instead of plenty of oil; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Cut the brown sugar in the filling and let cinnamon carry the flavour.
  • Make smaller pancakes, and enjoy one as a treat rather than a meal.

History

Hotteok is generally said to have been introduced by Chinese merchants who settled in Korea in the late 19th century. It became one of the most familiar winter street snacks in South Korea.
