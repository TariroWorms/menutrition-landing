Encebollado (Ecuadorian tuna and cassava soup)

Origin: Ecuador

Encebollado is a fish soup in which fresh albacore tuna is poached in a broth with tomato, cumin and onion, then served with chunks of soft cassava. Each bowl is crowned with a generous heap of red onion pickled in lime juice and chopped cilantro. It is tangy, warming and filling. It is eaten for breakfast or lunch, often with banana chips, popcorn or bread on the side.

Recipe

6 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g fresh tuna (albacore)
  • 800 g cassava (yuca), peeled
  • 2 tomatoes (200 g)
  • 1 white onion (150 g) for the broth
  • 2 red onions (300 g), thinly sliced
  • juice of 5 limes (100 ml)
  • 1 tsp cumin, cilantro, salt
  • 2.5 litres water

Method

  1. Cover the sliced red onion with lime juice and salt and leave to pickle.
  2. Make a broth with water, tomato, white onion, cumin and cilantro stems; simmer 15 minutes.
  3. Poach the tuna in the broth for 15 minutes, lift out and flake it.
  4. Boil the cassava in the broth until tender, then cut into chunks.
  5. Blend a little of the broth with some cassava to thicken it and return everything to the pot.
  6. Serve hot topped with pickled onion and cilantro.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip fried banana chips and add extra pickled onion.
  • Use a large portion of broth and fish, less cassava.
  • Season with lime rather than extra salt.

History

Encebollado is strongly associated with Guayaquil and the Ecuadorian coast, where it is sold in specialised eateries from early morning. It is often called Ecuador’s national dish and is popular as a cure after a night out.
