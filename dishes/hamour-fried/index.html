🐟 Fried hamour

Fried hamour is the Gulf's favourite fish dish: thick fillets or steaks of hamour (orange-spotted grouper) rubbed with turmeric, cumin and garlic, lightly dusted with flour and fried until golden. The flesh stays white, firm and moist under a thin, spiced crust. It is served with lemon wedges, usually alongside sweet muhammar rice or plain white rice.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g hamour fillets, cut into 4 pieces
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • Juice of 1 lemon, plus wedges to serve
  • 4 tbsp wheat flour
  • Oil for shallow-frying, salt

Method

  1. Mix the garlic, turmeric, cumin, lemon juice and salt and rub over the fish; marinate 20 minutes.
  2. Pat off excess marinade and dust the pieces lightly with flour.
  3. Heat 1 cm of oil in a frying pan and fry the fish 3–4 minutes per side until golden and just cooked through.
  4. Drain on paper and serve with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Pan-fry in a thin film of oil, or bake at 220 °C, instead of shallow-frying.
  • Shake off as much flour as possible — a thinner coating absorbs less oil.
  • Serve with salad and a modest scoop of rice rather than a full plate of muhammar.

History

Hamour is one of the most prized fish of the Arabian Gulf and a mainstay of coastal cooking in the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait. Today fried hamour is on almost every Gulf seafood menu.
