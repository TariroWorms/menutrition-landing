🥔 Bramboráky (Czech potato pancakes)

Origin: Czech Republic · Bohemia

Bramboráky are Czech potato pancakes made from raw grated potato bound with egg and a little flour, and seasoned generously with garlic and marjoram. They are pan-fried until the edges are lacy and crisp while the centre stays soft. They are eaten hot on their own or as a side, often with a spoonful of sour cream.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg floury potatoes, peeled
  • 1 small onion
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 eggs
  • 4 tbsp plain flour
  • 2 tsp dried marjoram, salt and pepper
  • Vegetable oil for frying
  • 150 g sour cream to serve

Method

  1. Grate the potatoes and onion finely, then squeeze out as much liquid as possible in a clean cloth.
  2. Mix in the garlic, eggs, flour, marjoram, salt and pepper.
  3. Heat a thin layer of oil in a frying pan and spread spoonfuls of the mixture into thin pancakes.
  4. Fry for 3–4 minutes per side until deep golden and crisp, then drain on paper towels.
  5. Serve hot with sour cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Make the pancakes thin and fry in a non-stick pan with just a film of oil — every extra tablespoon is about 120 kcal.
  • Bake them on a lined tray at 220 °C, brushed lightly with oil, for a crisp result with much less fat.
  • Use a small spoonful of sour cream and add a crunchy salad on the side.

History

Potato pancakes spread across Central Europe once potatoes became a staple in the 18th and 19th centuries. The Czech version stands out for its strong garlic and marjoram flavour. Today bramboráky are a fixture of home kitchens, pubs and street-food stalls at Czech fairs and Christmas markets.
