Paraguayan asado (grilled beef with sopa paraguaya and cassava)

Origin: Paraguay

Paraguayan asado is a plate of beef cuts cooked slowly over wood or charcoal, seasoned mostly with coarse salt. It comes with sopa paraguaya, a dense cornbread baked with cheese and onion, plus boiled cassava. The meat is smoky and juicy, and the sides soak up its juices. It is the centerpiece of weekend family gatherings.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef short ribs or ribeye, in large pieces
  • 2 tsp coarse salt
  • 600 g cassava, peeled and cut
  • 200 g cornmeal
  • 200 g fresh cheese, crumbled
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2 eggs and 150 ml milk
  • 2 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Salt the meat generously and let it rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Prepare embers and grill the meat slowly, turning, for about 90 minutes.
  3. For the sopa paraguaya, cook the onions in oil until soft.
  4. Mix cornmeal, eggs, milk, cheese and onions into a thick batter.
  5. Bake in a greased dish at 190 °C for 40 minutes until golden.
  6. Boil the cassava in salted water for 25 minutes until tender.
  7. Rest the meat for 10 minutes, slice and serve with both sides.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose leaner cuts and trim visible fat before grilling.
  • Use less cheese and oil in the sopa paraguaya to lower the calories.
  • Fill half the plate with a fresh tomato and onion salad.

History

Grilling beef over open fire is shared across the Rio de la Plata region, where cattle ranching has long shaped the diet. In Paraguay the meat is paired with local staples such as cassava and corn rather than bread. Sopa paraguaya is considered a national dish despite its misleading name, since it is a solid cake.
