Chinese lemon chicken

Origin: Chinese takeaway · UK and North America

Chinese lemon chicken is chicken breast coated in a flour-and-egg batter, deep-fried until crisp and topped with a glossy, sweet-and-sour lemon sauce. The crunchy coating soaks up the bright, sticky sauce. It is served sliced over rice, often with a scattering of spring onion.

What it is made of

chickenlemon sauceflouregg

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g chicken breast, sliced
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 80 g flour
  • Oil for frying
  • Juice and zest of 2 lemons
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp cornflour mixed with 150 ml water
  • Spring onion to serve

Method

  1. Season the chicken with salt, dip it in egg, then coat in flour.
  2. Fry in hot oil in batches until golden and cooked through; drain.
  3. For the sauce, simmer the lemon juice, zest, sugar and cornflour water, stirring, until glossy.
  4. Pour the sauce over the chicken, scatter spring onion and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Ask for the sauce on the side and use just a spoonful — it is mostly sugar.
  • Pan-fry or oven-bake the coated chicken to cut the oil.
  • Pair it with steamed vegetables and a small portion of plain rice instead of fried rice.

History

Lemon chicken is a staple of Cantonese-style restaurants and takeaways outside China, especially in the UK, Canada, the US and Australia. Its exact origin is not well documented, and it is best known today as a takeaway favourite.
