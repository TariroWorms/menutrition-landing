Muhammar is a sweet Bahraini rice: long-grain rice cooked with date syrup and sugar, scented with saffron, cardamom and rose water, and finished with ghee. The grains turn glossy and amber with a gentle caramel sweetness. It is traditionally served as a side to savoury main dishes.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g basmati rice, rinsed
  • 80 ml date syrup
  • 40 g sugar
  • 1 pinch saffron, soaked in 2 tbsp warm water
  • 4 cardamom pods, crushed
  • 1 tbsp rose water
  • 2 tbsp ghee
  • Salt

Method

  1. Boil the rice in salted water for 6–7 minutes until half-cooked; drain.
  2. Warm the date syrup, sugar, cardamom and 100 ml water until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Return the rice to the pot, pour the syrup over and fold gently.
  4. Drizzle with saffron water, rose water and melted ghee.
  5. Cover tightly and steam on very low heat for 25 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tbsp ghee instead of 2 (1 tbsp ghee ≈ 110 kcal).
  • Skip the added sugar and rely on the date syrup for sweetness.
  • Serve a small portion (about 150 g) next to a lean protein and salad.

History

Muhammar is a traditional dish of Bahrain and the wider Gulf coast, where it remains a familiar part of home and festive meals.
