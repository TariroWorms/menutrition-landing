Pescado a lo macho (fish in spicy seafood sauce)

Origin: Peru

Pescado a lo macho is a bold coastal dish: golden pan-fried fish fillets smothered in a thick, spicy sauce of ají amarillo, ají panca, garlic and tomato loaded with shrimp, squid and mussels. The sauce is rich and fiery, which is said to explain the name “macho”. It is served with white rice and sometimes boiled potatoes.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 4 fish fillets (sea bass or similar), 150 g each
  • Flour for dusting
  • 150 g shrimp
  • 150 g squid rings
  • 200 g mussels
  • 2 tbsp ají amarillo paste
  • 1 tbsp ají panca paste
  • 1 rocoto or hot chilli, chopped (optional)
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tomatoes, grated
  • 200 ml fish stock
  • 100 ml evaporated milk
  • 3 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Season the fish, dust with flour and pan-fry until golden; keep warm.
  2. Fry garlic, ají amarillo, ají panca and rocoto until fragrant.
  3. Add grated tomato and cook for 5 minutes.
  4. Pour in the stock, add mussels and cook until they open.
  5. Add shrimp and squid and cook for 2 minutes, then stir in the milk.
  6. Pour the sauce over the fish and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Grill the fish instead of pan-frying it.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk or skip it for a lighter sauce.
  • Reduce the chilli for a milder version.

History

The dish is a classic of Peruvian cevicherías and seafood restaurants along the coast, especially in Lima and Callao. Its name is commonly linked to the strength of its heat.
