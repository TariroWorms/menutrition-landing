Spanish flan is a chilled baked custard of eggs, milk, sugar and vanilla set over liquid caramel. The custard is smooth and firm enough to slice, while the caramel melts into a thin, glossy sauce once the flan is turned out. It is one of the most common desserts on Spanish menus, often offered as 'flan casero' when made in-house.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 130 g sugar (for the custard)
  • 100 g sugar (for the caramel)
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 2 tbsp water

Method

  1. Cook 100 g sugar with the water until it turns a deep amber caramel and pour it into a mould.
  2. Heat the milk with the vanilla until steaming.
  3. Whisk the eggs, yolks and 130 g sugar, then pour in the hot milk while whisking.
  4. Strain the mixture over the set caramel.
  5. Bake in a water bath at 160 °C for about 50 minutes until set with a slight wobble.
  6. Chill for at least 4 hours before unmoulding.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the extra yolks and use whole eggs only for a lighter custard.
  • Cut the flan into 8 slices instead of 6 for a smaller serving.
  • Pair a slice with fresh berries rather than a second helping.

History

Caramel custards spread through Spain over several centuries and travelled with Spanish cooks to Latin America and the Philippines, where local versions developed. Today it remains a staple of the Spanish 'menú del día'.
