Pupusas revueltas (Salvadoran mixed pupusas)

Origin: El Salvador

Pupusas revueltas are thick masa discs filled with a mix of seasoned pork, mashed beans and cheese, then browned on a hot griddle. The outside is lightly crisp and the inside is soft and gooey. They are eaten by hand with curtido and a thin tomato sauce.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g masa harina
  • 350 ml warm water
  • 150 g cooked minced pork
  • 120 g refried red beans
  • 120 g quesillo or mozzarella, shredded
  • 1/2 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Mix masa harina, water and a pinch of salt into a soft dough and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Mix the pork, beans, cheese and onion into a thick paste for the filling.
  3. Roll a ball of dough, press a hollow into it, add filling and close it.
  4. Pat gently into a disc about 1 cm thick.
  5. Cook on a lightly oiled griddle 3-4 minutes per side until spotted golden.
  6. Serve hot with curtido and tomato salsa.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less cheese and more beans for extra fibre.
  • Brown on a dry non-stick pan to cut added oil.
  • Fill with lean pork or vegetables only for a lighter version.

History

Pupusas are considered the national dish of El Salvador and have a long indigenous corn-based heritage in the region. Pupuserías serving them are found in almost every town, and the dish has a dedicated national day.
