Nanjing salted duck (yan shui ya)

Origin: China · Jiangsu

A whole duck is rubbed with salt and spices, rested, then simmered gently in a ginger and star anise broth until just cooked. It is cooled and sliced, giving pale, tender meat with a fine savory flavor and a thin layer of fat under the skin. It is typically eaten as a cold dish with a little of the cooking liquid.

What it is made of

whole ducksaltgingerscallionsstar aniseblack pepper

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 duck, about 1.8 kg
  • 60 g coarse salt
  • 10 Sichuan peppercorns or black pepper
  • 3 star anise
  • 30 g ginger
  • 3 scallions
  • 2 l water

Method

  1. Rub the duck inside and out with salt and pepper and cure in the fridge for 6 to 8 hours.
  2. Rinse lightly and drop into a pot with ginger, scallion and star anise.
  3. Add enough water to cover and bring to a bare simmer.
  4. Poach gently for 45 to 60 minutes, never boiling hard.
  5. Lift out and cool the duck, then chill.
  6. Carve into thin slices and serve cold with a spoonful of the broth.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin to cut fat considerably.
  • Serve with cucumber or other fresh vegetables.
  • Use the cooled broth to make a light soup, skimming off the fat.

History

Nanjing is known as the home of salted duck, a dish with a long local tradition and a mainstay of the city's restaurants. It is often eaten in autumn when the osmanthus blooms, so it is sometimes called osmanthus duck.
