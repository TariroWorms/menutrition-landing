🫓 Khubz Arabi (Arabic Pita Bread)

Origin: Levant · Middle East

Khubz arabi is the round, thin Arabic flatbread that puffs in a very hot oven to form a pocket. Made from flour, water, yeast and salt, it is soft and lightly chewy, with two thin layers that can be split and filled. It is the everyday bread of the Arab world, torn to scoop hummus and mutabbal or wrapped around shawarma and falafel.

Recipe

8 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g wheat flour
  • 320 ml warm water
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp sugar

Method

  1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in the warm water for 5 minutes.
  2. Mix with flour and salt and knead 8–10 minutes until smooth.
  3. Cover and let rise about 1 hour until doubled.
  4. Divide into 8 balls, rest 10 minutes, then roll into 20 cm rounds about 4 mm thick.
  5. Bake on a preheated tray at 250 °C for 3–5 minutes until puffed; cover with a cloth to keep soft.

How to make it lighter

  • One medium round is about 60 g — measure your portion rather than tearing freely.
  • Split the pocket and fill it with salad and grilled meat instead of pairing with extra bread.
  • Use half a round for scooping dips and add raw vegetables as scoops.

History

Leavened flatbreads baked in hot ovens have been eaten in the Middle East for thousands of years, and khubz arabi is today the standard table bread from the Levant to the Gulf.
