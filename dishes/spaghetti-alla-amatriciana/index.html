🍝 Spaghetti all'amatriciana

Amatriciana is a pasta of crisp guanciale (cured pork cheek), tomato and grated pecorino romano, with a touch of chilli. It has very few ingredients and a lot of flavour: the rendered pork fat carries the sauce. Bucatini are just as traditional as spaghetti.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g spaghetti or bucatini
  • 150 g guanciale, cut into strips
  • 400 g peeled tomatoes, crushed
  • 1 small dried chilli
  • 60 g pecorino romano, grated
  • Salt

Method

  1. Fry the guanciale in a dry pan over medium heat for 6–8 minutes until the fat renders and the edges crisp. Set a few crisp pieces aside.
  2. Add the tomatoes and chilli and simmer for 10 minutes. Salt lightly — guanciale and pecorino are already salty.
  3. Cook the pasta until al dente, then toss it in the sauce with a splash of the cooking water.
  4. Off the heat, stir in the pecorino and top with the reserved guanciale.

How to make it lighter

  • A 350 g plate is about 580 kcal; a 250 g plate with a green salad is about 410.
  • Guanciale and pecorino make the dish salty — keep the rest of the day's salt in mind.
  • Pour off part of the rendered fat before adding the tomatoes.

History

The dish takes its name from Amatrice, a mountain town in Lazio. Shepherds there are often said to have cooked pasta with guanciale and pecorino, a tomato-free version close to today's pasta alla gricia. Tomato joined the recipe later, and the dish became a classic of Roman trattorias. The town's official recipe insists on guanciale and pecorino and leaves out onion and garlic. After the 2016 earthquake, restaurants around the world raised money for Amatrice by serving amatriciana.
