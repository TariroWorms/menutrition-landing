Sukiyaki is a Japanese hot pot in which thinly sliced beef, tofu, shiitake mushrooms and napa cabbage simmer in a sweet, savoury broth of soy sauce and sugar. It is usually cooked at the table in a shallow iron pan, and each bite is dipped in beaten raw egg before eating. The result is tender, glossy beef and soft vegetables soaked in a rich sweet-salty sauce.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g thinly sliced beef
  • 300 g firm tofu, cubed
  • 8 shiitake mushrooms
  • 1/4 napa cabbage, cut into pieces
  • 4 spring onions, cut into lengths
  • 100 ml soy sauce
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 200 ml water
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 4 eggs for dipping

Method

  1. Mix the soy sauce, sugar and water to make the broth.
  2. Heat the oil in a shallow pan and briefly sear a few slices of beef.
  3. Pour in some of the broth and arrange the tofu, mushrooms, cabbage and spring onions in sections.
  4. Simmer for 5–8 minutes until the vegetables are soft, adding more beef as you go.
  5. Beat each egg in a small bowl and dip the hot pieces in it before eating, topping up the broth as needed.

How to make it lighter

  • Fill most of the pan with cabbage, mushrooms and tofu and cook less beef per person.
  • Choose lean beef slices instead of heavily marbled cuts.
  • Reduce the sugar in the broth and do not drink the remaining sauce — it is high in sugar and salt.

History

Sukiyaki became popular in Japan in the late 19th century, when beef eating spread during the Meiji era. Regional styles differ: in the Kansai area the beef is first seared with sugar, while in the Kanto area it is simmered in a premixed sauce.
