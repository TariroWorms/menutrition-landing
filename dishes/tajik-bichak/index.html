Bichak (Tajik pumpkin pastry)

Origin: Tajikistan

Bichak are small baked pastries, usually triangular or half-moon shaped, with a soft shortcrust-like dough. The most loved filling is grated pumpkin cooked with onion, butter and a little sugar, though meat versions exist too. They come out golden and are eaten warm with green tea.

What it is made of

flourpumpkinonionbuttersugar

Recipe

6 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 120 g butter
  • 150 ml yogurt or milk
  • 500 g pumpkin, grated
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • salt, pinch of black pepper

Method

  1. Rub butter into flour with a pinch of salt, add yogurt and knead a soft dough; chill 20 minutes.
  2. Cook onion in a little butter, add pumpkin and cook until dry; season with sugar, salt and pepper.
  3. Roll the dough and cut circles about 10 cm wide.
  4. Fill each circle and fold into a triangle, pinching the edges.
  5. Bake at 190 °C for 25–30 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Replace part of the butter with yogurt for a lighter dough.
  • Skip the sugar – ripe pumpkin is sweet enough.
  • Use half wholemeal flour for more fibre.

History

Pumpkin pastries are typical of autumn in Tajikistan and neighbouring Uzbekistan, when squash is plentiful. Bichak are baked at home and sold at bazaars as a snack.
