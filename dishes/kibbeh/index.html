Kibbeh

Origin: Levant · Lebanon and Syria

Kibbeh are torpedo-shaped croquettes with a shell of fine bulgur kneaded with lean ground lamb and onion, stuffed with a filling of spiced minced lamb, onion and toasted pine nuts. Deep-fried until deep brown, they have a crisp crust, a firm shell and a juicy, aromatic center. They are served hot as part of a mezze spread or as a main with salad.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g fine bulgur
  • 500 g lean ground lamb (300 g for shell, 200 g for filling)
  • 2 onions
  • 40 g pine nuts
  • 2 tsp seven-spice (baharat)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Soak bulgur 15 minutes, then squeeze dry.
  2. Blend bulgur with 300 g lamb, 1 onion, salt and half the spices into a smooth paste; chill 20 minutes.
  3. For the filling, fry the remaining lamb with the second chopped onion and spices, then stir in toasted pine nuts.
  4. Take a walnut-sized piece of shell paste, hollow it with a wet finger, add filling and seal into an oval.
  5. Deep-fry at 175 °C for 4–5 minutes until deep brown; drain on paper.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the kibbeh brushed with a little oil at 200 °C instead of deep-frying.
  • Use the leanest lamb you can find and brown the filling without added oil.
  • Count pieces: serve 3 kibbeh with a large salad rather than a full platter.

History

Kibbeh is often regarded as a national dish of Lebanon and Syria and is eaten throughout the Levant; Aleppo in particular is known for its many kibbeh varieties. Shaping the hollow shell by hand is a valued kitchen skill passed down in families.
