Ecuadorian shrimp ceviche (ceviche de camarón)

Origin: Ecuador

Unlike Peruvian ceviche, the Ecuadorian version uses shrimp that are briefly boiled and then bathed in a soupy mix of lime, orange and tomato. The result is bright, slightly sweet and very refreshing. It is served cold in a bowl with crunchy chifles (plantain chips), toasted corn or even popcorn on the side.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g shrimp, peeled
  • 1 red onion, thinly sliced
  • 150 ml lime juice
  • 100 ml orange juice
  • 2 tomatoes, blended
  • 2 tbsp ketchup (optional)
  • 1 handful of cilantro
  • salt

Method

  1. Soak the onion in salted water with a little lime juice for 10 minutes, then drain.
  2. Boil the shrimp for 1-2 minutes until just pink, then cool them in ice water; keep half a cup (120 ml) of the cooking water.
  3. Mix lime and orange juice, blended tomato, ketchup and the cooled shrimp water.
  4. Add the shrimp and onion, season with salt and stir in chopped cilantro.
  5. Chill for at least 20 minutes and serve with plantain chips or toasted corn.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the ketchup and add more blended tomato to cut sugar.
  • Serve with toasted corn instead of fried chips to save fat.
  • Do not overcook the shrimp: one to two minutes keeps them tender.

History

Ceviche is eaten along much of the Pacific coast of Latin America, and each country has its own style. In Ecuador, cooking the seafood first and adding tomato became the norm. Shrimp ceviche is a classic weekend and hangover dish in coastal cities like Guayaquil.
