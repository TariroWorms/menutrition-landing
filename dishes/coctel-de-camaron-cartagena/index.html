Cóctel de camarón (Cartagena shrimp cocktail)

Origin: Colombia · Cartagena

Cóctel de camarón is the Colombian Caribbean take on ceviche: cooked shrimp are mixed with a pink sauce of ketchup, mayonnaise and plenty of lime juice, plus chopped onion and cilantro. It is tangy, slightly sweet and refreshing, and is served cold in a plastic cup with saltine crackers or plantain chips. In Cartagena it is sold by beach and street vendors, often mixed with oysters or other seafood.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g medium shrimp, peeled
  • 4 tbsp ketchup
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • Juice of 3 limes
  • 1 small red onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp chopped cilantro
  • Salt, a dash of hot sauce (optional)

Method

  1. Boil the shrimp in salted water for 2 minutes until pink, then cool in iced water.
  2. Mix the ketchup, mayonnaise and lime juice into a sauce.
  3. Add the onion, cilantro and a little salt.
  4. Fold in the shrimp and chill for at least 15 minutes.
  5. Taste and add more lime or hot sauce if you like.
  6. Serve cold in glasses with crackers.

How to make it lighter

  • Swap the mayonnaise for plain yogurt to cut the fat in half.
  • Add diced avocado, tomato and cucumber for volume and fiber.
  • Serve with lettuce cups instead of crackers or chips.

History

Unlike Peruvian ceviche, where raw fish is cured in lime, the Cartagena style uses pre-cooked shellfish and a creamy sauce. The street carts selling it near the walled city and the beaches have become part of the city's food scene.
