🥓 Crispy roast pork belly

Crispy roast pork belly is a Cantonese roast of pork belly rubbed with five-spice and salt on the meat side, while the skin is brushed with vinegar and salted so it puffs into a crackling, bubbly crust in the oven. Under the crust lie juicy layers of fat and meat. It is chopped into bite-sized pieces and served with rice or as part of a roast meat platter.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork belly, skin on, in one piece
  • 1½ tsp five-spice powder
  • 2 tsp salt, plus 2 tbsp coarse salt for the skin
  • 1 tbsp white vinegar
  • ½ tsp ground white pepper

Method

  1. Pour boiling water over the skin, pat dry and prick the skin all over with a skewer.
  2. Rub the meat side (not the skin) with five-spice, salt and pepper. Brush the skin with vinegar and leave uncovered in the fridge overnight.
  3. Cover the skin with a layer of coarse salt and roast at 180 °C for 60 minutes.
  4. Remove the salt crust, brush with a little more vinegar and roast at 230 °C for 20–30 minutes until the skin puffs and crackles.
  5. Rest 15 minutes, then chop into bite-sized pieces.

How to make it lighter

  • Pork belly is very fatty: keep the portion to about 100 g and fill the plate with steamed vegetables.
  • Let the fat drip away by roasting it on a rack over a tray.
  • Eat it with plain rice and greens rather than in rich noodle or rice dishes.

History

Known in Cantonese as siu yuk, crispy roast pork is part of the siu mei tradition of roast meats in Guangdong and Hong Kong, where it hangs in the windows of roast meat shops. It is also a common offering at festivals and family celebrations in southern China and among Chinese communities abroad.
