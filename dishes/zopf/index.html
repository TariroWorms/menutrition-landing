🍞 Zopf

Zopf is a Swiss braided loaf made from white flour, milk, butter, egg and yeast. The dough is plaited into a long braid and brushed with egg before baking, giving a glossy golden crust and a soft, slightly rich crumb. It is traditionally eaten on Sunday mornings, sliced and served with jam or alongside a breakfast spread.

Recipe

10 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g white flour
  • 300 ml milk, lukewarm
  • 60 g butter, soft
  • 10 g salt
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 20 g fresh yeast (or 7 g dried)
  • 1 egg, beaten, for glazing

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the lukewarm milk with the sugar.
  2. Mix the flour and salt, add the milk and butter and knead for 10 minutes into a smooth dough.
  3. Cover and let rise for about 1 hour, until doubled.
  4. Divide into two strands, braid them and place on a lined tray; let rise 30 minutes.
  5. Brush with beaten egg and bake at 200 °C for 35–40 minutes until deep golden.

How to make it lighter

  • One 80 g slice is a sensible portion; weigh it once to calibrate your eye.
  • Top with fresh fruit instead of a thick layer of butter and jam (1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal).
  • Pair a slice with a protein-rich breakfast item to stay full longer.

History

Zopf is closely associated with Swiss and southern German Sunday baking, and the braided shape has a long tradition in Alpine bread culture. Today it is sold in almost every Swiss bakery, especially at weekends.
