Kurut (Kyrgyz dried sour milk balls)

Origin: Kyrgyzstan

Kurut are small balls of thick strained sour milk, salted and dried in the sun or wind until hard as stone. They taste intensely sour and salty and are slowly nibbled or sucked like a sweet. Crumbled and soaked in water, kurut also becomes a tangy sauce for soups and bread dishes. You will find bags of them at every Kyrgyz bazaar.

Recipe

4 servings4320 min

Ingredients

  • 2 l plain yogurt or sour milk
  • 1-2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Line a colander with cheesecloth and pour in the yogurt.
  2. Tie up the cloth and hang it over a bowl to drain for 24 hours until very thick.
  3. Mix in the salt thoroughly.
  4. Roll into balls the size of a walnut and place on a tray.
  5. Dry in a sunny, airy place or a very low oven for several days until hard.

How to make it lighter

  • It is very rich in protein, but also salt: a couple of balls is enough.
  • Use less salt if you plan to eat them fresh within a week.
  • Soak in water to make a light dressing instead of creamy sauces.

History

Kurut was a way for nomadic herders to preserve summer milk through the winter and on long journeys. It is made from cow, sheep or goat milk depending on the region. It remains a common snack for children and travellers across Central Asia.
