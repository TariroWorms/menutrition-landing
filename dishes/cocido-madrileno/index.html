Cocido madrileño is Madrid's great chickpea stew, simmered slowly with beef, chorizo, morcilla blood sausage, cabbage and potatoes. It is rich and deeply savoury, with soft chickpeas soaking up the paprika-tinted broth. Traditionally it is eaten in stages, or vuelcos: first the broth as a soup, then the chickpeas and vegetables, and finally the meats.

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g dried chickpeas, soaked overnight
  • 600 g beef shank or brisket
  • 1 chorizo (about 150 g)
  • 1 morcilla (about 150 g)
  • ½ small cabbage, cut into wedges
  • 4 medium potatoes
  • 2 carrots, 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves
  • Salt, 1 tbsp olive oil

Method

  1. Put the beef in a large pot with 3 litres of water, bring to the boil and skim.
  2. Add the drained chickpeas (in a net if you like), carrots and onion; simmer gently for 2 hours.
  3. Add the chorizo and potatoes and cook for 30 minutes more; season with salt.
  4. Meanwhile boil the cabbage and morcilla separately for 20 minutes so they do not cloud the broth, then fry the drained cabbage briefly with garlic in the oil.
  5. Serve the broth first, then the chickpeas and vegetables, then the sliced meats.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the broth and lift off the solidified fat before serving.
  • Go light on chorizo and morcilla, which are the fattiest parts, and take more chickpeas, cabbage and beef.
  • Make the broth course a meal on its own with a few chickpeas and vegetables.

History

Cocido is often linked to adafina, the slow-cooked chickpea stew of Spain's Sephardic Jews, though its exact origins are debated. It has long been one of Madrid's signature dishes and remains a classic winter lunch in the city's taverns.
