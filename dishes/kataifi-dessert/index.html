🍯 Kataifi

Origin: Eastern Mediterranean · Greece and Turkey

Kataifi is a pastry of fine shredded phyllo threads wrapped around a filling of chopped walnuts and cinnamon, baked until crisp and golden, then soaked in honey syrup. The result is a crunchy, nest-like roll that is sticky and sweet, with a warm spice note. It is cut into pieces and served at room temperature with coffee.

Recipe

12 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 450 g kataifi (shredded phyllo) dough
  • 250 g walnuts, chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 100 ml light oil
  • For the syrup: 250 g honey, 200 g sugar, 250 ml water, 1 cinnamon stick, a strip of lemon peel

Method

  1. Simmer the syrup ingredients for 10 minutes and let cool completely.
  2. Mix the walnuts with the cinnamon.
  3. Loosen the pastry threads, take a handful, place a spoon of walnuts at one end and roll tightly.
  4. Place the rolls in a tray, drizzle with the oil and bake at 180 °C for 40–45 minutes until golden.
  5. Pour the cool syrup over the hot pastry and leave for several hours to absorb.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small pieces — this is a dense, syrupy sweet.
  • Use less syrup; the pastry stays crisp with only a light soaking.
  • Reduce the walnut filling slightly and add more cinnamon for flavour.

History

Shredded-pastry sweets are found across the former Ottoman lands, known as kadayıf in Turkey and kunafa in the Arab world. The walnut-and-syrup roll is especially popular in Greece, where it is a standard of pastry shops.
