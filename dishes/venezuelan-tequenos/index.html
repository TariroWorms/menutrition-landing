Tequeños (Venezuelan cheese sticks)

Origin: Venezuela

Tequeños are Venezuela's favourite party snack: fingers of firm, salty white cheese wrapped in a ribbon of thin wheat dough and deep-fried. The dough turns crisp and golden while the cheese inside softens and stretches. They are served hot by the dozen at birthdays, weddings and office parties, often with a garlic or pink sauce for dipping.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g wheat flour
  • 60 g cold butter, 1 egg
  • 10 g sugar, 4 g salt
  • 90 ml cold water
  • 400 g firm white cheese (queso blanco duro or halloumi)
  • oil for frying

Method

  1. Rub butter into flour, sugar and salt, add egg and water and knead to a firm, smooth dough; rest 30 minutes.
  2. Cut the cheese into sticks about 1×1×7 cm.
  3. Roll the dough very thin and cut into strips 1.5 cm wide.
  4. Wrap each cheese stick in a spiral of dough, overlapping slightly and covering the ends.
  5. Fry in oil at 175 °C for 3–4 minutes until golden; drain and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake at 210 °C or use an air fryer, brushing with a little oil.
  • Use a lower-salt firm cheese.
  • Serve with a yogurt-garlic dip instead of mayonnaise sauces.

History

Tequeños are commonly linked to the town of Los Teques near Caracas, from which their name is said to come. Over the 20th century they became the standard snack at Venezuelan celebrations, and frozen tequeños are now sold wherever the diaspora lives.
