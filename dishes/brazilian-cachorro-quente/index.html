Cachorro-quente (Brazilian hot dog)

Origin: Brazil

The Brazilian cachorro-quente is a hot dog taken to the extreme. Sausages are simmered in a tomato and onion sauce, tucked into a soft bun and buried under sweetcorn, peas, grated cheese and crisp potato sticks. It is messy, generous and sold from carts outside schools, stadiums and bus stations.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 4 hot dog buns
  • 8 sausages (about 400 g)
  • 300 g tomato sauce
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 100 g sweetcorn
  • 80 g peas
  • 80 g grated mozzarella
  • 60 g potato sticks
  • Ketchup and mustard to serve

Method

  1. Soften the onion in a little oil for 5 minutes.
  2. Slice the sausages into rounds or leave whole and add them to the pan.
  3. Pour in the tomato sauce and simmer for 10 minutes.
  4. Warm the corn and peas in a separate pan.
  5. Split the buns and fill with sausages and plenty of sauce.
  6. Top with corn, peas, cheese and a handful of potato sticks just before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use chicken or turkey sausages to cut fat.
  • Swap potato sticks for extra corn or grated carrot for less frying oil.
  • Choose wholegrain buns and keep to one sausage per bun.

History

Hot dogs reached Brazil in the twentieth century and quickly took on local toppings. Every city has its own style, and in São Paulo and Curitiba the long list of extras has become a point of pride for street vendors.
