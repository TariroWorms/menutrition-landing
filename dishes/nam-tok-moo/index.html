🥗 Nam tok moo

Nam tok moo is a grilled pork salad from northeastern Thailand: slices of charred pork neck or shoulder are tossed with lime juice, fish sauce, dried chilli, toasted ground rice, shallots and lots of fresh mint. It is sour, salty, smoky and spicy, with a nutty crunch from the rice powder. It is eaten with sticky rice and raw vegetables such as cabbage and long beans.

Recipe

2 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g pork neck or shoulder
  • 3 tbsp lime juice
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tsp dried chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp toasted rice powder
  • 2 shallots, thinly sliced
  • 1 large handful mint leaves
  • 2 spring onions, sliced, and a little fresh coriander
  • 1 tsp sugar

Method

  1. Season the pork with a pinch of salt and grill over high heat until charred and just cooked; rest 5 minutes.
  2. Slice the pork thinly against the grain.
  3. Mix lime juice, fish sauce, sugar and chilli in a bowl.
  4. Toss in the warm pork, shallots and rice powder.
  5. Fold through mint, spring onion and coriander and serve with raw vegetables.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose pork tenderloin instead of fatty neck.
  • Serve with plenty of raw cabbage and cucumber and a small portion of sticky rice.
  • Trim the fat from the pork before grilling.

History

Nam tok means “waterfall” in Thai, a name usually explained by the juices that drip from the meat as it grills. The dish comes from Isan, the Lao-speaking northeast of Thailand, and is closely related to larb; it is now found at Isan restaurants throughout Thailand.
