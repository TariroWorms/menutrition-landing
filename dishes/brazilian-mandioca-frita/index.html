Mandioca frita (fried cassava)

Origin: Brazil

Mandioca frita is cassava root first boiled until tender, then cut into thick sticks and deep-fried. The outside turns golden and crackly while the inside stays soft and almost creamy, with a mild, slightly sweet taste. In Brazil it is a favourite bar snack and a side for barbecued meat, often served with a dip or a sprinkle of salt. It is naturally gluten-free.

What it is made of

cassavaoilsalt

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg fresh cassava (or frozen, peeled)
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • Salt

Method

  1. Peel the cassava, cut into 8 cm pieces and remove the woody core.
  2. Boil in salted water 20-25 minutes until tender but not falling apart.
  3. Drain, cool and cut into thick sticks.
  4. Deep-fry at 180 °C for 5-6 minutes until golden and crisp.
  5. Drain on paper and sprinkle with salt.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the boiled sticks brushed with a little oil at 220 °C instead of frying.
  • Let the sticks dry well before frying so they absorb less oil.
  • Pair with grilled fish and salad rather than other fried foods.

History

Cassava was domesticated in South America and was a staple for Indigenous peoples long before colonisation. Frying it is a more modern habit, and today mandioca frita is a standard item in Brazilian botecos and churrascarias.
