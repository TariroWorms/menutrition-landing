🧀 Croûte au fromage with ham

Origin: Switzerland

This version of the Swiss croûte au fromage adds a slice of cooked ham between the wine-soaked bread and the thick layer of melted cheese. Baked until golden and often topped with a fried egg, it combines salty ham, tangy bubbling cheese and bread that is soft inside and crisp at the edges. It is a filling dish of Alpine restaurants, served with pickles or salad.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thick slices of day-old bread
  • 200 g Gruyère or Vacherin cheese, grated
  • 100 ml dry white wine
  • 4 slices cooked ham (about 120 g)
  • 2 eggs
  • Black pepper, pinch of nutmeg

Method

  1. Place the bread in an ovenproof dish and drizzle each slice with the wine.
  2. Lay a slice of ham on each piece of bread.
  3. Mix the cheese with one egg, pepper and nutmeg and spread it thickly over the bread.
  4. Bake at 220 °C for 10–15 minutes until the cheese is melted and golden.
  5. Meanwhile fry the remaining egg (or one per person) and place it on top before serving, if you like.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 120 g of a strongly flavoured cheese instead of 200 g — the taste stays bold.
  • Skip the fried egg on top, or poach it instead of frying.
  • Serve one slice with a large salad of crisp leaves and pickles rather than two.

History

Cheese croûtes come from the Swiss and French Alps, where they were a thrifty way to use stale bread and local cheese. Adding ham is one of the most common variations on Swiss menus, particularly in ski resorts and mountain huts.
