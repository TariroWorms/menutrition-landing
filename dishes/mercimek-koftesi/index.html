🥬 Lentil köfte

Origin: Turkey

Mercimek köftesi are soft, vegan patties of red lentils and fine bulgur, kneaded with sautéed onion, tomato paste, spices and plenty of parsley. The patties themselves are not fried: the hot lentils simply soften the bulgur. The mixture is shaped by hand into small ovals, and they are eaten wrapped in lettuce leaves with a squeeze of lemon, as a meze or light meal.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g red lentils
  • 150 g fine bulgur
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp chili flakes
  • 1 bunch parsley and 4 spring onions, chopped
  • Juice of 1 lemon, salt
  • Lettuce leaves to serve

Method

  1. Simmer the lentils in 600 ml water for 15 minutes until soft and the water is absorbed.
  2. Take off the heat, stir in the bulgur, cover and leave for 20 minutes.
  3. Meanwhile fry the onion in the oil until soft, then stir in the tomato paste and spices.
  4. Knead the onion mixture into the lentils and bulgur, then add the herbs, lemon juice and salt.
  5. Shape into small oval patties and serve on lettuce leaves with lemon.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the oil to one tablespoon — each tablespoon is about 120 kcal.
  • Wrap each köfte in a large lettuce leaf to add volume without many calories.
  • Count portions: 4–5 small köfte make a sensible serving.

History

Lentil köfte is a home-style dish of modern Turkish cooking, often made for women's gatherings, picnics and as a meatless alternative to raw çiğ köfte. No reliable record of its first appearance exists; today it is a staple of Turkish home cooking and meze tables.
