🍡 Gulab jamun

Origin: India · South Asia

Gulab jamun are soft, spongy balls of dough made from milk powder, a little flour and ghee, deep-fried slowly until deep golden brown and then soaked in a warm sugar syrup scented with cardamom and rose water. They absorb the syrup and become juicy and melt-in-the-mouth. They are served warm or at room temperature at weddings, festivals such as Diwali and Eid, and as an everyday sweet.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g milk powder
  • 30 g flour
  • 30 g ghee, melted
  • ¼ tsp baking soda
  • About 60 ml water to bind
  • Syrup: 300 g sugar, 300 ml water, 4 crushed cardamom pods, 1 tbsp rose water
  • Ghee or oil for frying

Method

  1. Simmer the sugar, water and cardamom for 5 minutes, then add the rose water and keep the syrup warm.
  2. Mix the milk powder, flour and baking soda, rub in the melted ghee, then add water little by little to make a soft, smooth dough.
  3. Roll into about 18 small crack-free balls.
  4. Fry in ghee or oil over low heat, turning constantly, for 6–8 minutes until evenly dark golden.
  5. Drop the hot balls into the warm syrup and soak for at least 1 hour before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Eat one or two pieces and leave the extra syrup in the bowl — much of the sugar is in it.
  • Make the balls smaller: you still get a whole piece, with fewer calories.
  • Serve with fresh fruit to fill the plate instead of a third jamun.

History

Gulab jamun is eaten across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Nepal. Its name joins the Persian word gulab, rose water, with jamun, a dark South Asian fruit of similar size and colour; similar syrup-soaked fried dough sweets are found across the Middle East and Central Asia, and the dish is often linked to that wider tradition.
