Cantonese sesame balls (jian dui)

Origin: China · Guangdong
272kcal

Jian dui are hollow golden balls with a thin crisp shell, a chewy glutinous rice layer and a sweet red bean or lotus paste center. They puff up in the fryer, becoming large and light. They are a classic of dim sum and of Lunar New Year, eaten warm.

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g glutinous rice flour
  • 60 g sugar
  • 200 ml hot water
  • 250 g sweet red bean paste
  • 100 g white sesame seeds
  • Oil for deep frying

Method

  1. Mix flour and sugar with hot water into a soft dough and rest 10 minutes.
  2. Divide into 16 pieces and flatten each.
  3. Fill with a spoonful of bean paste and seal into a ball.
  4. Dampen the balls and roll in sesame seeds.
  5. Fry in 150 degrees C oil, pressing them gently so they expand, for 8 to 10 minutes until golden.
  6. Drain and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air fry for a lighter version, though they will be less puffy.
  • Make them smaller to keep the portion in check.
  • Use a lower sugar bean paste.

History

They are an old Chinese festive sweet, tied to Lunar New Year because the round golden shape stands for fullness and prosperity. The balls are found across southern China and in Chinese communities abroad.
