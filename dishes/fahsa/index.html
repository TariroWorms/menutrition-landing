Fahsa is a Yemeni stew of shredded lamb in a rich broth with tomato, garlic and chili, brought to the table still bubbling in a heavy stone pot. Just before serving it is topped with whipped fenugreek, which forms a frothy, slightly bitter layer. It is eaten hot, usually at lunch.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g lamb on the bone
  • 1 litre water for the broth
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1–2 green chilies, chopped
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp ground fenugreek, soaked in water for 2 hours
  • 1 small bunch cilantro
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Simmer the lamb in the water with salt and pepper for about 1½ hours until very tender; shred the meat and keep the broth.
  2. Fry the onion in the oil, then add the tomatoes, garlic and chilies and cook to a thick sauce.
  3. Add the shredded lamb and about 300 ml of the broth and simmer until thick.
  4. Drain the soaked fenugreek and whip it with a little cold water until light and foamy.
  5. Heat a stone or cast-iron pot until very hot, add the stew, spoon the fenugreek foam on top and sprinkle with cilantro. Serve bubbling.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb and skim the broth after cooling.
  • Measure the stew by the ladle so the portion stays clear.
  • Add more tomato and chili to the sauce for volume and flavour.

History

Fahsa is closely associated with Sana'a and is one of Yemen's best-known dishes. It is traditionally cooked and served in a heavy stone pot that keeps the stew boiling at the table.
