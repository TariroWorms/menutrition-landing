Dolmades

Origin: Greece

Dolmades are the Greek version of stuffed vine leaves: small, tight rolls filled with rice and plenty of fresh herbs such as dill, mint and parsley, simmered in lemon juice and olive oil. They taste bright, tangy and herby, with a tender bite. They are served cool or at room temperature as a meze, often alongside olives and salads.

What it is made of

grape leavesriceherbslemonolive oil

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 40 grape leaves in brine, rinsed
  • 250 g short-grain rice
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 1 bunch parsley, chopped
  • 1 small bunch dill or mint, chopped
  • 2 lemons, juiced
  • 80 ml olive oil
  • 500 ml water
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Soften the onions in half the olive oil, then stir in the rice, herbs, salt and pepper.
  2. Place a leaf vein-side up, add a teaspoon of filling, fold in the sides and roll tightly.
  3. Line a pot with a few leaves and pack the rolls in snug layers.
  4. Pour over the water, lemon juice and remaining oil, weigh down with a plate and simmer covered for 50 minutes.
  5. Cool in the pot and serve at room temperature with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the olive oil back to 2–3 tbsp; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Count your rolls: 5–6 pieces make a typical appetizer portion.
  • Serve with a large salad of tomato and cucumber to round out a light meal.

History

Stuffed vine leaves are shared by the cuisines of Greece, Turkey and the wider eastern Mediterranean. In Greece the meat-free, oil-based version is especially popular during Lent and in summer.
