🥔 Draniki (potato pancakes)

Origin: Belarus · Eastern Europe

Draniki are Belarusian potato pancakes made from finely grated raw potato mixed with onion, a little egg and flour, then fried until the edges are crisp and golden. Inside they stay soft and tender. They are eaten hot, straight from the pan, with a generous spoonful of sour cream, as a breakfast, a side dish or a meal on their own.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg potatoes
  • 1 onion
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 4 tbsp vegetable oil for frying
  • 150 g sour cream to serve

Method

  1. Peel and finely grate the potatoes and onion, then squeeze out excess liquid.
  2. Mix in the egg, flour, salt and pepper.
  3. Heat the oil in a frying pan and drop in spoonfuls of the mixture, flattening them slightly.
  4. Fry 3–4 minutes per side until crisp and golden, then drain on paper towels.
  5. Serve hot with sour cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Brush the pan with oil or bake the pancakes on a lined tray instead of shallow-frying.
  • Use light sour cream and keep it to 1–2 tbsp: 1 tbsp full-fat sour cream ≈ 30 kcal.
  • Serve 2–3 draniki with a large salad instead of a full plate.

History

Draniki are considered a national dish of Belarus, where potatoes became a staple in the 19th century. Similar grated-potato pancakes are eaten across Eastern and Central Europe, known as deruny in Ukraine and placki ziemniaczane in Poland.
