🟢 Klepon

Origin: Indonesia · Java

Klepon are Indonesian sweet rice cakes: small green balls of glutinous rice dough coloured and scented with pandan, filled with palm sugar and boiled until they float. Rolled in freshly grated coconut, they are chewy outside and release a burst of molten palm sugar when bitten. They are sold as a snack at markets.

What it is made of

glutinous rice flourpandanpalm sugarcoconut

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g glutinous rice powder
  • 150 ml pandan juice (blended pandan leaves and water, strained)
  • 80 g palm sugar, finely chopped
  • 100 g freshly grated coconut
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Mix the rice powder with the pandan juice into a smooth, soft dough.
  2. Roll into small balls, press a little palm sugar into each and seal well.
  3. Drop into boiling water and cook until they float, then 1 minute more.
  4. Lift out and roll in coconut mixed with a pinch of salt.

How to make it lighter

  • Make the balls smaller and count them: four to six is a typical snack portion.
  • Use a little less palm sugar in each ball.
  • Roll lightly in coconut rather than coating thickly.

History

Klepon is a traditional jajan pasar, or market snack, of Java and is eaten across Indonesia and in Malaysia, where similar balls are called onde-onde. It remains a common sight at morning markets and snack stalls.
