🍟 French Fries

French fries are sticks of potato deep-fried until golden and crisp on the outside and fluffy inside, then salted while hot. The best fries are cooked twice — first at a lower temperature to cook them through, then hotter to crisp them. They are served as a side to burgers, steak, fish and sandwiches, or on their own with sauces for dipping.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg floury potatoes
  • 1.5 l vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Peel the potatoes and cut into 1 cm sticks; rinse in cold water and dry very well.
  2. Heat the oil to 160 °C and fry the potatoes in batches for 5–6 minutes until soft but pale; drain.
  3. Raise the oil to 185 °C and fry again for 2–3 minutes until golden and crisp.
  4. Drain on paper towels, salt immediately and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Thicker-cut fries absorb less oil per gram than thin shoestring fries.
  • For a lighter version, toss the sticks with 1 tbsp oil and bake at 220 °C for 30–35 minutes, turning once.
  • Order a small portion and fill the rest of the plate with salad or grilled vegetables.

History

Both Belgium and France claim fried potatoes, and the true origin is disputed; a popular story places them in 19th-century Paris street stalls or along the Meuse valley in Belgium. The English name "French fries" is commonly linked to the "French" style of cutting vegetables into thin strips.
