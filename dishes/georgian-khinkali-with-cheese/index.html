Khinkali with cheese

Origin: Georgia
750kcal

Cheese khinkali are filled with a mix of crumbled Imeretian cheese and sulguni, sometimes with a little cottage cheese and butter. When boiled, the cheese melts into a soft, stretchy centre; they can also be fried afterwards for a crisp golden skin. They are eaten hot by hand, like meat khinkali, often with black pepper.

Recipe

4 servings80 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml water
  • 300 g Imeretian cheese
  • 200 g sulguni
  • 30 g butter
  • salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Knead a firm dough from flour, water and salt and rest it for 30 minutes.
  2. Crumble or grate the cheeses and mix with soft butter.
  3. Roll the dough thin and cut out 10 cm circles.
  4. Put a spoonful of cheese in the centre, pleat the edges and twist closed.
  5. Boil in salted water for 8–10 minutes, or boil and then fry in a little butter until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Boil rather than fry them to avoid extra fat.
  • Mix in cottage cheese to lower salt and fat while keeping protein.
  • Pair with a crisp vegetable salad to balance the dough and cheese.

History

Khinkali originated in the mountainous regions of eastern Georgia, and cheese fillings are a later, widely loved variation. Leftover boiled khinkali are often fried the next day.
