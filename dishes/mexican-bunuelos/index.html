Buñuelos (Mexican fried dough discs)

Origin: Mexico

Mexican buñuelos are thin rounds of dough stretched almost translucent, fried until bubbly and shatteringly crisp. They are either dusted with cinnamon sugar or soaked with a warm piloncillo syrup scented with cinnamon and guava. They are a Christmas and New Year treat, sold at fairs and posadas.

Recipe

12 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 1 egg
  • 30 g butter
  • 150 ml warm water
  • 1 tsp baking powder, pinch of salt
  • oil for frying
  • Syrup: 200 g piloncillo, 250 ml water, 1 cinnamon stick

Method

  1. Mix flour, baking powder, salt, egg, butter and water into a smooth dough; rest 30 minutes.
  2. Simmer piloncillo, water and cinnamon for 15 minutes into a light syrup.
  3. Divide the dough into 12 balls and roll or stretch each into a very thin round.
  4. Fry one at a time in hot oil (180 °C) for about 1 minute per side until golden; drain.
  5. Serve drizzled with syrup or dusted with cinnamon sugar.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose the cinnamon-sugar version with a light dusting instead of soaking in syrup.
  • Drain very well on paper to remove excess oil.
  • Share one buñuelo; they are large and rich.

History

Fried dough sweets arrived in Mexico with the Spanish, and the Mexican buñuelo developed its own thin, wide shape. Recipes and syrups vary by region.
