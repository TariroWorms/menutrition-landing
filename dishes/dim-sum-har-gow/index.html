🥟 Har Gow

Origin: China · Guangdong

Har gow are Cantonese shrimp dumplings wrapped in a thin, translucent dough made from wheat starch. Inside is a juicy filling of chopped shrimp with crunchy bamboo shoot and a little ginger. The pleated half-moon dumplings are steamed in bamboo baskets and served hot, usually four to a basket, as one of the signature items of dim sum.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g raw shrimp, peeled
  • 60 g bamboo shoot, finely diced
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 130 g wheat starch
  • 20 g tapioca starch
  • 180 ml boiling water
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt, white pepper, pinch of sugar

Method

  1. Chop half the shrimp finely and the rest coarsely; mix with bamboo shoot, ginger, salt, pepper and sugar, then chill.
  2. Mix the starches, pour in the boiling water, stir, add the oil and knead into a smooth dough.
  3. Roll small pieces into thin 7 cm rounds and fill each with a teaspoon of filling.
  4. Fold and pleat into half-moons, sealing the edges well.
  5. Steam on lined bamboo baskets over high heat for 6 minutes and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Steamed dumplings like har gow are a lighter choice than fried dim sum such as spring rolls.
  • Go easy on dipping sauces and chili oil; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Count dumplings rather than baskets: one basket of four is a typical single serving.

History

Har gow are usually credited to Guangzhou, where they became a fixture of teahouse dim sum. They are often seen as a test of a dim sum chef's skill because of the delicate, many-pleated wrapper.
