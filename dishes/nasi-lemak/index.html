🍚 Nasi Lemak

Origin: Malaysia

Nasi lemak is rice cooked in coconut milk with pandan leaf, served with a spicy-sweet sambal, crisp fried anchovies, roasted peanuts, a boiled or fried egg and cool cucumber slices. The rice is fragrant and rich, the sambal fiery, and the anchovies and peanuts add salty crunch. It is a classic Malaysian breakfast, often wrapped in banana leaf.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g jasmine rice
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 2 pandan leaves, knotted
  • 60 g dried anchovies
  • 60 g peanuts
  • 4 eggs
  • 1 cucumber, sliced
  • Sambal: 10 dried chilies, 1 onion, 2 cloves garlic, 1 tbsp sugar, tamarind water

Method

  1. Rinse the rice and cook with coconut milk, 150 ml water, pandan and a pinch of salt until fluffy.
  2. Blend soaked chilies, onion and garlic; fry the paste in 2 tbsp oil for 10 minutes, then add tamarind water, sugar and salt and simmer until thick.
  3. Fry the anchovies and then the peanuts separately in a little oil until crisp; drain.
  4. Boil the eggs for 8–9 minutes and halve them.
  5. Serve the rice with sambal, anchovies, peanuts, egg and cucumber.

How to make it lighter

  • Make the rice with half coconut milk and half water to cut the fat.
  • Dry-roast the peanuts and anchovies in the oven instead of frying them.
  • Choose a boiled rather than fried egg and add extra cucumber.

History

'Nasi lemak' means 'rich' or 'creamy rice' in Malay, referring to the coconut milk. It is widely considered Malaysia's national dish and is also eaten in Singapore, Brunei and southern Thailand, from roadside stalls to restaurants.
