Pan de mallorca (Puerto Rican sweet coiled bun)

Origin: Puerto Rico

Pan de mallorca is a soft, eggy, buttery coiled bun made from enriched dough and baked until golden, then thickly dusted with powdered sugar. It tastes lightly sweet and rich with a fluffy crumb. Puerto Ricans eat it for breakfast, often split and toasted with butter or filled with ham and cheese, alongside coffee.

Recipe

10 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g wheat flour
  • 200 ml warm milk
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 80 g sugar
  • 3 eggs
  • 100 g butter
  • 1 pinch salt
  • powdered sugar for dusting

Method

  1. Mix yeast with warm milk and a spoon of sugar and let it foam.
  2. Combine flour, sugar, salt, eggs and the yeast mixture and knead, then work in the soft butter until smooth.
  3. Let rise in a covered bowl for 1.5 hours.
  4. Divide into portions, roll each into a long rope and coil into a spiral.
  5. Let rise again for 45 minutes, then bake at 180 C for 15-18 minutes until golden.
  6. Cool slightly and dust generously with powdered sugar.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half whole-wheat flour and less butter.
  • Dust lightly with sugar.
  • Fill with ham and cheese for a more balanced breakfast.

History

The bun takes its name from the Spanish island of Mallorca, where the similar ensaimada comes from, and was brought to Puerto Rico by Spanish bakers. It became a local breakfast icon with its own island character.
