🦐 Shrimp Rice Noodle Rolls

Origin: China · Guangdong

Cheung fun are wide, silky sheets of steamed rice noodle rolled around a filling, here plump shrimp with chopped scallions. The rolls are soft, slippery and almost translucent, and they are cut into pieces and dressed at the table with a sweetened soy sauce and a few drops of sesame oil. They are a staple of Cantonese dim sum, eaten with tea in the morning or at lunch.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 4 fresh rice noodle sheets (about 400 g)
  • 250 g raw shrimp, peeled
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp water
  • 2 scallions, finely sliced

Method

  1. Lightly season the shrimp with salt and blanch them for 1 minute.
  2. Lay a rice noodle sheet flat, place a row of shrimp and scallion along one edge and roll up.
  3. Steam the rolls for 4–5 minutes until hot and glossy.
  4. Mix the soy sauce, sugar, water and sesame oil.
  5. Cut the rolls into pieces, pour the sauce over and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Pour the sauce sparingly; the soy dressing adds sodium and some sugar.
  • Choose shrimp rolls over fried-dough fillings, which absorb much more oil.
  • Share one plate and add steamed greens to make a fuller meal.

History

Rice noodle rolls are a classic of Cantonese cuisine and are usually traced to Guangdong province. They are now a standard item in dim sum restaurants in Hong Kong, southern China and Chinese communities worldwide.
