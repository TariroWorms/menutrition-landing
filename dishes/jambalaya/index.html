🥘 Jambalaya

Jambalaya is a one-pot Louisiana rice dish in which long-grain rice simmers in a spiced tomato broth with chicken, smoky sausage and shrimp. Onion, bell pepper and celery form the base, and cayenne, paprika and thyme give it a warm, peppery kick. The rice soaks up all the juices, so the result is moist and full of flavour. It is served straight from the pot as a hearty main course.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g long-grain rice
  • 400 g boneless chicken thighs, diced
  • 150 g chorizo sausage, sliced
  • 250 g peeled shrimp
  • 1 onion, 1 green bell pepper and 2 celery sticks, diced
  • 400 g chopped tomatoes
  • 2 tsp Cajun spice mix (paprika, cayenne, thyme, garlic)
  • 700 ml water or chicken stock, 1 tbsp oil, salt

Method

  1. Brown the chorizo in the oil, lift it out, then brown the chicken in the same pan.
  2. Add the onion, pepper and celery and cook for 5 minutes until soft.
  3. Stir in the spices, tomatoes and rice, then pour in the stock, add the chorizo back and season.
  4. Cover and simmer on low heat for 20 minutes without stirring.
  5. Lay the shrimp on top, cover and cook 5–7 minutes more until the shrimp are pink and the rice is tender.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less chorizo and more chicken and shrimp for a leaner pot with the same smoky taste.
  • Add extra peppers, celery and tomato so a full plate holds less rice.
  • Brown the meat in 1 tbsp oil only; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Jambalaya developed in Louisiana, where Spanish, French, West African and Caribbean cooking met; it is often compared to Spanish paella. The red, tomato-based style is usually called Creole jambalaya and is associated with New Orleans, while the Cajun countryside version is brown and made without tomato.
