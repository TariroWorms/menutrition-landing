🌶️ Sichuan Boiled Beef (Shui Zhu Niu Rou)

Origin: China · Sichuan

Sichuan boiled beef is a bold, spicy dish of thin beef slices poached until tender and served over bean sprouts, then topped with garlic, scallion, Sichuan peppercorns and a generous pour of hot chili oil. The meat is silky and the sauce numbing and fiery. It is a classic of Sichuan home and restaurant cooking, eaten with steamed rice.

Recipe

3 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g beef (flank or sirloin), thinly sliced
  • 250 g bean sprouts
  • 1 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns
  • 3 tbsp chili oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 scallions, chopped
  • 1 tsp chili flakes
  • 700 ml water
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Season the beef with a pinch of salt.
  2. Blanch the bean sprouts in boiling salted water for 1 minute and place in a deep bowl.
  3. Bring the water to a simmer with salt and half the garlic, add the beef and poach for 1–2 minutes until just cooked.
  4. Pour the beef with some broth over the sprouts.
  5. Top with the rest of the garlic, scallions, chili flakes and peppercorns, warm the chili oil until hot and pour it over; serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the chili oil in half: 1 tbsp oil is about 120 kcal.
  • Choose lean cuts like flank or sirloin.
  • Eat the beef and sprouts and leave the oily broth in the bowl.

History

Water-boiled beef is usually linked to Zigong in Sichuan, where, the story goes, salt workers cooked tough meat in spicy broth. It has since become one of the signature dishes of Sichuan cuisine.
