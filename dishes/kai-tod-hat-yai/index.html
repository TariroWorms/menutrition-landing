🍗 Hat Yai fried chicken

Origin: Thailand · Hat Yai, Songkhla

Kai tod Hat Yai is the fried chicken of southern Thailand: chicken pieces marinated with turmeric, garlic and spices, deep-fried until the skin is golden and crisp, then piled high with crunchy fried shallots. The turmeric gives it a deep yellow colour and an earthy aroma. It is typically eaten with sticky rice or plain rice as a snack or full meal.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces
  • 2 tsp ground turmeric
  • 6 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tsp ground coriander and 1/2 tsp white pepper
  • 1 tsp salt and 1 tsp sugar
  • 4 shallots, thinly sliced
  • Oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Rub the chicken with turmeric, garlic, coriander, pepper, salt and sugar; marinate for at least 1 hour.
  2. Fry the shallots in hot oil until golden, then drain on paper.
  3. Deep-fry the chicken at 170 °C for 12–15 minutes until cooked through and crisp.
  4. Drain and serve topped with the fried shallots.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin before eating, or use skinless chicken breast.
  • Bake at 220 °C or air-fry instead of deep-frying.
  • Use just a sprinkle of fried shallots — they absorb a lot of oil.

History

The dish is named after Hat Yai, the largest city in Songkhla province in southern Thailand, where it is a well-known street food. Today it is sold at stalls all over Thailand.
