Butifarra (Peruvian ham sandwich)

Origin: Peru

In Peru a butifarra is not a sausage but a sandwich. A crusty roll is filled with jamón del país, a spiced home-style boiled ham, plus lettuce and salsa criolla, the classic mix of thin red onion, lime, chile and cilantro. The ham is mild and tender and the onion relish makes it bright and tangy. It is a favourite breakfast or afternoon snack in Lima.

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 4 crusty bread rolls (320 g)
  • 300 g jamón del país or spiced cooked ham, thinly sliced
  • 1 red onion (150 g), thinly sliced
  • 1 ají amarillo, in thin strips
  • 60 ml lime juice
  • 4 lettuce leaves
  • cilantro, salt

Method

  1. Soak the onion slices in cold salted water for 5 minutes and drain.
  2. Mix onion, ají, lime juice, cilantro and salt for the salsa criolla.
  3. Split the rolls and warm them briefly.
  4. Lay lettuce and ham slices inside.
  5. Top with salsa criolla, close and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean ham and a wholemeal roll for more fibre.
  • Skip mayonnaise; the salsa adds enough moisture.
  • Have one sandwich with a fruit for a balanced breakfast.

History

Jamón del país is a traditional Peruvian cured and cooked pork leg seasoned with garlic and chile. Butifarras are sold in sandwich shops and at weekend breakfasts, alongside pan con chicharrón.
