🥩 Chicken-fried steak

Origin: United States · Texas and the South

Chicken-fried steak is a thin, tenderised beef steak coated in seasoned flour, egg and breadcrumbs and fried until crisp — cooked the way fried chicken is, which gives it its name. It is smothered in a peppery cream gravy and usually served with mashed potatoes and green beans. Expect a crunchy crust, tender beef and a rich, creamy sauce.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thin beef steaks (about 120 g each), tenderised
  • 100 g flour
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 80 g breadcrumbs
  • 1 tsp paprika, salt and black pepper
  • Oil for shallow frying
  • For the gravy: 2 tbsp flour, 400 ml milk, 1 tsp coarse black pepper

Method

  1. Season the steaks and pound them thin.
  2. Dredge each steak in seasoned flour, dip it in egg, then press it into the breadcrumbs.
  3. Shallow-fry in hot oil for 3–4 minutes per side until deep golden, then keep warm.
  4. Pour off all but 2 tbsp of the frying oil, stir in the flour and cook for 1 minute.
  5. Whisk in the milk and simmer until thick, season generously with pepper and salt, and spoon over the steaks.

How to make it lighter

  • Pour the gravy on the side and use a few spoonfuls rather than smothering the steak.
  • Fry in a thin layer of oil and drain well on paper — the crust soaks up fat quickly.
  • Serve with green beans or salad instead of a large mound of mashed potatoes.

History

The dish is usually linked to German and Austrian immigrants in Texas, whose breaded schnitzel was adapted to cheap cuts of beef. It became a staple of Southern diners and truck stops in the twentieth century and remains a symbol of Texas comfort food.
