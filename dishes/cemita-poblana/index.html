Cemita poblana (Puebla sesame roll sandwich)

Origin: Mexico · Puebla

A cemita is both the bread and the sandwich: a round, slightly sweet egg roll crowned with sesame seeds. In Puebla it is split and piled with a thin breaded beef or pork milanesa, sliced avocado, pulled strands of quesillo, onion, chipotle in adobo and leaves of pápalo, a pungent herb that defines the taste. It is large, messy and very filling.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 cemita rolls or sesame buns
  • 4 thin beef cutlets (400 g)
  • 1 egg
  • 80 g breadcrumbs
  • 2 avocados
  • 150 g quesillo (Oaxaca cheese)
  • 4 chipotles in adobo
  • 1 bunch pápalo (or arugula and cilantro)
  • 1 small onion, sliced, and oil for frying

Method

  1. Pound the cutlets thin, dip in beaten egg and coat in breadcrumbs.
  2. Shallow-fry 2 minutes per side and drain.
  3. Split and lightly toast the rolls.
  4. Spread avocado on the base, then add milanesa, pulled quesillo and chipotle.
  5. Top with pápalo leaves and onion, close and press gently.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the milanesa instead of frying it.
  • Use half the cheese and add sliced tomato.
  • Share one cemita; a single sandwich is close to a full meal.

History

Cemitas are a street-food emblem of Puebla, sold in markets such as the Mercado del Carmen. Pápalo, a wild herb with a strong, almost citrusy flavour, is used fresh and is what makes the cemita taste different from a torta.
