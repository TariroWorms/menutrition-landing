🫔 Tamales de Rajas

Tamales de rajas are vegetarian tamales filled with strips of roasted poblano pepper, melting cheese and sweet corn kernels. The corn masa steams into a soft, fluffy casing around a filling that is smoky, mild and cheesy. They are a popular meat-free choice at Mexican breakfasts, street stalls and holiday tamaladas.

What it is made of

masa corn doughpoblano peppercheesecorn

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g masa harina (nixtamalised corn)
  • 600 ml warm vegetable stock
  • 80 ml neutral oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 4 poblano peppers
  • 200 g fresh white cheese, cut into strips
  • 150 g corn kernels
  • 18 dried corn husks

Method

  1. Soak the corn husks in hot water for 30 minutes.
  2. Char the poblanos over a flame or under the grill, steam them in a covered bowl, peel, seed and cut into strips.
  3. Beat the masa harina with oil, salt, baking powder and warm stock into a soft dough.
  4. Spread masa on each husk, add pepper strips, a strip of cheese and a few corn kernels, then fold.
  5. Steam upright for 60–75 minutes until the dough releases from the husk.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a lower-fat fresh cheese and a smaller strip per tamal.
  • Add more poblano strips and corn so the filling stays generous with less cheese.
  • Reduce the oil in the masa: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Rajas, meaning strips, refers to the roasted poblano peppers that have long been a staple of central Mexican cooking. Rajas con queso is one of the classic tamal fillings sold alongside pork and chicken versions throughout Mexico today.
