Banku with grilled tilapia

Origin: Ghana

Banku is a smooth, slightly sour ball of fermented corn and cassava dough that is stirred over heat until stretchy. It is eaten by hand, torn in small pieces and dipped in a fresh pepper sauce of tomato, onion and chili. Grilled tilapia, rubbed with ginger and spices, is the classic partner. The meal is tangy, smoky and hot.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g fermented corn dough
  • 150 g fermented cassava dough
  • 800 g whole tilapia
  • 300 g tomatoes
  • 100 g onion
  • 10 g scotch bonnet pepper

Method

  1. Mix the fermented corn and cassava dough with water and salt.
  2. Cook over medium heat, stirring hard with a wooden paddle for 20 minutes until smooth and stretchy.
  3. Season whole tilapia with ginger, garlic and salt and grill 8 minutes per side.
  4. Grind tomato, onion and scotch bonnet into a rough sauce.
  5. Shape banku into balls and serve with fish and sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Grill the fish rather than frying it.
  • Use a smaller portion of banku and more fresh sauce.
  • Add sliced cucumber or okra stew for vegetables.

History

Banku is a staple of southern Ghana, especially among the Ga and Ewe, and is linked to lakeside and coastal fishing communities. Tilapia grown in Ghanaian waters is a favourite pairing.
