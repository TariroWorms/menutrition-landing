Kyrgyz beshbarmak (mutton with thin noodles)

Origin: Kyrgyzstan

In Kyrgyzstan beshbarmak, also called tuuralgan et, is made by boiling mutton for hours, then chopping the meat very finely and mixing it with thin squares or strips of noodle dough cooked in the broth. Onions softened in hot broth are poured over, making it rich, savoury and tender. It is eaten from a shared platter, with bowls of hot broth, called shorpo, on the side.

What it is made of

lambfloureggonionbroth

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg mutton on the bone
  • 400 g flour
  • 1 egg
  • 150 ml water
  • 2 onions, thinly sliced
  • Salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Simmer the mutton in salted water for 2.5 hours, skimming.
  2. Knead flour, egg, water and salt into a stiff dough, roll very thin and cut into small squares.
  3. Remove the meat, chop it finely and keep warm.
  4. Cook the noodles in some of the broth for 3-4 minutes and drain.
  5. Pour hot broth with pepper over the onions, then mix noodles, meat and onions and serve with broth in bowls.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat and chill the broth to lift off the fat.
  • Make a smaller noodle portion and more meat and onion.
  • Serve with a fresh tomato and cucumber salad.

History

The name means five fingers, a reference to the old custom of eating the dish by hand. It is the centrepiece of Kyrgyz celebrations and family gatherings, where cuts of meat are served to guests according to custom.
