🍲 Claypot chicken rice

Origin: Malaysia · Kuala Lumpur and the Malay Peninsula

Claypot chicken rice is a hawker favourite of rice cooked in a clay pot with soy-marinated chicken, slices of Chinese sausage, ginger and scallions. The chicken juices and soy sauce seep into the rice as it steams, and a crisp, caramelised rice crust forms at the bottom. It is served bubbling in the pot and mixed at the table, often with a dash of extra soy sauce.

Recipe

2 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g long-grain rice
  • 320 ml water or chicken stock
  • 300 g boneless chicken thigh, cut into pieces
  • 1 Chinese sausage, sliced
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp sugar
  • 20 g ginger, julienned
  • 2 scallions, sliced
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken with 2 tbsp soy sauce, the sugar and the ginger for 20 minutes.
  2. Rinse the rice, put it in an oiled clay pot with the water and bring to the boil uncovered.
  3. When the water is nearly absorbed, spread the chicken and sausage on top, cover and cook on low heat for 15 minutes.
  4. Drizzle the remaining soy sauce and a little oil around the rim and cook for 3–5 minutes more to form a crust.
  5. Scatter with scallions and mix at the table.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken thigh or breast and leave out the sausage for a leaner pot.
  • Add steamed greens on the side and serve a smaller scoop of rice.
  • Keep the oil around the rim to about a teaspoon — the crust still forms.

History

The dish has Cantonese roots and was brought to Malaysia and Singapore by Chinese immigrants. It is now a classic of Malaysian hawker centres, especially in Kuala Lumpur, where it is cooked in rows of clay pots over charcoal.
