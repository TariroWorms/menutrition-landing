🥓 Twice-cooked pork (hui guo rou)

Origin: China · Sichuan

Twice-cooked pork is a Sichuan classic: pork belly is first simmered whole, then sliced thin and stir-fried until the edges curl and crisp, with spicy chilli bean paste, peppers, garlic and soy sauce. It is rich, savoury and fiery, the fat rendering into a glossy red sauce. It is served with plain steamed rice.

Recipe

3 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g pork belly, in one piece
  • 2 tbsp chilli bean paste
  • 2 bell peppers or long green peppers, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Simmer the pork belly in water for 20–25 minutes until just cooked, then cool and slice very thinly.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok and fry the pork slices until they curl and release some fat.
  3. Push the pork aside, fry the chilli bean paste for 30 seconds until red and fragrant, then mix with the pork.
  4. Add the garlic and peppers and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
  5. Season with soy sauce and sugar, toss and serve hot with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Pour off the fat the pork releases before adding the bean paste.
  • Use more peppers and less pork per portion to cut fat while keeping the flavour.
  • Choose a leaner cut with a thinner fat layer; the sauce carries most of the taste.

History

Hui guo rou, literally “meat returned to the pot”, is one of the best-known home-style dishes of Sichuan cuisine. Its fermented chilli bean paste is closely associated with Pixian near Chengdu.
