🍬 Mann al-Sama

Origin: Iraq

Mann al-sama is an Iraqi sweet of soft, chewy pieces flavoured with cardamom and packed with whole pistachios and almonds. The pieces are rolled in flour so they do not stick, which gives them a powdery white coat over a sticky, nougat-like centre. It is served with tea and given as a gift at Eid and on family visits.

What it is made of

Recipe

20 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g glucose syrup
  • 200 g sugar
  • 80 ml water
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 100 g pistachios
  • 80 g almonds
  • 80 g flour, for coating

Method

  1. Toast the flour in a dry pan for a few minutes until lightly coloured and spread it on a tray.
  2. Boil the glucose syrup, sugar and water to a thick syrup at about 120 °C.
  3. Let it cool slightly, then pull and fold it with oiled spatulas until it turns pale and chewy.
  4. Work in the cardamom, pistachios and almonds.
  5. Tip onto the floured tray, cut into small pieces and roll each one in flour.

How to make it lighter

  • Have one or two pieces with tea rather than a plateful — it is dense and sweet.
  • Cut it into small bite-sized pieces so a portion feels larger.
  • Pair it with unsweetened tea or coffee to keep the total sugar down.

History

The name means 'manna from heaven'. The sweet was traditionally made with natural manna, a sugary substance collected from oak trees in the mountains of northern Iraq; today most mann al-sama is made with sugar syrup and remains a popular Iraqi gift sweet.
