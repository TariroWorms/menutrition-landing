🍬 Tahini Halva

Origin: Middle East · Eastern Mediterranean

Tahini halva is a firm, flaky confection made by beating hot sugar syrup into sesame paste, often with pistachios folded through. It has a nutty, toasted sesame flavor, a dry, crumbly texture that melts in the mouth and a deep sweetness. It is sliced thinly and eaten with bread at breakfast, with tea or coffee, or as a snack.

Recipe

10 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g tahini
  • 300 g sugar
  • 100 ml water
  • 50 g shelled pistachios

Method

  1. Line a small tin with baking paper.
  2. Warm the tahini gently.
  3. Boil the sugar and water to 118 °C.
  4. Stir the syrup into the tahini quickly until it thickens and turns matte, then fold in the pistachios.
  5. Press into the tin and leave to set for several hours before slicing.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thin 20–30 g slices and weigh them at first to learn the size.
  • Pair a slice with fruit for a sweet breakfast that feels larger.
  • Keep halva for occasional treats rather than daily snacking.

History

Sesame-based halva has been made for centuries across the Middle East, the Balkans and the eastern Mediterranean. Today it is a pantry staple sold in blocks in Turkey, Greece, the Levant and Iran, in plain, pistachio and other flavors.
