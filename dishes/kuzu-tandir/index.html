🍖 Kuzu tandır (slow-roasted lamb)

Origin: Turkey · Anatolia

Kuzu tandır is lamb roasted for hours, traditionally in a tandır pit oven, until it is so tender it can be pulled apart with a fork. It is cooked simply with onion, garlic, tomato and peppers, so the flavour of the meat stays central. It is usually served on rice pilaf with raw onion and fresh peppers.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg lamb shoulder on the bone
  • 2 onions, quartered
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 3 tomatoes, halved
  • 3 green peppers
  • 1 tsp salt, ½ tsp black pepper, 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 200 ml water

Method

  1. Rub the lamb with salt, pepper and thyme.
  2. Lay the onions, garlic, tomatoes and peppers in a deep roasting dish and set the lamb on top.
  3. Add the water, cover tightly with foil and roast at 150 °C for 3½–4 hours.
  4. Uncover, raise the heat to 220 °C and roast for 15 minutes to brown.
  5. Pull the meat into pieces and serve with the roasted vegetables and juices.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose leg instead of shoulder for a leaner roast.
  • Pour off the fat from the roasting juices before spooning them over the meat.
  • Serve with a large salad and a small portion of rice.

History

Tandır cooking — slow-roasting meat in a clay pit oven heated by embers — has long been practised in Anatolia and Central Asia. Kuzu tandır is a celebrated dish in Turkish restaurants today, many of which still use a pit or a closed slow oven.
