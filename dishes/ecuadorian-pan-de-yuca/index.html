Ecuadorian pan de yuca

Origin: Ecuador
320kcal

Ecuadorian pan de yuca are little golden rolls made with cassava starch and plenty of grated fresh cheese, baked until puffed outside and stretchy inside. They are naturally gluten-free and taste mildly salty and buttery. The classic pairing is a cold glass of fruit yogurt, often bought together at the same bakery.

Recipe

6 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g cassava starch
  • 250 g fresh cheese, finely grated
  • 40 g butter, softened
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tsp baking powder, pinch of salt
  • 2–3 tbsp milk if needed

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 200 °C and line a tray.
  2. Mix the starch, cheese, baking powder and salt.
  3. Work in the butter and egg, adding milk only until the dough holds together.
  4. Shape into small balls or crescents and place on the tray.
  5. Bake 15–18 minutes until lightly golden and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Pair with unsweetened yogurt and fresh fruit instead of sweetened drinking yogurt.
  • Use a part-skim cheese to lower fat while keeping the stretch.
  • Make them small: two rolls are a satisfying gluten-free snack.

History

Cassava is native to South America, and cheese breads made from its starch are found across the continent, from Brazil to Colombia. In Ecuador, bakeries selling pan de yuca with yogurt became a popular snack stop, particularly in Quito and Guayaquil.
