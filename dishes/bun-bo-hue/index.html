🍜 Bún bò Huế

Origin: Vietnam · Huế

Bún bò Huế is a spicy rice-vermicelli soup from central Vietnam: a deep broth of beef and pork bones scented with lemongrass, chili and fermented shrimp paste, served over thick round rice vermicelli. Slices of beef shank and pork knuckle sit on top. It is bold, aromatic and fiery, eaten with fresh herbs, lime and bean sprouts.

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g beef shank
  • 500 g pork knuckle or pork shoulder
  • 600 g thick round rice vermicelli
  • 4 lemongrass stalks, bruised
  • 2 tbsp fermented shrimp paste
  • 2 tbsp chili flakes fried in 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 onion, salt, sugar, herbs and lime to serve

Method

  1. Blanch the beef and pork, rinse, then cover with 4 l fresh water.
  2. Add the lemongrass and onion and simmer for 2–2½ hours, skimming.
  3. Dissolve the shrimp paste in a little water, let it settle and add the clear liquid to the broth; season with salt and sugar.
  4. Lift out and slice the meat; stir the chili oil into the broth.
  5. Cook the vermicelli, divide into bowls, add meat and ladle over hot broth; serve with herbs and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat off the broth after chilling it — it lifts off easily once cold.
  • Use more beef shank and less pork knuckle for a leaner bowl.
  • Add extra bean sprouts and herbs and fewer vermicelli.

History

The soup comes from Huế, the former imperial capital of Vietnam, and reflects the city's taste for spicy, strongly seasoned food. It is now served across Vietnam and in Vietnamese communities abroad, often as a heartier rival to phở.
