🍚 Portuguese arroz doce with lemon

Origin: Portugal · Minho

This is the classic Portuguese arroz doce, scented strongly with lemon: rice simmered in milk with plenty of lemon zest and a cinnamon stick, sweetened, thickened with egg yolks and finished with a lattice of ground cinnamon. It is creamy, fragrant and a little lighter than many rice puddings, and is served at room temperature from a large platter.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g short-grain rice
  • 1 litre milk
  • 500 ml water
  • 140 g sugar
  • 3 egg yolks
  • Zest of 1 lemon, in wide strips
  • 1 cinnamon stick and a pinch of salt
  • Ground cinnamon to decorate

Method

  1. Simmer the rice in the water with the salt, lemon zest and cinnamon stick for about 10 minutes until most of the water is absorbed.
  2. Add the hot milk a ladle at a time, stirring, and cook on low heat for 30 minutes until the rice is soft and creamy.
  3. Add the sugar and cook for 5 minutes more, then take off the heat and remove the zest and cinnamon stick.
  4. Temper the egg yolks with a little hot rice, stir them in and cook on the lowest heat for 2 minutes without boiling.
  5. Spread on a platter, let it cool slightly and draw a lattice of ground cinnamon on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Semi-skimmed milk gives almost the same creaminess with fewer calories.
  • Extra lemon zest and cinnamon let you reduce the sugar without losing flavour.
  • Keep to a small portion of about 150 g and pair it with fresh fruit.

History

Arroz doce is a staple of Portuguese festivals and Christmas tables, and every region has its own version. In the north, especially in the Minho, cooks are known for a rich yolk-thickened arroz doce decorated with cinnamon patterns, sometimes spelling out names or dates for special occasions.
