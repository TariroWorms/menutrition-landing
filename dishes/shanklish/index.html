🧀 Shanklish

Origin: Levant · Syria and Lebanon

Shanklish is a Levantine aged cheese, shaped into balls, coated in dried thyme or chilli and left to mature until firm and pungent. For the classic mezze salad it is crumbled and tossed with finely chopped onion and tomato, then dressed generously with olive oil. The result is salty, tangy and sharp, eaten with fresh vegetables alongside other mezze.

Recipe

2 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g shanklish cheese
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, finely diced
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • A pinch of chilli flakes
  • 2 tbsp olive oil

Method

  1. Crumble the shanklish into a bowl with a fork, breaking it into small pieces.
  2. Add the onion, tomato, thyme and chilli and toss gently.
  3. Drizzle with olive oil just before serving and eat with fresh vegetables.

How to make it lighter

  • Shanklish is rich and salty, so a small portion with plenty of tomato and onion goes a long way.
  • Use 1 tbsp olive oil instead of 2 — each tablespoon is about 120 kcal.
  • Scoop it with cucumber slices or lettuce leaves.

History

Shanklish is a traditional cheese of the mountain and coastal villages of Syria and Lebanon, where it was traditionally made to preserve surplus milk. Today it is a familiar part of mezze spreads throughout the Levant.
