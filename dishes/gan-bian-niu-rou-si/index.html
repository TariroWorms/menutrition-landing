Dry-fried beef shreds (gan bian niu rou si)

Origin: China · Sichuan

Gan bian means dry-frying: the beef is cooked slowly in a wok, stirred until its moisture evaporates and the strips turn chewy and concentrated in flavor. Celery and carrot sticks go in, with doubanjiang, chilies and Sichuan pepper. The dish ends up dark, glossy and slightly sticky, with a strong chewy bite and a lingering numbing heat.

What it is made of

beefcelerycarrotchili peppersichuan pepperdoubanjiangginger

Recipe

3 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g beef flank, cut in strips
  • 150 g celery, cut in sticks
  • 80 g carrot, cut in sticks
  • 1 tbsp doubanjiang
  • 10 dried chilies
  • 1 tsp ground Sichuan pepper

Method

  1. Cut the beef into thin strips along the grain.
  2. Fry the beef in a little oil over medium heat, stirring, until it is dry and browned.
  3. Push it aside and fry doubanjiang, ginger and chilies until the oil turns red.
  4. Add the celery and carrot and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
  5. Season with soy sauce and Sichuan pepper and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean beef and drain excess oil from the wok.
  • Add more celery and carrot for fibre.
  • Use less doubanjiang to cut sodium.

History

Dry-frying is a classic Sichuan method, also used for green beans and other dishes. The beef version is a restaurant staple and is often credited as one of the region's signature techniques.
