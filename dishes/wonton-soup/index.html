🍜 Wonton soup

Origin: China · Guangdong

Wonton soup is a clear, light broth with plump dumplings filled with pork and shrimp, wrapped in thin, silky wrappers. A few drops of sesame oil and chopped spring onion give it aroma. In Cantonese cooking it is often served as a quick meal, sometimes with thin noodles or leafy greens added to the bowl.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g minced pork
  • 150 g raw peeled shrimp, chopped
  • 24 wonton wrappers
  • 1.2 litres chicken or pork broth
  • 3 spring onions, chopped
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 2 tsp sesame oil
  • Salt and white pepper
  • A handful of leafy greens (optional)

Method

  1. Mix the pork, shrimp, ginger, half the spring onion, 1 tsp sesame oil, salt and white pepper.
  2. Place a teaspoon of filling on each wrapper, wet the edges, fold into a triangle and press the corners together.
  3. Bring the broth to a simmer and season with salt and white pepper.
  4. Boil the wontons in a separate pot of water for 4 minutes until they float, then transfer them to bowls.
  5. Ladle the hot broth over, add the greens if using, and finish with the remaining spring onion and sesame oil.

How to make it lighter

  • A broth-based soup is filling for few calories: add more greens and broth, fewer wontons.
  • Use lean pork or a higher share of shrimp in the filling.
  • Sesame oil is for aroma — a few drops are enough.

History

Wontons have a long history in Chinese cooking, and the Cantonese style with pork and shrimp in a clear broth became especially well known. Hong Kong and Guangzhou noodle shops made it a staple, and Chinese emigrants brought it to restaurants worldwide.
