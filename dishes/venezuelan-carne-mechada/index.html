Carne mechada (Venezuelan shredded beef)

Origin: Venezuela

Carne mechada is beef, usually flank, simmered until it can be pulled into long threads, then cooked again in a sofrito of tomato, onion, sweet pepper, garlic and cumin. The result is juicy, savoury and slightly sweet from the vegetables. It is one of the most useful dishes in Venezuelan cooking: the beef of pabellón criollo, a filling for arepas and empanadas, and a lunch on its own with rice.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef flank
  • 2 tomatoes (250 g), grated
  • 1 onion (150 g), 1 red pepper (150 g)
  • 4 garlic cloves, 1 tsp cumin
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt, pepper
  • bay leaf

Method

  1. Simmer the flank with bay leaf and salt in water for 1.5–2 hours until very tender.
  2. Cool in the broth, then pull the meat into thin strands; keep a cup of broth.
  3. Fry onion, pepper and garlic in oil until soft, add cumin and grated tomato and cook 10 minutes.
  4. Add the shredded beef and some broth and stew 15–20 minutes until juicy but not wet.
  5. Adjust the salt and serve with rice, beans or in arepas.

How to make it lighter

  • Flank is lean – chill the broth and lift off the fat.
  • Add extra peppers, carrots or zucchini to the sofrito.
  • Use the leftover broth as a soup base.

History

Shredded stewed beef is shared by many Spanish-speaking countries, often under the name ropa vieja. In Venezuela it took the name mechada, from the strands (mechas) of meat, and became a household basic.
