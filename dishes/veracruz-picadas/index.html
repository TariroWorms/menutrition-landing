Picadas (Veracruz pinched tortillas)

Origin: Mexico · Veracruz

Picadas are thick masa rounds whose edges are pinched up while still hot from the griddle, forming a shallow rim. The hollow is filled with red or green salsa, a little melted lard and crumbled cheese, then finished with onion. They are a typical breakfast in the port of Veracruz, often eaten alongside coffee.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g corn masa
  • 200 ml red or green salsa
  • 80 g queso fresco, crumbled
  • 1/2 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp lard or oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Knead the masa with salt and divide into 12 balls.
  2. Press each into a round about 10 cm wide and 5 mm thick.
  3. Cook on a hot griddle 2 minutes per side.
  4. While still hot, pinch the edges upward to make a rim.
  5. Brush with lard, fill with salsa, top with cheese and onion and warm 1 more minute.

How to make it lighter

  • Brush with oil instead of lard.
  • Choose a light fresh cheese or skip it.
  • Add black beans to make them more filling and higher in protein.

History

Picadas are closely related to the sopes of central Mexico, but in Veracruz they are thinner and usually served simply with salsa. The name refers to the pinching of the dough with the fingertips.
