Iahnie de fasole (Romanian white bean stew)

Origin: Romania

Iahnie de fasole is a thick stew of white beans slow-cooked with a lot of onion, a spoon of tomato paste and sweet paprika. The sauce turns glossy and thick while the beans stay soft and whole. It is served with pickled peppers, sauerkraut or smoked sausage on the side or, in the fasting weeks, with no meat at all.

What it is made of

white beansoniontomatopaprikagarlicoil

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g dried white beans, soaked overnight
  • 3 onions, sliced
  • 50 ml oil
  • 40 g tomato paste
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • bay leaf, savory or thyme, salt

Method

  1. Boil the soaked beans in fresh water for 45 minutes until nearly tender, then drain keeping 300 ml of the liquid.
  2. Cook the onions in oil over low heat for 15 minutes until golden.
  3. Stir in tomato paste, paprika and garlic for 1 minute.
  4. Add beans, bay leaf and the reserved liquid.
  5. Simmer for 30 minutes until thick, season and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use only a spoon of oil and cook the onions with a splash of water.
  • Pair with raw sauerkraut for extra vitamin C and fibre.
  • Skip smoked meat to keep it vegan and lower in sodium.

History

The word iahnie comes from the Turkish yahni, a stew, and shows the Ottoman influence on Romanian cooking. Beans are an old cheap source of protein in the Orthodox fasting calendar. The recipe is common to Wallachia and Moldavia with small regional differences.
