🍨 Falooda

Falooda is a cold, layered dessert drink of chilled milk sweetened with rose syrup, soft vermicelli and swollen basil seeds, crowned with a scoop of ice cream. Every sip mixes floral sweetness with slippery noodles and the gentle crunch of the seeds. It is served in tall glasses with a long spoon, especially in hot weather.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 600 ml cold milk
  • 4 tbsp rose syrup
  • 40 g thin wheat vermicelli
  • 2 tsp basil (sabja) seeds
  • 2 scoops vanilla ice cream
  • 1 tbsp sugar

Method

  1. Soak the basil seeds in 100 ml water for 15 minutes until they swell.
  2. Boil the vermicelli for 3–4 minutes, drain and rinse in cold water.
  3. Stir the sugar into the cold milk until dissolved.
  4. Pour 2 tbsp rose syrup into each tall glass, then add the seeds and vermicelli.
  5. Top up with the cold milk and finish each glass with a scoop of ice cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the ice cream or use a small scoop — it adds the most fat.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk and leave out the extra sugar; rose syrup is already sweet.
  • Serve it in a smaller glass and treat it as dessert rather than a drink with a meal.

History

Falooda is generally traced to the Persian frozen dessert faloodeh, which is said to have travelled to the Indian subcontinent with Persian and Mughal-era influences. Today it is a favourite at sweet shops in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh and in South Asian cafés in the Gulf.
