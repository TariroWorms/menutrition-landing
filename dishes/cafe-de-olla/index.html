Café de olla (cinnamon pot coffee)

Origin: Mexico

Café de olla is coffee boiled with cinnamon and piloncillo, traditionally in an earthenware pot that is said to add its own earthy note. It is sweet, spiced and less bitter than espresso. It is served in clay mugs with breakfast, sweet bread or after a meal at a fonda.

What it is made of

coffeepiloncillo (cane sugar)cinnamonwater

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 1 l water
  • 40 g coarsely ground dark coffee
  • 60 g piloncillo
  • 1 large cinnamon stick
  • Optional: 1 strip of orange peel, 2 cloves

Method

  1. Bring the water, piloncillo and cinnamon (and any spices) to a boil in a pot, ideally clay.
  2. Stir until the piloncillo is fully dissolved.
  3. Turn off the heat, stir in the coffee and cover.
  4. Leave to steep for 5 minutes.
  5. Strain through a fine sieve or cloth and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the piloncillo for a cup of about 20 kcal.
  • Let the cinnamon steep longer to add aroma without sugar.
  • Avoid it late in the day if you are sensitive to caffeine.

History

The drink is strongly linked to rural Mexico, where coffee was brewed in clay pots over a wood fire. It is often associated with the soldiers and camp followers of the Mexican Revolution, though that origin is told more as tradition than documented fact.
