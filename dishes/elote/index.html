🌽 Elote

Elote is Mexican street corn: a whole cob grilled until charred, then brushed with mayonnaise, rolled in crumbly cheese and finished with chilli powder, a squeeze of lime and chopped cilantro. The result is sweet, smoky, creamy and tangy all at once. It is sold on a stick from street carts and eaten by hand.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 4 corn cobs, husked
  • 4 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 60 g crumbly Mexican cheese (cotija or queso fresco)
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 1 lime, in wedges
  • 2 tbsp chopped cilantro
  • Salt

Method

  1. Grill the corn over high heat for 10–12 minutes, turning, until charred in spots.
  2. Brush each hot cob thinly with mayonnaise.
  3. Roll or sprinkle with the cheese, then dust with chilli powder and salt.
  4. Top with cilantro and serve with lime wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tsp mayonnaise per cob instead of a thick layer; 1 tbsp mayonnaise ≈ 90–100 kcal.
  • Choose a light mayonnaise and a sprinkle rather than a full coat of cheese.
  • Lean on lime, chilli and cilantro for flavour—they add almost no calories.

History

Corn has been a staple in Mexico since pre-Hispanic times, and elote, from the Nahuatl word elotl for tender corn, is a classic of Mexican street food today.
