Banosh (Hutsul cornmeal porridge)

Origin: Ukraine · Carpathians

Banosh is the signature dish of the Hutsul highlands: coarse cornmeal is cooked slowly in sour cream, sometimes diluted with milk, until thick, glossy and rich. It is stirred in one direction with a wooden spoon and finished with salty sheep's bryndza and crisp cracklings. Fried wild mushrooms are a classic addition.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g coarse cornmeal
  • 500 g sour cream (15–20%)
  • 300 ml milk or water
  • 150 g bryndza (sheep cheese)
  • 100 g smoked bacon or salo
  • 200 g mushrooms, salt

Method

  1. Bring the sour cream and milk to a gentle boil with a pinch of salt.
  2. Pour in the cornmeal in a thin stream, stirring all the time.
  3. Cook on low heat 20–25 minutes, stirring in one direction, until thick and oil starts to appear.
  4. Meanwhile fry the diced bacon until crisp and lift out.
  5. Fry the sliced mushrooms in the bacon fat.
  6. Serve the banosh topped with crumbled bryndza, cracklings and mushrooms.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half sour cream and half milk for a lighter porridge.
  • Skip the cracklings and double the mushrooms.
  • Serve a smaller portion with a large green salad.

History

Banosh comes from the Carpathian mountains of western Ukraine, where it was traditionally prepared by shepherds and is now cooked at festivals over open fires. It reflects the local abundance of dairy from mountain pastures.
