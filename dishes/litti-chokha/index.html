Litti chokha

Origin: India · Bihar

Litti chokha is the signature everyday meal of Bihar. Whole-wheat dough balls are filled with roasted gram flour (sattu), garlic, ginger and spices, then baked over coals until crusty. They are broken open, dipped in ghee and eaten with chokha, a mash of fire-roasted eggplant, potato and tomato seasoned with mustard oil.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g whole-wheat flour
  • 100 g sattu (roasted gram flour)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced, and 10 g ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp ajwain and 1 tsp mustard oil for the filling
  • 2 large eggplants
  • 2 potatoes, boiled
  • 2 tomatoes
  • Green chilli, onion, coriander, mustard oil, salt
  • Ghee for serving

Method

  1. Knead the flour with salt and water into a firm dough and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Mix sattu with garlic, ginger, ajwain, mustard oil, salt and a little water into a crumbly filling.
  3. Stuff balls of dough with the filling and seal them.
  4. Bake in a hot oven or on coals for 25-30 minutes, turning, until the crust is browned.
  5. Roast the eggplant and tomatoes over a flame until charred, then peel and mash with the potatoes.
  6. Season the chokha with onion, chilli, coriander, mustard oil and salt; serve with the litti dipped in ghee.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake instead of deep-frying, and use only a spoonful of ghee per serving.
  • Add more roasted vegetables to the chokha for fibre.
  • Use whole-wheat flour for the dough to keep the glycaemic load lower.

History

Litti is an old rural food of Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh, valued because it keeps well and needs little cooking equipment. Sattu has long been a staple of farmers and travellers in the region. The dish became widely known across India in recent decades through street vendors and restaurants.
