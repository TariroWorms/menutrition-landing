Chal (Turkmen fermented camel milk)

Origin: Turkmenistan

Chal is a traditional Turkmen drink made by fermenting fresh camel milk with a starter from a previous batch. It is white, thin and refreshing, with a sour, slightly fizzy taste and a faint saltiness. It is drunk cold, especially in the hot desert summer, and is valued as a nourishing everyday food.

Recipe

4 servings1440 min

Ingredients

  • 1 l fresh camel milk
  • 200 ml chal from a previous batch (starter)
  • pinch of salt (optional)

Method

  1. Warm the milk slightly to about 30 °C.
  2. Stir in the starter.
  3. Pour into a clean jar or traditional skin and cover loosely.
  4. Leave in a warm place for 12–24 hours, stirring a few times.
  5. Chill and serve; keep back some as starter for the next batch.

How to make it lighter

  • Drink it plain without added sugar.
  • Use it in place of sugary soft drinks on hot days.
  • Start with small glasses if you are not used to fermented milk.

History

Camel herding has long been part of life in the Turkmen deserts, and chal is closely associated with it. Similar fermented camel milk, called shubat, is made in Kazakhstan.
