🐟 Gravlax

Origin: Sweden · Scandinavia

Gravlax is raw salmon cured for two to three days under a blanket of salt, sugar and fresh dill. The cure firms the flesh and turns it silky and translucent, with a clean, gently sweet and herby taste. It is sliced paper-thin and served cold as a starter, almost always with a sweet mustard and dill sauce called hovmästarsås.

Recipe

8 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg very fresh salmon fillet, skin on, in two halves
  • 60 g coarse salt
  • 60 g sugar
  • 1 tsp crushed white peppercorns
  • 1 large bunch fresh dill, chopped
  • Mustard sauce to serve

Method

  1. Mix the salt, sugar and pepper.
  2. Lay one fillet skin-side down in a dish, cover with the salt mix and most of the dill, then place the second fillet on top, flesh to flesh.
  3. Wrap tightly, put a light weight on top and refrigerate for 48–72 hours, turning the parcel every 12 hours and spooning the brine back over.
  4. Scrape off the cure, pat dry and coat with the remaining dill.
  5. Slice very thinly on the diagonal, away from the skin, and serve cold with mustard sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • A 80 g starter portion is a light, protein-rich plate; the sauce is where the extra calories hide, so serve it on the side.
  • Use 1–2 tsp of mustard sauce per serving rather than a generous pour.
  • Pair gravlax with cucumber, lettuce or dill-dressed vegetables instead of heavier sides.

History

The name means 'grave salmon': medieval Nordic fishermen are said to have buried salted salmon in the sand above the tide line to let it ferment. Over the centuries the burying gave way to a quick cure with salt, sugar and dill. Today gravlax is a fixture of Swedish, Norwegian and Finnish festive tables, especially at Christmas and Midsummer.
