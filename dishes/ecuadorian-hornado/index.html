Hornado (Ecuadorian roast pork)

Origin: Ecuador · Andes

Hornado is pork, often a whole leg or even a whole pig, rubbed with garlic, cumin, achiote and bitter orange or beer marinade and roasted for hours in a wood oven. The meat is juicy and soft with crackling skin. Plates are built with llapingachos, mote (boiled hominy), avocado, pickled onion salad and a splash of the tangy roasting juices called agrio.

Recipe

10 servings300 min

Ingredients

  • 1 pork leg, about 4 kg
  • 1 head garlic, crushed
  • 2 tbsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp achiote paste
  • 300 ml bitter orange juice
  • 500 g cooked mote (hominy)
  • llapingachos and avocado to serve
  • salt, pepper

Method

  1. Score the skin and make deep cuts in the meat.
  2. Blend garlic, cumin, achiote, salt and orange juice and rub it all over; marinate overnight.
  3. Roast at 160 °C for about 4-5 hours, basting with the juices.
  4. Raise the heat at the end to crisp the skin.
  5. Mix the pan juices with a little orange juice and onion to make the agrio.
  6. Slice and serve with llapingachos, mote, avocado and agrio.

How to make it lighter

  • Eat the lean meat and leave most of the crackling.
  • Fill half the plate with salad and avocado instead of extra potatoes.
  • Skim the fat from the juices before making the agrio.

History

Pigs arrived with the Spanish, and slow roasting in clay or brick ovens became a highland tradition. Markets in towns like Sangolquí and Riobamba are famous for their hornado stalls. It is a typical dish for celebrations and weekend family meals.
