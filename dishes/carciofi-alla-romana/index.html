🌿 Carciofi alla romana (Roman-style artichokes)

Origin: Italy · Rome · Lazio

Carciofi alla romana are whole artichokes stuffed with a mixture of mint, garlic and parsley, then braised upside down in olive oil and water until meltingly tender. The herbs perfume the hearts, and the cooking liquid becomes a light, fragrant sauce. They are served warm or at room temperature as a side dish or antipasto.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 4 large artichokes
  • 1 lemon (for the trimming water)
  • A handful of fresh mint leaves, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • A handful of parsley, chopped
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • Water, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Trim off the tough outer leaves, peel the stems and keep the artichokes in water with lemon juice.
  2. Mix the mint, garlic and parsley with salt, pepper and 1 tbsp olive oil.
  3. Open the leaves slightly and push the herb mixture into the centre of each artichoke.
  4. Stand them head-down in a snug pot, add the remaining oil and water to come a third of the way up.
  5. Cover and cook gently for 30–40 minutes until a knife slides easily into the heart.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 2 tbsp oil instead of 4 and a little more water: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve with the cooking juices instead of extra oil.
  • Pair with grilled chicken or a bean salad for a light, filling plate.

History

This is one of the classic springtime dishes of Roman cooking, made with the round local artichokes; the traditional herb is often the local wild mint called mentuccia, which ordinary mint usually replaces outside Rome.
