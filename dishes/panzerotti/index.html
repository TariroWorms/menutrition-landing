🥟 Panzerotti

Origin: Italy · Apulia

Panzerotti are small half-moon pockets of pizza dough filled with mozzarella and tomato, then deep-fried until golden and puffed. The crust is crisp and slightly chewy, while the inside turns molten and stringy. They are a beloved street snack in southern Italy, eaten hot straight from the fryer, usually wrapped in paper.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g pizza dough
  • 250 g mozzarella, diced and drained
  • 200 g tomato passata or chopped tomatoes
  • A pinch of dried oregano
  • Salt
  • Vegetable oil for frying

Method

  1. Season the tomato with salt and oregano and let the diced mozzarella drain well.
  2. Divide the dough into 8 balls and roll each into a 15 cm round.
  3. Place tomato and mozzarella on one half, fold over and press the edges firmly to seal.
  4. Fry in oil at 170–175 °C for 2–3 minutes per side until golden.
  5. Drain on paper and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake them at 220 °C for 15–18 minutes instead of deep-frying to save a large share of the fat.
  • Make them smaller and eat one or two with a big green salad.
  • Use less mozzarella and more tomato in the filling.

History

Panzerotti are most closely associated with Apulia, especially Bari, where they are a fixture of bakeries and street stalls. The name is usually explained as coming from a dialect word for belly, after their rounded shape. Italian emigrants later took them abroad.
