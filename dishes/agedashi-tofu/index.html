🍢 Agedashi tofu

Agedashi tofu is a classic Japanese appetizer of silken tofu cubes dusted in potato starch and deep-fried until a thin, crisp shell forms around a soft, custard-like centre. The tofu is served in a warm, savoury broth of dashi and soy sauce and topped with grated ginger, grated daikon, spring onion and bonito flakes, so the coating turns silky as it soaks.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g silken or soft tofu
  • 3 tbsp potato starch
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • 200 ml dashi
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce and 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 2 tbsp grated daikon and 1 spring onion, sliced
  • A pinch of bonito flakes

Method

  1. Wrap the tofu in kitchen paper and press lightly for 20 minutes to remove excess water, then cut into 6 cubes.
  2. Heat the dashi with the soy sauce and sugar until just simmering and keep warm.
  3. Coat the tofu cubes in potato starch and deep-fry at 170–180 °C for 2–3 minutes until lightly golden and crisp.
  4. Drain on paper, place in bowls and pour the warm broth around the tofu.
  5. Top with ginger, daikon, spring onion and bonito flakes and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Drain the fried tofu well on paper towels — deep-fried coatings soak up oil quickly.
  • Shallow-fry in a non-stick pan with 2 tbsp oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Serve it with steamed vegetables to make a light meal.

History

Agedashi tofu has been eaten in Japan for centuries and appears in a well-known 18th-century tofu recipe collection, Tofu Hyakuchin. Today it is a standard dish at izakaya and home tables across Japan.
