Hunter's stew is the festive version of Polish bigos: sauerkraut and fresh cabbage braised for hours with pork, smoked sausage, dried mushrooms and prunes, finished with a glass of dry red wine. The wine deepens the colour and rounds off the sourness of the cabbage. It is served hot in bowls, often after resting a day or two, when the flavours have fully melded.

Recipe

6 servings190 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g sauerkraut, drained and chopped
  • 500 g fresh white cabbage, shredded
  • 500 g pork shoulder, cut into 3 cm cubes
  • 300 g smoked sausage, sliced
  • 20 g dried mushrooms, soaked in 300 ml hot water
  • 8 prunes, chopped
  • 200 ml dry red wine
  • 1 onion, chopped, and 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 bay leaves, 5 allspice berries, 1 tsp black pepper
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt

Method

  1. Simmer the sauerkraut with 300 ml water in a large pot for 30 minutes.
  2. Brown the pork in the oil, add the onion and sausage and fry for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the meat, fresh cabbage, mushrooms with their strained soaking water, bay leaves and allspice to the pot.
  4. Cover and cook on low heat for 1.5–2 hours, stirring occasionally.
  5. Pour in the wine, add the prunes and tomato paste, season and simmer uncovered for 30 minutes.
  6. Rest for a day if you can and reheat gently before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the pork and lift off the fat that sets on top after the stew cools.
  • Use half the sausage and more cabbage — the wine and mushrooms keep the flavour rich.
  • Most of the alcohol cooks off in 30 minutes of simmering, but some remains; leave the wine out for an alcohol-free bowl.

History

The English name reflects the tradition that bigos was cooked after hunts in the Polish-Lithuanian countryside, once with game as well as pork. Recipes vary from house to house, and adding red wine or Madeira is a common touch in richer, holiday versions. Today it is eaten across Poland, especially at Christmas and in the cold months.
