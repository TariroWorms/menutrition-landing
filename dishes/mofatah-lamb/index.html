🍖 Lamb mofatah

Mofatah (mufattah) is a festive Saudi platter of slow-steamed lamb set on a mound of spiced rice. The meat is cooked until it falls from the bone, the rice absorbs the rich broth and baharat spices, and a spoonful of yogurt or a yogurt sauce on the side cools the dish. It is served on large trays for weddings, Eid and family gatherings and eaten by hand.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg lamb shoulder or leg pieces, on the bone
  • 600 g basmati rice
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 tbsp baharat
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil, salt
  • 250 g plain yogurt to serve

Method

  1. Brown the onion in oil, add the lamb and baharat and turn to coat.
  2. Cover with water, season with salt and simmer covered for about 2 hours, or steam until very tender.
  3. Rinse the rice, then cook it in 1.2 l of the lamb broth for about 20 minutes until fluffy.
  4. Spread the rice on a large platter and arrange the lamb on top.
  5. Serve with yogurt on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb and skim the fat off the broth before cooking the rice.
  • Keep the rice to one cupped handful and add a large salad.
  • Use low-fat yogurt as the sauce.

History

Mofatah belongs to the family of Gulf rice-and-meat dishes such as kabsa and mandi, and is eaten today mainly in Saudi Arabia, especially at celebrations and when hosting guests.
