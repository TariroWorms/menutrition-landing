Fondue Moitié-Moitié

Origin: Switzerland · Fribourg

Fondue moitié-moitié, "half-and-half", is one of the best-known Swiss fondues: equal parts Gruyère and Vacherin Fribourgeois melted with dry white wine and garlic. The Gruyère gives a nutty, savoury depth and stretch, while the Vacherin makes it creamy and smooth. It is served in a caquelon at the table, with potatoes and vegetables for dipping.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g Gruyère and 300 g Vacherin Fribourgeois, grated
  • 300 ml dry white wine
  • 1 garlic clove, halved
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch
  • Black pepper and nutmeg to taste
  • Boiled small potatoes and raw vegetables for dipping

Method

  1. Rub the inside of a fondue pot with the cut garlic, then leave the garlic in the pot.
  2. Add the wine and warm over medium heat until steaming, without boiling.
  3. Keep the heat gentle from here on: the Vacherin half melts at a lower temperature than Gruyère.
  4. Toss the cheese with the cornstarch and add it by handfuls, stirring in a figure-eight until each addition melts.
  5. Season with pepper and nutmeg, set the pot over a burner at the table and dip potatoes and vegetables on long forks.

How to make it lighter

  • Dip mostly vegetables such as broccoli, peppers, cherry tomatoes and mushrooms instead of loading up on potatoes.
  • Fondue is dense: decide on your share before you start — about 150–200 g of the melted cheese is a realistic single serving.
  • Serve it with a large green salad dressed with vinegar and a little oil so the cheese is not the whole meal.

History

Moitié-moitié comes from the canton of Fribourg, where both Gruyère and Vacherin Fribourgeois are made. It was popularised as a Swiss national dish in the 20th century, notably through promotion by the Swiss Cheese Union from the 1930s.
