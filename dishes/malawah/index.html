🫓 Malawah

Malawah is a Yemeni flatbread made from a simple dough of flour, water and salt that is stretched thin, brushed with ghee, folded and coiled, then cooked on a hot griddle. The result is flaky, buttery and layered, crisp outside and soft inside. It is a breakfast favourite, eaten warm with honey, spicy tomato sauce or tea.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 280 ml water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 100 g ghee, melted

Method

  1. Knead the flour, water and salt into a soft dough and rest it for 30 minutes.
  2. Divide into 6 balls, stretch each one paper-thin on an oiled surface and brush with ghee.
  3. Fold into a long strip, roll it into a coil and rest for 15 minutes.
  4. Flatten each coil into a round and cook on a hot griddle for 3–4 minutes per side until golden and flaky.

How to make it lighter

  • Brush each layer lightly — every tablespoon of ghee is roughly 110–120 kcal.
  • Have half a malawah with a tomato salad instead of a whole one.
  • Serve with fresh tomato sauce rather than honey to keep sugar down.

History

Malawah comes from Yemen, and Yemeni Jewish communities also carried it to Israel, where it became a popular frozen and street-food bread. In Yemen and the Gulf it remains a common breakfast bread today.
