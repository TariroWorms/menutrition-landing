Obi non (Uzbek tandoor bread)

Origin: Uzbekistan

Obi non, often simply called non, is the bread served with every Uzbek meal. It has a puffy, soft rim and a thin, crisp centre pressed with a patterned stamp called a chekich, which stops it from rising there. Freshly baked, it is chewy and fragrant, and it is torn by hand rather than cut.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 300 ml warm water
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 10 g salt
  • 1 tsp sesame or nigella seeds

Method

  1. Mix flour, water, yeast and salt into a smooth dough.
  2. Let it rise for 1 hour until doubled.
  3. Divide into 4 balls and flatten into thick discs with a raised rim.
  4. Prick the centre densely with a fork or chekich and sprinkle with seeds.
  5. Bake at 250 °C on a hot stone or tray for 12–15 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use part wholemeal flour for more fibre.
  • Brush with water instead of butter before baking.
  • Pair it with salads and soups rather than fatty meats.

History

Bread holds a respected place in Uzbek culture: it is never put upside down and is broken at the start of the meal for everyone. Bread styles differ from city to city, with Samarkand and Tashkent loaves among the best known.
