🍖 Ossobuco alla milanese

Origin: Italy · Milan, Lombardy

Ossobuco alla milanese is the classic Milanese way of braising veal shank: the slices are browned, then cooked slowly with onion, carrot, white wine and broth until the meat is meltingly soft. This version keeps the sauce light-coloured, without tomato, so the flavour of wine, broth and marrow comes through. It is finished with gremolata and traditionally served with saffron risotto.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 4 veal shank slices, about 300 g each
  • 1 onion and 1 carrot, finely diced
  • 150 ml dry white wine
  • 500 ml beef or vegetable broth
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, salt, pepper
  • Gremolata: zest of 1 lemon, 1 garlic clove, a handful of parsley

Method

  1. Tie the shanks with kitchen string and season them.
  2. Brown them in oil in a wide pot on both sides, then set aside.
  3. Cook the onion and carrot for 8 minutes until soft, add the wine and reduce for 2 minutes.
  4. Return the meat, pour in the broth to come halfway up, cover and simmer for about 2 hours, adding a little broth if needed.
  5. Finely chop the gremolata ingredients and scatter over the meat before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat from the braising liquid once it has cooled slightly.
  • Use 1 tbsp of oil for browning — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Pair it with vegetables or a modest spoon of risotto rather than a full plate.

History

The dish is a cornerstone of Milanese home cooking and is commonly described as the older, tomato-free form of osso buco. Pairing it with risotto alla milanese is a long-standing local custom, and it is one of the few cases in Italian cooking where a risotto is served with a main dish.
