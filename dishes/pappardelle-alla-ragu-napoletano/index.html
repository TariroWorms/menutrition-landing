🍝 Pappardelle with Neapolitan ragù

Origin: Italy · Campania

This dish pairs wide pappardelle with Neapolitan ragù, a sauce in which large pieces of beef and pork cook for hours in tomato with onion and red wine. The meat turns meltingly tender and the sauce deep, dark and glossy. In Naples the sauce often dresses the pasta while the meat follows as a second course, but here it is shredded into the ragù.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g pappardelle
  • 500 g beef chuck in large pieces
  • 400 g pork ribs or shoulder
  • 1.2 kg tomato passata
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 200 ml red wine
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and basil

Method

  1. Brown the beef and pork in the oil in a heavy pot, then add the onion and cook until soft.
  2. Pour in the wine and let it evaporate.
  3. Add the passata, season and bring to a bare simmer.
  4. Cook partly covered for 3–4 hours, stirring occasionally, until the meat is very tender; shred it into the sauce.
  5. Cook the pappardelle, drain and toss with the ragù and basil.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean beef and trim visible fat from the pork.
  • Chill the sauce and lift off the solidified fat before reheating.
  • Keep the pasta to about 80 g dry per person and add a salad.

History

Ragù napoletano is a centuries-old Sunday tradition in Naples, where the sauce is famously left to "pippiare", or barely simmer, for many hours. Unlike the Bolognese style, it uses whole cuts of meat rather than minced meat. Pairing it with pappardelle is a home variation; in Naples it is more often served with ziti or rigatoni.
