Colombian morcilla (rellena)

Origin: Colombia

Colombian morcilla, often called rellena, is a blood sausage packed with cooked rice, peas and green onion. It is boiled and then sliced and fried until the casing crisps. The taste is earthy and savory, softened by the rice. It is eaten with arepas, potatoes and lime in fritanga platters.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1 l fresh pork blood
  • 300 g cooked rice
  • 150 g green peas
  • 100 g scallions, chopped
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 150 g pork fat, diced
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp salt, pepper
  • 2 m natural pork casing

Method

  1. Strain the blood and stir in a little salt so it does not clot.
  2. Mix in rice, peas, scallions, onion, fat and spices.
  3. Fill the rinsed casings loosely and tie into links.
  4. Simmer in barely bubbling water for 30-40 minutes, pricking any air bubbles.
  5. Cool, then slice and pan-fry until crisp.
  6. Serve with arepa and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Grill slices instead of frying to drop fat.
  • Eat one piece with salad rather than a full fritanga.
  • Add more vegetables and less fat to the filling.

History

Blood sausages came to the Americas with the Spanish, and Colombia developed a version bulked out with rice and vegetables. It is a classic of Sunday family meals and roadside grills in the Andean regions.
