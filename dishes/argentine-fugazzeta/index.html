Fugazzeta (cheese-stuffed onion pizza)

Origin: Argentina

Fugazzeta is the indulgent cousin of fugazza. Two layers of thick pizza dough enclose a generous filling of mozzarella, and the top is covered with sliced onions and oregano. When it is cut, the melted cheese stretches out from the middle, while the onion top is soft and sweet. It is a heavy, much-loved order in Buenos Aires pizzerias, eaten hot by the slice.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g pizza dough (homemade or bought)
  • 400 g mozzarella, sliced or grated
  • 3 white onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 30 g grated parmesan
  • salt

Method

  1. Salt the onions for 20 minutes, then squeeze out the liquid.
  2. Divide the dough in two and roll out two rounds the size of your pan.
  3. Place one round in an oiled pan and cover with mozzarella, leaving a border.
  4. Lay the second round on top and seal the edges well.
  5. Spread the onions over the top, drizzle with oil and let rest 20 minutes.
  6. Bake at 220 °C for 25–30 minutes, then add oregano and parmesan.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the cheese, or a lighter part-skim mozzarella.
  • Roll the dough thinner to cut the carbohydrates.
  • One slice with a big salad is plenty for a meal.

History

Fugazzeta grew out of the Genoese-style fugazza in early twentieth-century Buenos Aires. The stuffed version is often credited to pizzerias in the La Boca area, though several places claim it. Today it is a fixture on almost every Argentine pizzeria menu.
