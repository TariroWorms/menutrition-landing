🍲 Egyptian bamia

Egyptian bamia is okra and lamb stewed in a rich tomato sauce, finished with taqleya — garlic and ground coriander sizzled in ghee and stirred in at the end. The okra turns soft and the sauce thick and glossy, with a strong garlic aroma. It is served with white rice or vermicelli rice, or baked in a clay pot.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g lamb, cubed
  • 500 g small okra, trimmed
  • 400 g tomatoes, grated, and 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 5 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1½ tbsp ghee
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Brown the lamb in ½ tbsp ghee, cover with water and simmer for 1 hour.
  2. Add the tomatoes, tomato paste, salt and pepper and cook for 10 minutes.
  3. Add the okra and simmer for 20 minutes until tender.
  4. Fry the garlic and coriander in the remaining ghee until fragrant.
  5. Pour the taqleya into the stew, simmer 2 minutes and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use one tablespoon of ghee for the taqleya — each tablespoon is about 110 kcal.
  • Choose lean lamb and skim off fat from the broth.
  • Fill half the plate with stew and keep the rice to a small scoop.

History

Bamia is one of the classic stews of Egyptian home cooking. The garlic-and-coriander taqleya is a hallmark of Egyptian stews, and small whole okra is especially prized for it.
