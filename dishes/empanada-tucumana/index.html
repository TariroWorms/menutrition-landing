Empanada tucumana

Origin: Argentina · Tucumán

Empanada tucumana is filled with beef cut by hand with a knife, never minced, along with plenty of onion, spring onion, cumin and chopped egg. It has no potato, which sets it apart from the Salta style. Baked in a domed clay oven, the crust turns blistered and slightly smoky while the filling stays juicy.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 24 empanada discs made with lard (about 700 g)
  • 500 g beef (matambre or flank), knife-cut
  • 3 onions and 4 spring onions, chopped
  • 2 hard-boiled eggs, chopped
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tbsp sweet paprika, chili flakes to taste
  • 40 g lard or oil

Method

  1. Briefly blanch the cut beef in boiling water and drain.
  2. Cook the onions in lard until soft, then add the spices.
  3. Add the beef, season and cook 3 minutes; stir in spring onion and cool.
  4. Fill each disc with meat and some egg.
  5. Close with a repulgue edge.
  6. Bake in a very hot oven (250 °C) or clay oven 10–12 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use oil instead of lard in the filling.
  • Make them smaller and serve with salad.
  • Choose lean cuts and skim off fat while cooking.

History

Tucumán holds a national empanada festival in the town of Famaillá, which reflects how important the dish is locally. The knife-cut filling is the feature cooks there insist on most.
