Macher jhol (Bengali fish curry)

Origin: India · West Bengal

Macher jhol is an everyday fish stew with a thin, golden broth. Pieces of fish are lightly fried in mustard oil and simmered with potatoes, turmeric, cumin and green chillies. It tastes light, fresh and gently spicy. It is eaten with plain rice at both lunch and dinner.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g rohu or other firm fish steaks
  • 2 potatoes (250 g), cut in wedges
  • 2 tbsp mustard oil (30 ml)
  • 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp cumin
  • 2 green chillies, 1 tomato (100 g)
  • salt, 400 ml water

Method

  1. Rub the fish with salt and turmeric and fry lightly in mustard oil; set aside.
  2. In the same oil fry the potatoes until golden at the edges.
  3. Add cumin, tomato and a little turmeric and cook until soft.
  4. Pour in the water, add chillies and simmer 10 minutes.
  5. Slide in the fish and cook gently for 7-8 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip frying the fish and poach it in the broth for less oil.
  • Add spinach or green beans to the pot.
  • Pair with a small portion of rice and extra vegetables.

History

Fish and rice are the heart of Bengali home food, thanks to the region's rivers and delta. Jhol, the thin broth-style curry, is the everyday way of cooking freshwater fish such as rohu.
