🥚 Gyeran-jjim (Korean steamed eggs)

Origin: South Korea

Gyeran-jjim is a Korean steamed egg dish: eggs are whisked with water and salt, topped with chopped scallion and cooked gently until they puff up into a soft, fluffy, custard-like cloud. A few drops of sesame oil finish it with a nutty aroma. It is served bubbling hot in its small pot as a side dish (banchan), especially alongside spicy grilled meat and stews.

Recipe

2 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 250 ml water
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 scallion, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp sesame oil

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs with the water and salt until smooth; strain for a silkier texture.
  2. Pour into a small heatproof pot or bowl and sprinkle with most of the scallion.
  3. Steam over simmering water, covered, for 10–12 minutes on low heat until just set and puffed.
  4. Drizzle with sesame oil, add the rest of the scallion and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use just a few drops of sesame oil to finish — the aroma is strong; 1 tsp ≈ 40 kcal.
  • More water and fewer eggs give a lighter, softer custard.
  • Pair it with vegetables and rice rather than with a second high-fat main.

History

Gyeran-jjim is a staple of Korean home cooking and is commonly served in Korean barbecue restaurants, where its mild, soothing texture balances spicy dishes. Many versions exist, from smooth steamed custards to the dramatic 'volcano' style that rises above the rim of an earthenware pot.
