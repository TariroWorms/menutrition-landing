🍳 Pork Sisig

Origin: Philippines · Pampanga

Sisig is a sizzling Filipino dish of grilled pork cheek chopped finely and tossed with onion, chili and sour calamansi juice, often finished with a raw egg cracked on top and a spoon of mayonnaise. It arrives crackling on a hot plate, crispy, tangy and rich. It is a favourite with rice or as a bar snack.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork cheek (or pork jowl)
  • 1 large red onion, finely chopped
  • 2 red chilies, finely chopped
  • 4 calamansi (or 1 lime), juiced
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tbsp mayonnaise
  • Salt and black pepper to taste

Method

  1. Simmer the pork cheek in salted water for 45 minutes until tender, then drain and pat dry.
  2. Grill over high heat until crisp and lightly charred on both sides.
  3. Chop the pork very finely and toss with onion, chili, calamansi juice, salt and pepper.
  4. Heat a cast-iron plate or skillet until very hot, add the sisig and let it sizzle.
  5. Crack the egg on top, add the mayonnaise and stir together at the table.

How to make it lighter

  • Leave out the mayonnaise or use a teaspoon: 1 tbsp mayonnaise is about 90–100 kcal.
  • Trim some of the fat from the pork cheek after boiling.
  • Share a plate and add plenty of raw onion and a side salad.

History

Sisig is strongly associated with Angeles City in Pampanga, where the modern sizzling version is usually credited to a roadside cook named Lucia Cunanan in the 1970s. The dish has since spread across the Philippines and to Filipino restaurants abroad.
