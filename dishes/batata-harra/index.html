🥔 Batata harra (spicy potatoes)

Batata harra, 'spicy potatoes', is a Lebanese mezze favourite of cubed potatoes fried until crisp and golden, then tossed in olive oil with lots of garlic, fresh cilantro, chilli and a good squeeze of lemon. The cubes are crunchy outside and fluffy inside, with a bright, garlicky, lemony heat. It is served hot as a side dish or as part of a mezze spread.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g potatoes, peeled and cut into 2 cm cubes
  • Vegetable oil for frying, or 2 tbsp olive oil for oven-roasting
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 5 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1–2 fresh red chillies, chopped, or 1 tsp chilli flakes
  • A large bunch of cilantro, chopped
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • Salt

Method

  1. Rinse the potato cubes, dry them well and deep-fry at 180 °C for 8–10 minutes until golden and crisp; drain on paper.
  2. Heat the olive oil in a large pan, add the garlic and chilli and cook for 1 minute without browning.
  3. Add most of the cilantro and stir for 30 seconds, then add the potatoes and toss to coat.
  4. Season with salt, take off the heat, squeeze over the lemon juice and sprinkle with the rest of the cilantro. Serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Roast the potato cubes at 220 °C with 2 tbsp olive oil for 30–35 minutes instead of deep-frying.
  • Oil is the main extra here: 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal, so measure it rather than pouring.
  • Serve a small portion next to grilled chicken or green salads to keep the meal balanced.

History

Batata harra is a staple of Lebanese mezze and is found on restaurant tables and in homes across Lebanon and the wider Levant today.
