Shámbar (wheat and bean soup)

Origin: Peru · Trujillo

Shámbar is a thick soup of peeled wheat berries cooked with a mix of pulses such as beans, chickpeas, broad beans and peas, plus smoked ham, pork skin or other cured meats. A sofrito of onion, garlic and ají gives colour, and a handful of mint is stirred in at the end. It is served with toasted corn kernels (cancha) sprinkled on top for crunch.

Recipe

8 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g peeled wheat berries, soaked overnight
  • 100 g each dried beans, chickpeas and broad beans, soaked
  • 100 g peas
  • 200 g smoked ham or bacon, 200 g pork
  • 1 onion, 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ají panca paste
  • 1 bunch mint
  • 3 l water, salt, toasted corn to serve

Method

  1. Cook the pulses and meats together in water for about 1.5 hours.
  2. Add the soaked wheat and cook 40 minutes more.
  3. Sauté onion, garlic and ají panca and add to the pot.
  4. Add the peas and simmer 15 minutes until thick.
  5. Stir in chopped mint and adjust salt.
  6. Serve with toasted corn on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less cured meat and more pulses to reduce salt and fat.
  • Skip the toasted corn or use only a pinch.
  • A meat-free version with smoked paprika keeps the flavour and is vegan.

History

Shámbar is the traditional Monday dish of Trujillo and the La Libertad region, a custom still followed in many homes and restaurants. Its mix of grains and pulses reflects the farming of the northern Andean valleys that supply the city.
