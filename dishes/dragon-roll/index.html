🍣 Dragon roll

Origin: United States · Japanese-American sushi

The dragon roll is an inside-out sushi roll filled with crunchy shrimp tempura and topped with thin slices of avocado and grilled eel, arranged to look like dragon scales. Sushi rice and nori hold it together, and the roll is finished with a drizzle of sweet, glossy eel sauce. Each bite combines crisp, creamy and smoky-sweet textures.

Recipe

2 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g cooked sushi rice
  • 2 sheets nori
  • 6 pieces shrimp tempura
  • 1 ripe avocado, thinly sliced
  • 120 g grilled eel (unagi), sliced
  • 3 tbsp eel sauce

Method

  1. Lay a nori sheet on a mat covered with plastic wrap and spread half the rice over it, then flip it rice-side down.
  2. Place 3 pieces of shrimp tempura along the edge and roll tightly.
  3. Lay alternating slices of avocado and eel over the top of the roll.
  4. Cover with plastic wrap and press gently with the mat to shape.
  5. Cut into 8 pieces with a wet knife and drizzle with eel sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Ask for the eel sauce on the side and use a little: it is mostly sugar.
  • Order half a dragon roll with sashimi or a salad to cut the portion.
  • Rice is the main source of calories: 8 pieces of a rich roll can be a full meal.

History

The dragon roll is a product of the American sushi boom of the late 20th century, when inside-out rolls with rich fillings became popular in the United States. It is not a traditional Japanese dish, and no single chef is reliably credited with inventing it; today it is a fixture on sushi menus worldwide.
