🍛 Misir wat

Misir wat is an Ethiopian stew of red lentils simmered with slowly cooked onion, garlic, ginger and the chili spice blend berbere. The lentils break down into a thick, spicy and earthy sauce with a deep red colour. It is ladled onto injera, the spongy sourdough flatbread made from teff, and eaten by hand, tearing off pieces of injera to scoop it up.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g red lentils, rinsed
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2–3 tbsp berbere
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 750 ml water
  • Salt
  • Injera, to serve

Method

  1. Cook the onions in a dry pot over medium heat for 8–10 minutes, stirring, until soft and reduced.
  2. Add the oil, garlic, ginger and berbere and cook for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the lentils and water, bring to a boil, then simmer for 25–30 minutes, stirring, until thick.
  4. Season with salt and add a splash of water if it gets too thick.
  5. Serve on injera.

How to make it lighter

  • Cooking the onions dry first keeps the flavour while using less oil.
  • Fill half the plate with a vegetable wat or salad and keep injera to one or two pieces.
  • Lentils are filling on their own, so a moderate portion of the stew goes a long way.

History

Misir wat belongs to the Ethiopian and Eritrean tradition of wat stews. Because it contains no animal products, it is a staple on the many fasting days of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church. Today it is one of the most common dishes on Ethiopian restaurant platters worldwide.
