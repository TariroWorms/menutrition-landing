Espumilla (guava meringue)

Origin: Ecuador

Espumilla looks like soft-serve ice cream but is actually a light, sweet foam of egg whites whipped with sugar and guava pulp. It is piled into a wafer cone and often topped with colorful sprinkles or a drizzle of berry syrup. Street vendors sell it in parks and plazas, and it is a favorite treat for children.

Recipe

6 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 3 egg whites
  • 200 g guava pulp (or thick guava purée)
  • 150 g sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 6 wafer cones
  • Berry syrup or sprinkles to decorate

Method

  1. Cook the guava pulp with half the sugar for 5 minutes into a thick jam and cool completely.
  2. Whip the egg whites with the salt until foamy.
  3. Add the remaining sugar gradually and whip to stiff, glossy peaks.
  4. Add the guava jam a spoonful at a time, whipping until the foam is pink and very thick.
  5. Pipe or spoon into cones, decorate and serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • It is almost fat-free; keep it light by skipping sprinkles and syrup.
  • Use very ripe guava so you can reduce the sugar.
  • Serve in a small cup instead of a cone to save calories, and use pasteurized egg whites for safety.

History

Espumilla is a long-standing Ecuadorian street sweet, especially common in Quito and other highland cities. Guava is the classic flavor, though versions with naranjilla, mora (Andean blackberry) or other fruits are also sold.
