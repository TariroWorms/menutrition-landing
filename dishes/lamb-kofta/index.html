🍢 Lamb kofta

Origin: Middle East · Turkey and the Levant

Lamb kofta are skewers or patties of minced lamb mixed with grated onion, cumin, coriander and fresh mint, then grilled until browned and smoky. The outside crisps over the heat while the inside stays juicy and herby. They are served with salad, grilled vegetables and dips, or tucked into flatbread.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g minced lamb
  • 1 onion, grated and squeezed dry
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 3 tbsp chopped fresh mint
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Lemon wedges and salad to serve

Method

  1. Mix the lamb, onion, spices, mint, salt and pepper and knead for 2–3 minutes until sticky.
  2. Chill for 30 minutes so the mixture firms up.
  3. Shape into 8 sausages around skewers or into small patties.
  4. Grill over high heat for 8–10 minutes, turning, until browned and cooked through.
  5. Serve with lemon and a fresh salad.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced lamb, or mix lamb half and half with a leaner minced meat.
  • Grill rather than fry so the fat drips away.
  • Make the plate half salad and grilled vegetables.

History

Kofta, from the Persian word for 'pounded' meat, is a family of minced-meat dishes found from the Balkans and Turkey through the Middle East to South Asia. Grilled lamb kofta are a staple of street grills and family barbecues across the region.
