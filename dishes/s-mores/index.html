🔥 S'mores

Origin: United States

S'mores are a campfire treat made by toasting a marshmallow over a flame until golden and molten inside, then sandwiching it with a square of chocolate between two graham crackers. The heat of the marshmallow softens the chocolate, giving a gooey, sticky centre between crisp, honey-flavoured crackers. They are eaten straight away by hand, often around a bonfire.

Recipe

4 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 8 graham crackers
  • 4 marshmallows, large
  • 40 g chocolate

Method

  1. Break the graham crackers in half and place a piece of chocolate on four halves.
  2. Toast each marshmallow on a skewer over a fire or flame until golden.
  3. Place the hot marshmallow on the chocolate and press the second cracker half on top.
  4. Wait a few seconds for the chocolate to melt, then eat.

How to make it lighter

  • Make one s'more and savour it rather than several in a row.
  • Use a thin square of chocolate instead of a thick piece.
  • Serve with fresh fruit such as strawberries to round out the treat.

History

S'mores are closely tied to American camping and scouting culture; a recipe for 'Some Mores' appeared in a Girl Scouts publication in 1927, and the name is usually explained as a contraction of 'some more'. They remain a summer campfire classic across the United States and Canada.
