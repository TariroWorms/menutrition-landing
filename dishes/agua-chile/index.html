🦐 Shrimp aguachile

Origin: Mexico · Sinaloa

Aguachile is a Mexican dish of raw shrimp “cooked” in lime juice blended with fresh green chili. The shrimp is butterflied, drenched in the bright, fiery liquid and layered with thin slices of cucumber and red onion, then finished with cilantro and avocado. It is citrusy, very spicy and refreshing, and is eaten cold, straight after mixing, as a starter or a light meal.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g very fresh raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 200 ml fresh lime juice (about 8 limes)
  • 2–3 serrano chilies
  • A bunch of cilantro
  • 1 cucumber, thinly sliced
  • 1 small red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 avocado, sliced
  • Salt

Method

  1. Butterfly the shrimp by cutting them almost in half lengthwise, lay them in a shallow dish and season with salt.
  2. Blend the lime juice with the chilies, half the cilantro, a pinch of salt and 50 ml cold water until smooth.
  3. Pour the green chili-lime mixture over the shrimp and leave for 10–15 minutes in the fridge, until the shrimp turn opaque.
  4. Layer cucumber and red onion over the shrimp, top with avocado slices and the remaining cilantro, and serve at once, very cold.

How to make it lighter

  • The shrimp and lime base is light; avocado brings most of the fat, so half an avocado for four keeps it lean.
  • Add extra cucumber to make a bigger plate for few calories.
  • Serve it as a main with a crisp vegetable salad rather than with fried sides.

History

Aguachile — literally “chili water” — comes from the Pacific coast of north-west Mexico, especially Sinaloa. It is usually described as a close relative of ceviche, but marinated for minutes rather than hours. Today it is a staple of mariscos stands and seafood restaurants across Mexico, with green, red and black versions.
