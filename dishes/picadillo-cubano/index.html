Cuban picadillo is a homestyle hash of ground beef simmered in a tomato sauce with cumin, briny green olives, sweet raisins and small cubes of potato. The result is savory, slightly sweet and a little tangy, with a loose, spoonable texture. It is usually served with white rice and black beans, and sometimes fried ripe plantains.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g ground beef
  • 2 potatoes (300 g), diced small
  • 1 onion and 1 green bell pepper, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 400 g crushed tomatoes
  • 60 g green olives, sliced
  • 40 g raisins
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, salt, pepper
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Fry the diced potato in the oil until golden, then set aside.
  2. Sauté onion, pepper and garlic until soft.
  3. Add the beef and brown it, breaking it up, then pour off excess fat.
  4. Stir in tomatoes, cumin, olives and raisins and simmer for 15 minutes.
  5. Return the potatoes, cook 5 more minutes and season.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean ground beef (5–10% fat) and drain the fat after browning.
  • Roast the potatoes in the oven instead of frying them.
  • Serve with a smaller portion of rice and a big salad.

History

Picadillo, from the Spanish picar ('to chop'), is a dish found across Spain and Latin America, and the Cuban version is known for its olives and raisins. It is a fixture of Cuban home cooking and of Cuban-American kitchens in Miami.
