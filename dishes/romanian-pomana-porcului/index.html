Pomana porcului (Romanian fried pork feast)

Origin: Romania

Pomana porcului means the pig's alms, a meal offered to family and neighbours when the household pig is slaughtered in winter. Fresh pork pieces are fried slowly in lard until golden outside and juicy inside, then finished with plenty of crushed garlic. It is served hot with mămăligă, pickles and a spoon of horseradish or pickled peppers.

What it is made of

pork shouldergarlicsaltblack pepperlard

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork shoulder or belly, cubed
  • 60 g lard
  • 6 cloves garlic, crushed
  • salt and black pepper to taste
  • 100 g pickled peppers or cucumbers, to serve

Method

  1. Cut the pork into cubes of about 3 cm and salt lightly.
  2. Melt the lard in a wide heavy pan over medium heat.
  3. Add the pork and fry for 30 to 35 minutes, turning now and then, until browned and cooked through.
  4. Stir in the crushed garlic and a little black pepper for the last 3 minutes.
  5. Serve hot with polenta and pickles.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean shoulder and pour off part of the fat to cut calories.
  • Serve with a big plate of pickled vegetables or cabbage salad for fibre.
  • Oven-roast the cubes on a rack instead of frying in lard.

History

The dish belongs to the old rural custom of slaughtering a pig around St Ignatius on 20 December, before Christmas. Neighbours who helped with the work were fed from the first fresh meat. Today it is still made in villages and on family weekends.
