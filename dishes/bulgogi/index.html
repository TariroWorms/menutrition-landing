🥩 Bulgogi

Origin: South Korea

Bulgogi, 'fire meat', is thinly sliced beef marinated in soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic and grated Asian pear, then grilled or seared over high heat. The pear tenderises the meat and the sugar caramelises at the edges, giving a sweet-savoury, smoky flavour. It is eaten with rice, often wrapped in lettuce leaves.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g beef sirloin or ribeye, very thinly sliced
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp sesame oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, grated
  • ½ Asian pear, grated
  • 1 onion, sliced, and 2 spring onions

Method

  1. Mix the soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic and pear.
  2. Toss the beef and onion in the marinade and leave for at least 30 minutes.
  3. Heat a grill pan or skillet until very hot.
  4. Cook the beef in batches for 2–3 minutes until browned at the edges.
  5. Scatter with spring onion and serve with rice and lettuce leaves.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose a lean cut like sirloin and trim visible fat.
  • Use 1 tbsp sugar and let the pear give most of the sweetness.
  • Wrap the beef in lettuce and keep the rice to a small bowl.

History

Grilled marinated meat has a long history in Korea, and bulgogi is often traced to an older court dish called neobiani. The modern style became a national favourite in the 20th century and is now one of the best-known Korean dishes abroad.
