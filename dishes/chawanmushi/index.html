🥚 Chawanmushi

Origin: Japan

Chawanmushi is a Japanese savoury egg custard steamed in a lidded cup. Eggs are mixed with dashi stock and poured over small pieces of chicken, prawn and mushroom, then gently steamed until just set. The result is silky and wobbly, with a delicate savoury flavour, and it is eaten hot with a spoon as a starter.

What it is made of

eggdashishrimpchickenmushrooms

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 3 eggs
  • 450 ml dashi, cooled
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 100 g chicken thigh, diced small
  • 4 raw prawns, peeled
  • 4 shiitake mushrooms, sliced
  • A few sprigs of mitsuba or parsley

Method

  1. Beat the eggs gently without making foam, mix with the dashi and salt and strain through a fine sieve.
  2. Divide the chicken, prawns and mushrooms between four heatproof cups.
  3. Pour the egg mixture over and skim off any bubbles.
  4. Cover the cups with foil or lids and steam over low heat for 12–15 minutes until just set.
  5. Top with mitsuba or parsley and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Chawanmushi is already a light dish — add extra mushrooms to make it more filling.
  • Use skinless chicken breast instead of thigh to cut the fat.
  • Serve it as a starter before a smaller main course.

History

The name means 'steamed in a tea bowl'. Steamed egg dishes are thought to have reached Japan through Nagasaki's contact with Chinese cooking, and chawanmushi became part of formal kaiseki meals. Today it is a common side dish in Japanese homes, sushi bars and set meals.
