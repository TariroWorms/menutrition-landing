🌿 Casarecce with pesto genovese

Casarecce are short, twisted pasta shapes with a groove down the middle that holds sauce well. Here they are tossed with raw pesto genovese: fresh basil pounded or blended with pine nuts, garlic, grated parmesan and olive oil. The result is bright green, fragrant and herby, with a nutty, savoury finish. It is served straight away, warm or at room temperature.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 350 g dried casarecce
  • 60 g fresh basil leaves
  • 30 g pine nuts
  • 50 g parmesan, finely grated
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 80 ml extra-virgin olive oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Cook the casarecce in well-salted boiling water until al dente; keep a cup of the cooking water.
  2. Meanwhile blend or pound the basil, pine nuts, garlic and a pinch of salt to a coarse paste.
  3. Stir in the parmesan, then add the olive oil gradually until creamy.
  4. Drain the pasta and toss off the heat with the pesto, loosening with a little cooking water.
  5. Serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • Olive oil is the biggest calorie source: 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal, so use less oil and more pasta water to loosen the pesto.
  • Serve a smaller pasta portion alongside a large green salad or grilled vegetables.
  • A spoonful of pesto goes a long way; measure it rather than pouring it freely.

History

Pesto genovese comes from Genoa and the Ligurian coast, where basil grows abundantly; casarecce themselves are a Sicilian shape. Pairing the two is a common modern combination found across Italy and abroad.
