Berner Platte is a hearty Bernese platter of boiled and smoked meats served on a bed of sauerkraut, green beans and boiled potatoes. The meats typically include smoked pork belly and shoulder, a spiced pork sausage and sliced tongue. Everything is simmered gently so the vegetables absorb the smoky meat flavours, and it is brought to the table as one large sharing platter in autumn and winter.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g smoked pork shoulder
  • 300 g smoked pork belly
  • 4 Bernese pork sausages
  • 400 g pre-cooked tongue
  • 600 g sauerkraut
  • 500 g green beans
  • 800 g small potatoes
  • 1 onion, 6 juniper berries, 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Put the pork shoulder in a large pot, cover with water and simmer gently for about 1 hour.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion in the oil, add the sauerkraut, juniper, bay leaves and 200 ml water, lay the pork belly on top, cover and cook over low heat for 1 hour.
  3. Cook the green beans in a little salted water for 10–12 minutes and boil the potatoes until tender.
  4. Add the sausages and tongue to the pork shoulder pot for the last 20 minutes to heat through without boiling.
  5. Slice the meats and arrange them on a large platter with the sauerkraut, beans and potatoes.

How to make it lighter

  • Fill most of your plate with sauerkraut and green beans, which are very low in calories, and take one or two kinds of meat.
  • Smoked pork belly and sausage are the fattiest parts: choose lean shoulder and tongue and trim visible fat.
  • Skip the extra fat sometimes stirred into the sauerkraut — 1 tbsp of oil adds about 120 kcal.

History

Berner Platte is a traditional dish of the canton of Bern. The story goes that it was first served on 5 March 1798, when villagers celebrated a victory over French troops at Neuenegg by putting together whatever meat and vegetables they had. Historians treat the tale as legend, but the platter remains a symbol of Bernese hospitality.
