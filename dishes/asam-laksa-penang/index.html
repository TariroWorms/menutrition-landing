Asam laksa (Penang sour fish noodle soup)

Origin: Malaysia · Penang

Penang asam laksa is a sour, spicy and refreshing noodle soup. Flaked mackerel is simmered with tamarind, lemongrass and chilies to give a thin, tangy broth, then poured over thick rice noodles. It is crowned with cucumber, pineapple, red onion, mint and a swirl of sweet shrimp paste.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g mackerel
  • 300 g thick rice noodles
  • 1 litre water
  • 3 tbsp tamarind pulp
  • 2 lemongrass stalks
  • 4 dried chilies, 4 shallots
  • 1 tbsp shrimp paste
  • cucumber, pineapple, red onion, mint

Method

  1. Poach the mackerel in water with tamarind and lemongrass for 10 minutes.
  2. Remove the fish, flake it and discard the bones.
  3. Blend chilies, shallots and shrimp paste; simmer the paste in the broth 20 minutes.
  4. Return the flaked fish and season with salt and a little sugar.
  5. Cook the noodles, add to bowls, ladle the broth and add the toppings.

How to make it lighter

  • The broth is naturally low in fat; skip extra shrimp paste to cut sodium.
  • Load up on cucumber and pineapple.
  • Use fewer noodles and more vegetables.

History

Asam laksa is a signature street dish of Penang and is often listed among the island's best-loved foods. The sour note comes from asam, the tamarind or garcinia used across Malay cooking.
