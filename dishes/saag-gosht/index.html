Saag gosht is a North Indian curry of lamb braised slowly with a thick purée of spinach, onion, garlic and ginger, seasoned with garam masala. The lamb becomes tender while the spinach cooks down into a smooth, deep-green sauce that clings to the meat. It is earthy and warmly spiced rather than fiery, and is usually served with rice or flatbread.

Recipe

4 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g lamb, cubed
  • 500 g spinach
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 30 g ginger
  • 2 tsp garam masala
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 300 ml water, salt

Method

  1. Fry the onions in the oil until golden, then add the crushed garlic and ginger.
  2. Add the lamb and brown on all sides; stir in the garam masala and salt.
  3. Add the water, cover and simmer for 60 minutes until the lamb is tender.
  4. Wilt the spinach in a separate pan, then blend to a coarse purée.
  5. Stir the spinach into the lamb and simmer uncovered for 10 minutes to thicken.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before cooking.
  • Add extra spinach for more volume per portion.
  • Serve with a measured portion of rice or one flatbread.

History

Saag, meaning leafy greens, is a staple of Punjabi cooking in both India and Pakistan, and pairing it with meat (gosht) is a common home style. The dish became widely known abroad through Indian restaurants in Britain.
