🍙 Supplì al Telefono

Supplì al telefono are the meat-free version of Rome's fried rice croquettes: rice cooked in tomato, shaped around a cube of mozzarella, breaded and deep-fried until crisp. When one is broken open, the melted cheese stretches into long strings. They are a favourite snack in Roman pizzerias, eaten hot with the fingers.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g short-grain rice
  • 400 g canned chopped tomatoes
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 600 ml hot water
  • 125 g mozzarella, cut into 12 cubes
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 100 g breadcrumbs
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Soften the onion in the olive oil, add the tomatoes and simmer for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the rice and the hot water gradually, stirring until cooked and thick, about 18 minutes; season and cool completely.
  3. Shape spoonfuls of rice into ovals with a cube of mozzarella in the centre.
  4. Dip each in beaten egg, then roll in breadcrumbs.
  5. Deep-fry at 175°C for 4–5 minutes until golden, drain and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Oven-bake them at 200°C for 20–25 minutes, brushed with a little oil, instead of deep-frying.
  • Use light mozzarella and a smaller cube in each supplì.
  • Count one supplì as a starter portion and pair it with a salad or vegetable soup.

History

The name 'al telefono' is usually explained by the strings of melted mozzarella, which recall an old telephone cord stretching between the two halves. Supplì of this kind are sold in pizzerias and fry shops all over Rome.
