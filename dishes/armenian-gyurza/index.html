Gyurza (Armenian meat dumplings)

Origin: Armenia

Gyurza are small dumplings with a characteristic braided or crimped edge, filled with minced beef or lamb and onion. They are cooked in meat broth and served in a bowl with some of the broth, topped with garlicky yogurt and sometimes melted butter. The taste is homely and savoury, a little like a richer cousin of manti.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 1 egg
  • 130 ml water
  • 300 g minced beef or lamb
  • 1 onion (120 g)
  • 1 l meat broth
  • 200 g matsun or yogurt
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • salt, pepper, dried basil

Method

  1. Knead the flour, egg, water and a pinch of salt into a firm dough and rest it 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the mince with grated onion, salt, pepper and basil.
  3. Roll the dough thin and cut into 6 cm circles.
  4. Place a little filling on each circle, fold and pinch the edge into a braided seam.
  5. Simmer the dumplings in the broth for 8–10 minutes until they float and the filling is cooked.
  6. Stir crushed garlic into the yogurt and serve the dumplings with broth and garlic yogurt.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef mince instead of fatty lamb to cut the fat noticeably.
  • Skip the melted butter on top; the garlic yogurt is enough.
  • Serve more broth and fewer dumplings per bowl for a lighter meal.

History

The name is often linked to the gyurza viper because the crimped seam resembles snake scales. The dish is especially associated with Armenians from the Syunik and Artsakh areas and with the wider Caucasus.
