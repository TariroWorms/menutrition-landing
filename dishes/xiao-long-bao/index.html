🥟 Xiao long bao (soup dumplings)

Origin: China · Shanghai

Xiao long bao are small steamed dumplings with a thin, pleated wrapper around a pork filling and a spoonful of hot broth. The broth comes from jellied stock mixed into the filling, which melts during steaming. They are served straight from bamboo steamers, eaten carefully from a spoon, often with shredded ginger and vinegar.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g minced pork (not too lean)
  • 250 ml strong pork or chicken broth, chilled until set
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger, plus shredded ginger to serve
  • 2 spring onions, finely chopped
  • 250 g plain flour and 130 ml warm water for the wrappers
  • Salt and white pepper
  • Black or rice vinegar to serve

Method

  1. Mix the flour and warm water into a smooth dough, cover and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Chop the set broth into small cubes and mix with the pork, ginger, spring onion, salt and pepper.
  3. Roll the dough into a log, cut into 24 pieces and roll each into a thin 8 cm circle.
  4. Place a spoonful of filling in the centre and pleat the edges up and around, twisting to seal at the top.
  5. Steam on a lined bamboo steamer over boiling water for 8–10 minutes.
  6. Serve immediately with shredded ginger and vinegar.

How to make it lighter

  • Count dumplings rather than baskets: decide on 6–8 pieces as a portion.
  • Pair them with a plate of steamed greens to fill up.
  • Dip in vinegar and ginger, which add flavour without extra fat.

History

Xiao long bao are usually traced to Nanxiang, a town near Shanghai, in the 19th century. The name means "small basket buns", after the bamboo baskets they are steamed in. They are now a signature of Shanghai cuisine and of dumpling restaurants worldwide.
