Tula pryanik (Russian gingerbread)

Origin: Russia · Tula

The Tula pryanik is a flat, dense gingerbread made with honey and warm spices, filled with a layer of jam or condensed milk and covered with a thin, crackly sugar glaze. Its top is pressed with an embossed pattern or inscription from a carved board. It is cut into pieces and eaten with tea.

Recipe

10 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 150 g honey
  • 150 g sugar
  • 2 eggs
  • 80 g butter
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • cinnamon, cloves, cardamom
  • 250 g thick apricot or plum jam
  • 150 g icing sugar and 3 tbsp water for the glaze

Method

  1. Warm the honey, sugar and butter until dissolved, then cool slightly.
  2. Beat in the eggs and spices and stir in the flour mixed with soda to a soft dough.
  3. Divide in two, roll each into a rectangle, spread one with jam and cover with the other; seal the edges.
  4. Bake at 190 °C for 20–25 minutes.
  5. Brush the hot pryanik with icing sugar mixed with water and let the glaze set.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small squares; it is very sweet.
  • Use a fruit purée filling with no added sugar.
  • Replace a third of the flour with whole-wheat or rye flour.

History

Pryaniki have been baked in Russia for centuries, and the town of Tula south of Moscow is the most famous centre; the city even has a museum devoted to them. Printed pryaniki were popular gifts for holidays and celebrations.
