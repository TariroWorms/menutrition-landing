Shutki bhorta (dried fish mash)

Origin: Bangladesh

Shutki bhorta is made by frying or roasting dried fish and pounding it with garlic, onion, green chilli and mustard oil. It is intensely savoury, salty and fiery, with a strong aroma. A small spoonful is eaten with plenty of rice, so it works as a condiment as much as a side dish.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g dried fish
  • 1 onion (80 g), chopped
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 3 green chillies
  • 2 tbsp mustard oil
  • salt

Method

  1. Rinse the dried fish, soak it in water for 15-20 minutes, then drain well.
  2. Fry or dry-roast the fish until crisp.
  3. Fry the garlic, onion and chillies lightly in mustard oil.
  4. Pound everything together in a mortar to a coarse mash.
  5. Season with salt only if needed and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Soak the fish well to lower the salt.
  • Use only a small portion; it is very concentrated.
  • Pair with plain rice and vegetables.

History

Dried fish, called shutki, is a traditional preserved food in Bangladesh, especially in the coastal and Chittagong areas. Bhorta, a mash seasoned with mustard oil and chilli, is one of the most common ways to eat it.
