Shah plov (Azerbaijani crown pilaf)

Origin: Azerbaijan

Shah plov is the showpiece of Azerbaijani festive tables. A pot is lined with buttered lavash, filled with layers of parboiled saffron rice and a mix of lamb, onion, chestnuts, dried apricots and raisins, then baked until the bread turns golden. It is turned out whole like a crown and cut open at the table. Inside, the rice is fragrant and buttery, with sweet and savoury bites in every spoonful.

Recipe

8 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g basmati rice
  • 700 g boneless lamb
  • 5–6 sheets lavash
  • 150 g butter
  • 200 g peeled chestnuts
  • 150 g dried apricots and raisins
  • 2 onions (300 g)
  • 1 g saffron, salt, cinnamon

Method

  1. Soak the rice, then boil in salted water for 6–7 minutes and drain.
  2. Brown the lamb in some butter with the onions, add a little water and stew 40 minutes; add chestnuts and dried fruit for the last 10.
  3. Brush a deep pot with butter and line it with overlapping lavash sheets, leaving overhang.
  4. Layer rice drizzled with saffron water and butter, then the lamb mix, then rice again.
  5. Fold the lavash over the top, brush with butter, cover and bake at 180 °C for 45–50 minutes.
  6. Rest 10 minutes, invert onto a platter and cut the crust open at the table.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the butter between layers; the lavash still crisps.
  • Use lean leg of lamb and trim the visible fat.
  • Serve a small portion with a fresh herb and tomato salad.

History

The name means "king's pilaf", and the dish is served at weddings and celebrations. It is one of dozens of plov styles in Azerbaijan, where rice dishes are a central part of cuisine.
