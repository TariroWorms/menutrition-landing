Arepa boyacense

Origin: Colombia · Boyacá

Arepa boyacense is thicker and sweeter than most Colombian arepas: the dough mixes cornmeal with a little wheat flour, butter, milk and sugar or panela. Two thin rounds are sandwiched around fresh cheese and slowly griddled, so the outside browns while the cheese melts. It tastes somewhere between a pancake and a cheese bread and is usually eaten with hot chocolate or coffee.

Recipe

8 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g precooked white cornmeal
  • 100 g wheat flour
  • 60 g butter
  • 60 g sugar or grated panela
  • 250 ml warm milk
  • 250 g fresh white cheese, crumbled

Method

  1. Mix cornmeal, flour, sugar and a pinch of salt; add the butter and milk and knead to a soft dough.
  2. Rest the dough 15 minutes, then divide into 16 balls.
  3. Flatten each into a 10 cm round; place cheese on half of them and cover with the rest, sealing the edges.
  4. Cook on a low, lightly greased griddle 6–7 minutes per side until golden.
  5. Serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Reduce the sugar and use a low-fat fresh cheese.
  • Make them smaller and enjoy one as a snack rather than a meal.
  • Pair with black coffee instead of sweetened hot chocolate.

History

The recipe comes from the cool highland department of Boyacá, a dairy and farming area northeast of Bogotá. These arepas are a popular roadside stop for travellers crossing the region.
