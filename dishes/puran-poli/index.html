Puran poli

Puran poli is a festive Maharashtrian flatbread with a soft, sweet heart. A thin wheat dough is wrapped around a paste of cooked chana dal and jaggery scented with cardamom and nutmeg. It is rolled out, cooked on a griddle and eaten hot with plenty of ghee, or with milk.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g chana dal
  • 200 g jaggery, grated
  • 1/2 tsp cardamom powder and a pinch of nutmeg
  • 250 g wheat flour
  • 2 tbsp oil for the dough
  • Salt
  • 4 tbsp ghee for serving and cooking

Method

  1. Knead the flour with oil, salt and water into a soft dough and rest it for 30 minutes.
  2. Boil the dal until very soft, drain and mash.
  3. Cook the mashed dal with jaggery until it thickens into a dry paste, then add spices and cool.
  4. Wrap a ball of filling inside a ball of dough.
  5. Roll gently into a thin round without letting the filling break through.
  6. Cook on a hot griddle with ghee until golden spots appear on both sides.

How to make it lighter

  • Use whole-wheat flour for extra fibre.
  • Reduce the jaggery by a quarter; the filling will still be sweet enough.
  • Cook with just a teaspoon of ghee per poli to keep it lighter.

History

Puran poli is traditionally made for festivals such as Holi, Gudi Padwa and Diwali in Maharashtra. Similar stuffed sweet breads, such as holige and obbattu, are made across southern India. Using jaggery rather than refined sugar is a long-standing tradition in rural kitchens.
