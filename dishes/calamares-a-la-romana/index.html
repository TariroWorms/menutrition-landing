Calamares a la romana are squid rings dipped in flour and beaten egg and deep-fried until puffed and golden. The coating is light and crisp, the squid inside tender. In Spain they are served as a tapa or starter with lemon wedges and a garlicky aioli for dipping.

What it is made of

squidflouregglemonaioli

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g cleaned squid tubes, cut into 1 cm rings
  • 80 g plain flour
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • Olive oil for frying
  • Salt
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges
  • Aioli to serve

Method

  1. Pat the squid rings dry and season with salt.
  2. Toss them in flour, shake off the excess, then dip in beaten egg.
  3. Fry in hot oil (about 180 °C) in small batches for 1–2 minutes until golden.
  4. Drain on paper towels and serve hot with lemon wedges and aioli.

How to make it lighter

  • Share a portion as a starter rather than eating a full plate.
  • Squeeze lemon instead of dipping in aioli — 1 tbsp aioli is about 90–100 kcal.
  • Fry at the right temperature: the coating absorbs less oil when the oil is hot.

History

Battered squid rings 'a la romana' are a classic tapa across Spain, especially in Madrid, where they are also stuffed into a bread roll; despite the name, the link to Rome is unclear.
