Pan con chicharrón (crackling bread)

Origin: Argentina

Pan con chicharrón is a savoury bread found in Argentine bakeries, made by kneading chopped pork cracklings into a lard-enriched dough. The rolls or loaves bake with a soft, slightly flaky crumb studded with salty, crunchy bits of crackling. They are eaten on their own as a snack, often with mate, or served with cheese and cold cuts.

Recipe

10 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml warm water
  • 10 g dry yeast
  • 60 g lard
  • 150 g pork cracklings, finely chopped
  • 10 g salt

Method

  1. Mix flour, water, yeast, lard and salt into a smooth dough.
  2. Knead in the chopped cracklings until evenly spread.
  3. Let rise 1 hour until doubled.
  4. Divide into rolls or shape into a loaf and place on a tray.
  5. Let rise 30 minutes and score the tops.
  6. Bake at 200 °C for 25–30 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the cracklings and add chopped herbs for flavour.
  • Eat one roll with a salad or soup instead of as a stand-alone snack.
  • Replace part of the lard with olive oil.

History

Chicharrones are a by-product of rendering pork fat, a common task on farms when pigs were slaughtered in winter. Adding them to bread was a thrifty way to use every part of the animal. The bread remains a typical bakery item across Argentina, especially in cooler months.
