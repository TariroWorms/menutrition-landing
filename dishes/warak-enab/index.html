🍃 Stuffed Grape Leaves

Origin: Levant · Middle East

Warak enab are grape leaves rolled around a filling of rice, minced beef, tomato and fresh mint, then simmered slowly with lemon juice until tender. The rolls are soft and juicy with a tangy, herby flavor. They are served warm as a main dish or as part of a mezze spread.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 40 grape leaves
  • 250 g short-grain rice, rinsed
  • 300 g minced beef
  • 2 tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 bunch fresh mint, chopped
  • Juice of 2 lemons
  • 1 tsp seven spices, salt
  • 600 ml water

Method

  1. Blanch the grape leaves in hot water for 1–2 minutes and drain.
  2. Mix the rice, beef, tomato, mint, spices and salt.
  3. Place a leaf shiny side down, add a teaspoon of filling, fold the sides and roll tightly.
  4. Pack the rolls snugly in a pot, seam side down, and weigh them with a plate.
  5. Add the water and lemon juice, bring to a boil, then simmer covered for 60–75 minutes until the rice is tender.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef to cut the fat.
  • Serve 6–8 rolls with a large salad instead of a full plate.
  • Skip extra oil when cooking — the meat already provides fat.

History

Stuffed vine leaves are made across the former Ottoman lands, from the Balkans and Turkey to the Levant and Iraq, under names such as dolma, sarma and warak enab. The meat-filled version is a family favorite cooked for gatherings throughout the Levant.
