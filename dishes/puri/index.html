Puri is a small round Indian bread made from whole wheat flour, water and a little oil and salt, rolled thin and deep-fried until it puffs into a golden balloon. It is crisp at the edges and soft inside, with a light, toasty wheat flavour. Puris are eaten hot with potato curry, chickpeas or sweet halwa, often at breakfast and festivals.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g whole wheat flour (atta)
  • 1 tbsp oil, plus oil for deep-frying
  • ½ tsp salt
  • About 130 ml water

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt and 1 tbsp oil, then add water gradually to make a firm, smooth dough.
  2. Rest the dough covered for 15 minutes and divide into 12 balls.
  3. Roll each ball into a 10 cm disc, without flour if possible.
  4. Heat oil to about 180 °C and fry one puri at a time, pressing gently so it puffs; turn once.
  5. Drain on paper and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Count your puris: one small puri is roughly 50 g, so decide on a number before eating.
  • Drain them well on paper to remove surface oil.
  • Pair them with a vegetable or lentil dish rather than a rich, oily curry.

History

Puri is eaten across India, Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh and is a fixture of festive and temple meals. Its exact origins are not documented, but it has long been part of North Indian home cooking.
