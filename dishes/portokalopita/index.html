🍊 Portokalopita

Portokalopita is a Greek orange cake made not with flour but with dried, crumbled phyllo pastry, soaked in a batter of yogurt, eggs, sugar and fresh orange, then baked and drenched in an orange syrup with cinnamon. The texture is moist and custardy with a light crunch at the edges, and the flavour is bright and citrusy. It is served in squares, often chilled.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 450 g phyllo pastry
  • 250 g Greek yogurt
  • 3 eggs
  • 300 g sugar (150 g for batter, 150 g for syrup)
  • 3 oranges (zest and juice)
  • 120 ml sunflower oil
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 250 ml water

Method

  1. Make the syrup: simmer the water, 150 g sugar, juice of 1 orange, some peel and the cinnamon stick for 5 minutes; let it cool.
  2. Tear the phyllo into pieces, spread on trays and let dry for 1–2 hours or dry in a 120 °C oven for 15 minutes.
  3. Whisk the eggs with 150 g sugar, then add the yogurt, oil, the remaining orange zest and juice, and the baking powder.
  4. Fold in the crumbled phyllo, pour into an oiled 25 × 35 cm tin and bake at 180 °C for 40–45 minutes until golden.
  5. Pour the cooled syrup over the hot cake and let it soak for at least 1 hour before cutting.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut 12 squares; one square is a satisfying dessert portion.
  • Use half the syrup quantity; the cake stays moist with less.
  • Serve with fresh orange segments rather than ice cream.

History

Portokalopita is a home-style dessert popular throughout Greece; it is often described as a newer recipe that spread widely in recent decades. Its use of shredded phyllo is a practical way to use up leftover pastry.
