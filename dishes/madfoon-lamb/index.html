Lamb madfoon is a slow-roasted lamb and rice dish from Yemen and Saudi Arabia. Lamb seasoned with baharat and cardamom is cooked in a sealed pit or covered pot until it falls off the bone, picking up a light smoky note, and is served over rice cooked with onion and the meat juices. It is a festive dish for gatherings and weddings.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg lamb shoulder or leg, in large pieces
  • 500 g basmati rice, soaked
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 tbsp baharat
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Rub the lamb with baharat, cardamom and salt.
  2. Wrap the lamb tightly in foil and roast at 160 °C for about 3 hours until very tender.
  3. Fry the onions in the oil, add the rice and 1 litre water with the meat juices and salt.
  4. Simmer covered for about 20 minutes until the rice is cooked.
  5. Serve the lamb over the rice on a large tray.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose leg rather than shoulder for a leaner cut, and trim visible fat.
  • Skim the fat from the meat juices before adding them to the rice.
  • Keep rice to about a cup cooked and add a large salad.

History

The name means 'buried', after the old technique of cooking meat in a sealed pit dug in the ground and heated with embers. Today it is also made in ovens and is common in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia.
