🥩 Tagliata di Manzo

Origin: Italy · Tuscany

Tagliata di manzo is a thick beef sirloin steak, grilled until pink inside, rested and cut into slices on a bed of peppery arugula. It is finished with shavings of parmesan, a drizzle of balsamic vinegar and good olive oil. The result is smoky, juicy meat against sharp greens and salty cheese, served as a main course in trattorias across Italy.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g beef sirloin, about 3 cm thick
  • 60 g arugula
  • 30 g parmesan, shaved
  • 2 tbsp balsamic vinegar
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Take the steak out of the fridge 30 minutes before cooking and pat it dry.
  2. Heat a grill pan until very hot, season the steak and grill 3–4 minutes per side for medium-rare.
  3. Rest the meat for 5 minutes, loosely covered.
  4. Spread the arugula on a platter and slice the steak thinly against the grain.
  5. Lay the slices over the greens, scatter parmesan and rosemary, and drizzle with olive oil and balsamic.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim the visible fat from the sirloin before grilling to save calories without losing flavour.
  • Measure the olive oil: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal, and a light drizzle is enough.
  • Use a generous bed of arugula and a smaller portion of meat: the plate stays filling with fewer calories.

History

The name comes from the Italian verb tagliare, to cut, because the steak is served already sliced. The dish is often associated with Tuscany and its grilling tradition, and today it is a common main course in trattorias across Italy.
