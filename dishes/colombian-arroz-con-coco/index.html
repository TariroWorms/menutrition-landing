Colombian arroz con coco (coconut rice)

Origin: Colombia · Caribbean coast
380kcal

Colombian coconut rice is made by reducing coconut milk until the fat separates and the solids fry into brown, toasty bits called titoté. Rice is then cooked in that caramelised base with water, salt, a little sugar and often raisins, turning a light brown colour. It is fragrant, gently sweet and savoury at once, and is the essential side for fried fish and posta negra.

What it is made of

ricecoconut milkcoconutsugarraisins

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g long-grain rice
  • 800 ml coconut milk
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 50 g raisins (optional)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Water as needed

Method

  1. Simmer the coconut milk in a wide pot, stirring, until the oil separates and brown bits form (20 minutes).
  2. Add the sugar and let it caramelise lightly with the coconut solids.
  3. Pour in 600 ml water, the salt and raisins and bring to a boil.
  4. Add the rice, cook uncovered until the water drops to the level of the rice.
  5. Cover, lower the heat and steam 20 minutes; fluff and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk and skip the sugar.
  • Keep the portion to about 150 g as a side.
  • Mix in some brown rice for extra fibre.

History

Coconut rice is a pillar of the Afro-Caribbean cooking of Cartagena, Barranquilla and the islands of San Andrés. In coastal kitchens the coconut is often grated and pressed by hand to make fresh milk.
