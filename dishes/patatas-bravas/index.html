🥔 Patatas Bravas

Patatas bravas are chunks of potato fried until crisp and golden outside and fluffy inside, served with a spicy sauce of tomato, garlic and chilli. The 'brava' (fierce) sauce varies from mildly smoky to properly hot. They are one of the most popular tapas in Spain, shared from a small plate with toothpicks alongside drinks.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g potatoes, cut into 3 cm chunks
  • Olive oil for frying
  • Sauce: 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 300 g tomato passata
  • 1 tsp hot smoked paprika or chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • Pinch of sugar, salt

Method

  1. For the sauce, soften the garlic in 1 tbsp olive oil, add both paprikas and chilli, then the passata, sugar and salt; simmer 15 minutes and blend smooth.
  2. Parboil the potato chunks for 5 minutes and drain well.
  3. Fry the potatoes in olive oil at about 170 °C until crisp and golden, 8–10 minutes.
  4. Drain on paper, salt lightly and serve with the sauce spooned over.

How to make it lighter

  • Roast the parboiled potatoes in the oven with 1–2 tbsp olive oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Keep the tomato sauce generous: it adds flavour with few calories.
  • Share one plate as a tapa rather than eating it as a main.

History

Patatas bravas are closely associated with the taverns of Madrid, where they are often said to have become a bar staple during the 20th century. Every region and bar has its own version of the sauce, and today the dish is served in tapas bars across Spain and abroad.
