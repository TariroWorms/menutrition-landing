Carbonara is Rome's famous egg-and-cheese pasta: crisp guanciale (cured pork cheek), egg yolks beaten with pecorino romano and black pepper, tossed off the heat with the hot pasta. The eggs thicken into a silky, golden, peppery sauce without any cream. Short ridged mezze maniche catch the sauce and bits of guanciale inside each tube.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g mezze maniche
  • 150 g guanciale, cut into strips
  • 4 egg yolks and 1 whole egg
  • 70 g pecorino romano, finely grated
  • 1–2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • Salt

Method

  1. Cook the pasta in lightly salted water until al dente.
  2. Fry the guanciale in a dry pan until crisp; turn off the heat.
  3. Beat the yolks, egg, pecorino and most of the pepper together.
  4. Add the drained pasta to the guanciale pan, toss, then pour in the egg mixture with a splash of pasta water and toss quickly until creamy but not scrambled.
  5. Serve with the remaining pepper.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 100 g guanciale for four and pour off part of the rendered fat.
  • Use 2 whole eggs instead of 4 yolks for a lighter sauce.
  • Weigh the pasta: about 80 g dry per person is a standard portion.

History

Mezze maniche ('half sleeves') are short, wide ridged tubes, a shorter cut of rigatoni popular in Rome. Carbonara first appears in print in the years after the Second World War, and its origin is debated: one story credits Italian cooks using eggs and bacon from Allied rations, another the charcoal workers ('carbonari') of the Apennines. Today it is one of the signature dishes of Roman trattorias.
