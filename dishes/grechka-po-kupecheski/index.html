Buckwheat merchant-style (grechka po-kupecheski)

Origin: Russia

Grechka po-kupecheski is a one-pan dish in which buckwheat groats cook directly with browned pork, onion and carrot. The grains soak up the meat juices and turn nutty, fluffy and savoury, with a light tomato note. It is a complete meal, often served with pickled cucumbers or a fresh salad.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g buckwheat groats
  • 400 g pork shoulder
  • 1 large onion (150 g)
  • 1 carrot (120 g)
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp sunflower oil
  • 600 ml hot water
  • salt, pepper, bay leaf

Method

  1. Cut the pork into small cubes and brown it in oil in a deep pan.
  2. Add the chopped onion and grated carrot and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and fry for 1 minute.
  4. Rinse the buckwheat and scatter it over the meat.
  5. Pour in the hot water, season, add a bay leaf and bring to a boil.
  6. Cover and cook on low heat for 20-25 minutes until the water is absorbed.
  7. Leave covered for 10 minutes, then stir and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use chicken breast or turkey instead of pork for a leaner dish.
  • Add extra vegetables such as mushrooms or bell pepper and reduce the meat.
  • Serve with a large salad of fresh cucumber and tomato.

History

The name means 'merchant-style', a common Russian way of describing generous, well-stocked dishes with plenty of meat. Buckwheat itself has been a cornerstone of Russian cooking for centuries.
