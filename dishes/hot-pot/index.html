🍲 Chinese Hot Pot

Origin: China · Sichuan and Chongqing

Hot pot is a communal meal in which a pot of simmering broth sits in the middle of the table and diners cook their own ingredients in it. This version uses thinly sliced beef, tofu, mushrooms and leafy vegetables in a chili-spiced broth. Everything cooks in seconds to minutes, so the meal is slow, social and varied, with the broth growing richer as it goes.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 litres beef or vegetable broth
  • 3–6 dried chilies
  • 3 slices ginger and 3 garlic cloves
  • 400 g beef, very thinly sliced
  • 300 g firm tofu, in cubes
  • 250 g mixed mushrooms
  • 300 g leafy greens such as napa cabbage or spinach
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Bring the broth to a simmer with the chilies, ginger and garlic for 15 minutes; season with salt.
  2. Arrange the beef, tofu, mushrooms and greens on platters.
  3. Set the pot on a portable burner in the centre of the table and keep it at a gentle simmer.
  4. Let everyone cook ingredients in the broth: beef 20–30 seconds, mushrooms and tofu a few minutes, greens until just wilted.
  5. Lift out with a slotted spoon and eat at once; sip the broth at the end.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean cuts of beef and load up on mushrooms and greens.
  • Skip oily chili oil layers on the broth surface; dried chilies give heat with little fat.
  • Dipping sauces add up quickly; use a light one, such as vinegar with garlic and herbs.

History

Cooking at the table in a shared pot has a long history in China, with many regional styles. The fiery, chili-laden version is most closely associated with Sichuan and Chongqing, and hot pot restaurants are now popular across China and in Chinese communities worldwide.
