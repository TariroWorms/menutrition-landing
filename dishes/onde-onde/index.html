Onde-onde (palm sugar glutinous rice balls)

Origin: Malaysia
170kcal

Onde-onde are green, chewy rice balls that burst with liquid palm sugar when bitten. The dough of glutinous rice flour and sweet potato is flavoured and coloured with pandan, filled with a piece of gula melaka and boiled until the balls float. They are rolled in fresh grated coconut and eaten at room temperature.

Recipe

8 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g glutinous rice flour
  • 100 g steamed sweet potato, mashed
  • 100 ml pandan juice
  • 100 g palm sugar, chopped
  • 100 g fresh grated coconut
  • a pinch of salt

Method

  1. Knead flour, sweet potato, pandan juice and salt into a soft dough.
  2. Pinch off small pieces, flatten and fill with palm sugar.
  3. Seal tightly and roll into balls.
  4. Boil in water until the balls float, then 1 minute more.
  5. Drain, roll in coconut mixed with a pinch of salt and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Make smaller balls to control portions.
  • Use less palm sugar in each ball.
  • Fresh coconut, not desiccated, adds moisture and less added sugar.

History

Onde-onde are a popular kuih across Malaysia and Singapore, with similar sweets in Indonesia. The surprise of the molten sugar centre is the main attraction.
