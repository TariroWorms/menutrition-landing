🦆 Duck à l'orange

Origin: France · classic French cuisine

Duck à l'orange is a French classic of duck breast seared skin-side down until the fat renders and the skin turns crisp, then served sliced and pink with a glossy orange sauce. The sauce balances sweet and sour: caramelised sugar deglazed with vinegar and orange juice, finished with zest and a little butter. It is served with potatoes or green vegetables.

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 2 duck breasts (about 350 g each)
  • 2 oranges: juice and zest
  • 30 g sugar
  • 2 tbsp vinegar
  • 100 ml water
  • 15 g butter
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Score the duck skin, season and place skin-side down in a cold pan.
  2. Cook over medium heat 8–10 minutes until the skin is crisp, pour off the fat, turn and cook 3–4 minutes more; rest 5 minutes.
  3. In a small pan melt the sugar to a light caramel, add the vinegar, then the orange juice and water.
  4. Simmer until slightly syrupy, add the zest and whisk in the butter.
  5. Slice the duck and spoon the sauce over.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin after cooking: most of the fat in duck breast is in the skin.
  • Skip the butter at the end, the sauce is still glossy.
  • Serve with steamed vegetables rather than roast potatoes.

History

Pairing duck with bitter oranges is an old European idea, and the dish became a showpiece of French haute cuisine. Its exact origin is debated, with some accounts tracing it to Italian cooks at the French court, and it enjoyed a famous revival in mid-20th-century French restaurants.
