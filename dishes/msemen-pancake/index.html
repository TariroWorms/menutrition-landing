🫓 Yeasted msemen

Msemen is a Moroccan square flatbread made from flour and semolina dough that is stretched paper-thin, brushed with butter, sprinkled with semolina and folded into layers before being pan-fried. It comes out flaky and crisp outside, soft and chewy inside. It is eaten warm at breakfast or with afternoon mint tea. This version uses a little yeast for a softer, puffier pancake.

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 150 g fine semolina
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp dried yeast
  • About 250 ml warm water
  • 60 g butter, melted
  • 2 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Mix the flour, semolina, salt, yeast and water into a smooth, soft dough and knead for 10 minutes.
  2. Divide into 8 balls, oil them and rest for 15 minutes.
  3. On an oiled surface, stretch each ball very thin, brush with butter and sprinkle with a little semolina.
  4. Fold into a square: sides to the middle, then top and bottom to the middle; flatten gently.
  5. Cook in a dry or lightly oiled pan over medium heat for 3–4 minutes per side until golden and crisp.

How to make it lighter

  • Brush with just a thin layer of butter between the folds — 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal.
  • Cook in a non-stick pan with no extra oil.
  • Have one msemen with mint tea and fresh fruit rather than adding honey or jam.

History

Msemen is a traditional bread of Morocco and is also made in Algeria and Tunisia. It is a common breakfast and street food across the Maghreb today, cooked at home and sold from griddles in markets.
