Pollo a la brasa (Peruvian rotisserie chicken)

Origin: Peru

Pollo a la brasa is Peru’s beloved rotisserie chicken: a whole bird marinated with garlic, cumin, soy sauce, vinegar and ají panca, then turned slowly over charcoal or wood until the skin is bronzed and smoky. It is served with thick fries, a simple salad and a set of sauces, especially creamy ají and huacatay sauces. Sunday family dinners and celebrations often revolve around it.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 1.6 kg
  • 6 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp ají panca paste
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp vinegar
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 800 g potatoes for fries

Method

  1. Blend garlic, cumin, ají panca, soy sauce, vinegar, oregano, oil, salt and pepper into a marinade.
  2. Rub the chicken all over and under the skin; marinate overnight in the fridge.
  3. Roast on a rotisserie or at 200 °C for about 75 minutes, until the juices run clear.
  4. Rest the chicken for 10 minutes before carving.
  5. Meanwhile cut and fry or bake the potatoes.
  6. Serve in quarters with fries, salad and ají sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin before eating to cut fat.
  • Swap fries for a large green salad or roasted vegetables.
  • Choose a yoghurt-based ají sauce instead of mayonnaise-based ones.

History

The rotisserie style became popular in Lima in the mid-twentieth century, with the Chaclacayo area often credited as an early centre. Pollo a la brasa is now so widespread that Peru celebrates a national day devoted to it.
