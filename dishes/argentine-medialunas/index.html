Medialunas (Argentine sweet crescents)

Origin: Argentina

Medialunas are small crescent pastries found in every Argentine bakery and café. They are smaller, softer and sweeter than a French croissant, and the butter version is brushed with a sticky sugar syrup straight from the oven. A second type, de grasa, is made with fat instead of butter and is less sweet. They are eaten for breakfast or afternoon merienda with coffee, café con leche or mate, sometimes filled with ham and cheese.

Recipe

12 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 200 ml warm milk
  • 80 g sugar
  • 1 egg
  • 10 g dry yeast
  • 250 g cold butter for laminating
  • 1 tsp vanilla, pinch of salt
  • Syrup: 100 g sugar, 100 ml water, 1 tbsp honey

Method

  1. Mix the flour, milk, sugar, egg, yeast, vanilla and salt into a soft dough and chill 30 minutes.
  2. Roll out, place the flattened butter in the middle and fold the dough over it.
  3. Roll and fold three times, chilling 20 minutes between folds.
  4. Roll to 4 mm, cut long triangles and roll each from the base into a crescent.
  5. Let them rise until puffy, brush with egg and bake at 200 °C for 12–15 minutes.
  6. Boil the syrup ingredients for 3 minutes and brush over the hot medialunas.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the syrup or brush on only a thin coat.
  • Eat one medialuna with fruit and yogurt rather than three.
  • Choose a ham and cheese medialuna for a more filling breakfast with protein.

History

Crescent pastries reached Argentina with European bakers, and local workshops adapted the recipe to a sweeter, breadier style. Bakery pastries in Argentina are known collectively as facturas, and medialunas are by far the most popular. Ordering a coffee with two or three medialunas is a national habit.
