🍝 Mentaiko pasta

Mentaiko pasta is a Japanese-style spaghetti tossed with mentaiko — salted, chili-marinated pollock or cod roe — plus butter and a little cream. The tiny eggs coat the noodles in a pale pink, creamy, gently spicy sauce with a briny pop. It is finished with shredded nori and fresh shiso leaves and eaten straight away while warm.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g spaghetti
  • 80 g mentaiko (1–2 sacs)
  • 20 g butter, softened
  • 3 tbsp cream
  • 1 sheet nori, cut into thin strips
  • 4 shiso leaves, finely shredded
  • Salt

Method

  1. Cook the spaghetti in salted water until al dente.
  2. Slit the mentaiko sacs and scrape the roe into a large bowl; mix with the butter and cream.
  3. Drain the pasta, keeping a few spoons of cooking water.
  4. Toss the hot spaghetti in the bowl with the mentaiko sauce, loosening with a little pasta water.
  5. Serve topped with nori and shiso.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the butter — 10 g of butter is about 75 kcal — and use pasta water for creaminess.
  • Serve a smaller portion of pasta with a side salad.
  • Go light on the cream; the roe itself makes the sauce rich.

History

Mentaiko pasta is a classic of yōshoku, the Western-influenced cooking of Japan. It is often credited to a Tokyo spaghetti restaurant in the 1960s known for combining Japanese ingredients with pasta. Today it is a standard on café menus and in convenience stores across Japan.
