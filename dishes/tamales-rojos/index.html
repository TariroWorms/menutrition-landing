🫔 Tamales Rojos

Tamales rojos are red pork tamales: corn masa beaten with lard, filled with pork simmered in a sauce of dried guajillo chilies, and steamed in corn husks. The masa is rich and tender, and the filling is brick-red, mildly hot and earthy. They are among the most traditional tamales for Christmas, New Year and Día de la Candelaria.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g masa harina (nixtamalised corn)
  • 600 ml warm pork stock
  • 120 g lard
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 500 g pork shoulder
  • 6 dried guajillo chilies
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 18 dried corn husks

Method

  1. Simmer the pork with salt and garlic for about 1.5 hours; keep the stock and shred the meat.
  2. Toast, seed and soak the guajillos, then blend with a little stock into a smooth red sauce and simmer the pork in it for 10 minutes.
  3. Beat the lard until fluffy, then beat in masa harina, salt, baking powder and warm stock.
  4. Spread masa on soaked husks, add the red pork, and fold.
  5. Steam upright for 60–75 minutes until the masa releases from the husk.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less lard in the masa for a lighter dough: fat is the main source of calories here.
  • Choose lean pork and skim the fat from the stock before using it.
  • One tamal with a crunchy cabbage or radish salad makes a smaller, filling meal.

History

Tamales go back to pre-Columbian Mesoamerica, while lard and pork entered Mexican cooking after the Spanish conquest. Red pork tamales with guajillo sauce are now a classic across central and northern Mexico and are often made in large batches by families for the holidays.
