Lori cheese and herb lavash wrap

Origin: Armenia

This everyday Armenian bite pairs Lori, a firm, salty brined cow's-milk cheese, with soft lavash and a handful of fresh herbs such as tarragon, basil, coriander and spring onion. Cucumber and tomato are often added. Everything is rolled up by hand at the table, and the flavour is fresh, salty and fragrant. It is eaten for breakfast, as a snack or as part of a spread of greens and cheese.

Recipe

2 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 2 sheets lavash (120 g)
  • 100 g Lori cheese (or another brined cheese)
  • 1 cucumber (150 g)
  • 1 tomato (120 g)
  • 1 handful of tarragon, basil, coriander and spring onion (40 g)

Method

  1. Soak the cheese in cold water for 15 minutes if it is very salty, then slice it.
  2. Lay out the lavash and spread the cheese across one edge.
  3. Add sliced cucumber, tomato and plenty of herb sprigs.
  4. Roll up tightly, tucking in the sides.
  5. Cut in half and eat right away.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose a lower-fat brined cheese to cut calories.
  • Double the herbs and vegetables to bulk up the wrap with few calories.
  • Use wholemeal lavash for extra fibre.

History

Lori cheese takes its name from the Lori province in northern Armenia and is one of the country's best-known cheeses. A plate of cheese, herbs and lavash is a standard start to an Armenian meal.
