🥙 Shami kebab

Origin: Pakistan · Indian subcontinent

Shami kebabs are soft, round patties of minced beef cooked with split chickpeas (chana dal), onion, ginger, garlic and warm spices, then ground smooth, bound with egg and shallow-fried. They are crisp outside and almost melting inside, and are served as a snack or starter with green chutney, sliced onion and lemon, or tucked into a meal of rice and curry.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g lean minced beef
  • 100 g chana dal (split chickpeas), soaked 1 hour
  • 1 onion, quartered, plus 1 onion finely chopped
  • 2 cm ginger and 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp garam masala, 1 tsp cumin seeds, 4 cloves, 1 cinnamon stick, 2 dried chillies
  • 1 egg
  • Chopped coriander and mint
  • 3 tbsp oil for frying, salt

Method

  1. Put the beef, drained dal, quartered onion, ginger, garlic, whole spices and salt in a pot with 400 ml water.
  2. Simmer covered for 40 minutes, then uncover and cook until completely dry.
  3. Cool slightly, remove the cinnamon stick and grind the mixture to a smooth paste.
  4. Mix in the egg, chopped onion, coriander, mint and garam masala and shape 12 flat patties.
  5. Shallow-fry in oil over medium heat for 2–3 minutes per side until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef and pan-fry in a non-stick pan with a thin film of oil.
  • Patties can be brushed with oil and baked at 200 °C for 15–20 minutes instead of fried.
  • Serve two kebabs with a large onion-and-cucumber salad rather than with paratha or rice.

History

Shami kebabs are usually associated with the Mughal and Nawabi kitchens of North India, especially Lucknow and Hyderabad. The name is usually linked to Sham, the old Arabic name for the Levant, though the exact story is uncertain. Today they are a staple of Ramadan iftar tables, weddings and tea-time across Pakistan and North India.
