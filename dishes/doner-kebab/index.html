🥙 Döner kebab

Origin: Turkey · Bursa

Döner kebab is seasoned lamb and beef stacked on a vertical spit, roasted slowly as it turns, and shaved off in thin slices. The outside caramelises while the inside stays juicy. It is served in pita or on a plate with salad and a garlicky yogurt sauce, and is one of the best-known street foods in the world.

Recipe

4 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g lamb and 400 g beef, thinly sliced
  • 1 onion, grated
  • 1 tsp each cumin, paprika, oregano and black pepper, salt
  • 4 pita breads
  • Lettuce, tomato, cucumber and red onion
  • 200 g plain yogurt with 1 garlic clove and a pinch of salt

Method

  1. Mix the meat with the grated onion, spices and salt and marinate for at least 2 hours.
  2. Stack the slices tightly on a skewer or press into a loaf tin and roast at 200 °C for about 45 minutes; or sear in batches in a very hot pan.
  3. Slice the roasted meat very thinly and crisp the slices briefly in a hot pan.
  4. Warm the pita, fill with meat and salad and spoon over the yogurt sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose a döner plate with salad instead of bread to save carbohydrates.
  • Leaner beef in the mix keeps the fat lower than all-lamb döner.
  • Use the yogurt sauce generously but skip extra oily sauces.

History

Roasting meat on a vertical spit is usually credited to cooks in Bursa in the 19th century, with İskender Efendi the name most often mentioned. Turkish immigrants made döner in pita a fast food icon in Germany from the 1970s, and from there it spread across Europe and beyond, inspiring relatives such as shawarma and gyros.
