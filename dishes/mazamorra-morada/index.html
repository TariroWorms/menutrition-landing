Mazamorra morada (purple corn pudding)

Origin: Peru

Mazamorra morada is a deep violet spoon dessert from Lima. Purple corn cobs are simmered with pineapple peel, quince, cinnamon and cloves to make a dark, fragrant liquid, which is then sweetened and thickened with sweet potato starch or cornstarch. Chunks of fruit and prunes or apricots are stirred in, and the pudding is finished with ground cinnamon. It is served warm or cold, often in the same bowl as arroz con leche, a pairing known in Lima as a "combinado".

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g dried purple corn on the cob
  • peel and core of 1 pineapple, plus 200 g pineapple chunks
  • 1 quince or 1 apple, diced
  • 100 g dried apricots and prunes
  • 2 cinnamon sticks, 6 cloves
  • 200 g sugar, 80 g sweet potato starch or cornstarch, juice of 1 lime

Method

  1. Break the cobs and boil them in 3 litres of water with pineapple peel, cinnamon and cloves for 40 minutes.
  2. Strain the purple liquid; you need about 2 litres.
  3. Add the sugar, dried fruit, pineapple chunks and quince and simmer for 15 minutes.
  4. Stir in the starch dissolved in cold water and cook, stirring, until thick and glossy.
  5. Off the heat add the lime juice; serve dusted with cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the sugar; the fruit adds natural sweetness.
  • Serve in small cups as a light dessert.
  • Skip the arroz con leche for a lighter, fat-free dessert.

History

Purple corn is an ancient Andean crop, and mazamorras, thick porridges, were made with it well before colonial times. The sweet, spiced version with fruit took shape in colonial Lima and is especially associated with October and the Señor de los Milagros processions.
