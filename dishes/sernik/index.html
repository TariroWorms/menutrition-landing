🍰 Sernik (Polish cheesecake)

Sernik is Polish baked cheesecake made from twaróg, a fresh curd cheese similar to quark, mixed with eggs, sugar and vanilla and often studded with raisins. It sits on a thin shortcrust base and bakes into a dense yet creamy, mildly tangy cake. It is served in squares at room temperature, with coffee or tea, and is a fixture of Easter and Christmas tables.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg quark (or twaróg), drained
  • 4 eggs
  • 180 g sugar
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 80 g raisins
  • 300 g ready shortcrust pastry
  • 1 tbsp potato starch

Method

  1. Line a 24 cm springform tin with the rolled pastry and blind-bake at 180 °C for 15 minutes.
  2. Press the quark through a sieve or blend until smooth.
  3. Beat in the sugar, eggs one by one, vanilla and starch, then fold in the raisins.
  4. Pour over the base and bake at 160 °C for about 60 minutes until just set in the centre.
  5. Cool in the switched-off oven with the door ajar, then chill before slicing.

How to make it lighter

  • Use low-fat quark; it bakes just as well.
  • Cut 12 slices from a 24 cm cake instead of 8.
  • Cut the sugar by a quarter and let the raisins add sweetness.

History

Curd-cheese cakes have a long history in Polish baking, and sernik is one of the country's best-known cakes. Regional styles exist, such as the Kraków version with a lattice top and the Viennese-style sernik without a base.
