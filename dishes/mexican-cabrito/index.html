Cabrito (Monterrey roast kid goat)

Origin: Mexico · Northern Mexico

Cabrito is a young, milk-fed kid goat split open, salted and roasted slowly beside or above wood embers. The meat is pale, mild and very tender, with crisp, smoky skin. It is served in pieces with flour or corn tortillas, salsa, guacamole and charro beans, and is a centrepiece of northern Mexican feasts.

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kid goat (about 5 kg), split
  • 40 g coarse salt
  • 1 head garlic
  • 4 limes
  • flour tortillas, salsa and beans to serve

Method

  1. Rub the goat with crushed garlic, lime juice and salt and leave for an hour.
  2. Spread it on a stake or rack beside a bed of hot wood embers.
  3. Roast slowly for 3–4 hours, turning regularly so it cooks evenly.
  4. Baste occasionally with salted water to keep the meat moist.
  5. Cut into pieces and serve with tortillas, salsa and beans.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose leg and loin pieces, which are the leanest.
  • Remove the crisp skin if you want to cut fat.
  • Pair with salsa and salad instead of extra tortillas.

History

Goat herding came to northern Mexico with Spanish and Sephardic settlers in the colonial period. Over time cabrito became the emblem of Monterrey and Nuevo León, where many restaurants roast it in open view.
