Chocolate santafereño (hot chocolate with cheese)

Origin: Colombia · Bogotá

Chocolate santafereño is hot chocolate made from tablets of cacao and panela, whisked until foamy with a wooden molinillo. It comes with a slice of soft white cheese that diners drop into the cup to melt, along with bread, almojábanas or buñuelos. The drink is thick, mildly sweet and cinnamon-scented. It is a classic afternoon snack, called "onces", in Bogotá.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g chocolate tablet with sugar
  • 800 ml milk (or half water)
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 150 g fresh white cheese, sliced
  • 4 bread rolls or almojábanas

Method

  1. Heat the milk with cinnamon in a chocolatera or pot.
  2. Break in the chocolate and stir until melted.
  3. Bring to a boil and whisk vigorously with a molinillo to make foam.
  4. Repeat boiling and whisking twice more.
  5. Serve with cheese slices and bread on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Make it with half milk, half water.
  • Use dark chocolate with less sugar.
  • Choose one small bread rather than several.

History

The name refers to Santa Fe, the historic name of Bogotá. Drinking chocolate was popular in colonial times, and the habit of adding cheese has become a well-known Bogotá tradition.
