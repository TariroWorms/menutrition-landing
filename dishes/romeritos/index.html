Romeritos (seepweed in mole with shrimp patties)

Origin: Mexico

Romeritos are the young sprigs of a wild seepweed plant, blanched and simmered in a thick mole sauce with potatoes, nopales and little fritters of dried shrimp and egg. The greens taste mildly salty and herbal, and the mole adds sweetness, spice and depth. The dish is served on its own or with tortillas and rice.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g romeritos, cleaned
  • 500 g prepared mole paste
  • 1 litre chicken or vegetable broth
  • 4 potatoes, diced and boiled
  • 300 g nopales, cooked
  • 60 g ground dried shrimp
  • 3 eggs, separated
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Blanch the romeritos in boiling water for 3 minutes and drain.
  2. Dilute the mole paste in the broth and simmer 15 minutes until it thickens.
  3. Beat the egg whites stiff, fold in the yolks and the dried shrimp powder.
  4. Fry spoonfuls of the mixture until golden to make the patties.
  5. Add romeritos, potatoes, nopales and patties to the mole and heat for 10 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less mole paste and more broth to lower the fat.
  • Bake or pan-fry the shrimp patties in minimal oil.
  • The dish is salty from the shrimp, so add no extra salt until you taste it.

History

Romeritos are a fixture of Christmas Eve and Lent in central Mexico, especially around Mexico City, where they replace meat on fasting days. The plant is a native quelite, one of the wild greens Mexicans have gathered since ancient times.
