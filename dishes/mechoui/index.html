🍖 Méchoui

Méchoui is a whole lamb rubbed with butter, garlic, cumin and paprika and roasted slowly on a spit or in a pit oven until the meat falls from the bone. The skin turns crisp and smoky while the meat inside stays tender and juicy. It is pulled apart by hand at the table and eaten with a pinch of cumin and salt, often for weddings and religious feasts.

Recipe

8 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1 lamb shoulder or leg on the bone (about 2.5 kg), in place of a whole lamb
  • 60 g butter, softened
  • 6 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 2 tsp ground cumin, plus extra to serve
  • 2 tsp sweet paprika
  • salt

Method

  1. Mash the butter with garlic, cumin, paprika and salt.
  2. Make small cuts in the lamb and rub the butter mixture all over and into the cuts.
  3. Roast covered at 160 °C for about 3½ hours, basting every hour.
  4. Uncover, raise the heat to 220 °C and roast 20 minutes more until the skin is crisp.
  5. Pull the meat apart and serve with a small bowl of cumin and salt for dipping.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean leg meat over fatty shoulder and trim the visible fat cap.
  • Use half the butter and baste with the pan juices instead.
  • Serve the lamb with a large salad or grilled vegetables to round out the plate.

History

Méchoui, from the Arabic word for 'grilled' or 'roasted', is a festive dish of the Maghreb, especially Morocco and Algeria, and is closely linked to Eid al-Adha and large family celebrations. Roasting a whole animal over embers or in a clay pit is a long-standing tradition among rural and Berber communities. Today it is served at feasts and in specialist restaurants across North Africa.
