🍮 Kazandibi

Kazandibi is a Turkish milk pudding whose bottom is deliberately scorched in the pan until it forms a dark, caramelised skin. The set pudding is cut into squares and rolled so the burnt-sugar layer sits on top. Traditionally the pudding is thickened with very finely shredded chicken breast, which gives a subtly chewy, stretchy texture without any meaty taste. It is served cold, often with a dusting of cinnamon.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 litre milk
  • 150 g sugar
  • 60 g cornstarch
  • 100 g chicken breast, poached and shredded into fine threads
  • 20 g butter
  • 1 tsp sugar for the pan
  • Cinnamon to serve

Method

  1. Rinse the poached chicken threads several times in cold water and pound them until very fine.
  2. Whisk the milk, sugar and cornstarch in a pan and cook, stirring, until thick; stir in the chicken threads.
  3. Butter a heavy pan or tray, sprinkle with sugar and heat until the sugar starts to caramelise.
  4. Pour in the pudding and cook on low heat for 10–15 minutes without stirring until the bottom turns dark brown.
  5. Cool, then chill for at least 4 hours. Cut into squares, roll each so the caramelised side is on top, and dust with cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut smaller squares — a dense, sweet pudding is easy to overeat.
  • Reduce the sugar in the pudding by a third; the caramel layer still gives plenty of sweetness.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk and grease the pan with just a thin film of butter.

History

The name means 'bottom of the pot' in Turkish. Kazandibi is a variation of tavuk göğsü, the chicken-breast milk pudding of Ottoman palace kitchens, made by caramelising the bottom of the pudding. Today it is a fixture of Istanbul's muhallebi (pudding) shops.
