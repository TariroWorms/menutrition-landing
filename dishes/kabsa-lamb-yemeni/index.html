🍖 Yemeni lamb kabsa

Origin: Yemen · Arabian Peninsula

Yemeni lamb kabsa is a one-pot rice dish in which lamb is slow-simmered with onion, tomato and whole spices, and the rice is then cooked in the rich, aromatic broth. Cardamom, cinnamon and dried lime give it a warm, slightly tangy perfume. The tender meat is served on top of the rice on a large shared platter.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g lamb on the bone, in pieces
  • 400 g long-grain rice, rinsed
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, grated
  • 2 dried limes, pierced
  • 5 cardamom pods and 1 cinnamon stick
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 1.2 l water

Method

  1. Brown the onion in oil, add the lamb and sear on all sides.
  2. Add the tomatoes, cardamom, cinnamon, dried limes, salt and pepper, cover with water and simmer for about 1.5 hours until the lamb is tender.
  3. Lift out the meat; measure about 800 ml of the broth back into the pot.
  4. Add the rice, bring to a boil, then cover and cook on low heat for 20 minutes.
  5. Serve the rice on a platter with the lamb on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb and skim the fat off the broth before adding the rice.
  • Use 1 tbsp oil instead of 2 — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve a smaller rice portion with a large tomato and cucumber salad.

History

Kabsa-style rice and meat dishes are cooked across the Arabian Peninsula, and Yemeni cooks prepare their own versions with lamb and local spice blends. Today it is a common dish for family lunches and gatherings in Yemen and among Yemeni communities abroad.
