Peshawari kulcha (leavened tandoor flatbread)

Origin: Pakistan · Peshawar

Peshawari kulcha is a round, fairly thick flatbread made from a leavened wheat dough enriched with milk and yogurt, baked against the wall of a tandoor. It comes out soft inside with a golden, slightly crisp crust, brushed with butter and often sprinkled with sesame. It is eaten for breakfast with tea or with chana (chickpea curry), or alongside kebabs and stews.

Recipe

8 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar, 1 tsp salt
  • 150 ml milk
  • 100 g yogurt
  • 30 ml oil, 30 g butter
  • sesame seeds

Method

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, sugar and salt; add milk, yogurt and oil and knead to a soft dough.
  2. Cover and rise for 1 hour.
  3. Divide into 8 balls and flatten into rounds, pricking the centre with a fork.
  4. Brush with water, sprinkle with sesame and bake at 250 °C for 8–10 minutes, or in a tandoor.
  5. Brush with butter and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half wholemeal flour for more fibre.
  • Limit the butter on top.
  • Enjoy with chickpeas or lentils for a complete breakfast.

History

Kulcha is a bread common to Peshawar's bazaars, where bakers work tandoors from early morning. Similar breads exist across the region but the Peshawari version is known for being plain and fluffy.
