Mole coloradito (Oaxacan red mole)

Origin: Mexico · Oaxaca

Mole coloradito sits between the dark mole negro and the lighter moles in richness. Ancho and guajillo chiles are blended with roasted tomato, almonds, sesame, raisins, fried bread and plantain, then sweetened with Oaxacan chocolate. The result is a glossy red sauce, gently sweet and warm with cinnamon, ladled over chicken with rice and tortillas.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces
  • 6 ancho chiles
  • 3 guajillo chiles
  • 2 tomatoes
  • 40 g almonds
  • 2 tbsp sesame seeds
  • 30 g raisins
  • 1/2 ripe plantain
  • 1 slice bread
  • 40 g Oaxacan chocolate
  • cinnamon, oregano, garlic, onion

Method

  1. Poach the chicken with onion and garlic for 35 minutes; reserve the broth.
  2. Toast and soak the chiles; roast the tomatoes, onion and garlic.
  3. Lightly fry the almonds, sesame, raisins, bread and plantain.
  4. Blend everything with some broth into a smooth paste.
  5. Fry the paste in a heavy pot, stirring, for 10 minutes, then add chocolate and broth.
  6. Simmer 20 minutes, season, and spoon over the chicken.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve a modest ladle of sauce; most of the calories sit in it.
  • Use skinless chicken breast for a leaner plate.
  • Pair with salad or beans instead of a large mound of rice.

History

It is one of the classic moles of Oaxaca and a common dish at weddings and family celebrations there. Many Oaxacan families buy the paste ready-made at markets and thin it with broth at home.
