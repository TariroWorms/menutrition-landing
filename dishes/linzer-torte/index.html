🥧 Linzer torte

Origin: Austria · Linz

Linzer torte is an Austrian tart with a crumbly, cinnamon-spiced pastry made from ground almonds, flour, butter and egg, filled with raspberry jam. The top is covered with a lattice of the same dough, through which the deep red jam shows. It is served in thin slices with coffee, especially around Christmas.

Recipe

10 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g flour
  • 150 g ground almonds
  • 150 g butter, cold and diced
  • 100 g sugar
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 300 g raspberry jam

Method

  1. Rub the butter into the flour, almonds, sugar and cinnamon, then add the egg and bring together into a dough. Chill 30 minutes.
  2. Press two-thirds of the dough into a 24 cm tart tin and spread with the jam.
  3. Roll the remaining dough, cut into strips and lay a lattice on top.
  4. Bake at 180 °C for 35–40 minutes until golden. Cool completely before slicing.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut 10–12 thin slices from one tart; a small slice satisfies thanks to the rich almond pastry.
  • Choose a jam with less added sugar or more fruit to trim the sweetness.
  • Enjoy it with black coffee or tea rather than with whipped cream.

History

Linzer torte is named after the city of Linz in Upper Austria, and a recipe for it in a manuscript from the 17th century is often cited as one of the oldest known cake recipes. It remains a Christmas classic in Austria, Germany and Switzerland.
