Areeka is a dense, sweet dish of freshly baked flatbread torn into pieces and kneaded by hand with soft dates and ghee until it becomes a smooth, warm dough. It is shaped into a mound with a well in the centre, which is filled with more ghee and honey. Everyone eats from the shared plate, pinching off pieces and dipping them into the honey.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g whole-wheat flour
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 200 ml warm water
  • 150 g soft pitted dates
  • 60 g ghee
  • 4 tbsp honey

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt and water into a soft dough and rest it for 10 minutes. Divide into 3–4 pieces and roll into thin rounds.
  2. Cook each round in a dry, hot pan for 2–3 minutes per side until cooked through and lightly charred.
  3. While still hot, tear the bread into small pieces in a large bowl. Add the dates and half the ghee and knead firmly with your hands (or a wooden pestle) until it forms a smooth, uniform dough.
  4. Shape it into a mound on a serving plate and press a well in the centre.
  5. Fill the well with the remaining ghee, warmed, and the honey. Serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • A 200 g portion is about 520 kcal — it is very filling, so a smaller share of 100–120 g goes a long way.
  • Halve the ghee in the well: 1 tbsp of ghee is about 120 kcal and the honey still carries the flavour.
  • Let the dates do the sweetening and drizzle 1 tbsp of honey instead of filling the well (1 tbsp honey ≈ 60 kcal).

History

Areeka is a traditional dish of the south of Saudi Arabia, especially the Asir region, and is also made in neighbouring Yemen. It is commonly served at breakfast, to guests and on cold days, as well as on special occasions.
