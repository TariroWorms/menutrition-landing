Samsa (Kyrgyz baked meat pastry)

Origin: Kyrgyzstan

Kyrgyz samsa is a hand-sized pastry, usually triangular, with a filling of finely diced lamb or beef, plenty of onion and a little tail fat. Traditionally it is baked on the inner wall of a clay tandoor, which gives a blistered, crisp crust. Inside, the meat stays juicy and peppery. It is sold everywhere from bazaars to roadside stalls and eaten hot with tea.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml warm water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 400 g lamb, finely diced
  • 3 onions, finely chopped
  • 60 g lamb tail fat, diced
  • 1 tsp cumin, black pepper
  • 1 egg yolk, sesame seeds

Method

  1. Make a smooth dough from flour, water and salt and rest it 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the meat, onion, fat, cumin, salt and pepper.
  3. Divide the dough, roll each piece into a thin circle and add a spoon of filling.
  4. Fold into triangles and pinch the edges firmly.
  5. Brush with yolk, sprinkle sesame and bake at 220 °C for 25-30 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Replace tail fat with extra onion to reduce fat.
  • Chicken breast makes a leaner filling.
  • Eat one samsa with a big salad instead of two.

History

Samsa is a relative of the samosa and sambusa found from India to the Middle East, and it has long been a staple across Central Asia. In Kyrgyzstan the tandoor-baked version is the most prized. Each region has its preferred dough, from plain to flaky.
