Uchepos (fresh corn tamales)

Origin: Mexico · Michoacán
262kcal

Uchepos are made not from dried masa but from tender fresh corn kernels ground with milk, a little sugar and butter. The batter is wrapped in fresh green corn husks and steamed until it sets into a soft, moist, pudding-like tamale. They are eaten warm with cream, crumbled cheese or a mild tomato salsa, for breakfast or supper.

What it is made of

cornmilksugarbuttercorn husk

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 8 ears fresh young corn
  • 150 ml milk
  • 50 g sugar
  • 60 g butter, melted
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • Pinch of salt
  • Fresh corn husks

Method

  1. Cut the kernels off the cobs and keep the green husks.
  2. Blend the kernels with milk, sugar, butter, baking powder and salt to a thick batter.
  3. Spoon 2–3 tablespoons onto each husk and fold it closed.
  4. Stand the uchepos upright in a steamer.
  5. Steam for about 1 hour until firm, then rest 10 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Reduce the sugar; young corn is naturally sweet.
  • Use low-fat milk and half the butter.
  • Serve with salsa and fresh cheese rather than cream.

History

Uchepos are a speciality of Michoacán and are tied to the Purépecha cooking of the region. They are traditionally made during the season of young corn, when the ears are milky and sweet.
