Guobaorou (crispy sweet-sour pork)

Origin: China · Dongbei
562kcal

Guobaorou is made from thin slices of pork coated in a starch batter and double-fried until very crisp. They are tossed in a glossy sauce of vinegar and sugar with ginger, scallion and carrot threads. The contrast between the crunchy coating and tangy sauce is the point of the dish.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g pork loin or tenderloin
  • 100 g potato starch
  • 200 ml vegetable oil for frying
  • 3 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 1 carrot, 1 scallion and ginger, cut into thin strips

Method

  1. Slice the pork thinly and marinate with salt and a splash of water for 10 minutes.
  2. Mix the starch with water to make a thick batter and coat the pork.
  3. Fry at 170 C for 3 minutes, remove, then fry again for 1 minute until crisp.
  4. Simmer vinegar, sugar and a spoon of starch slurry until glossy.
  5. Toss the pork in the sauce with the vegetable strips and serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the coated slices to cut the oil.
  • Use less sugar in the sauce.
  • Serve with extra vegetables and steamed rice.

History

The dish comes from the northeast of China and is often linked with Harbin. It is widely believed to have been adapted for foreign visitors in the early twentieth century, and it remains very popular in Dongbei restaurants.
