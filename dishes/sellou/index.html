🥄 Sellou (sfouf)

Origin: Morocco

Sellou is a rich Moroccan sweet made from toasted flour, toasted sesame seeds and ground almonds, bound with honey and scented with anise and cinnamon. It is not baked into a cake but served as a dense, crumbly powder, often shaped into a mound and decorated with whole almonds. It is eaten by the spoonful with mint tea, especially during Ramadan.

Recipe

12 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g plain flour
  • 150 g sesame seeds
  • 200 g almonds
  • 100 g honey
  • 2 tsp ground anise
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 60 ml vegetable oil
  • A pinch of salt

Method

  1. Toast the flour in a dry pan or in the oven at 170 °C, stirring often, until light brown and nutty, about 20 minutes.
  2. Toast the sesame seeds and almonds separately until golden, then cool.
  3. Grind the almonds and most of the sesame to a fine powder.
  4. Mix the flour, ground nuts and seeds, anise, cinnamon and salt.
  5. Warm the honey with the oil and stir it in until the mixture is crumbly and holds together when pressed; mound it and decorate with almonds.

How to make it lighter

  • Sellou is very energy-dense: two level tablespoons are a typical serving.
  • Spoon it into a small bowl rather than eating from the shared mound.
  • Pair it with unsweetened mint tea so the honey is the only sweetness.

History

Sellou, also called sfouf or zmita, is a traditional Moroccan preparation closely tied to Ramadan, when it is made in large batches to keep for weeks. It is also offered after childbirth and at family celebrations in Morocco.
