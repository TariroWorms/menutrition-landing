🍮 Salzburger Nockerl

Origin: Austria · Salzburg

Salzburger Nockerl is a towering sweet soufflé from Salzburg, made of whipped egg whites folded with yolks, sugar, a little flour, vanilla and lemon zest. Baked in a buttered dish, it rises into three golden peaks with a soft, airy, custardy centre. It is dusted with sugar and served straight from the oven, before it sinks.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 5 eggs, separated
  • 60 g sugar
  • 25 g flour
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Grated zest of 1 lemon
  • 15 g butter for the dish
  • Sugar for dusting

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200 °C and butter a shallow oval baking dish.
  2. Whisk the egg whites to soft peaks, then gradually add the sugar and whisk until stiff and glossy.
  3. Gently fold in the yolks, vanilla and lemon zest, then sift in the flour and fold just until combined.
  4. Spoon the mixture into the dish in three large mounds.
  5. Bake for 10–12 minutes until golden outside but still soft inside, dust with sugar and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Share one dish between four — it is airy but sweet.
  • Cut the dusting sugar and grease the dish with a thin film of butter.
  • Serve with fresh berries instead of extra sauces.

History

The dessert is a symbol of Salzburg, and its three peaks are said to represent the hills around the city. Its creation is often credited to Salome Alt, mistress of the 17th-century Prince-Archbishop Wolf Dietrich, but this story is not documented.
