Wet thar hin (Burmese pork curry)

Origin: Myanmar

Wet thar hin is the everyday pork curry of Myanmar. Chunks of shoulder cook slowly with onion, tomato, garlic and ginger until the oil separates and the meat is soft. It is mild rather than fiery, and is served with plain rice, a bowl of broth and fresh or pickled vegetables.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork shoulder, cubed
  • 2 onions (250 g), blended
  • 2 tomatoes (200 g)
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 20 g ginger
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 50 ml oil
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce

Method

  1. Blend onion, garlic and ginger into a paste.
  2. Fry the paste in oil until golden and the water has cooked off.
  3. Add turmeric and chopped tomato and cook until soft.
  4. Add the pork and coat it well, then pour in a little water.
  5. Cover and simmer on low heat for 40–45 minutes until tender and the oil rises.
  6. Season with fish sauce and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim off part of the oil at the end to cut fat.
  • Use a leaner cut such as loin with a splash more water.
  • Serve with plenty of steamed greens and a smaller portion of rice.

History

Oil-rich, slow-cooked curries are the core of home cooking in Myanmar, where the oil rising to the top is a sign of a well-made dish. Pork curry is common in the lowlands and among non-Muslim communities.
