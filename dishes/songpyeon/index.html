Songpyeon (half-moon rice cakes steamed on pine needles)

Origin: South Korea
220kcal

Songpyeon are small half-moon dumplings of rice dough filled with a sweet mixture of ground sesame and honey, red beans or chestnut. They are steamed over pine needles, which leave a faint resinous scent and keep the cakes from sticking. The texture is soft and chewy, the taste gently sweet and nutty.

What it is made of

rice floursesame seedsred beanhoney

Recipe

10 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g rice flour
  • 200 ml hot water
  • 100 g ground sesame seeds
  • 3 tbsp honey (60 g)
  • 100 g red bean paste
  • pine needles, 1 tsp sesame oil

Method

  1. Knead the rice flour with hot water into a smooth soft dough.
  2. Mix sesame seeds with honey for one filling; use bean paste for another.
  3. Shape the dough into balls, fill them and pinch into half-moons.
  4. Steam over a bed of pine needles for 20 minutes.
  5. Rinse with cold water and brush with sesame oil.

How to make it lighter

  • Eat one or two pieces as they are calorie dense.
  • Use less honey in the filling.
  • Try savory or vegetable fillings for variety.

History

Songpyeon are the traditional food of Chuseok, the autumn harvest festival, when families gather to shape them together. The name relates to the pine needles used for steaming.
