Tuo zaafi (northern Ghanaian corn porridge)

Origin: Ghana · North

Tuo zaafi, or TZ, is a stiff, smooth porridge of cornmeal, sometimes mixed with millet, shaped into a ball and served with a green soup. The soup of ayoyo (jute) leaves is slippery, earthy and tangy, enriched with dried fish and locust beans. The porridge is mild and filling, and is pinched off by hand and dipped in the soup. It is the staple meal of northern Ghana.

What it is made of

cornmealayoyo (jute) leaves, or spinachdried fish powderfermented locust beansred palm oil

Recipe

4 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g fermented corn dough
  • 300 g ayoyo (jute) leaves, or spinach
  • 50 g dried fish powder
  • 15 g fermented locust beans
  • 30 g red palm oil

Method

  1. Boil water and whisk in a little cornmeal to make a thin base.
  2. Add the rest of the cornmeal gradually, stirring hard until stiff and smooth.
  3. Chop ayoyo leaves or spinach and simmer with water, locust beans and dried fish for 20 minutes.
  4. Season the soup and add palm oil.
  5. Shape the porridge into balls and serve with the soup.

How to make it lighter

  • Use more leaves and less oil in the soup.
  • Choose wholegrain cornmeal for more fibre.
  • Keep the porridge portion fist-sized.

History

Tuo zaafi comes from the savanna north of Ghana, where cereals such as maize, millet and sorghum are the staple foods. The name is often translated as thick porridge, and it is eaten daily at home and in local eateries.
