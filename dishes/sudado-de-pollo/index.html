Sudado de pollo (Colombian chicken stew)

Origin: Colombia

Sudado de pollo, meaning "sweated chicken", is a home-style stew in which chicken pieces cook slowly in a covered pot with tomato, onion, garlic and cumin, releasing their own juices into a light, aromatic sauce. Potatoes, and sometimes yuca, are added to soak up the flavor. It is served with white rice, avocado and a sprinkling of cilantro.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces, skinless
  • 600 g potatoes, halved
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion and 3 spring onions, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp achiote
  • Cilantro, salt, pepper and 150 ml water

Method

  1. Season the chicken with salt, pepper, cumin and garlic.
  2. In a heavy pot, soften the onions and tomatoes with the achiote to make a hogao.
  3. Add the chicken, stir to coat and cover tightly; cook on low for 20 minutes.
  4. Add the potatoes and 150 ml water, cover again and cook 25 minutes.
  5. Check the seasoning and stir in chopped cilantro.
  6. Serve with rice and avocado.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless pieces and no added oil; the hogao can be cooked in a splash of water.
  • Swap half the potatoes for carrots or chayote.
  • Serve a small portion of rice and more of the broth and vegetables.

History

Sudados of chicken, beef or fish are part of everyday cooking all over Colombia. The technique of cooking covered with little added liquid is typical of Colombian home kitchens.
