Frita cubana (Cuban-style burger)

Origin: Cuba

The frita is a Cuban street burger made from a mix of ground beef and pork seasoned with paprika, garlic and cumin. It is griddled and served on a soft bun with a tangy ketchup-based sauce, chopped onion and a handful of crisp shoestring potatoes. The result is juicy, slightly smoky and crunchy at the same time.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g ground beef
  • 200 g ground pork
  • 1 tbsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 egg
  • 4 soft buns
  • 4 tbsp ketchup
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 100 g shoestring potatoes

Method

  1. Mix the two meats with paprika, cumin, garlic, salt and the egg.
  2. Shape into four thin patties.
  3. Sear on a hot griddle for 3-4 minutes per side until cooked through.
  4. Warm the buns and spread with ketchup mixed with a little paprika.
  5. Place the patties on the buns and top with chopped onion.
  6. Finish with a generous handful of shoestring potatoes and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean ground beef or turkey to reduce the fat.
  • Swap the bun for a whole-grain one.
  • Add lettuce and tomato to make the plate lighter.

History

Fritas became popular in Havana in the first half of the 20th century and were carried to Miami by Cuban emigrants, where they are still sold at small walk-up restaurants. They are loosely related to the American hamburger but have a distinct spice profile.
