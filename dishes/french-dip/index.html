🥪 French Dip Sandwich

A French dip is an American hot sandwich of thinly sliced roast beef piled into a crusty French roll, often topped with melted provolone, and served with a small bowl of au jus — the savoury pan juices from roasting the beef — for dipping. Each bite is dunked so the bread soaks up the warm, beefy liquid. It is usually served with fries or a pickle.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef roasting joint (topside or sirloin)
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 500 ml beef broth
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 4 French rolls
  • 8 slices provolone

Method

  1. Season the beef with salt, pepper and thyme and roast on the sliced onion and garlic at 200 °C for 60–75 minutes for medium.
  2. Rest the beef for 15 minutes, then slice it very thinly.
  3. Pour the broth into the roasting tin, scrape up the browned bits and simmer for 5 minutes; strain to make the au jus.
  4. Split the rolls, fill with beef, top with provolone and grill for 2 minutes until melted.
  5. Serve each sandwich with a small bowl of hot au jus for dipping.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose a lean cut like topside and trim visible fat before slicing.
  • Skim the fat off the au jus — it adds flavour without the extra fat.
  • Swap the fries for a side salad or raw vegetables to keep the meal lighter.

History

Two Los Angeles restaurants, Philippe's and Cole's, both claim to have invented the French dip in 1908. The name is usually explained by the French roll it is served on rather than any link to France.
