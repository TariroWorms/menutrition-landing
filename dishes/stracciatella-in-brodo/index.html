Stracciatella in brodo is a light Italian soup made by whisking eggs, grated parmesan, a little semolina and nutmeg into simmering broth. The egg sets into soft, delicate shreds that float in the clear, savory liquid. It is a simple, comforting first course, often served at Easter in Rome.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 l broth
  • 3 eggs
  • 40 g parmesan, finely grated
  • 2 tbsp fine semolina
  • A pinch of nutmeg
  • 1 tbsp chopped parsley
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Bring the broth to a gentle simmer.
  2. Beat the eggs with parmesan, semolina, nutmeg, salt and pepper.
  3. Pour the mixture into the broth in a thin stream, stirring with a fork.
  4. Simmer for 2–3 minutes until the egg forms soft shreds.
  5. Serve hot, sprinkled with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a fat-skimmed broth.
  • Use 20 g of parmesan instead of 40 g.
  • Serve as a starter to take the edge off hunger before the main course.

History

The soup is traditional in Rome and central Italy; its name comes from straccetti, 'little rags', describing the shreds of cooked egg.
