🥣 Jareesh with chicken

Origin: Saudi Arabia · Arabian Peninsula

Jareesh is a comforting Saudi porridge of coarsely crushed wheat simmered slowly with chicken until thick and creamy, then enriched with yogurt. It is topped with onions fried in butter, which add sweetness and a toasty aroma. The texture is soft but slightly grainy, and it is served hot in a deep dish, often during Ramadan and on cold winter evenings.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g jareesh (crushed wheat), rinsed and soaked for 1 hour
  • 500 g chicken pieces
  • 1.5 litres water
  • 250 g plain yogurt
  • 1 onion, finely sliced
  • 20 g butter
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in the water with salt for 30 minutes, then lift it out, shred it and keep the broth.
  2. Add the drained jareesh to the broth and cook on low heat for about 45 minutes, stirring often, until thick and soft.
  3. Stir in the shredded chicken and the yogurt and cook for 10 minutes more, adding hot water if it gets too thick. Season with cumin and pepper.
  4. Fry the onion in the butter until deep golden.
  5. Spoon the jareesh into a dish and top with the onions and butter.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken and skim the fat from the broth before adding the wheat.
  • Use only a small knob of butter for the onions, or fry them in a non-stick pan.
  • A medium bowl is filling; serve it with a fresh salad instead of bread.

History

Jareesh is a long-standing traditional dish of central Arabia and is especially associated with the Najd region. It is also cooked across the Gulf, in Kuwait, Qatar and the UAE.
