Ttu lavash (sour fruit leather)

Origin: Armenia

Ttu lavash means “sour lavash”: a fruit purée, usually of sour plums or apricots, spread very thin and dried until it becomes a flexible, glossy sheet. It is tart, fruity and chewy, and is torn into strips and eaten as a snack. It is also soaked or melted into stews and sauces to add sourness. Rolled sheets keep for months.

What it is made of

plumsapricots

Recipe

10 servings480 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg ripe plums or apricots, pitted
  • 2–3 tbsp water
  • 1 tbsp sugar (optional)
  • a little oil for the tray

Method

  1. Simmer the fruit with the water for 15 minutes until very soft.
  2. Purée until smooth and sieve out the skins; add sugar if the fruit is very sour.
  3. Spread 2–3 mm thick on an oiled, paper-lined tray.
  4. Dry in the oven at 60–70 °C with the door slightly open for 6–8 hours, until no longer sticky.
  5. Peel off, roll up in paper and store in a dry place.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the sugar — the natural tartness is the point.
  • A 30 g strip is a fat-free, portable snack.
  • Dissolve a piece in stews instead of adding lemon or vinegar.

History

Drying fruit purée in the sun is an old way of preserving the summer harvest in Armenia and the wider Caucasus. Rolls of ttu lavash are a common sight at Yerevan's markets.
