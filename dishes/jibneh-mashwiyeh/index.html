🧀 Grilled cheese (jibneh mashwiyeh)

Origin: Levant · Middle East

Jibneh mashwiyeh is a simple Levantine mezze of thick slices of halloumi or akkawi cheese seared on a grill or hot pan. The outside chars and blisters while the inside turns soft and slightly squeaky. It is finished with a drizzle of olive oil and dried or fresh mint and served hot with tomato slices.

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g halloumi
  • 150 g akkawi cheese, soaked in water for 1 hour to remove salt
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried mint or a few fresh leaves
  • 2 tomatoes, sliced

Method

  1. Pat the cheeses dry and cut into 1 cm thick slices.
  2. Heat a griddle or non-stick pan over medium-high heat and brush it lightly with oil.
  3. Sear the slices 1–2 minutes per side until golden and blistered.
  4. Arrange on a plate with the tomato slices, drizzle with the remaining oil and scatter mint. Serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Grilled cheese is rich: 2–3 slices with plenty of tomato make a balanced mezze portion.
  • Use a non-stick pan and just a brush of oil; each tablespoon of olive oil is about 120 kcal.
  • Pair it with salad and vegetables rather than several other fried mezze.

History

Grilling firm brined cheeses is an old habit across the eastern Mediterranean, where halloumi and akkawi hold their shape over heat instead of melting. Today grilled cheese is a standard item on mezze tables in Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Palestine.
