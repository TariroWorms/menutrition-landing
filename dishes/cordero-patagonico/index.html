Cordero patagónico al asador (spit-roasted lamb)

Origin: Argentina · Patagonia

Cordero al asador is a whole lamb opened flat and fixed to an iron cross, which is planted at an angle next to a wood fire. The lamb cooks for hours from the side heat rather than over embers, basted only with salted water. The meat is smoky and tender, and the skin turns crisp and golden. It is carved straight from the cross and eaten with chimichurri and bread.

What it is made of

lambcoarse saltgarlicchimichurri

Recipe

12 servings300 min

Ingredients

  • 1 whole lamb, 10–12 kg, opened flat
  • 1 l warm water
  • 4 tbsp coarse salt
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • Chimichurri to serve

Method

  1. Fix the opened lamb to the iron cross with wire, bone side facing the fire.
  2. Light a large wood fire and plant the cross at an angle about 1 m from it.
  3. Dissolve the salt with the garlic in the water to make a brine (salmuera).
  4. Roast bone side to the fire for about 3 hours, basting with brine and feeding the fire.
  5. Turn the cross and roast the skin side 1–1.5 hours until crisp, then carve and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose leg and loin pieces, which are leaner than ribs and belly.
  • Remove crisp skin if you are watching fat intake.
  • Serve with a large mixed salad and grilled vegetables.

History

Sheep farming has shaped Patagonia since the late 19th century, and lamb is the region's signature meat. Lamb roasted on the cross is a centrepiece of rural festivals and of tourist estancias in the south.
