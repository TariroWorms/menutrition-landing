🐟 Sayadieh

Sayadieh is a Lebanese fisherman's dish of white fish served over rice cooked in a dark broth of deeply caramelised onions, cumin and cinnamon. The rice turns brown and fragrant from the onions, and the plate is finished with toasted pine nuts and a tangy tahini sauce. It is usually served as a generous main course with more tahini sauce on the side.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g white fish fillets (sea bass or cod)
  • 300 g long-grain rice, rinsed
  • 3 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 4 tbsp vegetable oil, salt
  • 30 g pine nuts
  • 100 g tahini sauce (tahini, lemon juice, water, garlic)

Method

  1. Fry the onions in 3 tbsp oil over medium heat for 25–30 minutes until very dark brown but not burnt.
  2. Add 800 ml water, the cumin, cinnamon and salt and simmer for 10 minutes, then strain, keeping the onion broth and some onions for garnish.
  3. Cook the rice in 600 ml of the onion broth, covered, on low heat for 18–20 minutes.
  4. Season the fish with salt and cumin and pan-fry or bake it until it flakes easily.
  5. Toast the pine nuts in a dry pan until golden.
  6. Serve the fish over the rice, topped with the reserved onions, pine nuts and a drizzle of tahini sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the fish instead of frying it to save the frying oil — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Use a teaspoon of pine nuts per plate as a garnish rather than a handful.
  • Thin the tahini sauce with extra lemon juice and water so a smaller amount covers the plate.

History

Sayadieh (also spelled sayadiyah) takes its name from sayyad, Arabic for “fisherman”. It is a dish of the Levantine coast, especially Lebanon and the coastal cities of Syria and Palestine, and today it is a fixture of Lebanese seafood restaurants.
