🍍 Sweet and Sour Pork

Origin: China · Guangdong

Sweet and sour pork is a Cantonese dish of bite-sized pork pieces coated in starch, deep-fried until crisp and tossed in a glossy sauce of vinegar and sugar. Chunks of pineapple and bell pepper add juiciness, colour and crunch. The result balances crunchy, tangy and sweet, and it is usually served with steamed rice.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g pork shoulder, cubed
  • 60 g cornstarch
  • oil for frying
  • 200 g pineapple chunks
  • 1 red bell pepper, in pieces
  • 1 green bell pepper, in pieces
  • 60 ml rice vinegar
  • 50 g sugar
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 100 ml water

Method

  1. Season the pork with salt and coat well in cornstarch.
  2. Deep-fry in hot oil in batches until golden and crisp; drain.
  3. Mix vinegar, sugar, tomato paste and water; simmer in a wok until slightly thick, adding 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with water if needed.
  4. Stir-fry the peppers and pineapple for 2 minutes in the sauce.
  5. Add the pork, toss quickly to coat and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Pan-fry or air-fry the pork with a little oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Cut the sugar in the sauce by a third and add more pineapple for sweetness.
  • Use lean pork loin and add extra peppers to bulk up the plate.

History

The dish is generally traced to Guangdong, where it is known as gu lou yuk. It travelled with Cantonese emigrants and became one of the best-known dishes in Chinese restaurants around the world, often in sweeter versions than the original.
