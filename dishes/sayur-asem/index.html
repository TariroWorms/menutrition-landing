Sayur asem (Sundanese sour vegetable soup)

Origin: Indonesia · Sunda

Sayur asem is a clear, refreshing soup whose sourness comes from tamarind and sometimes young tamarind leaves. It holds sweet corn, chayote, long beans and peanuts, with a touch of palm sugar for balance. It is eaten with rice and fried fish or tempeh.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 2 corn cobs (400 g), cut in rounds
  • 1 chayote (250 g), cubed
  • 100 g long beans, in short pieces
  • 50 g raw peanuts
  • 1.5 litres water
  • 3 tbsp tamarind pulp
  • 5 shallots and 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tbsp palm sugar and salt

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil with the shallots, garlic and peanuts and cook 10 minutes.
  2. Add the corn and cook for 10 minutes.
  3. Add the chayote and long beans.
  4. Stir in tamarind, palm sugar and salt.
  5. Simmer 10 minutes until the vegetables are just tender.
  6. Taste for a balance of sour and sweet and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • It is naturally low in fat and calories, so it suits a light dinner.
  • Serve with brown rice and grilled fish for a balanced plate.
  • Skip the added sugar if the corn is sweet enough.

History

The dish is closely tied to Sundanese home cooking in West Java and is also popular in Jakarta. Its name simply means "sour vegetables".
