Kapama (Bulgarian layered meat and sauerkraut)

Origin: Bulgaria · Bansko
490kcal

Kapama is a festive dish of several kinds of meat layered with sauerkraut or cabbage and rice, sealed in a clay pot and baked very slowly for hours. The result is soft, tangy and deeply savoury. It is traditionally served at large winter gatherings.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g pork ribs or shoulder
  • 200 g pork sausage
  • 100 g smoked bacon
  • 500 g sauerkraut
  • 100 g rice
  • 2 onions
  • 1 tbsp paprika
  • 200 ml water
  • bay leaves, peppercorns

Method

  1. Sauté the onions with paprika in a little fat rendered from the bacon and set aside.
  2. Layer sauerkraut, rice, meats and sausage in a clay pot.
  3. Tuck bay leaves and peppercorns between the layers.
  4. Add water and cover tightly with a lid or foil.
  5. Bake at 160 °C for about 3 hours.
  6. Rest for 15 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean cuts and trim visible fat.
  • Rinse the sauerkraut to reduce salt.
  • Use more cabbage and less rice for a lighter dish.

History

The name comes from the way the pot is covered and sealed so all the aromas stay inside. It is especially tied to the mountain town of Bansko and to winter holidays in Bulgaria.
