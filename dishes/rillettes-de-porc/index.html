🥖 Pork Rillettes

Origin: France · Loire Valley (Tours, Le Mans)

Rillettes de porc is a French spread of pork slowly cooked in its own fat with thyme and bay until it falls apart, then shredded and packed into jars under a layer of fat. It is soft, rich and savoury, with fine strands of meat. It is served cool as a spread, often with cornichons, as a starter or snack.

What it is made of

Recipe

10 servings270 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork shoulder, cut into 5 cm cubes
  • 300 g pork fat, diced
  • 4 sprigs thyme
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 tsp salt
  • Black pepper
  • 150 ml water

Method

  1. Put everything in a heavy pot and bring to a gentle simmer.
  2. Cover and cook on the lowest heat for about 4 hours, stirring occasionally, until the meat falls apart.
  3. Remove the herbs, drain the meat and shred it with two forks, adding back some of the fat.
  4. Pack into jars, cover with a thin layer of fat and chill overnight.

How to make it lighter

  • Rillettes are very rich, so a 30–50 g serving goes a long way.
  • Mix in less of the cooking fat when shredding and skip the top layer if eating within a few days.
  • Serve with plenty of raw vegetables or pickles alongside.

History

Rillettes are a speciality of the Loire Valley, with Tours and Le Mans both famous for their versions. Slow-cooking meat in fat was a traditional way to preserve pork after the autumn slaughter, long before refrigeration.
