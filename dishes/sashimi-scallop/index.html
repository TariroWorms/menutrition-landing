🐚 Scallop sashimi

Origin: Japan

Scallop sashimi is a simple Japanese plate of sashimi-grade scallops, served with fine threads of daikon radish and fresh shiso leaves. The raw scallop is sweet, tender and almost creamy, with a delicate taste of the sea. The crisp daikon and the minty, peppery shiso freshen the palate between bites. It is served chilled as a light starter or as part of a larger sashimi selection.

What it is made of

scallopdaikon radishshiso leaves

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g sashimi-grade scallops
  • 150 g daikon radish
  • 6 fresh shiso leaves
  • Wasabi paste (optional)

Method

  1. Pat the scallops dry and slice each one horizontally into two or three discs.
  2. Peel the daikon and cut it into very fine threads; soak in cold water for 5 minutes and drain well.
  3. Pile the daikon on chilled plates, lay a shiso leaf alongside and arrange the slices over it. Serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Scallops are very lean; a few drops of citrus are all they need.
  • Eat plenty of the daikon — it adds crunch and volume for very few calories.
  • Sashimi without rice is a much lighter plate than nigiri or rolls.

History

Scallops are farmed and fished in large quantities around Hokkaido and northern Japan, where they are commonly eaten raw as sashimi.
