🍰 Donauwelle (Danube wave cake)

Origin: Germany

Donauwelle is a German sheet cake of vanilla and chocolate sponge baked together with sour cherries, so the layers form a wavy pattern when sliced. It is topped with a thick layer of vanilla buttercream and a thin chocolate glaze, often marked with a fork. The result is soft, moist and rich, with tart cherry pockets, and it is served in squares with coffee.

Recipe

8 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g butter, softened, plus 150 g for the buttercream
  • 200 g sugar and 4 eggs
  • 350 g wheat flour and 2 tsp baking powder
  • 2 tbsp cocoa powder
  • 350 g pitted sour cherries, drained
  • 300 g ready-made vanilla custard, cooled
  • 150 g dark chocolate for the glaze

Method

  1. Beat 250 g butter with the sugar until pale, then beat in the eggs one at a time. Fold in the flour and baking powder.
  2. Spread half of the batter in a lined tray. Stir the cocoa into the rest and spread it on top.
  3. Press the cherries into the batter and bake at 180 °C for 35–40 minutes. Cool completely.
  4. Beat 150 g butter until fluffy and gradually beat in the room-temperature custard. Spread over the cake and chill for 1 hour.
  5. Melt the chocolate, pour it over the buttercream, draw wavy lines with a fork and chill until set before cutting.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small squares: one 120 g piece of a rich buttercream cake is already a full dessert.
  • A thinner buttercream layer saves a noticeable amount of fat — 10 g of butter is about 72 kcal.
  • Pair a slice with unsweetened coffee or tea rather than a sweet drink.

History

The name means “Danube wave”, after the wavy pattern the chocolate and vanilla sponge forms in cross-section. Today it is a fixture of German home baking and bakery counters.
