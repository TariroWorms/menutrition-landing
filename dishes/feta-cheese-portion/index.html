🧀 Feta cheese

Feta is a white brined cheese, crumbly and pleasantly salty with a tangy, slightly sharp finish. It is sold in blocks kept in brine and is usually eaten in small cubes or slices: on a breakfast plate with olives, tomatoes and cucumber, crumbled over salads, or as part of a mezze spread. A typical portion is a cube of about 30 g.

Recipe

1 servings2 min

Ingredients

  • 30 g feta, cut into a cube or slices
  • a few cherry tomatoes
  • a few cucumber slices
  • a pinch of dried oregano

Method

  1. Take the feta out of the brine and pat it dry.
  2. Cut it into cubes or slices.
  3. Serve with tomatoes and cucumber and sprinkle with oregano.

How to make it lighter

  • Feta is calorie-dense and salty: weigh a 30 g portion rather than cutting by eye.
  • Crumble it over a large salad so a small amount flavours the whole bowl.
  • Rinse the cube briefly in water to remove some surface salt.

History

Brined white cheese has been made in Greece and the eastern Mediterranean for a very long time. Since 2002 the name feta has been a protected designation of origin in the European Union, reserved for cheese from specific Greek regions made from sheep's milk or a mix with goat's milk. Similar white cheeses are eaten across the Balkans, Turkey and the Arab world.
