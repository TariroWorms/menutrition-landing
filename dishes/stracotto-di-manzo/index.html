🍖 Stracotto (Italian beef pot roast)

Origin: Italy · Northern Italy

Stracotto di manzo is a large piece of beef cooked very slowly in red wine with tomato, carrot, celery and onion until it can be cut with a spoon. The long braise turns the vegetables and wine into a thick, deep-flavoured sauce. The meat is sliced and served with the sauce, often with polenta or mashed potatoes.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg beef chuck in one piece
  • 300 ml red wine
  • 400 g chopped tomatoes
  • 1 carrot, 1 celery stalk, 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 sprig rosemary, 2 bay leaves
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Season the beef and brown it on all sides in olive oil.
  2. Add the chopped vegetables and garlic and cook for 10 minutes.
  3. Pour in the wine and let it reduce by half.
  4. Add tomatoes, herbs and a little water, cover and cook on very low heat for 3–3½ hours, turning occasionally.
  5. Slice the meat and serve with the reduced sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim the fat from the beef before cooking and skim the sauce.
  • Serve with vegetables instead of a large portion of polenta.
  • Keep the portion to about 150 g of meat with sauce.

History

Stracotto means 'overcooked' or 'long-cooked' in Italian, and the dish is traditional in northern and central regions such as Emilia-Romagna, Lombardy and Tuscany, where its sauce is also used to dress pasta or fill ravioli.
