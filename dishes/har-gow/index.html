🥟 Har gow (shrimp dumplings)

Origin: China · Guangdong

Har gow are the classic Cantonese dim sum shrimp dumplings: plump, juicy shrimp and crunchy bamboo shoot wrapped in a thin, translucent starch skin pleated into a crescent. Steamed in bamboo baskets, they are chewy, delicate and sweet, with a hint of sesame oil. They are usually served four to a basket with tea.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 50 g bamboo shoots, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • ½ tsp salt, ½ tsp sugar, pinch of white pepper
  • 150 g tapioca starch, plus extra for dusting
  • About 120 ml boiling water and 1 tsp oil for the dough

Method

  1. Chop half the shrimp coarsely and leave the rest in pieces; mix with bamboo shoots, sesame oil, salt, sugar and pepper. Chill.
  2. Pour boiling water onto the tapioca starch, stir, add the oil and knead into a smooth dough while warm; keep covered.
  3. Roll small pieces into very thin 7 cm circles on an oiled surface.
  4. Place a teaspoon of filling in the centre, fold over and pleat one edge to seal into a crescent.
  5. Steam on parchment over boiling water for 6–7 minutes until the skins turn translucent.

How to make it lighter

  • Steamed har gow are among the lighter dim sum choices — favour them over fried items.
  • Dip lightly in vinegar with chili rather than in oily sauces.
  • Count your dumplings: four make a snack, so add steamed greens for a fuller meal.

History

Har gow are generally traced to the Cantonese teahouse tradition of Guangdong, where dim sum grew alongside tea drinking. They are often seen as a test of a dim sum chef's skill and are served in teahouses across southern China, Hong Kong and Chinese communities worldwide.
