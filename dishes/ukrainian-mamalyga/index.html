Mamalyga (firm cornmeal porridge)

Origin: Ukraine · Bukovina

Mamalyga is cornmeal cooked in salted water until it is so thick that it can be turned out onto a board in one piece. Once it sets slightly it is sliced with a thread and served warm instead of bread. It is mild and comforting, and comes alive with salty bryndza, sour cream, melted butter or a meat stew.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g cornmeal
  • 1 l water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 30 g butter
  • 150 g bryndza
  • 150 g sour cream

Method

  1. Bring the salted water to a boil in a heavy pot.
  2. Pour in the cornmeal in a thin stream, whisking to avoid lumps.
  3. Switch to a wooden spoon and cook on low heat 25–30 minutes, stirring often.
  4. When it pulls away from the sides, stir in half the butter.
  5. Turn it out onto a wooden board, let it set 5 minutes and cut with a thread.
  6. Serve with crumbled bryndza, sour cream and the rest of the butter.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the butter and serve with a vegetable stew.
  • Use yogurt instead of sour cream.
  • Grill leftover slices the next day for a crisp side dish.

History

Mamalyga is especially common in Bukovina and other areas of western and south-western Ukraine bordering Moldova and Romania, where the similar mămăligă is a national dish. Maize spread in the region from the seventeenth century onwards.
