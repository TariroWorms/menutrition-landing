🥩 Chapli kebab

Origin: Pakistan · Peshawar

Chapli kebab is a large, flat patty of minced beef mixed with onion, tomato, green chili, crushed coriander seeds and dried pomegranate seeds, bound with egg and shallow-fried until the edges are crisp. It is juicy, coarse and boldly spiced, usually eaten hot with raw onion, sliced tomato and a squeeze of lemon.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g minced beef (not too lean)
  • 1 onion, finely chopped and squeezed dry
  • 1 tomato, sliced
  • 2 green chilies, chopped
  • 1 tbsp coriander seeds, crushed
  • 1 tbsp dried pomegranate seeds (anardana), crushed
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tsp chili flakes, salt
  • Oil for shallow-frying

Method

  1. Mix the beef with the onion, chilies, coriander, pomegranate seeds, chili flakes, salt and egg; knead well.
  2. Shape into 8 thin, wide patties and press a slice of tomato into each.
  3. Shallow-fry in a hot pan for 4–5 minutes per side until crisp at the edges and cooked through.
  4. Drain on paper and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use leaner beef mince and a non-stick pan with 1 tbsp of oil instead of shallow-frying.
  • Grill the patties on a griddle or barbecue to let the fat drip away.
  • Serve with a big onion-tomato salad and keep to one or two patties.

History

Chapli kebab is associated with Peshawar and the Pashtun regions of north-west Pakistan and Afghanistan; the name is often said to come from a Pashto word meaning flat. It is a famous street food in Peshawar.
