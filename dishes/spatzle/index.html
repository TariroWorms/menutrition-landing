🍝 Käsespätzle

Origin: Germany · Swabia

Spätzle are small, soft egg dumplings made from a loose batter of flour, eggs and milk pressed into boiling water. In this version they are tossed in butter and layered with melted cheese, giving a chewy, rich and savoury dish. They are served as a main course or as a side for meat and gravy.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 4 eggs
  • 150 ml milk
  • 30 g butter
  • 150 g grated hard cheese
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • Salt, pepper, chives

Method

  1. Beat flour, eggs, milk and a pinch of salt into a thick, sticky batter; rest 10 minutes.
  2. Press or scrape the batter into simmering salted water; lift out the spätzle once they float.
  3. Fry the onion in half the butter until golden and set aside.
  4. Toss the spätzle in the rest of the butter, layer with the cheese and heat until melted.
  5. Top with the onions, pepper and chives.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the cheese and a sharper variety for the same flavour — cheese is about 400 kcal per 100 g.
  • Serve a smaller portion alongside a big green salad.
  • Skip frying the onions in butter and roast them dry in the pan instead.

History

Spätzle are strongly associated with Swabia in south-west Germany and with neighbouring Alpine regions of Austria and Switzerland. The cheese version, Käsespätzle, is a classic of Alpine inns and home kitchens.
