Satay ayam is the Malay name for chicken satay: small cubes of chicken marinated in turmeric, lemongrass and coconut milk, grilled on skewers over charcoal. In Malaysia it is served with a chunky peanut sauce and ketupat, compressed rice cakes cut into cubes, which soak up the sauce and turn the snack into a meal.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g boneless chicken thighs, cut into 2–3 cm cubes
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 lemongrass stalk, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 100 ml coconut milk
  • 1 tsp sugar, salt
  • 150 g ready peanut sauce
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil for brushing
  • 300 g ketupat (compressed rice cakes), cubed

Method

  1. Mix the turmeric, lemongrass, garlic, sugar, salt and half the coconut milk, toss with the meat and marinate for at least 1 hour.
  2. Soak bamboo skewers in water for 20 minutes, then thread on the meat.
  3. Grill over hot charcoal or under a hot grill for 8–10 minutes, turning and brushing with oil, until charred at the edges and cooked through.
  4. Warm the peanut sauce with the remaining coconut milk until smooth.
  5. Serve the skewers hot with the peanut sauce and the ketupat cubes.

How to make it lighter

  • The peanut sauce is the richest part: spoon a small portion for dipping rather than pouring it over the skewers.
  • Use chicken breast or trim visible fat from the meat for a leaner skewer.
  • Pair the satay with sliced cucumber and onion instead of extra rice to keep the plate lighter.

History

Satay is most closely associated with Java in Indonesia, and it is often suggested that it developed in the 19th century under the influence of kebabs brought by Indian Muslim and Arab traders. It is now a beloved street food across Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. In Malaysia, the town of Kajang in Selangor is especially famous for its satay.
