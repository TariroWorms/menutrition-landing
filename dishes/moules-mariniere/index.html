🦪 Moules marinières

Moules marinières are mussels steamed in white wine with shallot, garlic and parsley, finished with a splash of cream that turns the cooking juices into a silky, briny sauce. The mussels are plump and tender. They are served piping hot in a deep bowl with the sauce poured over and a spoon for the broth.

What it is made of

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg fresh mussels
  • 150 ml dry white wine
  • 2 shallots, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 100 ml cream
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • A handful of chopped parsley
  • Black pepper

Method

  1. Scrub and debeard the mussels, discarding any that are cracked or stay open when tapped.
  2. Soften the shallot and garlic in the oil in a large pot for 2–3 minutes.
  3. Add the wine and bring to the boil.
  4. Add the mussels, cover and steam for 4–6 minutes, shaking the pot, until they open; discard any that stay closed.
  5. Stir in the cream and parsley, season with pepper, warm through and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the cream or leave it out for a lighter broth.
  • Mussels are high in protein and low in fat, so a big bowl is a filling, moderate meal.
  • Enjoy the broth with a spoon rather than soaking up large amounts with extra sides.

History

The name means "sailor-style mussels", and the dish is associated with the French Atlantic and Channel coasts, where mussels have long been farmed. Today it is a standard bistro dish across France and Belgium; the cream is a common addition, especially in Normandy.
