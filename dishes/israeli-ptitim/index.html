Ptitim (Israeli couscous)

Origin: Israel

Ptitim are small toasted pearls of wheat pasta, cooked in broth the way rice is cooked. They turn out chewy, nutty and tender, and absorb the flavour of onion and stock. They are served as a side dish or tossed with vegetables and herbs.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g ptitim
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp olive oil (30 ml)
  • 450 ml hot vegetable or chicken broth
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste, salt, pepper
  • parsley to finish

Method

  1. Fry the onion in oil until soft.
  2. Add the ptitim and toast for 2 minutes.
  3. Stir in tomato paste, pour in the hot broth and season.
  4. Cover and simmer for 8 to 10 minutes until the liquid is absorbed.
  5. Rest 5 minutes, fluff with a fork and sprinkle with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Use wholemeal ptitim for more fibre.
  • Add peas, carrots or zucchini.
  • Measure the oil rather than pouring it.

History

Ptitim was created in the early 1950s, when rice was scarce in Israel, as a wheat-based alternative. It quickly became a favourite childhood food and is now sold abroad as Israeli couscous.
