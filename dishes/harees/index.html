🥣 Harees

Harees is a classic Gulf dish of whole wheat and lamb simmered for hours and then beaten until they merge into a smooth, thick, slightly elastic porridge. It is seasoned simply with salt and cinnamon and finished with a pool of melted butter. Comforting and mild, it is eaten with a spoon from a shared bowl, above all at Ramadan iftar and during Eid.

Recipe

6 servings300 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g whole wheat (hulled wheat berries), soaked overnight
  • 600 g lamb on the bone (shoulder or neck)
  • 1 cinnamon stick, plus ground cinnamon to serve
  • About 2.5 litres water
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • 30 g butter, melted, to serve

Method

  1. Drain the wheat and put it in a large pot with the lamb, cinnamon stick and water.
  2. Bring to the boil, skim, then cover and simmer on very low heat for 4 hours, stirring now and then and adding water if needed.
  3. Remove the bones and any fat, shred the meat and return it to the pot with the salt.
  4. Beat vigorously with a wooden spoon or blend briefly until smooth and stretchy, cooking 20–30 minutes more.
  5. Serve in bowls with a little melted butter and a pinch of ground cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Use only a teaspoon of butter per bowl, or skip it.
  • Choose lean lamb and skim the fat off the pot as it simmers.
  • Harees is filling; one small bowl with a salad is enough for iftar.

History

Harees is among the oldest recorded dishes of the Arab world: a dish of pounded wheat and meat appears in medieval Arabic cookbooks. It remains a Ramadan and Eid essential in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar and across the Gulf, and the related haleem is eaten in South Asia.
