Beiju (cassava flatbread)

Origin: Brazil · North

Beiju is a flatbread made from moistened cassava starch or grated cassava, sprinkled on a hot griddle where it binds into a disc without any flour or water dough. It comes out chewy in the middle and crisp at the edges. It is folded around butter, cheese, grated coconut or condensed milk and is eaten for breakfast across northern and northeastern Brazil.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g hydrated cassava starch (goma)
  • 40 g grated coconut
  • Pinch of salt
  • 20 g butter to serve

Method

  1. Rub the hydrated starch through a sieve to get a fine, damp powder.
  2. Mix in the salt and grated coconut.
  3. Heat a non-stick pan or griddle without oil over medium heat.
  4. Sprinkle an even layer of starch to cover the base.
  5. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes until the disc holds together, flip and cook briefly.
  6. Spread with butter, fold in half and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Fill with eggs or fresh cheese for a more balanced meal.
  • Skip the butter and use a little coconut instead.
  • It is gluten-free, so it suits a coeliac diet.

History

Beiju is one of the oldest foods in Brazil, prepared by Indigenous peoples from cassava long before Portuguese arrival. Today the same technique gives the tapioca crepe sold on street corners, while larger, thicker beijus are still made in Amazonian communities.
