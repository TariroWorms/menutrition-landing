Tandyr samsa

Origin: Uzbekistan

Tandyr samsa are crisp, layered pastries slapped onto the hot clay walls of a tandoor oven. Inside is chopped lamb with plenty of onion, cumin and black pepper, which turns juicy as it bakes. The dough is laminated with butter or fat, so it shatters into flakes. They are sold hot from bakeries and street stalls and eaten with tea.

Recipe

8 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour, 250 ml water, salt
  • 100 g softened butter
  • 500 g lamb, finely chopped
  • 400 g onions, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp cumin, black pepper, salt
  • 1 egg yolk, sesame seeds

Method

  1. Make a firm dough, rest 30 minutes, roll very thin, spread with butter and roll into a tight log; chill 1 hour.
  2. Mix lamb, onions, cumin, pepper and salt.
  3. Cut the log into discs, roll each flat and fill; fold into triangles.
  4. Brush with yolk and sprinkle with sesame.
  5. Bake at 220 °C for 25-30 minutes (or in a tandoor) until deep golden.

How to make it lighter

  • One samsa is a snack; pair it with a salad for a meal.
  • Use lean lamb and a thinner layer of butter.
  • Bake small samsa to control portions.

History

Samsa belongs to a large family of baked filled pastries found from Central Asia to South Asia. In Uzbekistan the tandoor-baked version is a classic street food, and many towns are proud of their own style.
