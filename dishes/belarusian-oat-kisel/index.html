Oat kisel (aŭsiany kisiel)

Origin: Belarus

Oat kisel is an old Belarusian dish made by fermenting oats in water, straining the liquid and boiling it until it thickens. It sets into a soft, pale jelly with a gentle sour taste. It is eaten warm or cold, either sweet with honey or savory with sunflower oil and fried onion.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g rolled oats
  • 1.5 l water
  • 1 slice rye bread (as starter)
  • salt
  • honey or sunflower oil for serving

Method

  1. Cover the oats with lukewarm water and add the rye bread.
  2. Leave in a warm place for 1-2 days until it smells pleasantly sour.
  3. Strain, pressing the oats well; discard the solids.
  4. Bring the liquid to a boil with a pinch of salt, stirring constantly, until thick.
  5. Pour into bowls and let set.
  6. Serve with honey or with oil and fried onion.

How to make it lighter

  • The savory version with a little oil is lighter than the honey one.
  • Use certified gluten-free oats and skip the bread if needed.
  • Top with berries for extra vitamins.

History

Oat kisel is one of the oldest dishes of the Eastern Slavs and was traditionally part of the Christmas Eve table in Belarus. It was also a common fasting food.
