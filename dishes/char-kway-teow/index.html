🍜 Char kway teow

Char kway teow is a plate of flat rice noodles stir-fried over a fierce flame with prawns, egg, bean sprouts, chilli and soy sauce. The high heat gives the noodles a smoky, slightly charred taste known as wok hei, while the sauce makes them glossy and savoury-sweet. It is cooked to order one plate at a time and eaten hot, often with extra chilli on the side.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g fresh flat rice noodles
  • 200 g raw prawns, peeled
  • 2 eggs
  • 150 g bean sprouts
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1–2 fresh red chillies, chopped
  • 2 tbsp light soy sauce and 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • A handful of garlic chives, cut into 4 cm lengths

Method

  1. Separate the noodle strands gently; mix the soy sauces with the sugar.
  2. Heat a wok until smoking, add the oil, garlic and chilli and stir for 20 seconds.
  3. Add the prawns and cook for 1–2 minutes until pink.
  4. Add the noodles and the sauce and toss over high heat for 2 minutes, letting them catch slightly.
  5. Push the noodles aside, crack in the eggs, scramble briefly, then mix through.
  6. Add the bean sprouts and chives, toss for 30 seconds and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Double the bean sprouts and chives and cut the noodles back by a quarter for more volume with fewer calories.
  • Use a non-stick wok and measure the oil: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Add extra prawns rather than more noodles to make the plate more filling.

History

The dish is usually linked to Teochew and Hokkien Chinese communities in Malaya and Singapore; 'kway teow' is the Hokkien name for flat rice noodles. It is often said to have started as cheap, filling street food for workers. Penang hawker stalls are now the best-known place to eat it, and Singapore has its own sweeter version.
