Karkadeh (hibiscus tea)

Origin: Egypt · Nile Valley

Karkadeh is a deep ruby drink made by steeping dried hibiscus flowers in water and sweetening it with sugar. It tastes tart and fruity, a little like cranberry. In Egypt and Sudan it is poured over ice in summer and served hot in winter, and it is a traditional drink for breaking the fast in Ramadan and for welcoming guests.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g dried hibiscus flowers
  • 1 litre water
  • 60 g sugar
  • Ice to serve

Method

  1. Rinse the hibiscus flowers.
  2. Pour 1 litre of boiling water over them, or soak in cold water overnight for a smoother taste.
  3. Steep for 15 minutes, then strain.
  4. Stir in the sugar while warm and chill; serve over ice or hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Sweeten lightly: 1 tbsp sugar is about 50 kcal, and the tartness balances well with less.
  • Add a strip of orange peel or mint for flavour instead of extra sugar.
  • Dilute with sparkling water for a lighter, larger glass.

History

Hibiscus has long been grown in Upper Egypt and Sudan, and karkadeh is a traditional drink throughout the Nile Valley. It is still sold by street vendors and served at weddings and family celebrations.
