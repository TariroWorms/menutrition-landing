🍖 Hashi mandi (camel mandi)

Hashi mandi is mandi made with hashi, the meat of a young camel, slow-cooked in a pit oven until it falls apart over spiced rice. The rice is fragrant with turmeric, cardamom and cloves, sweetened with fried onion and raisins, and carries a smoky aroma. It is a festive dish served on large platters for gatherings.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg young camel meat on the bone, in large pieces
  • 500 g basmati rice
  • 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp ground cardamom, 6 cloves
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 60 g raisins
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil, salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Rub the meat with salt, pepper, turmeric and cardamom.
  2. Place in a deep pan with 1 litre of water, cover tightly with foil and cook at 160 °C for 3 hours until very tender.
  3. Fry the onions in the oil until golden; set half aside with the raisins.
  4. Add the rice, cloves, salt and 750 ml of the meat stock to the onions, cover and cook on low heat for 20 minutes.
  5. Brown the meat under the grill for 10 minutes, then serve on the rice topped with the fried onion and raisins.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean cuts and remove visible fat before cooking.
  • Halve the raisins and fried onion topping.
  • Take a moderate rice portion and add a large salad.

History

Hashi mandi is a camel-meat version of the Yemeni mandi tradition and is especially popular in Saudi Arabia and the Gulf, where young camel is valued for its tender meat. It is typically ordered for family feasts and celebrations.
