🥖 Bánh mì

Origin: Vietnam · Saigon (Ho Chi Minh City)

Bánh mì is the Vietnamese baguette sandwich: a light, crackly roll filled with savoury roast or grilled pork, a smear of pâté, quick-pickled carrot, cucumber, fresh cilantro and slices of hot jalapeño. Every bite mixes crunch, richness, tang and heat. It is sold from street carts and bakeries all over Vietnam and eaten at any time of day, often on the go.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 small baguettes (about 15 cm each)
  • 400 g pork shoulder or tenderloin, thinly sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tbsp oil and black pepper for the marinade
  • 100 g pâté
  • 2 carrots, cut into matchsticks
  • 60 ml rice vinegar, 1 tbsp sugar and a pinch of salt for pickling
  • 1 cucumber, cut into long strips
  • 1–2 jalapeños, sliced
  • A handful of fresh cilantro

Method

  1. Toss the carrot with the vinegar, sugar and salt and leave to pickle for at least 20 minutes.
  2. Mix the pork with the crushed garlic, sugar, oil, salt and pepper and marinate for 15 minutes.
  3. Cook the pork in a hot pan or on a grill for 2–3 minutes per side until caramelised and cooked through.
  4. Warm the baguettes in the oven for 3–4 minutes until crisp, then split them open.
  5. Spread with pâté, fill with pork, drained pickled carrot, cucumber, jalapeño and cilantro, and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose pork tenderloin over fatty shoulder and grill it instead of frying.
  • Pâté is rich, so a thin smear goes a long way; skipping it saves a noticeable share of the calories.
  • Pull out some of the soft crumb from the baguette and pile in extra cucumber and pickled vegetables.

History

The baguette arrived in Vietnam with French colonial rule in the 19th century. Vietnamese bakers developed a lighter, thinner-crusted loaf, and the filled sandwich as it is known today is usually traced to Saigon in the 1950s. The term bánh mì simply means 'bread', and the sandwich has since become popular around the world.
