Dograma (Turkmen bread and mutton in broth)

Origin: Turkmenistan
560kcal

Dograma is one of Turkmenistan's signature dishes: fresh tandoor bread is torn into small pieces by hand and mixed with finely chopped boiled mutton, raw onion and tomato. Everything is then soaked in the hot, fatty broth the meat was cooked in. The result is a thick, warming bowl that is half soup, half bread pudding, and very filling.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g mutton on the bone
  • 2 l water
  • 2 round chorek breads (about 600 g)
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, diced
  • salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Simmer the mutton in salted water for about 2 hours, skimming.
  2. Lift out the meat, debone it and chop it finely.
  3. Tear the bread into small pieces by hand into a large bowl.
  4. Mix the bread with the meat, onion and tomato, and season with pepper.
  5. Ladle the hot broth over and serve at once in deep bowls.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat from the broth before pouring it over.
  • Use wholemeal bread for more fibre.
  • Increase tomato and onion and use less meat.

History

The name comes from a word meaning 'to chop' or 'cut up', describing how the dish is prepared. Dograma is traditionally made for holidays, memorial meals and gatherings, and tearing the bread together is part of the ritual.
