🍝 Garganelli al ragù

Origin: Italy · Emilia-Romagna

Garganelli are ridged egg-pasta tubes rolled by hand from small squares, and in Emilia-Romagna they are classically served with a meat ragù. The sauce is slow-cooked from minced beef and pork with carrot, tomato and a splash of wine until thick and deeply savoury. The ridges and hollow centres catch the ragù in every bite.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g garganelli
  • 250 g minced beef
  • 150 g minced pork
  • 1 carrot, finely diced
  • 400 g crushed tomatoes
  • 100 ml red wine
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt, pepper

Method

  1. Soften the carrot in the olive oil for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the beef and pork and brown well, breaking up the meat.
  3. Pour in the wine and let it evaporate, then add the tomatoes and a little water.
  4. Simmer, partly covered, for 2 hours, adding water if needed; season.
  5. Cook the garganelli until al dente, drain and toss with the ragù.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and drain off excess fat after browning.
  • Serve 80 g dry pasta per person and add extra carrot to the sauce for volume.
  • Start the meal with a green salad so a smaller pasta portion is enough.

History

Garganelli come from Romagna, and local tradition credits their invention to a cook in the area of Imola in the 18th century, though the story is hard to verify. Slow-simmered meat ragù is a hallmark of Emilia-Romagna. Today the pairing is a Sunday-lunch classic in the region.
