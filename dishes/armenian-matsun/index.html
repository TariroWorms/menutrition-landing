Matsun (Armenian cultured yogurt)

Origin: Armenia

Matsun, also spelled mazoon, is the everyday yogurt of Armenia: thick enough to hold a spoon, pleasantly sour and very fresh-tasting. It is eaten on its own with honey or herbs, poured over dumplings and noodles, and used as the base for soups and drinks. Many families keep a spoon of the previous batch to start the next one.

Recipe

8 servings600 min

Ingredients

  • 1 l whole milk
  • 2 tbsp matsun or live plain yogurt as starter

Method

  1. Bring the milk to a boil, then let it cool to about 40–45 °C (warm to a finger).
  2. Mix the starter with a little of the warm milk until smooth.
  3. Stir the starter into the rest of the milk.
  4. Cover the pot and wrap it in a towel or blanket to keep it warm.
  5. Leave undisturbed for 6–8 hours until set.
  6. Refrigerate for at least 2 hours before eating; keep 2 tablespoons as starter for next time.

How to make it lighter

  • Make it with semi-skimmed milk for fewer calories; it will be slightly thinner.
  • Eat it plain with fresh herbs or cucumber instead of sweetening it.
  • Use it in place of sour cream or mayonnaise in sauces and dressings.

History

Fermented milk has been part of the diet of pastoral peoples in the Caucasus for a very long time. Matsun is considered a national food in Armenia and is closely related to Georgian matsoni.
