Lavash (Azerbaijani thin tandir bread)

Origin: Azerbaijan

Azerbaijani lavash is a large, thin, oval flatbread that is soft and pliable when fresh and turns crisp as it dries. The dough is stretched over a padded cushion and slapped onto the hot wall of a tandir, where it bakes in under a minute. It is used to wrap kebabs, cheese and herbs, and to cover plov while it is served.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 300 ml warm water
  • 3 g dry yeast or a piece of old dough
  • 8 g salt

Method

  1. Mix flour, yeast, salt and water and knead into a smooth, elastic dough.
  2. Let it rest covered for 45 minutes.
  3. Divide into 6 pieces and roll each into a large, very thin oval.
  4. Bake one at a time on a very hot baking steel or inverted wok for 1–2 minutes, turning once, until lightly blistered.
  5. Stack under a damp towel so the breads stay soft.

How to make it lighter

  • Lavash is very low in fat, making it a good wrap for lean fillings.
  • Fill with grilled vegetables, herbs and yogurt for a light meal.
  • Dry leftover lavash in the oven into crisps instead of buying chips.

History

Lavash baking, together with that of neighbouring countries, was inscribed by UNESCO in 2016 as shared intangible cultural heritage of Azerbaijan, Armenia, Iran, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Türkiye. Lavash is traditionally exchanged at weddings and family celebrations.
