Yukha (Azerbaijani paper-thin flatbread)

Origin: Azerbaijan

Yukha is an unleavened flatbread of flour, water and salt, rolled out so thin you can almost see through it. It cooks in seconds on a domed saj griddle and can be stored dry for a long time, then softened with a sprinkle of water. People wrap cheese, herbs or meat in it, and it is also the base for dishes like dushbara and qutab.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 220 ml water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp oil

Method

  1. Mix flour, salt, oil and water into a firm, smooth dough and rest it covered for 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into 8–10 small balls.
  3. Roll each ball with a thin rolling pin into a very thin round, dusting with flour.
  4. Cook on a hot dry saj or wide pan for 20–30 seconds per side until spotted.
  5. Stack the breads; to soften dried yukha later, sprinkle with water and cover with a cloth.

How to make it lighter

  • Yukha is naturally low in fat, so it is a light choice for wraps.
  • Fill it with vegetables, herbs and lean cheese for a balanced snack.
  • Use wholemeal flour for extra fibre; roll slightly thicker as it tears more easily.

History

Yukha belongs to a family of thin breads made across the Turkic world, where it was a practical, long-keeping staple for rural households. Baking it in large batches is still a social activity in some Azerbaijani villages.
