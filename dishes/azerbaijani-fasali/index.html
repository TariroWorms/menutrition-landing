Fasali (Azerbaijani flaky fried bread)

Origin: Azerbaijan

Fasali is a rich, flaky bread made by stretching dough very thin, brushing it with melted butter and folding or coiling it into many layers before frying it in a pan. The outside turns crisp and golden while the inside pulls apart in buttery sheets. It is served hot for breakfast with honey, jam, cheese or a glass of tea.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 220 ml warm water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 120 g melted butter
  • 2 tbsp oil for frying
  • Honey to serve

Method

  1. Knead flour, salt and water into a soft, elastic dough and rest it for 30 minutes.
  2. Divide into 4 pieces and stretch each one on an oiled surface as thin as possible.
  3. Brush generously with butter, fold into an envelope, then roll it into a coil.
  4. Rest the coils for 10 minutes and press them flat into rounds 1 cm thick.
  5. Fry in a little oil over medium-low heat for 5–6 minutes per side until golden and cooked through.
  6. Serve hot with honey or cheese.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter and brush thinly; the layers will still separate.
  • Cook in a non-stick pan with minimal oil instead of shallow-frying.
  • Pair with cheese and fresh herbs rather than honey to limit sugar.

History

Fasali is a festive home bake in Azerbaijan, often made for guests and family gatherings. Similar butter-layered breads appear in neighbouring Turkish and Iranian cooking.
