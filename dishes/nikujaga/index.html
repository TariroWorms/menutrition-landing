Nikujaga is a Japanese home-style stew of thinly sliced beef, potatoes, onion and carrot simmered in a sweet soy broth with sugar and mirin. The potatoes soak up the broth and turn soft at the edges, while the beef stays tender. It is served in a bowl with rice and is one of the best-known examples of Japanese comfort food.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g thinly sliced beef
  • 500 g potatoes, cut in chunks
  • 1 large onion, cut in wedges
  • 1 carrot, sliced
  • 400 ml water
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 3 tbsp mirin
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a pot and briefly fry the beef until it changes colour.
  2. Add the onion, carrot and potatoes and stir for 2 minutes.
  3. Pour in the water, add the sugar and mirin, bring to a boil and skim.
  4. Add the soy sauce, cover loosely and simmer for 20 minutes until the potatoes are tender.
  5. Rest for a few minutes so the vegetables absorb the broth, then serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and skip the oil — the meat can be browned in a dry non-stick pot.
  • Reduce the sugar by half; the onion and carrot add natural sweetness.
  • Swap part of the potato for more carrot and onion for a lighter bowl.

History

Nikujaga is usually traced to the Meiji era, and a popular story links it to navy cooks trying to recreate a Western beef stew with Japanese seasonings — a tale that is not firmly documented. Today it is a staple of home cooking and set-meal restaurants across Japan.
