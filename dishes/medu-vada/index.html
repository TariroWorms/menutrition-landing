🍩 Medu Vada

Medu vada are South Indian savoury doughnuts made from a batter of soaked and ground urad dal, seasoned with ginger, green chilli, curry leaves and cumin. Deep-fried, they become crisp and golden outside while staying light and fluffy inside. They are a breakfast favourite, served hot with coconut chutney and sambar.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g urad dal, soaked 4 hours
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2 green chillies, finely chopped
  • 10 curry leaves, chopped
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • salt, a few tbsp cold water
  • oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Drain the dal and grind it with as little cold water as possible into a thick, fluffy batter.
  2. Beat the batter by hand for 3–4 minutes to aerate it, then mix in the ginger, chillies, curry leaves, cumin and salt.
  3. With wet hands shape a ball, flatten it and poke a hole in the centre.
  4. Slide into oil at about 170 °C and fry 4–5 minutes, turning, until crisp and golden; drain on paper.

How to make it lighter

  • Keep the oil hot enough (about 170 °C) so the vada absorb less oil.
  • Eat two vada with sambar, which fills you up for fewer calories than extra vada.
  • Try cooking the batter in an appe (paniyaram) pan with a few drops of oil instead of deep-frying.

History

Medu vada are a classic breakfast and festival food of South India, especially Tamil Nadu and Karnataka; 'medu' is usually explained as meaning soft. Today they are served in homes, temple kitchens and tiffin restaurants across India and the diaspora.
