🌮 Cochinita pibil

Origin: Mexico · Yucatán

Cochinita pibil is pork marinated in bright-red achiote paste and bitter orange juice, wrapped in banana leaves and slow-cooked until it falls apart. The meat is tangy, earthy and gently spiced, with heat coming from habanero salsa on the side. It is served shredded in tortillas or tortas and always topped with pink pickled red onion.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg pork shoulder, in large chunks
  • 100 g achiote paste
  • 250 ml bitter orange juice (or orange juice with 2 tbsp vinegar)
  • 4 garlic cloves, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp dried oregano, salt
  • Banana leaves to wrap
  • 2 red onions, thinly sliced, in vinegar with salt (pickled onion)
  • 1–2 habanero chillies, finely chopped

Method

  1. Blend the achiote, orange juice, garlic, cumin, oregano and salt into a smooth marinade.
  2. Coat the pork and marinate for at least 4 hours or overnight.
  3. Line a baking dish with banana leaves, add the pork and marinade and fold the leaves over to seal; cover with foil.
  4. Bake at 160 °C for 3–3½ hours until the meat shreds easily.
  5. Shred the pork in its juices and serve with the pickled onion and habanero.

How to make it lighter

  • Use pork loin or trim the shoulder of visible fat — the achiote marinade keeps the meat moist.
  • Skim the fat from the cooking juices before mixing them back in.
  • Serve it over lettuce or with beans and salad instead of a stack of tortillas.

History

The dish comes from the Yucatán Peninsula, and its name joins cochinita, “little pig”, with the Maya word pib, an earth oven in which the meat was traditionally buried and cooked. Today it is a classic of Yucatecan cooking, sold at markets and taquerías especially as a weekend breakfast.
