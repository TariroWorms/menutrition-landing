🍲 Canh chua (Vietnamese sour fish soup)

Origin: Vietnam · Mekong Delta

Canh chua is a light, tangy Vietnamese soup of white fish simmered in a tamarind broth with pineapple, tomato and bean sprouts. It balances sour, sweet and savoury, and is finished with a generous handful of fresh herbs such as rice paddy herb, coriander and Thai basil. It is usually served with steamed rice as part of a family meal.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g white fish fillets or steaks
  • 2 tbsp tamarind pulp soaked in 100 ml hot water, strained
  • 150 g fresh pineapple, sliced
  • 2 tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 150 g bean sprouts
  • 1.2 l water
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce, 1 tsp sugar, salt
  • 1 tsp oil and 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • Fresh herbs: coriander, Thai basil, rice paddy herb, sliced chilli

Method

  1. Bring the water to the boil with the tamarind liquid, sugar and fish sauce.
  2. Add the fish and simmer gently for 8–10 minutes until just cooked.
  3. Add the pineapple and tomato and cook for 3 minutes, then add the bean sprouts and turn off the heat.
  4. Fry the garlic in the oil until golden and pour over the soup. Finish with the herbs and chilli and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • The soup itself is light — the rice is where most of the calories are, so keep that portion modest.
  • Fry the garlic in just a teaspoon of oil or skip frying it altogether.
  • Add extra vegetables such as okra or tomatoes to make it more filling.

History

Canh chua is closely associated with the Mekong Delta in southern Vietnam, where freshwater fish, tamarind and tropical fruit are plentiful. Catfish and snakehead are common local choices. Today it is an everyday home soup across southern Vietnam and in Vietnamese restaurants abroad.
