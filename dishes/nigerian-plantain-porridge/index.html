Plantain porridge (Ayamase-style unripe plantain pottage)

Origin: Nigeria

Nigerian plantain porridge is a thick pottage in which chunks of unripe plantain cook until they break down and thicken the sauce. It is savoury and peppery, coloured orange by palm oil and flavoured with smoked fish and crayfish. Spinach is stirred in at the end. It is eaten as a main meal on its own.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g unripe plantain
  • 60 g red palm oil
  • 150 g onion
  • 10 g scotch bonnet pepper
  • 100 g smoked fish
  • 100 g spinach

Method

  1. Peel the plantains and cut into 2 cm chunks.
  2. Fry chopped onion and blended pepper in palm oil for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the plantain, smoked fish and 400 ml water, then season.
  4. Simmer covered for 20 minutes, mashing a few chunks to thicken.
  5. Stir in spinach and cook 3 more minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the palm oil and add extra greens.
  • Choose firm green plantain, which is more filling and slower to digest.
  • Rinse the smoked fish to reduce its salt.

History

Plantain pottages are a common home-cooking style in southern Nigeria, where plantains are a daily staple. They are a thrifty one-pot way to turn firm green plantain into a filling meal.
