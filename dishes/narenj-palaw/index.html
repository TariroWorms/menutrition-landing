Narenj palaw (Afghan orange peel rice with chicken)

Origin: Afghanistan

Narenj palaw is a festive rice dish glittering with slivers of candied orange peel, almonds and pistachios. The rice is perfumed with saffron and tastes both sweet and slightly bitter. It is served with tender braised chicken and is a favourite at weddings and holiday tables.

Recipe

4 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g basmati rice
  • 4 oranges, peel only (about 60 g)
  • 4 chicken thighs (600 g)
  • 60 g sugar
  • 40 g almonds and 40 g pistachios, slivered
  • 1 onion, 1 pinch of saffron, 4 tbsp oil, salt

Method

  1. Cut the orange peel into thin strips, boil twice in fresh water and drain to remove bitterness.
  2. Simmer the peel with sugar and 100 ml water for 10 minutes until glossy.
  3. Brown the chicken with onion and salt, add a little water and braise 35 minutes.
  4. Parboil the rice, then layer with oil and saffron water and steam 25 minutes.
  5. Fold in half the peel and nuts and mound the rice on a platter.
  6. Top with the chicken and the remaining peel and nuts.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the sugar for less sweetness.
  • Use skinless chicken breast to cut fat.
  • Serve with a cucumber and tomato salad.

History

The dish comes from the Persian tradition of sweet-and-sour rice with citrus and nuts, shared across Iran and Afghanistan. Afghan cooks traditionally use the peel of bitter oranges, though sweet orange is a common substitute.
