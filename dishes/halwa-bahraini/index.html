🍮 Bahraini Halwa

Bahraini halwa is a glossy, jelly-like sweet made by slowly cooking cornstarch, sugar and water with ghee until thick and translucent. It is perfumed with saffron, cardamom and rose water and studded with almonds. Dense, chewy and fragrant, it is served in small portions with Arabic coffee to guests and at celebrations.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g cornstarch
  • 600 ml water
  • 400 g sugar
  • 80 g ghee
  • 1/2 tsp saffron, soaked in 2 tbsp rose water
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 50 g almonds, blanched and halved

Method

  1. Whisk the cornstarch into the water until smooth.
  2. Caramelize 100 g of the sugar lightly in a heavy pot, then add the cornstarch mixture and the remaining sugar.
  3. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, for about 45 minutes as it thickens.
  4. Add the ghee gradually and keep stirring until glossy and pulling from the sides.
  5. Stir in the saffron rose water, cardamom and almonds, then spoon into bowls to set.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve a small spoonful (30–40 g) alongside coffee.
  • Use less ghee; 1 tbsp ghee ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Share a bowl among several people rather than serving individual dishes.

History

Halwa is a long-standing part of Gulf hospitality, and Bahrain has a strong tradition of halwa-making families, with shops in Muharraq known for it. It is offered at weddings, Eid gatherings and when welcoming visitors.
