A chocolate chunk cookie is a large, thick, soft-baked cookie made with flour, butter, sugar and egg and packed with big pieces of chopped chocolate instead of small chips. The edges are lightly crisp, the centre gooey, and the chunks melt into wide pools. It is a bakery and coffee-shop favourite, usually sold one cookie at a time.

Recipe

8 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g plain flour
  • 1 tsp baking soda, ½ tsp salt
  • 170 g butter, melted
  • 200 g brown sugar
  • 50 g white sugar
  • 1 egg
  • 250 g dark chocolate, roughly chopped

Method

  1. Whisk the melted butter with both sugars, then whisk in the egg.
  2. Stir in the flour, baking soda and salt, then fold in the chopped chocolate. Chill the dough for 30 minutes.
  3. Heat the oven to 180 °C. Shape 8 large balls and place them well apart on lined trays.
  4. Bake for 12–14 minutes until the edges are set and the centres still look soft.
  5. Cool on the tray for 10 minutes before moving.

How to make it lighter

  • One bakery cookie of 80 g is close to a full snack — share it or save half.
  • Shape 16 smaller cookies from the same dough for portions of about 40 g.
  • Choose dark chocolate with a high cocoa content: it is more intense, so fewer chunks satisfy.

History

The chocolate chunk cookie is a bakery variation of the American chocolate chip cookie; large, thick versions became popular in US cookie shops and cafés from the late 20th century.
