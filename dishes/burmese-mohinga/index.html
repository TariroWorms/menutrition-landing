Mohinga (Burmese fish noodle soup)

Origin: Myanmar

Mohinga is a thick, golden soup of fish, lemongrass, onion, ginger and banana stem, thickened with toasted rice or chickpea flour and poured over thin rice noodles. It is savoury, gently sour and aromatic. It is served with boiled egg, crispy fritters, lime and chili flakes.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g catfish or other white fish
  • 200 g thin rice noodles
  • 3 garlic cloves, 2 cm ginger, ½ tsp turmeric
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 stalks lemongrass
  • 2 tbsp chickpea flour, toasted
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 2 boiled eggs
  • Lime, chili flakes, cilantro

Method

  1. Poach the fish in water with lemongrass, then flake it and keep the stock.
  2. Fry onion, garlic, ginger and turmeric in a little oil until soft.
  3. Add the flaked fish and stock and simmer 20 minutes.
  4. Stir in chickpea flour dissolved in water to thicken.
  5. Pour over cooked noodles and add egg, lime and herbs.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip fried fritters and use more herbs and lime.
  • Use little oil to fry the base.
  • Add vegetables such as banana stem or greens.

History

Mohinga is widely seen as Myanmar's national dish and is eaten mainly at breakfast, from street stalls in the early morning. Recipes vary between regions, with catfish being the classic fish.
