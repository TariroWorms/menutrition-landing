Higos con queso (candied figs with cheese)

Origin: Ecuador

Higos con queso pairs whole unripe figs, cooked for hours in dark panela syrup with cinnamon and cloves, with a slice of mild, salty fresh cheese. The figs turn deep brown, soft and glossy, and the cheese balances their intense sweetness. It is a traditional Ecuadorian dessert, often served at home with a cup of coffee or herbal tea.

What it is made of

figspanela (chancaca)cinnamonfresh cheese

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg small green (unripe) figs
  • 500 g panela, grated
  • 1.5 l water
  • 2 cinnamon sticks, 4 cloves
  • 300 g fresh cheese, sliced

Method

  1. Cut a small cross at the base of each fig.
  2. Blanch the figs in boiling water for 10 minutes, drain and repeat once with fresh water to remove bitterness.
  3. Dissolve the panela in the water with cinnamon and cloves.
  4. Add the figs and simmer gently for 2–3 hours until dark and the syrup is thick.
  5. Cool and serve 3–4 figs with a slice of fresh cheese and a spoon of syrup.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve two figs and drain most of the syrup.
  • Choose a lower-fat fresh cheese or ricotta.
  • Add a squeeze of lime to the syrup for brightness so less sugar is needed.

History

Fig trees were brought to the Andes by the Spanish, and preserving fruit in syrup is a colonial-era convent tradition. Panela, the unrefined cane sugar of the region, gives the Ecuadorian version its dark color and molasses flavor.
