Azerbaijani tea (armudu çay)

Origin: Azerbaijan

Azerbaijani tea is strong, deep red black tea brewed in a pot and poured into small pear-shaped armudu glasses. It is never served with milk; instead it comes with lemon, sugar cubes and little dishes of fruit jam such as cherry or quince. Guests are offered it before and after meals, and tea houses (çayxana) are central to social life.

What it is made of

black tea leaveswatersugarlemonfruit jam

Recipe

4 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 10 g black tea leaves
  • 800 ml boiling water
  • 1 lemon, sliced
  • sugar cubes
  • 100 g fruit jam
  • optional: a few cardamom pods or thyme

Method

  1. Warm the teapot with boiling water and pour it out.
  2. Add the tea leaves (and spices if using) and fill with boiling water.
  3. Set the pot over a kettle or on low heat and let it steep 5-7 minutes without boiling.
  4. Pour into armudu glasses, leaving space at the top.
  5. Serve with lemon, sugar and jam on small saucers.

How to make it lighter

  • Drink it unsweetened and taste the jam with a spoon instead of stirring it in.
  • Add thyme or mint for flavour without sugar.
  • Limit strong tea in the evening because of its caffeine.

History

Tea spread in Azerbaijan in the 19th and 20th centuries, and the Lankaran region in the south became the country's main tea-growing area. Offering tea is the basic gesture of hospitality, and the armudu glass is a national symbol.
