🥟 Pork Shumai

Origin: China · Guangdong

Pork shumai are small, open-topped steamed dumplings with a juicy filling of minced pork and chopped shrimp, seasoned with ginger and soy sauce and wrapped in thin wheat wrappers. The filling is springy and savoury, and the wrapper soft at the edges. They are a classic of dim sum, served in bamboo steamers with tea.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g minced pork
  • 150 g raw shrimp, chopped
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 spring onions, finely chopped
  • 24 round wonton wrappers
  • Salt and white pepper to taste

Method

  1. Mix the pork, shrimp, ginger, soy sauce, spring onions, salt and pepper and stir firmly in one direction until sticky.
  2. Hold a wrapper in a loose fist, add a heaped teaspoon of filling and squeeze gently into a cup shape, leaving the top open.
  3. Smooth the top and tap the base flat.
  4. Line a steamer with baking paper and steam the shumai over boiling water for 10–12 minutes until cooked through.
  5. Serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced pork to cut the fat without losing juiciness from the shrimp.
  • Count your pieces — shumai are small and easy to overeat; pair a few with steamed greens.
  • Dip sparingly: a little soy sauce adds flavour without adding many calories, but salt adds up.

History

Shumai are usually traced to northern China, and the pork-and-shrimp version became one of the best-known items of Cantonese dim sum in Guangzhou and Hong Kong teahouses. Today they are eaten across China, Japan and Southeast Asia.
