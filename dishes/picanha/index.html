Picanha (grilled rump cap)

Origin: Brazil · South

Picanha is the triangular rump cap of beef, the most prized cut of Brazilian barbecue. It is cut into thick steaks with the fat cap left on, bent into a C shape on skewers or grilled whole, and seasoned only with coarse salt. As it cooks, the fat melts over the meat and crisps on the outside, keeping it juicy and beefy. It is carved thinly against the grain and served medium-rare with farofa, rice and salsa.

What it is made of

beefsaltgarlic

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 1 picanha (about 1.2 kg) with fat cap
  • Coarse salt
  • Optional: 2 garlic cloves, crushed

Method

  1. Take the meat out of the fridge 30 minutes before grilling.
  2. Cut into 3–4 thick steaks across the grain, keeping the fat.
  3. Score the fat lightly and bend each steak into a C on a skewer.
  4. Coat generously with coarse salt.
  5. Grill over hot embers, fat side first, until browned.
  6. Turn and cook to medium-rare, about 15–20 minutes in total.
  7. Rest 5 minutes, slice thinly across the grain and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim the fat cap on your plate after cooking; it keeps the meat moist on the grill.
  • Keep portions to about 150 g of meat.
  • Serve with vinaigrette salsa and greens rather than fries.

History

Picanha became the star of the churrascarias that spread from the south of Brazil to the whole country in the twentieth century. Outside Brazil the same cut is known as rump cap or top sirloin cap.
