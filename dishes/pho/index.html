🍜 Pho

Pho is Vietnam's famous noodle soup: a clear beef broth slowly simmered with charred onion and ginger, star anise and cinnamon, poured over flat rice noodles and slices of beef. The broth is light yet deeply aromatic. It is served in large bowls with bean sprouts, Thai basil and lime wedges on the side, so each diner can season the soup to taste.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg beef bones and 500 g beef brisket
  • 300 g beef sirloin, very thinly sliced
  • 400 g flat rice noodles
  • 1 onion and a 5 cm piece of ginger, charred
  • 3 star anise and 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 litres water, salt and a little sugar
  • Bean sprouts, Thai basil, lime wedges and sliced chili to serve

Method

  1. Blanch the bones for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Cover the bones and brisket with fresh water, add the charred onion and ginger, star anise and cinnamon, and simmer gently for 3 hours, skimming often.
  3. Remove the brisket when tender and slice it; strain the broth and season with salt and a little sugar.
  4. Soak or cook the rice noodles and divide them into bowls with the brisket and raw sirloin slices.
  5. Pour the boiling broth over so it cooks the raw beef, and serve with sprouts, basil, lime and chili.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the broth and lift off the solid fat before reheating.
  • Ask for less noodles and more bean sprouts and herbs.
  • Choose lean cuts such as sirloin or eye of round instead of fatty brisket.

History

Pho emerged in northern Vietnam, around Hanoi and Nam Định, in the early 20th century. Its exact origins are debated, with influences often traced to Vietnamese rice-noodle soups and to French and Chinese cooking of the period. After 1954 it spread south and later around the world with the Vietnamese diaspora.
