🍜 Mì Quảng

Origin: Vietnam · Quảng Nam

Mì Quảng is a turmeric-yellow rice noodle dish from central Vietnam. Wide flat noodles are served with just a little concentrated broth, topped with shrimp and pork, roasted peanuts, fresh herbs and pieces of crunchy toasted rice cracker. It is eaten more like a noodle salad than a soup, mixed together with lime and chili at the table.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g fresh wide turmeric noodles
  • 300 g pork belly or shoulder, thinly sliced
  • 300 g shrimp, peeled
  • 3 shallots and 3 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp sugar, salt, pepper
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 50 g roasted peanuts, roughly crushed
  • Lettuce, mint, Vietnamese coriander and banana blossom
  • 2 rice crackers, lime wedges and chili

Method

  1. Season the pork and shrimp with turmeric, sugar, salt and pepper.
  2. Fry the shallots and garlic in the oil, add the pork and cook for 5 minutes, then the shrimp for 2 minutes.
  3. Add 600 ml water and simmer for 15 minutes to make a small amount of rich broth.
  4. Warm the noodles in boiling water and drain.
  5. Put herbs and noodles in bowls, add a ladle of broth with pork and shrimp, top with peanuts and broken rice crackers, and serve with lime and chili.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork shoulder instead of belly.
  • Go easy on peanuts — a small handful is enough for crunch.
  • Fill half the bowl with herbs and greens and use fewer noodles.

History

Mì Quảng comes from Quảng Nam province, around Hội An and Đà Nẵng, where it has long been the region's best-known everyday dish. Today it is also sold across Vietnam.
