Encocado de pescado (fish in coconut sauce)

Origin: Ecuador · Esmeraldas

Encocado is a gentle stew of firm white fish cooked in a sauce of coconut milk, tomato, sweet peppers and plenty of cilantro. The sauce is silky, slightly sweet and fragrant rather than spicy. It is spooned over white rice and eaten with fried green plantain. Shrimp or crab versions are also common.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g firm white fish fillets
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 1 red and 1 green pepper, sliced
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp achiote oil
  • 1 bunch cilantro, lime, salt

Method

  1. Season the fish with lime, garlic and salt and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Soften onion and peppers in achiote oil, then add tomatoes and cook 5 minutes.
  3. Pour in the coconut milk and simmer for 5 minutes.
  4. Lay the fish in the sauce, cover and cook gently for 8-10 minutes without stirring.
  5. Scatter with chopped cilantro and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk or half coconut milk, half fish stock.
  • Serve with brown rice or steamed plantain instead of fried.
  • Add extra peppers and tomato to bulk up the sauce with vegetables.

History

Encocado is closely tied to the Afro-Ecuadorian communities of Esmeraldas, where coconut palms grow along the coast. Coconut milk is the backbone of much of the province's cooking. The dish is now popular across the coast and in Quito restaurants.
