🥘 Boeuf en daube (Provençal beef stew)

Boeuf en daube is a Provençal beef stew in which chunks of beef are marinated and slowly braised in red wine with tomatoes, mushrooms, black olives and a strip of orange zest. The result is tender meat in a dark, aromatic sauce with a hint of citrus and herbs. It is served with boiled potatoes, rice or polenta.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg beef chuck or shin, cut into 5 cm pieces
  • 750 ml red wine
  • 2 strips of orange zest
  • 2 onions and 2 carrots, sliced; 3 garlic cloves
  • 400 g tomatoes, chopped
  • 250 g mushrooms, quartered
  • 100 g black olives
  • Bay leaf, thyme, rosemary; 2 tbsp olive oil, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Marinate the beef overnight in the wine with the onions, carrots, garlic, orange zest and herbs.
  2. Lift out the beef and pat dry; keep the marinade.
  3. Brown the beef in the olive oil in a heavy casserole, in batches.
  4. Add the vegetables from the marinade, the tomatoes and the wine. Bring to a simmer, cover and cook at 150 °C for 3 hours.
  5. Add the mushrooms and olives and cook for 30 minutes more, until the meat is very tender.
  6. Remove the zest and herbs, check the seasoning and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean shin or trimmed chuck and skim the fat from the surface after cooking.
  • Olives are rich in fat — a small handful is enough for flavour.
  • Serve with a modest scoop of potatoes or rice and a big green salad.

History

The name comes from the daubière, the deep, lidded pot in which the stew was traditionally cooked slowly by the hearth. Daube is a family dish of Provence and the south of France, often made a day ahead, and many households have their own version.
