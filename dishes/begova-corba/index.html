🍲 Begova čorba (Bosnian bey's soup)

Origin: Bosnia and Herzegovina · Sarajevo

Begova čorba, the 'bey's soup', is a velvety Bosnian chicken soup with okra, carrot and celery, enriched with cream and brightened with a squeeze of lemon just before serving. The chicken is shredded into the broth, the okra gives it body and a silky texture, and the lemon keeps it fresh rather than heavy. It is often served as the opening course of a festive meal.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g chicken thighs or breast, bone-in
  • 150 g okra, small pods, trimmed
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 1 small celery root or 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 150 ml cooking cream
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, salt, pepper, chopped parsley

Method

  1. Cover the chicken with 2 litres of water, bring to the boil, skim, add salt and simmer for 40 minutes. Lift out the chicken and shred the meat.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil, add the carrot and celery and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the vegetables and the okra to the broth and simmer for 20–25 minutes until tender.
  4. Return the chicken, lower the heat and stir in the cream without letting the soup boil.
  5. Take off the heat, add the lemon juice, season and sprinkle with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Use breast meat and skim the fat off the broth once it cools.
  • Swap half the cream for extra broth — the okra still keeps the soup silky.
  • A big bowl is filling for few calories: make it the main course with a side salad.

History

As the name suggests, the soup is usually associated with the beys, the Ottoman-era nobility of Bosnia, and is considered one of the classic dishes of Sarajevo's traditional kitchen. Today it is a standard first course at weddings, family celebrations and Ramadan iftar meals in Bosnia and Herzegovina.
