Bizcochos (Uruguayan pastries)

Origin: Uruguay

In Uruguay, bizcochos are not sponge cakes but a whole family of small bakery pastries bought by weight in a paper bag. The mix usually includes flaky croissants, horn-shaped cuernitos, savoury lard bizcochos called de grasa, and sweet pieces filled with quince paste, dulce de leche or pastry cream. They are shared at home or at work in the afternoon, always with a gourd of mate.

Recipe

12 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml water
  • 15 g fresh yeast
  • 10 g salt, 30 g sugar
  • 150 g lard or butter for laminating
  • Fillings: 100 g quince paste, 100 g dulce de leche
  • 1 egg for brushing

Method

  1. Mix flour, water, yeast, salt and sugar into a smooth dough and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Roll out, spread the soft fat over two thirds and fold in three.
  3. Repeat the roll-and-fold twice more, resting between turns.
  4. Roll to 5 mm and cut into squares, triangles and strips.
  5. Fill some with quince paste or dulce de leche, shape into horns, rolls and pockets.
  6. Rise for 40 minutes, brush with egg and bake at 200 °C for 15–18 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose the plain de grasa pieces, which have no sugar.
  • Limit yourself to two pieces and drink mate without sugar.
  • Bake smaller pieces to keep portions modest.

History

The tradition grew from European, especially Spanish and Italian, baking brought by immigrants to Montevideo. Bakeries became neighbourhood institutions, and a bag of bizcochos on a rainy afternoon or during a long shared mate is a typical Uruguayan custom.
