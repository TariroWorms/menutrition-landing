🥪 Ayvalık toast

Origin: Turkey · Ayvalık, Balıkesir

Ayvalık toast is Turkey's most overloaded toasted sandwich: a long, soft loaf split and stuffed with spicy sucuk sausage, salami and melting kaşar cheese, pressed on a hot grill, then opened up and piled with pickles, tomato and a spoonful of Russian salad. It is crunchy outside and gooey and saucy inside, usually finished with ketchup and mayonnaise. It is a classic of seaside kiosks and late-night snack bars.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 1 long soft toast loaf or 2 large soft rolls (about 250 g)
  • 80 g beef sucuk, thinly sliced
  • 60 g beef salami, sliced
  • 100 g kaşar cheese, grated
  • 4 tbsp Russian salad (diced potato, carrot, peas and pickles in mayonnaise)
  • Pickled cucumbers and 1 tomato, sliced
  • Ketchup to serve

Method

  1. Fry the sucuk slices briefly in a dry pan until the fat runs.
  2. Split the bread, fill it with the cheese, sucuk and salami and press in a hot sandwich press or grill pan for 4–5 minutes until crisp and the cheese has melted.
  3. Open the toast, add the Russian salad, pickles and tomato, close it and cut in half. Serve hot with ketchup.

How to make it lighter

  • Share one toast between two — a whole loaded Ayvalık toast is a big meal on its own.
  • Ask for it without Russian salad and mayonnaise; keep the pickles and tomato for crunch.
  • Pick one meat instead of both sucuk and salami, and drink ayran rather than soda.

History

The sandwich is named after Ayvalık, a town on Turkey's northern Aegean coast, where the loaded version is said to have become popular at seaside toast stands. Today it is sold all over Turkey, and almost every vendor has a slightly different filling.
