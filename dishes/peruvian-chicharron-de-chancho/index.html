Chicharrón de chancho (Peruvian fried pork)

Origin: Peru

Peruvian chicharrón de chancho is made from pork pieces cooked in salted water until the liquid evaporates, then left to fry in their own rendered fat until golden outside and tender inside. It is served with boiled hominy (mote), potatoes or fried sweet potato and a sharp salsa of red onion, lime and mint. In Lima it is the classic weekend breakfast, often tucked into a bread roll as pan con chicharrón.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork shoulder or belly, cut into large chunks
  • 1 l water
  • 1 tsp cumin, 3 garlic cloves
  • 400 g cooked hominy
  • 400 g potatoes, boiled
  • 1 red onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 limes, a few mint leaves
  • salt

Method

  1. Put the pork, water, garlic, cumin and salt in a wide pot.
  2. Boil uncovered until all the water evaporates, about 1 hour.
  3. Keep cooking over medium heat so the pork fries in its own fat until golden.
  4. Mix the onion with lime juice, mint and salt for the salsa.
  5. Warm the hominy and potatoes.
  6. Serve the pork with the sides and salsa.

How to make it lighter

  • Use pork shoulder rather than belly to cut fat.
  • Drain the pieces well on paper after frying.
  • Serve with extra salsa and a salad and treat it as an occasional dish.

History

Pork was brought to Peru by the Spanish, and the technique of frying it in its own lard spread throughout the country. Highland versions in Cusco and Arequipa favour mote and potatoes, while coastal cities prefer the sandwich with sweet potato.
