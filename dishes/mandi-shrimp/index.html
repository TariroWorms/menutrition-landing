🍤 Shrimp Mandi

Shrimp mandi is a seafood take on the classic mandi: long-grain rice cooked with onion, turmeric, cardamom, cloves and dried black lime, finished with a light smoky note and topped with seared shrimp. The rice is fluffy and fragrant and the shrimp sweet and juicy. It is served on a shared platter, often with a tomato chilli sauce on the side.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g large shrimp, peeled
  • 400 g basmati rice
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 4 cardamom pods
  • 3 cloves
  • 1 dried black lime, pierced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 700 ml water
  • Salt

Method

  1. Fry the onion in 1 tbsp oil until golden, then add the spices and black lime.
  2. Add the rinsed rice, water and salt; cover and cook on low heat for 18 minutes.
  3. Season the shrimp with salt and a pinch of turmeric and sear them in the remaining oil for 2 minutes per side.
  4. For a smoky note, set a small heat-proof cup with a glowing piece of charcoal on the rice, add a few drops of oil, cover for 5 minutes, then remove it.
  5. Serve the rice topped with the shrimp.

How to make it lighter

  • Sear the shrimp in a non-stick pan with 1 tsp oil instead of 1 tbsp — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Shrimp are lean, so a larger shrimp portion with less rice keeps the plate filling.
  • Serve with lemon wedges and a big green salad instead of a second scoop of rice.

History

Mandi is generally traced to the Hadramawt region of Yemen as a slow-roasted meat dish. Shrimp mandi is a seafood variation found today on menus in Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Oman.
