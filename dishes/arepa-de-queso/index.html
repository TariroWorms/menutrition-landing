Arepa de queso (cheese arepa)

Origin: Colombia

Arepa de queso is one of the most common breakfasts in Colombia. Grated fresh cheese is worked into the corn dough, and the arepas are grilled until crisp outside and soft inside; many cooks then split them and add more cheese to melt. They are salty, buttery and stretchy, and pair well with coffee, hot chocolate or scrambled eggs.

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g precooked white cornmeal
  • 500 ml warm water
  • 200 g grated fresh cheese (campesino or mozzarella)
  • 30 g butter
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Mix cornmeal, salt and warm water; knead in the butter and half the cheese.
  2. Shape 6 discs about 1.5 cm thick.
  3. Cook on a medium griddle 6–7 minutes per side until golden and crisp.
  4. Split each arepa and fill with the remaining cheese.
  5. Return to the griddle briefly until the cheese melts and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a lower-fat cheese and skip the extra butter on top.
  • Add spinach or tomato inside for vegetables.
  • One arepa with eggs makes a balanced breakfast.

History

Arepas of many kinds are made across Colombia and neighbouring Venezuela, and the cheese version is a staple of bakeries, street carts and home kitchens alike. Each region uses its local fresh cheese.
