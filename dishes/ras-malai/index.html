🍮 Ras Malai

Origin: India · West Bengal

Ras Malai is a Bengali dessert of soft, spongy discs of fresh chenna (Indian fresh cheese) soaked in chilled, sweetened milk flavoured with cardamom and saffron and topped with chopped pistachios. The dumplings absorb the creamy, fragrant milk and melt in the mouth. It is served cold, especially at festivals, weddings and Diwali.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 litres full-fat milk for the cheese, plus 1 litre for the syrup milk
  • 3 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar
  • 300 g sugar
  • 1 litre water
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • A pinch of saffron
  • 20 g pistachios, chopped

Method

  1. Boil 1.5 litres of milk, add the lemon juice to curdle it, then strain the curds through cloth, rinse and hang for 30 minutes.
  2. Knead the curds until smooth and shape into 12 small flat discs.
  3. Boil the water with 200 g sugar, add the discs, cover and cook 15 minutes until doubled in size.
  4. Simmer the remaining milk until reduced by a third, then add 100 g sugar, cardamom and saffron.
  5. Squeeze the discs gently, place them in the warm milk, top with pistachios and chill for at least 4 hours.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve 1–2 pieces as a portion; they are rich despite their light texture.
  • Use less sugar in the milk — 1 tbsp sugar ≈ 50 kcal — and let the cardamom and saffron carry the flavour.
  • Make the syrup milk with semi-skimmed milk for a lighter version.

History

Ras malai is closely linked to Bengal, and at least one Kolkata sweet maker claims its invention. It is now popular throughout India, Pakistan and Bangladesh.
