Jonjoli (pickled bladdernut buds)

Origin: Georgia

Jonjoli are the young flower buds of the Colchian bladdernut shrub, salted or pickled and kept for the whole year. Before serving they are rinsed and dressed with thin onion rings, oil and sometimes a little vinegar and cilantro. The taste is sour and slightly grassy, a bit like a mix of capers and asparagus, and it is a classic starter on the Georgian table.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g pickled jonjoli
  • 1 onion (100 g)
  • 4 tsp sunflower oil (20 ml)
  • 1 tsp wine vinegar (5 ml)
  • a few sprigs of cilantro (10 g)

Method

  1. Rinse the pickled jonjoli and squeeze out excess brine.
  2. Slice the onion into very thin half-rings.
  3. Mix the buds with the onion in a bowl.
  4. Dress with sunflower oil and a splash of vinegar if you like it sharper.
  5. Sprinkle with chopped cilantro and serve as a cold starter.

How to make it lighter

  • Rinse well to reduce the salt, which is the main drawback of pickles.
  • Use just a teaspoon of oil per portion; the buds do not need much.
  • Serve alongside grilled meat to balance a rich meal with something sour and light.

History

The bladdernut grows wild in the forests of western Georgia, and its buds have long been gathered in spring. Jonjoli is a very typical Georgian zakuska that is rarely found in other cuisines.
