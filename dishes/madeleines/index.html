🧁 Madeleines

Madeleines are small French sponge cakes baked in shell-shaped moulds, made from flour, eggs, sugar and melted butter, usually perfumed with lemon zest. They have crisp golden edges, a soft, airy crumb and a characteristic hump on the flat side. They are eaten with tea or coffee, often still slightly warm.

What it is made of

floureggbuttersugarlemon

Recipe

12 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 90 g sugar
  • 100 g butter, melted and cooled
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs and sugar until pale and thick.
  2. Fold in the flour, baking powder, salt and lemon zest, then the melted butter.
  3. Cover and chill the batter for at least 30 minutes.
  4. Spoon into greased shell moulds, filling them three-quarters full.
  5. Bake at 200 °C for 9–11 minutes until golden with a hump; unmould while warm.

How to make it lighter

  • One madeleine weighs about 20–25 g, so count them individually rather than by the plate.
  • Make mini madeleines for a smaller portion with the same taste.
  • Pair with unsweetened tea or coffee instead of a sweet drink.

History

Madeleines are associated with the town of Commercy in Lorraine, where they have been made and sold since at least the 18th century. Several legends name a young cook called Madeleine as their creator, but none is documented. Their fame grew further after Marcel Proust described them in In Search of Lost Time.
