Chimichurri

Origin: Argentina

Chimichurri is Argentina's best-known sauce: finely chopped parsley, garlic and dried oregano loosened with vinegar and oil, with a pinch of chili flakes. It is bright, tangy and pungent rather than creamy. It is spooned over steaks, sausages and grilled vegetables, and every asador has a personal version.

Recipe

8 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g fresh parsley, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp chili flakes
  • 60 ml red wine vinegar
  • 120 ml olive or sunflower oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Chop the parsley very finely by hand.
  2. Mix the garlic with salt and vinegar and let it sit 5 minutes.
  3. Add parsley, oregano and chili flakes.
  4. Pour in the oil and stir.
  5. Rest at least 1 hour, or overnight, before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use one tablespoon per serving; it is mostly oil.
  • Replace part of the oil with water or lemon juice for a lighter sauce.
  • Keep it in a jar in the fridge for up to a week.

History

Chimichurri is closely tied to the asado culture of Argentina and Uruguay. The origin of its name is debated, and no single story is accepted as fact.
