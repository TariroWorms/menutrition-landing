Sancocho venezolano (Venezuelan sancocho)

Origin: Venezuela

Venezuelan sancocho is a large pot of soup made with hen, beef or a mix of meats, simmered with chunks of cassava, ocumo (taro), green plantain, corn on the cob and pumpkin. The starchy vegetables thicken the broth, and plenty of cilantro and a little garlic finish it. It is a weekend and family-gathering dish, often cooked outdoors over a wood fire and served with arepas and avocado.

Recipe

8 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg hen or chicken pieces (or 500 g chicken + 500 g beef ribs)
  • 3 l water
  • 400 g cassava, 300 g ocumo or taro
  • 1 green plantain, 2 corn cobs in chunks
  • 300 g pumpkin
  • 1 onion, 1 leek, 4 garlic cloves, 1 pepper
  • 30 g cilantro, salt

Method

  1. Simmer the meat in the water with onion, leek, garlic and pepper for 45 minutes, skimming the foam.
  2. Add the corn and the cassava and cook 15 minutes.
  3. Add the taro, plantain and pumpkin and cook 25–30 minutes more until all are tender.
  4. Mash a few pieces of pumpkin against the pot to thicken the broth.
  5. Season with salt, stir in chopped cilantro and serve in deep bowls.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the chicken skin and skim the fat for a lighter soup.
  • Use more pumpkin and less cassava to reduce starch.
  • Serve a smaller bowl with a salad instead of extra arepas.

History

Sancocho is shared across the Hispanic Caribbean and northern South America, with each country adding its own roots and meats. In Venezuela, cooking a sancocho for friends on a Sunday or at the beach is a social event in itself.
