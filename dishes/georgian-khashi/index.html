Khashi (tripe and cow's foot soup)

Origin: Georgia

Khashi is made by simmering cleaned beef tripe and cow's feet for many hours until the broth turns white and gelatinous and the meat falls apart. It is served unsalted so each person can add salt and a generous amount of crushed garlic mixed with broth at the table. Pieces of dried bread are crumbled in, and some cooks enrich the broth with a little milk.

What it is made of

beef tripebeef feetgarlicmilkbread

Recipe

6 servings480 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg beef tripe
  • 2 cow's feet (about 1.5 kg)
  • 1 head of garlic
  • 200 ml milk
  • dried bread (150 g)
  • salt to taste

Method

  1. Clean the tripe and cow's feet thoroughly and soak them in cold water overnight, changing the water.
  2. Cover with fresh water, bring to the boil and skim well.
  3. Simmer gently for 6–8 hours, topping up with water so the meat stays covered.
  4. Remove the bones, cut the tripe into strips, add a little milk to the broth and return the meat.
  5. Serve hot with crushed garlic mixed with broth, salt and pieces of toasted bread.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat from the surface before serving.
  • Keep the bread on the side and add only a small amount.
  • The bread is optional; leave it out for a lighter bowl.

History

Khashi is traditionally eaten early in the morning, especially in winter, and is famous in Georgia as a remedy after a long feast. Similar soups exist in Armenia, Azerbaijan, Turkey and Iran.
