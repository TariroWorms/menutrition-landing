Kalguksu (knife-cut noodle soup)

Origin: South Korea

Kalguksu is a comforting bowl of hand-cut wheat noodles served in a clear broth of clams or dried anchovies. The noodles are soft and slightly starchy, which thickens the broth. Zucchini, potato and scallions add sweetness and freshness. It is a typical warm meal in rainy or cold weather.

Recipe

2 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g fresh wheat noodles
  • 500 g clams
  • 1 litre water or anchovy stock
  • 150 g zucchini
  • 100 g potato
  • 2 garlic cloves, 2 scallions
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)

Method

  1. Simmer clams in water for 10 minutes and strain the broth.
  2. Add potato and cook 8 minutes.
  3. Add the noodles, stirring so they do not stick.
  4. Cook 4 minutes, then add zucchini.
  5. Season with soy sauce, garlic and scallions and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use whole wheat noodles for more fibre.
  • Skip most of the broth to reduce sodium.
  • Add more vegetables or an egg for protein.

History

The name literally means knife noodles, referring to the way the dough is rolled and cut by hand. Wheat was once a luxury in Korea, so noodle soups like this became popular in summer and on special days.
