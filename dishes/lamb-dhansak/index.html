Lamb dhansak is a Parsi curry of lamb braised with lentils and pumpkin until the lentils melt into a thick, golden sauce. Tamarind brings a sweet-sour tang, while fenugreek and garam masala add warmth and depth. The texture is hearty and stew-like, and it is traditionally served with caramelised brown rice.

Recipe

6 servings105 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g boneless lamb, cubed
  • 200 g split lentils (toor or red), rinsed
  • 300 g pumpkin, cubed
  • 2 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 1 tsp ground fenugreek
  • 2 tsp garam masala
  • 2 onions, chopped; 3 garlic cloves; 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt
  • 1 litre water

Method

  1. Simmer the lentils and pumpkin in the water for 30 minutes until soft, then mash or blend.
  2. Fry the onions in the oil until golden, add garlic, ginger, fenugreek and garam masala.
  3. Add the lamb and brown for 5 minutes.
  4. Pour in the lentil-pumpkin purée, add salt and simmer covered for 60 minutes until the lamb is tender.
  5. Stir in the tamarind, adjust the seasoning and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean lamb leg and trim the fat; the lentils keep the sauce thick without extra oil.
  • Add more pumpkin and lentils and less meat for a lighter, filling bowl.
  • Serve with a modest portion of plain rice or a salad.

History

Dhansak comes from the Parsi community of western India, descendants of Zoroastrians who settled in Gujarat and later Mumbai. The name is usually explained as dhan, 'rice', and saak, 'vegetables' or 'greens'. In Parsi homes it is often eaten as a Sunday lunch, and it became widely known through Indian restaurants in Britain.
