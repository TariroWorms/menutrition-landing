🌮 Tlayuda

A tlayuda is a large, thin, crisp tortilla from Oaxaca spread with refried black beans and topped with stringy Oaxaca cheese, chorizo, avocado and salsa. It is cooked over coals or on a griddle until the base crackles and the cheese melts. Sometimes called a Mexican pizza, it is eaten open or folded in half and shared as a street-food meal.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 2 large tortillas (tlayudas)
  • 200 g cooked black beans, mashed
  • 150 g Oaxaca cheese, pulled into strings
  • 100 g chorizo, crumbled
  • 1 avocado, sliced
  • 100 g salsa
  • Shredded cabbage, salt

Method

  1. Fry the chorizo in a dry pan until crisp and set aside.
  2. Warm the beans, mash and season with salt.
  3. Toast a tortilla on a hot griddle, then spread with beans.
  4. Top with cheese and chorizo and cook until the base is crisp and the cheese melts.
  5. Finish with avocado, cabbage and salsa, then fold or cut to serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Drain the chorizo well on paper after frying, or use half the amount.
  • Use half the cheese and add extra cabbage and salsa.
  • Share one tlayuda between two and add a side salad.

History

Tlayudas come from the Valley of Oaxaca in southern Mexico, where the large tortillas have long been a staple. Today they are a famous street food of Oaxaca City, cooked at night stalls over charcoal.
