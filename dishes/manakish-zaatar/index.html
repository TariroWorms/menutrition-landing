🫓 Manakish with za'atar and sesame

This manakish is a thin round of bread dough topped with za'atar, olive oil and an extra scattering of sesame seeds, then baked in a very hot oven. It comes out thin, herby and slightly oily, with a toasty crunch from the sesame. It is sold from early morning in Levantine bakeries and eaten fresh, folded in half or rolled with vegetables.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g bread dough (flour, water, yeast, salt)
  • 5 tbsp za'atar mix (thyme, sumac, sesame)
  • 2 tbsp sesame seeds
  • 5 tbsp olive oil
  • Tomato, cucumber and mint to serve

Method

  1. Divide the risen dough into 4 balls and rest for 15 minutes.
  2. Roll each ball out thinly into a round about 22 cm across.
  3. Mix the za'atar, sesame seeds and olive oil and spread over the dough, leaving a thin border.
  4. Bake at 250 °C for 6–8 minutes until crisp at the edges.
  5. Serve warm, folded or rolled with vegetables.

How to make it lighter

  • Brush the za'atar mix with less oil — each tablespoon of olive oil is about 120 kcal.
  • Make the base thin and eat it rolled with tomato, cucumber, mint and olives.
  • Have one manousheh with fresh vegetables rather than two on their own.

History

Manakish is the everyday baked flatbread of the Levant, traditionally baked in the morning at the neighbourhood bakery or in a home oven. Za'atar topping is the most common version, especially in Lebanon, Syria and Palestine.
