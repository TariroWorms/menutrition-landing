🍮 Mafroukeh

Origin: Lebanon · Levant

Mafroukeh is a Lebanese dessert of semolina toasted in butter and cooked with sugar syrup into a soft, grainy paste. It is pressed into a tray, topped with a layer of ashta cream and scattered with chopped pistachios, and the syrup is often scented with orange blossom water. It is rich, buttery and sweet, served cold in squares.

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g fine semolina
  • 100 g butter
  • 200 ml sugar syrup
  • 1 tbsp orange blossom water
  • 300 g ashta cream
  • 40 g pistachios, chopped

Method

  1. Melt the butter and toast the semolina in it, stirring, until golden.
  2. Add the syrup and orange blossom water and stir over low heat until it forms a soft paste.
  3. Spread it in a tray and let it cool slightly.
  4. Cover with the ashta cream and sprinkle with pistachios; chill before cutting.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small squares of about 60 g as a single serving.
  • Reduce the syrup by a quarter; the ashta keeps it sweet enough.
  • Use pistachios as a light garnish rather than a thick layer.

History

Mafroukeh, from the Arabic for 'rubbed', is a sweet of Lebanon and Syria sold by traditional sweet shops, especially in Beirut and Tripoli. It is popular during Ramadan and at celebrations.
