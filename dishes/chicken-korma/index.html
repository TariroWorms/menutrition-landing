🍛 Chicken korma

Chicken korma is a mild, luxurious curry in which chicken is braised in yogurt, then finished with cream and a smooth paste of ground cashews. Cardamom and a few threads of saffron give it a delicate, floral perfume. The sauce is pale, creamy, nutty and only gently spiced, which makes it a favourite for those who prefer little heat. It is served with rice or naan.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g boneless chicken, in chunks
  • 200 g plain yogurt
  • 60 ml cream
  • 50 g cashews, soaked in hot water 15 minutes
  • 6 cardamom pods, lightly crushed
  • A pinch of saffron in 2 tbsp warm water
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt

Method

  1. Blend the soaked cashews with a little water to a smooth paste.
  2. Heat the oil, fry the cardamom and onion until soft and pale gold, then add the ginger-garlic paste.
  3. Add the chicken and cook for 5 minutes, then lower the heat and stir in the yogurt.
  4. Cover and simmer gently for 20 minutes, stirring now and then.
  5. Stir in the cashew paste, cream, saffron water and salt and simmer for 5 minutes until silky.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the cream and rely on yogurt and cashew paste for richness.
  • Halve the cashews: nuts are among the most calorie-dense ingredients.
  • Pair it with plenty of plain vegetables and a small portion of rice.

History

Korma is linked to the Mughal court kitchens of northern India in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, where meat braised in yogurt and enriched with nuts was a mark of refinement. The word is usually explained as meaning braising. Creamy chicken korma later became one of the best-known dishes in Indian restaurants abroad.
