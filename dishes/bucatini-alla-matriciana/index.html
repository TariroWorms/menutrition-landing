🍝 Bucatini alla matriciana

Origin: Italy · Rome, Lazio

Bucatini alla matriciana is the Roman-dialect name for the famous guanciale and tomato pasta, here in a version that adds gently softened onion. The onion makes the sauce slightly sweeter and rounder, balancing the salty cured pork cheek and sharp pecorino romano. Thick hollow bucatini hold the sauce well. It is served hot with extra grated pecorino.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g bucatini
  • 150 g guanciale, cut into strips
  • 1 small onion, finely sliced
  • 400 g canned peeled tomatoes, crushed
  • 60 g pecorino romano, grated
  • Salt, a pinch of chili flakes (optional)

Method

  1. Render the guanciale in a dry pan over medium heat until crisp at the edges; lift it out.
  2. Soften the onion in the rendered fat for 5–6 minutes without browning.
  3. Add the tomatoes and a pinch of salt and simmer for 15 minutes; return the guanciale.
  4. Cook the bucatini until al dente, drain and toss in the sauce with a little pasta water.
  5. Off the heat, stir in pecorino and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Pour off most of the rendered fat before adding the onion — 1 tbsp of fat is about 120 kcal.
  • Use more onion and tomato and less guanciale for a lighter, larger sauce.
  • Keep the portion to 80 g of dry pasta and add a vegetable side.

History

'Matriciana' is how Romans traditionally say 'amatriciana', after the town of Amatrice in Lazio. Whether onion belongs in the sauce is a long-running Roman debate: Amatrice's own recipe leaves it out, while many Roman trattorias add it.
