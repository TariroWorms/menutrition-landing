🍙 Arancini

Arancini are Sicilian rice balls: arborio rice cooked until sticky, wrapped around a filling of beef simmered in tomato sauce and a cube of mozzarella, then rolled in breadcrumbs and deep-fried. The shell is crisp and golden, the rice inside soft, and the cheese melts into strings when you bite in. They are sold warm as street food and served as a snack or starter.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g arborio rice
  • 1.1 l water and a pinch of saffron
  • 250 g ground beef
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 250 ml tomato sauce
  • 150 g mozzarella, cut into small cubes
  • 150 g breadcrumbs, plus a batter of 100 g flour and 150 ml water
  • Oil for deep-frying, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Cook the rice in salted water with the saffron until all the water is absorbed and the rice is sticky, about 18 minutes. Spread on a tray and cool completely.
  2. Fry the onion in a little oil, add the beef and brown it, then stir in the tomato sauce and simmer 20 minutes until thick. Season and cool.
  3. With wet hands, flatten a handful of rice in your palm, add a spoonful of the beef and a cube of mozzarella, and close the rice around it into a ball.
  4. Dip each ball in the flour-and-water batter, then roll in breadcrumbs to coat evenly.
  5. Deep-fry in oil at 175 °C for 4–5 minutes until deep golden. Drain on paper and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • One large arancino of about 180 g is roughly 470 kcal — a full snack or a light meal on its own.
  • Bake instead of frying: spray the coated balls with oil and bake at 220 °C for 20–25 minutes until golden.
  • Make them smaller, about 60 g each, and pair two with a big green salad.

History

Arancini take their name from the Italian for little oranges, because of their round shape and golden colour. Their origin is often linked to the period of Arab rule in Sicily in the 9th–11th centuries, when rice and saffron were brought to the island, though the fried, breaded version is thought to be much later. In Palermo they are traditionally eaten on 13 December, the feast of Saint Lucy.
