🍲 Kare-kare

Kare-kare is a Filipino stew of slow-braised oxtail in a thick, golden peanut sauce with eggplant, banana blossom and bok choy. The sauce is mild and nutty, coloured with annatto, and deliberately under-seasoned: each bite is eaten with a dab of salty fermented shrimp paste called bagoong. It is served with steamed rice and is a classic of fiestas and Sunday lunches.

Recipe

6 servings210 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg oxtail, cut into pieces
  • 250 g peanut sauce
  • 1 onion and 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 tbsp annatto seeds soaked in 3 tbsp warm water
  • 2 eggplants, cut into chunks
  • 1 banana blossom, sliced
  • 300 g bok choy
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt
  • Bagoong (shrimp paste) to serve

Method

  1. Simmer the oxtail in salted water for 2½–3 hours until tender; keep 1 litre of the broth.
  2. Fry the onion and garlic in oil, add the strained annatto water and the oxtail.
  3. Stir in the peanut sauce and the broth and simmer for 15 minutes until thick.
  4. Add the banana blossom and eggplant and cook for 8 minutes, then the bok choy for 2 minutes.
  5. Serve with rice and a little bagoong on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the broth and lift off the fat before making the sauce.
  • Load the bowl with extra vegetables and take a smaller piece of oxtail.
  • Use bagoong sparingly — a teaspoon is enough to season a portion.

History

Kare-kare is strongly associated with Pampanga, a province known for its cooking, though several origin stories exist and none is certain. Some link the name to the word curry, but this is debated. Today it is a celebratory dish across the Philippines.
