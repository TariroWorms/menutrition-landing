🥗 Duck confit salad

Origin: France · Southwest (Gascony)

Duck confit salad is a hearty French bistro salad of slightly bitter frisée lettuce topped with warm, crisp-skinned shredded duck confit, toasted walnuts and a soft-cooked egg, all dressed with a sharp vinaigrette. The rich duck and creamy yolk contrast with the peppery leaves and crunchy nuts, making it a full meal on a plate.

What it is made of

duck confitfrisée lettucewalnutsvinaigretteegg

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 2 duck confit legs
  • 1 head frisée lettuce, torn
  • 40 g walnuts
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp red vinegar
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Warm the duck legs skin-side down in a pan until crisp, then shred the meat and discard the bones.
  2. Toast the walnuts in a dry pan for 2–3 minutes.
  3. Poach or soft-boil the eggs for 6 minutes.
  4. Whisk the oil, vinegar, salt and pepper into a vinaigrette and toss with the frisée.
  5. Top the leaves with the duck, walnuts and eggs and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the duck skin and use only the meat.
  • Halve the walnuts: 30 g walnuts ≈ 200 kcal.
  • Use 1 tbsp oil in the dressing and add more vinegar or lemon juice for sharpness.

History

Duck confit, duck legs slowly cooked and preserved in their own fat, is a traditional product of southwestern France, especially Gascony. Salads built around it, often with walnuts from the nearby Périgord, are a staple of bistros in the region and across France.
