🫓 Lahm bi ajeen (Levantine meat flatbread)

Origin: Levant · Syria and Lebanon

Lahm bi ajeen, literally 'meat with dough', is a thin, open flatbread spread with minced lamb, tomato and onion, sharpened with pomegranate molasses and often scattered with pine nuts. Baked in a very hot oven, the base turns crisp while the topping stays juicy. It is eaten hot from bakeries, rolled up or folded, often with a squeeze of lemon.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g bread dough (flour, yeast, water, salt)
  • 300 g minced lamb
  • 2 tomatoes, finely chopped and drained
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp pomegranate molasses
  • 1 tsp seven spices, salt
  • 2 tbsp pine nuts
  • Lemon wedges to serve

Method

  1. Mix the lamb, tomato, onion, pomegranate molasses, spices and salt.
  2. Divide the dough into 8 balls and roll each into a thin round.
  3. Spread a thin layer of the meat mixture to the edges and scatter with pine nuts.
  4. Bake on a hot tray at 250 °C for 8–10 minutes until the edges are crisp.
  5. Serve hot with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced lamb or a lamb-and-vegetable mix.
  • Skip the pine nuts or use just a few for garnish.
  • Two pieces with a big salad make a balanced meal.

History

Meat-topped flatbreads are baked across Syria, Lebanon, Turkey and Armenia, with the Turkish lahmacun as a close relative. In Syria and Lebanon the topping is often sharpened with pomegranate molasses.
