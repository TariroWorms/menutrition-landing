🍜 Desi chicken chow mein

Desi chow mein is the South Asian street-food take on Chinese stir-fried noodles. Wheat noodles are tossed in a hot wok with strips of chicken, shredded cabbage and carrot, scrambled egg, soy sauce and a generous splash of chili sauce. The result is slick, chewy and noticeably spicier than its Chinese ancestor, served straight from the wok in a plate or takeaway box.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g dried wheat noodles
  • 300 g chicken breast, cut into thin strips
  • 200 g cabbage, shredded
  • 1 carrot, cut into thin sticks
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp chili sauce
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Boil the noodles until just tender, drain, rinse with cold water and toss with a few drops of oil.
  2. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a wok and scramble the eggs, then set them aside.
  3. Add the remaining oil and stir-fry the chicken with the garlic over high heat for 4–5 minutes.
  4. Add the cabbage and carrot and toss for 2 minutes so they stay crisp.
  5. Add the noodles, soy sauce, chili sauce and egg, toss everything together for 2 minutes, season and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the noodles and double the cabbage and carrot for a bigger, lighter plate.
  • Measure the oil: 1 tbsp is about 120 kcal, and a non-stick wok needs very little.
  • Ask for extra chicken instead of extra noodles when ordering from a stall.

History

The dish grew out of Indo-Chinese cooking, which Chinese communities in Kolkata helped shape in the twentieth century. From there chow mein spread to street stalls, wedding buffets and food courts across India and Pakistan, picking up local heat along the way.
