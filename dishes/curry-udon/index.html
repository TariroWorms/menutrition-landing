Curry udon is a bowl of thick, chewy wheat noodles in a glossy Japanese curry broth with thin slices of pork and fresh scallion. The curry is mild, slightly sweet and thick enough to cling to every noodle. It is a warming everyday dish served in noodle shops and made at home with leftover curry.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 2 portions udon noodles (about 400 g cooked)
  • 150 g thinly sliced pork
  • 50 g Japanese curry roux
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 700 ml water
  • 2 scallions, sliced
  • 1 tsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Fry the onion in the oil for 3 minutes, add the pork and cook until no longer pink.
  2. Pour in the water, bring to a simmer and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Turn off the heat, dissolve the curry roux, then simmer for 3–4 minutes until thickened.
  4. Cook the udon according to the packet, drain and divide between bowls.
  5. Ladle the curry broth over the noodles and top with scallions.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork loin and skim any fat from the surface of the broth.
  • Use a little less roux and more water for a thinner, lighter broth.
  • Add extra onion or other vegetables to the broth so a smaller portion of noodles still fills the bowl.

History

Curry reached Japan from Britain in the late 19th century and became a national comfort food. Curry udon, which pairs it with thick wheat noodles, is now a standard of Japanese noodle shops and home cooking.
