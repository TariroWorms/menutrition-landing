Socată (Romanian elderflower drink)

Origin: Romania

Socată is a pale, aromatic summer drink made by steeping elderflower heads in sweetened water with lemon and letting it ferment for two or three days. It turns lightly sparkling, tangy and floral. Served ice cold, it is a favourite village refreshment during harvest and is sold in bottles in summer.

What it is made of

elderflowerlemonsugarwateryeast

Recipe

8 servings4320 min

Ingredients

  • 6 elderflower heads, shaken to remove insects
  • 2 litres water
  • 150 g sugar
  • 2 lemons, sliced
  • 1 pinch of dry yeast

Method

  1. Dissolve the sugar in the water.
  2. Add the elderflowers, lemon slices and a pinch of yeast to a clean glass jar.
  3. Pour the sugar water over and cover with a cloth.
  4. Leave at room temperature for 2 to 3 days, stirring daily, until slightly bubbly.
  5. Strain, bottle and refrigerate; drink within a week.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the sugar by a third for a lighter drink; fermentation will be slower.
  • Dilute with sparkling water to reduce sugar per glass.
  • Refrigerate once fizzy to stop fermentation and keep alcohol negligible.

History

Elderflower grows wild across Romania and has long been used for syrups, teas and fermented drinks. Socată is a traditional home fermentation, close to elderflower cordials found throughout Central Europe. Only brief fermentation is used, so the alcohol content remains negligible.
