🥣 Jareesh with lamb

Origin: Saudi Arabia · Najd

Jareesh with lamb is the richer version of the classic Arabian porridge: crushed wheat cooked slowly with tender lamb, cumin and dried black lime until thick and creamy, then finished with yogurt. A topping of onions fried in butter adds sweetness and crunch. It is hearty, tangy and warming, and is served hot from a shared dish at family gatherings and during Ramadan.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g jareesh (crushed wheat), rinsed and soaked for 1 hour
  • 500 g lamb shoulder, cut into pieces
  • 1 dried black lime, pierced
  • 1.5 litres water
  • 200 g plain yogurt
  • 1 onion, finely sliced, and 20 g butter
  • 1 tsp cumin, salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Simmer the lamb with the black lime, salt and water for about 1 hour until tender. Remove the lamb, shred it and keep the broth.
  2. Add the jareesh to the broth and cook on low heat for 45 minutes, stirring often, until thick.
  3. Stir in the lamb, yogurt and cumin and cook for 10 more minutes, loosening with hot water if needed.
  4. Fry the onion in the butter until deep golden.
  5. Serve the jareesh hot, topped with the onions.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean lamb and skim the fat from the broth after simmering.
  • Keep the butter for the onion topping small, or use more onion and less butter.
  • Serve a medium bowl with a salad of tomato and cucumber.

History

Jareesh has been cooked in central Arabia for generations and is strongly linked with Najd. Versions made with lamb are common across Saudi Arabia and the Gulf, where black lime is a typical seasoning.
