Chipsi mayai (Tanzanian chips omelette)

Origin: Tanzania

Chipsi mayai literally means chips and eggs: freshly fried potato fingers are poured into beaten eggs and cooked into a thick omelette. It is crisp in places, soft in the middle and very filling. Vendors serve it with kachumbari and a squeeze of chilli sauce or ketchup.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g potatoes, cut into fries
  • 5 eggs
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 1 tomato, diced
  • oil for frying
  • salt and pepper

Method

  1. Fry the potato fingers in hot oil until golden and drain.
  2. Beat the eggs with onion, tomato, salt and pepper.
  3. Heat a little oil in a wide pan and add the fries.
  4. Pour the egg over the fries and cook on low heat until set.
  5. Flip carefully, cook briefly and serve with kachumbari.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the potato fingers instead of deep-frying them.
  • Add chopped spinach or peppers to the eggs.
  • Serve with a big fresh salad to balance the meal.

History

The dish is a product of Tanzania's urban street-food culture, where cheap potatoes and eggs make a fast, filling meal. It spread from Dar es Salaam to cities across East Africa.
