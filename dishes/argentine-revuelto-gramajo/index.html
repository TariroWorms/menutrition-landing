Revuelto Gramajo (egg, ham and potato scramble)

Origin: Argentina

Revuelto Gramajo combines very thin fried potato sticks with soft scrambled eggs, strips of ham and sautéed onion, often with peas. The potatoes stay partly crisp while soaking up the creamy egg. It is a fast, hearty plate found in classic neighborhood restaurants and late-night kitchens across Buenos Aires.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g potatoes, cut into very thin sticks
  • Oil for frying
  • 4 eggs
  • 100 g cooked ham, in strips
  • 1 onion (120 g), sliced
  • 80 g green peas
  • 10 g butter

Method

  1. Rinse and dry the potato sticks and fry them in hot oil until golden and crisp; drain and salt.
  2. In a wide pan melt the butter and cook the onion until soft.
  3. Add the ham and peas and warm through.
  4. Pour in the beaten eggs and stir gently over low heat.
  5. When the eggs are almost set, fold in the fried potatoes.
  6. Serve immediately while the potatoes are still a little crunchy.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the potato sticks in the oven with a spray of oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Use half the yolks and add extra egg whites.
  • Add more peas and onion to stretch the portion with vegetables.

History

The dish is named after a man called Gramajo, said to have been an army officer or a socialite of early twentieth-century Buenos Aires, though accounts differ. It became a fixture on bodegón menus.
