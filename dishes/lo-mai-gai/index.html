🍙 Lo mai gai (sticky rice in lotus leaf)

Origin: China · Guangdong

Lo mai gai is a Cantonese dim sum dish of glutinous rice wrapped in a lotus leaf around a filling of chicken, shiitake mushrooms and sweet Chinese sausage, then steamed. The leaf gives the rice a subtle, earthy fragrance, and the rice turns sticky and savoury from the filling's juices. It is unwrapped at the table and eaten hot.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g glutinous rice, soaked 4 hours
  • 300 g chicken thigh, diced
  • 6 dried shiitake mushrooms, soaked and sliced
  • 2 Chinese sausages, sliced
  • 2 dried lotus leaves, soaked
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt, white pepper, ½ tsp five-spice

Method

  1. Drain the rice and steam it for 25 minutes; season with salt.
  2. Stir-fry the chicken, mushrooms and sausage in the oil for 5 minutes with salt, pepper, five-spice and a little mushroom soaking water.
  3. Cut each lotus leaf in half. Place a layer of rice, then filling, then more rice, and fold into tight parcels.
  4. Steam the parcels for 30 minutes and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • One parcel is a full meal in a dim sum spread — share it if ordering other dishes.
  • Use less sausage and more chicken and mushrooms for a leaner filling.
  • Serve with steamed greens to balance the starchy rice.

History

Lo mai gai is a staple of Cantonese dim sum, served in teahouses in Guangzhou and Hong Kong. Smaller versions wrapped in a single leaf are also sold as a snack in Chinese bakeries and dim sum restaurants abroad.
