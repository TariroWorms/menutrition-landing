🍕 Pizza napoletana (anchovy and caper)

Outside Naples, “pizza napoletana” on Italian menus usually means a pizza topped with tomato, mozzarella, salted anchovies and capers. The anchovies melt into the tomato and give a deep, salty flavour, while the capers add sharp, briny bursts against the soft cheese. It is baked fast in a very hot oven and eaten whole.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g ready pizza dough (or homemade)
  • 200 g crushed tomatoes
  • 125 g mozzarella, torn
  • 6–8 salted anchovy fillets
  • 1 tbsp capers, rinsed
  • 1 tbsp olive oil

Method

  1. Preheat the oven with a stone or tray inside for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Stretch the dough by hand into a 30 cm round, leaving a slightly thicker rim.
  3. Spread the tomato thinly and scatter the mozzarella.
  4. Lay the anchovies on top and add the capers.
  5. Bake on the hottest setting of the oven (250–280 °C) on a preheated stone or tray for 7–10 minutes until the crust is blistered.
  6. Drizzle with the oil and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less mozzarella and spread it thinly — cheese is the most calorie-dense topping.
  • Rinse the anchovies and capers to cut the salt; the flavour stays strong.
  • Eat half a pizza with a large green salad instead of a whole pizza alone.

History

In much of Italy, and especially in Rome, this anchovy pizza is called napoletana, while in Naples a similar topping is often called romana — a well-known naming muddle. Anchovies and capers were cheap pantry staples in southern Italy, which made them a natural pizza topping. Today it is a fixture on pizzeria menus across the country.
