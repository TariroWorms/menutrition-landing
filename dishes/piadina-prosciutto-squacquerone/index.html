🥙 Piadina with Prosciutto and Squacquerone

Origin: Italy · Emilia-Romagna

This is the most classic filled piadina of Romagna: a warm, thin flatbread folded around slices of prosciutto crudo, a generous spread of squacquerone — a soft, tangy, spreadable fresh cheese from the region — and a handful of peppery arugula. The bread is griddled until spotted and pliable, so the cheese softens slightly against the salty ham.

Recipe

2 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 2 piadine (ready-made flatbreads)
  • 80 g prosciutto crudo, thinly sliced
  • 100 g squacquerone
  • 40 g arugula

Method

  1. Heat a dry griddle or heavy pan over medium-high heat.
  2. Warm each piadina for about 1 minute per side until hot and lightly spotted.
  3. Spread half of each with squacquerone, add prosciutto and arugula.
  4. Fold in half and serve immediately while warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less squacquerone and more arugula for volume.
  • Trim the visible fat from the prosciutto slices.
  • Share one piadina and add a side salad to make a full meal.

History

Piadina is the traditional flatbread of Romagna, and squacquerone di Romagna is a local fresh cheese now protected as a PDO product. The prosciutto, squacquerone and arugula combination is the standard offering of the roadside piadina kiosks along the Adriatic coast around Rimini and Cesena.
