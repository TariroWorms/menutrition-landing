Asado con cuero (beef roasted in its hide)

Origin: Argentina

Asado con cuero is a festive way of cooking a large section of beef, or even a whole young animal, with the hide still attached. The hide side faces away from the heat and works like a natural oven bag, so the meat steams in its own juices while the open side browns over the fire. After many hours the meat is extremely tender and juicy, and it is carved straight from the hide. It is a dish for big gatherings, not for an everyday home grill.

What it is made of

beefcoarse saltgarlicchimichurri

Recipe

30 servings600 min

Ingredients

  • 1 large section of beef with hide (about 25 kg), cleaned
  • 1 l brine of water, coarse salt and garlic
  • Plenty of hardwood for the fire
  • Chimichurri to serve

Method

  1. Clean and singe the hide well, then fix the meat to a large iron frame or cross.
  2. Build a big wood fire and set the frame beside it, meat side facing the heat.
  3. Roast for 6–8 hours, basting with brine and keeping the fire steady.
  4. Turn briefly hide side toward the fire at the end to dry and firm the edges.
  5. Cut the meat in thick slices straight from the hide and serve with chimichurri.

How to make it lighter

  • Ask for leaner pieces from the leg and avoid fatty brisket.
  • Fill up on salads and grilled vegetables offered at the feast.
  • Keep chimichurri to a spoonful, as it is mostly oil.

History

The technique goes back to the rural Pampas, where gauchos had whole animals and open fires at hand. Today it is mostly prepared at country festivals, estancias and large celebrations, often by specialist asadores.
