Hungarian goulash (gulyás) is a hearty soup-stew of beef chunks simmered with onions and plenty of sweet paprika, together with tomato, bell pepper, potatoes and caraway. The broth is deep red, warming and fragrant rather than fiery. It is served in bowls as a main course.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g beef shin or chuck, cubed
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2 tbsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp caraway seeds
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 bell peppers, sliced
  • 400 g potatoes, cubed
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt, 1.2 l water

Method

  1. Soften the onions in the oil over medium heat for 10 minutes.
  2. Take the pot off the heat, stir in the paprika and caraway, then add the beef and stir to coat.
  3. Return to the heat, add a little water, cover and simmer gently for about 1 hour, adding water as needed.
  4. Add the tomatoes, peppers, potatoes and the rest of the water and salt; simmer 30 minutes more until everything is tender.
  5. Adjust the seasoning and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the beef before cooking.
  • Use 1 tbsp of oil instead of 2; 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve it on its own — the potatoes already make it a complete meal.

History

Goulash takes its name from the gulyás, the cattle herdsmen of the Hungarian plains, who cooked meat stews in kettles over open fires. Paprika became central to the dish after it spread in Hungarian cooking in the 18th and 19th centuries, and goulash came to be seen as a national symbol.
