🍢 Lamb kebab

A kebab in the Turkish style is seasoned lamb grilled over charcoal until charred at the edges and juicy inside. Cubes or minced lamb are mixed with onion, cumin and parsley, threaded onto skewers and cooked over high heat. It is served with warm pita, a spoon of cool yogurt and fresh herbs, and often grilled tomatoes and peppers.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g lamb shoulder, cubed or minced
  • 1 onion, grated
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • A handful of parsley, chopped
  • 1 tsp chili flakes, salt, pepper
  • 4 pita breads
  • 200 g yogurt
  • Tomatoes and green peppers for grilling

Method

  1. Mix the lamb with the grated onion, cumin, chili, salt and pepper; rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Thread the meat onto skewers (press minced meat firmly around flat skewers).
  3. Grill over hot charcoal or under a grill for 8–10 minutes, turning, until charred outside and just cooked inside.
  4. Warm the pita on the grill, then serve the kebabs on it with yogurt, parsley and the grilled vegetables.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean lamb leg rather than fatty shoulder or minced lamb.
  • Have one pita and fill the rest of the plate with grilled vegetables and salad.
  • Use plain low-fat yogurt as the sauce.

History

Grilling meat on skewers has been practised across Anatolia, Persia and the Middle East for many centuries, and the word kebab is found in Ottoman and Persian cookbooks. Many regional styles developed in Turkey, such as Adana and Urfa kebabs named after their cities. Today kebabs are among the most widely eaten grilled dishes from the Balkans to Central Asia.
