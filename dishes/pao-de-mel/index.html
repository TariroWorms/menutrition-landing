Pão de mel (Brazilian honey spice cake)

Origin: Brazil

Pão de mel are small round cakes made with honey, cocoa and warm spices such as cinnamon and clove. Each one is split, filled with doce de leite and dipped in chocolate, which sets into a thin snappy shell. They are sold individually wrapped in bakeries and sweet shops and are a common gift at Easter and Christmas.

Recipe

12 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g flour
  • 150 g honey
  • 100 g brown sugar
  • 200 ml milk
  • 1 egg
  • 20 g cocoa powder
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp ground cloves
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 200 g doce de leite
  • 300 g dark chocolate

Method

  1. Warm the milk with the honey and sugar until dissolved, then cool slightly.
  2. Whisk in the egg, then add the flour, cocoa, spices and baking soda.
  3. Pour into small greased moulds or a muffin tin.
  4. Bake at 180 °C for 15 to 18 minutes and cool completely.
  5. Split each cake horizontally and fill with doce de leite.
  6. Melt the chocolate and dip each cake to coat, then let set on baking paper.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a thinner chocolate coat or drizzle only the top.
  • Make mini cakes to keep each portion small.
  • Swap part of the flour for rye or wholemeal flour for a deeper flavour and more fibre.

History

Honey spice breads have long European roots, and the Brazilian version grew from that tradition. The filled, chocolate-coated cake became a staple of Brazilian confectioners and small home businesses, many of which sell them by the dozen.
