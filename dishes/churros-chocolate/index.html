Churros con chocolate pairs freshly fried churros — sticks of flour-and-water dough piped into hot oil and dusted with sugar — with a cup of thick, almost pudding-like hot chocolate for dipping. The churros are crisp and light, the chocolate dark and velvety. It is a classic Spanish breakfast, afternoon treat and late-night snack, served in churrerías and cafés.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • For the churros: 250 ml water, 150 g plain flour, a pinch of salt
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • 2 tbsp sugar for dusting
  • For the chocolate: 500 ml milk
  • 120 g dark chocolate, chopped
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp cornflour

Method

  1. Boil the water with the salt, tip in the flour and stir hard until a smooth, stiff dough forms. Cool slightly.
  2. Heat the oil to 180 °C. Pipe lengths of dough through a star nozzle into the oil and fry for 2–3 minutes until golden; drain and dust with sugar.
  3. For the chocolate, whisk the cornflour into a little cold milk. Heat the rest of the milk with the sugar and chocolate until melted.
  4. Add the cornflour mixture and simmer, whisking, for 2–3 minutes until thick.
  5. Serve the chocolate in cups with the warm churros for dipping.

How to make it lighter

  • Share a portion: 3 churros and half a cup of chocolate is a satisfying treat.
  • Make the chocolate with semi-skimmed milk and 80 g chocolate instead of 120 g.
  • Skip the sugar dusting on the churros — the chocolate is sweet enough.

History

Drinking chocolate arrived in Spain from Mexico in the 16th century, and by the 19th century dipping fried dough into thick chocolate had become a Madrid tradition. Today churros con chocolate are eaten all over Spain and much of Latin America.
