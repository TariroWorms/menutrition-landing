Num ansom (Cambodian banana-leaf sticky rice cake)

Origin: Cambodia

Sticky rice is wrapped in banana leaves around a filling of pork belly and mung bean, or around banana for a sweet version, then steamed for hours. The cakes are soft, slightly sweet and fragrant with banana leaf. They are a key food at Khmer New Year and Pchum Ben.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g glutinous rice, soaked
  • 150 g pork belly
  • 100 g split mung beans
  • 100 ml coconut milk
  • ½ tsp salt
  • banana leaves and string

Method

  1. Season the soaked rice with coconut milk and salt.
  2. Cook the mung beans until soft and mash.
  3. Spread rice on a banana leaf, add mung bean and pork, and top with more rice.
  4. Roll tightly into a cylinder and tie.
  5. Steam for 3 hours.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork or banana for a lighter filling.
  • Eat one with vegetables or soup instead of several.
  • Cut the coconut milk with water.

History

Num ansom is made for Khmer New Year and for the Pchum Ben festival. Families often gather to wrap and cook many at a time, and the cakes are exchanged and offered at pagodas.
