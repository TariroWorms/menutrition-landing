🍖 Lamb kabsa

Lamb kabsa is the festive form of Saudi Arabia's signature rice dish: pieces of lamb simmered with onion, tomato, baharat and loomi (dried lime), then long-grain rice cooked in the same rich broth. The rice turns fragrant and reddish-gold and the lamb falls off the bone. It is served heaped on a large platter with the meat on top, often with salad and daqqus.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg lamb on the bone (shoulder or leg), in pieces
  • 500 g basmati rice
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, grated, and 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tsp baharat
  • 2 loomi (dried limes), pierced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt
  • About 1.8 litres water

Method

  1. Brown the onion in the oil, add the lamb and baharat and sear 5 minutes.
  2. Add the tomatoes, paste, loomi, salt and water; cover and simmer 1½ hours until the lamb is tender.
  3. Soak the rice 30 minutes. Lift out the lamb and keep about 900 ml of broth in the pot.
  4. Add the rice, bring to the boil, cover and cook on low heat 20–25 minutes.
  5. Optionally roast the lamb at 220 °C for 10 minutes to brown, then serve on the rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb and skim the broth before adding the rice.
  • Choose leg rather than shoulder for a leaner cut.
  • Half the usual rice plus a big salad keeps the plate lighter.

History

Kabsa comes from the Arabian Peninsula, where rice arrived through Indian Ocean trade and became central to both Bedouin and town cooking. Lamb kabsa is a traditional dish for weddings, Eid and large family gatherings across Saudi Arabia and the Gulf.
