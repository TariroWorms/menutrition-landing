Cognac is a brandy distilled from white grapes and aged in oak casks, named after the town of Cognac in western France. It is warm, smooth and aromatic, with notes of dried fruit, vanilla and oak that deepen with age. It is usually served neat in a small tulip or balloon glass after dinner, a standard measure being about 40–50 ml.

Recipe

1 servings2 min

Ingredients

  • 50 ml cognac
  • A tulip or small balloon glass
  • A glass of still water to serve alongside

Method

  1. Pour the cognac into the glass at room temperature.
  2. Hold the glass by the bowl for a moment to warm it slightly.
  3. Let it rest a minute, then sip slowly.

How to make it lighter

  • A 50 ml glass of cognac is about 120 kcal, almost all from alcohol.
  • Stick to one measure and sip it slowly with a glass of water alongside.
  • Use a 25–30 ml pour for a lighter after-dinner drink.

History

Cognac can only be made in a legally defined region around the town of Cognac, distilled twice in copper pot stills and aged in oak. Dutch and English merchants helped build its trade from the 17th century, and age labels such as VS, VSOP and XO indicate the minimum time in cask.
