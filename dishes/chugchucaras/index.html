Chugchucaras (fried pork platter)

Origin: Ecuador · Latacunga
1192kcal

Chugchucaras is a generous combination plate built around fritada, pork cooked in its own fat until browned, and crisp chicharrón. Around it come boiled hominy, toasted corn, fried ripe plantain, small potatoes, popcorn and cheese empanadas. It is rich and salty-sweet, meant for sharing or for a very hungry person, and is usually eaten at lunchtime.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork shoulder, in chunks, plus 200 g pork skin
  • 4 garlic cloves, 1 tsp cumin, salt
  • 400 g cooked hominy
  • 2 ripe plantains, sliced
  • 8 small potatoes, boiled
  • 100 g toasted corn kernels, popcorn to serve
  • 4 small cheese empanadas
  • Lard or oil for frying

Method

  1. Rub the pork with garlic, cumin and salt and leave 30 minutes.
  2. Simmer the pork in a pot with a cup of water until the water evaporates, then let it fry in its own fat until golden.
  3. Fry the pork skin separately until puffed and crisp.
  4. Fry the plantain slices and the boiled potatoes until browned; fry the empanadas.
  5. Arrange pork, crackling, hominy, potatoes, plantain, toasted corn, popcorn and empanadas on each plate.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the crackling and use lean pork leg to cut fat considerably.
  • Bake the plantain and potatoes instead of frying them.
  • Share one platter between two and add a tomato-onion salad.

History

The dish is closely tied to the city of Latacunga in Cotopaxi province, where many restaurants specialize in it. Its name is commonly said to come from Kichwa words, and it brings together Andean corn and Spanish-introduced pork.
