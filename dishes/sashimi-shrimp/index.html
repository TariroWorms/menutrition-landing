🍤 Shrimp sashimi (ebi)

Origin: Japan

Ebi sashimi is a Japanese plate of peeled shrimp, here briefly cooked so the flesh turns pink, sweet and tender. The shrimp are laid out on a bed of finely shredded daikon radish with fragrant shiso leaves. It is served chilled, often as part of a mixed sashimi platter, with a little wasabi on the side.

What it is made of

shrimpdaikon radishshiso leaves

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g sashimi-grade shrimp, peeled
  • 150 g daikon radish
  • 8 shiso leaves
  • 1 tsp grated wasabi
  • Lemon wedges to serve

Method

  1. Peel the daikon, cut it into very fine threads and soak them in iced water for 10 minutes, then drain well.
  2. Drop the shrimp into simmering salted water for 1–2 minutes until just pink, chill in iced water, pat dry and halve lengthwise if large.
  3. Arrange a mound of daikon on a chilled plate, lean the shiso leaves against it and lay the slices over them.
  4. Serve at once with wasabi and lemon on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Sashimi itself is light; most extra calories come from what is eaten alongside, such as rice or rich dipping sauces.
  • Fill up on the daikon and shiso garnish — they add volume and crunch for very few calories.
  • A squeeze of lemon and a little wasabi add flavour without adding fat or sugar.

History

Sashimi developed in Japan, where serving fresh seafood in thin slices has a long history; the modern style, eaten raw with wasabi, spread widely from the Edo period onward. Shrimp is one of the classic items on a mixed sashimi plate in Japan today.
