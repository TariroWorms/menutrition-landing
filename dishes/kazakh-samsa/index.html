Kazakh samsa (baked lamb pastry)

Origin: Kazakhstan

Samsa are triangular or round pastries made from layered dough and filled with diced lamb, plenty of onion, cumin and a little tail fat. Traditionally they are slapped onto the walls of a clay tandoor; at home they are baked in a hot oven. The crust is crisp and flaky and the inside juicy, and they are sold hot at bazaars and eaten as a snack with tea.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 100 g butter, softened
  • 500 g lamb, finely diced
  • 3 onions (350 g), chopped
  • 50 g lamb tail fat, diced
  • 1 tsp cumin, salt, black pepper
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 1 tbsp sesame seeds

Method

  1. Knead flour, water and salt into a firm dough and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Roll it into a thin sheet, spread with soft butter, roll up tightly and chill 30 minutes.
  3. Mix the lamb, onion, fat, cumin, salt and pepper.
  4. Cut the roll into pieces, flatten each into a round and fill with a spoonful of filling.
  5. Fold into triangles, pinch the seams and place seam side down on a tray.
  6. Brush with egg yolk, sprinkle with sesame and bake at 210 °C for 30–35 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Leave out the tail fat and use lean lamb or beef.
  • Add pumpkin or onion to stretch the filling with fewer calories.
  • Eat one samsa with a salad rather than several as a meal.

History

Samsa is shared across Central Asia and is related to the samosa family of filled pastries. In Kazakhstan it is a common street and bazaar food, often linked with Uzbek and Uyghur bakers who run tandoor ovens.
