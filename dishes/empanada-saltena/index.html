Empanada salteña

Origin: Argentina · Salta

Empanada salteña is a small, plump turnover from Salta in northwestern Argentina, sealed with a decorative braided edge. Inside is diced beef with onion, spring onion, paprika and cumin, plus tiny cubes of potato and boiled egg. It is famously juicy, so locals eat it leaning forward to catch the broth.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 24 empanada discs (about 700 g)
  • 500 g beef, finely diced
  • 2 onions and 4 spring onions, chopped
  • 150 g potato, in tiny cubes and parboiled
  • 2 hard-boiled eggs, chopped
  • 1 tbsp sweet paprika, 1 tsp cumin
  • 30 g beef fat or oil and 100 ml broth

Method

  1. Fry the onions in the fat until soft.
  2. Add the beef, paprika, cumin and salt, cook 5 minutes and add the broth.
  3. Stir in the potato and spring onion, cool completely.
  4. Place filling and a bit of egg on each disc.
  5. Fold, seal and braid the edge (repulgue).
  6. Bake at 230 °C for 12–15 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and a spoon of oil instead of fat.
  • Eat two with a big salad as a meal.
  • Brush with milk rather than egg yolk for a lighter crust.

History

Empanadas are a deep tradition in the Argentine northwest, and Salta is especially proud of its version. Local cooks debate details such as the cut of beef and whether to add potato.
