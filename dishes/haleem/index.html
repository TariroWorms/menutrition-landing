🍲 Haleem

Origin: South Asia · Middle East

Haleem is a thick, slow-cooked stew of wheat, barley and lentils cooked with beef until everything breaks down into a smooth, porridge-like mass. It is spiced with ginger and garam masala and finished with ghee and crisp fried onions. Rich and warming, it is eaten with a spoon, often during Ramadan.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g boneless beef, cubed
  • 100 g cracked wheat, soaked overnight
  • 50 g pearl barley, soaked
  • 100 g mixed lentils, soaked
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2 tsp garam masala
  • 40 g ghee
  • Salt, lemon and coriander to serve

Method

  1. Simmer the beef with half the ginger, salt and water for 1.5 hours until very tender.
  2. Cook the wheat, barley and lentils in water for about 1 hour until soft.
  3. Combine the grains and meat with some cooking liquid and garam masala and simmer 1 hour, stirring and mashing until smooth and stringy.
  4. Fry the onions in the ghee until deep golden.
  5. Serve topped with fried onions, ginger, lemon and coriander.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and skim fat from the cooking liquid.
  • Fry the onions in 1 tbsp ghee and use them as a garnish only; 1 tbsp ghee ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Add extra lemon and coriander for flavor instead of extra ghee.

History

Haleem descends from harees, a wheat-and-meat dish described in medieval Arabic cookbooks, which travelled to South Asia. It is especially associated with Hyderabad in India and is widely eaten in Pakistan, India and Bangladesh, particularly during Ramadan and Muharram.
