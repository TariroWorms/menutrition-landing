Khanum (steamed vegetable roll)

Origin: Uzbekistan

Khanum, also called khoshan in some homes, is a large steamed roll made from thin unleavened dough. The sheet is spread with grated pumpkin or diced potato mixed with onion, rolled up like a strudel and coiled into a steamer. Once cooked, it is cut into thick slices that show the spiral of filling. It is served with sour cream, yogurt or a tomato sauce.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 150 ml water
  • 600 g pumpkin
  • 2 onions
  • 40 g butter
  • Salt, black pepper
  • Sour cream to serve

Method

  1. Knead flour, water and salt into a stiff dough; rest 30 minutes.
  2. Grate or finely dice the pumpkin, mix with chopped onion, salt and pepper.
  3. Roll the dough very thin, brush with melted butter and spread the filling evenly.
  4. Roll into a log, coil it onto an oiled steamer tray and steam 40 minutes.
  5. Slice and serve with sour cream or yogurt.

How to make it lighter

  • Use yogurt instead of sour cream.
  • Add grated carrot to the pumpkin for more vegetables.
  • Skip the butter on the dough; the steam keeps it soft.

History

Khanum is cooked in the same multi-tiered steamer used for manti and is a common family dish in Uzbekistan. Meat-filled versions exist too, but pumpkin khanum is especially popular in autumn.
