🥟 Empanada de pino

The empanada de pino is the best-known Chilean empanada: a baked pastry filled with “pino”, a mix of minced beef and plenty of onion, with a piece of hard-boiled egg and an olive. It is large, savoury and slightly sweet-spiced. It is the classic dish of Chilean national holidays.

Recipe

10 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 100 g butter or lard, melted
  • about 150 ml warm water
  • 1 egg for the dough
  • 500 g minced beef
  • 700 g onions, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cumin and 1 tsp paprika
  • 3 hard-boiled eggs, quartered
  • 10 black olives, 1 egg for brushing

Method

  1. Make a smooth dough from flour, salt, butter, egg and water; rest 30 minutes.
  2. Cook the onions slowly in a little oil for 15 minutes until very soft, then add the beef, cumin, paprika and salt and cook until the meat is done.
  3. Cool the pino completely.
  4. Roll out the dough into circles of about 15 cm.
  5. Fill each with a spoonful of pino, a piece of egg and an olive, fold over and seal the edges.
  6. Brush with beaten egg and bake at 200 °C for 25–30 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use extra-lean mince and cook the onion with a little water instead of oil; the filling stays juicy.
  • Increase the onion and reduce the beef a little for more volume and fewer calories.
  • Eat one empanada with a salad, since the pastry brings most of the carbohydrates and fat.

History

The empanada de pino is regarded as the national empanada of Chile and is a staple of national celebrations. Pino is the name of the filling, built around minced beef cooked with a lot of onion and cumin. The pastry is baked, unlike many other fried Chilean empanadas. It is also sold at bakeries and street stalls across the country.
