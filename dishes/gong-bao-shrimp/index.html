🍤 Kung Pao shrimp

Origin: China · Sichuan

Kung Pao (gong bao) shrimp is a Sichuan-style stir-fry of plump shrimp with roasted peanuts, dried chilies, garlic and ginger, finished with a tangy splash of vinegar. It is spicy, slightly sour and crunchy, with the peanuts contrasting the tender shrimp. It is served hot, usually with steamed rice.

Recipe

3 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g shrimp, peeled
  • 60 g roasted peanuts
  • 8 dried red chilies, halved
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tbsp ginger, cut into matchsticks
  • 2 tbsp vinegar
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp oil, salt

Method

  1. Pat the shrimp dry and season lightly with salt.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok and fry the dried chilies for a few seconds until fragrant.
  3. Add the shrimp and stir-fry over high heat for 2 minutes until just pink.
  4. Add the garlic and ginger and stir-fry for 30 seconds.
  5. Pour in the vinegar with the sugar and toss until glossy.
  6. Stir in the peanuts and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Peanuts are calorie-dense — halve the amount and you still keep the crunch.
  • Stir-fry with 1 tbsp oil only; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Bulk up the wok with peppers or celery to make a larger plate.

History

The gong bao style is traditionally linked to Ding Baozhen, a 19th-century governor of Sichuan whose official title was gongbao; the story goes that the dish was named after him. The original is made with chicken, and shrimp is a popular variation in Chinese restaurants worldwide.
