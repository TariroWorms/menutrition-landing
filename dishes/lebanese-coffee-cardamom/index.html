☕ Lebanese coffee with cardamom

Origin: Lebanon · Levant

Lebanese coffee is very finely ground dark coffee boiled with water and ground cardamom in a small long-handled pot called a rakweh. It is poured unfiltered into small cups, so a soft layer of grounds settles at the bottom. The taste is strong, slightly bitter and fragrant with cardamom, and it is served plain or sweetened to order.

What it is made of

coffeecardamomwatersugar

Recipe

2 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 150 ml cold water
  • 2 heaped tsp very finely ground coffee
  • 1/4 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1–2 tsp sugar (optional)

Method

  1. Put the water and sugar, if using, into a rakweh or small pot and bring to a simmer.
  2. Take the pot off the heat, stir in the coffee and cardamom.
  3. Return to low heat and let the coffee rise into a foam; lift it off before it boils over. Repeat twice.
  4. Rest for a minute so the grounds settle, then pour slowly into small cups.

How to make it lighter

  • Unsweetened, a small cup has only a few kcal — the sugar is what adds up.
  • Each teaspoon of sugar adds about 16 kcal; ask for 'medium' or no sugar.
  • Extra cardamom gives more aroma, so less sugar is needed.

History

Coffee boiled in a pot spread across the Levant during Ottoman times, and cardamom became the signature spice of the Lebanese version. Today it is offered to guests in almost every Lebanese home and is part of both everyday visits and condolence gatherings.
