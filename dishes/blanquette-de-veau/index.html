🥘 Blanquette de veau

Blanquette de veau is a classic French stew of veal gently simmered with carrots, onion and herbs, then bathed in a velvety white cream sauce with mushrooms and a squeeze of lemon. The meat is never browned, so it stays pale and very tender. It is traditionally served with rice and is a favourite of French family Sunday lunches.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg veal shoulder or breast, cut into 5 cm pieces
  • 3 carrots, cut into chunks
  • 1 onion studded with 2 cloves, plus 200 g small onions
  • 1 leek top, 1 bay leaf, a few sprigs of thyme and parsley
  • 300 g button mushrooms
  • 250 ml single cream (15–20% fat)
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp oil, salt, white pepper

Method

  1. Put the veal in a pot, cover with cold water, bring to the boil for 2 minutes, then drain and rinse to keep the sauce clear.
  2. Return the veal to the pot with the carrots, clove-studded onion, leek and herbs, cover with about 1.5 litres of water, salt lightly and simmer gently for 1 ½ hours until tender.
  3. Cook the small onions and mushrooms in the oil with a ladle of the veal stock for 10–15 minutes.
  4. Lift out the meat and carrots, strain the stock and boil it until reduced to about 500 ml.
  5. Stir in the cream and simmer until the sauce coats a spoon, then add the lemon juice and season.
  6. Return the veal, carrots, onions and mushrooms to the sauce and warm through before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the veal and skim the stock well — this keeps the sauce lighter without losing flavour.
  • Use single cream and let the reduced stock give the sauce body instead of adding more cream.
  • Pair it with a smaller scoop of rice and steamed green beans or a salad.

History

The name comes from the French word blanc, “white”, describing the pale sauce and unbrowned meat. Blanquette has long been a staple of French home cooking and is often named among the country’s favourite dishes.
