Abacha (African salad)

Origin: Nigeria · Igbo

Abacha is a cold or room-temperature salad of dried, shredded cassava softened in water and mixed with palm oil, potash, ugba (fermented oil bean), crayfish and onion. It is chewy, savory, slightly bitter and spicy. It is served with garden egg leaves, fresh vegetables and sometimes fish or ponmo.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g dried shredded cassava (abacha)
  • 100 ml palm oil
  • 1 tsp potash, dissolved
  • 150 g ugba (oil bean)
  • 2 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 1 onion
  • 2 chili peppers
  • 1 garden egg or tomato
  • utazi leaves

Method

  1. Soak the dried cassava in cold water, then drain and set aside.
  2. Whisk palm oil with dissolved potash until yellow and thick.
  3. Add crayfish, chili and seasoning.
  4. Fold in ugba and cassava.
  5. Mix gently until coated.
  6. Top with sliced onion, garden egg and utazi.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less palm oil.
  • Serve with more fresh vegetables.
  • Share it as a snack since it is rich.

History

Abacha comes from Igbo cooking in south-eastern Nigeria and is often served as a snack or side at gatherings. Its distinctive flavour comes from the fermented oil bean seeds.
