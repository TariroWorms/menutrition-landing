🌴 Rutab (Fresh Dates)

Origin: Arabian Peninsula

Rutab are dates picked at the soft, half-ripe stage between the crunchy yellow khalal and the fully dried tamr. They are juicy and syrupy with a thin, tender skin and a gentle caramel sweetness, lighter in texture than dried dates. In the Gulf they are eaten by the handful in late summer, often with Arabic coffee or to break the fast during Ramadan.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings5 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g fresh rutab dates
  • cold water, for rinsing
  • Arabic coffee or water, to serve

Method

  1. Rinse the dates gently under cold water and pat dry.
  2. Keep them chilled until serving, as rutab ferment quickly at room temperature.
  3. Serve in a bowl alongside coffee or water.

How to make it lighter

  • A few dates go a long way; count them rather than eating straight from the box.
  • Pair them with coffee or water instead of sweet drinks.
  • Remove the pits before serving so the portion is easier to see.

History

The date palm has been cultivated in the Arabian Peninsula and Mesopotamia for thousands of years, and Arabic has separate names for each stage of ripeness. Rutab is the seasonal stage, appearing in markets across Saudi Arabia, the Gulf and Iraq at the height of the summer harvest.
