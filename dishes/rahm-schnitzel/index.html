Rahmschnitzel

Origin: Germany

Rahmschnitzel is a German pork schnitzel served with a creamy sauce; it is often left unbreaded, but in this version thin cutlets are coated in breadcrumbs and fried until crisp. The cream sauce is sharpened with lemon and briny capers, which balance the rich, crunchy meat. It is typically served with fries, spätzle or boiled potatoes.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thin pork cutlets (about 150 g each)
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 100 g breadcrumbs
  • 50 g flour
  • Oil for frying
  • 200 ml cream
  • 1 tbsp capers
  • Juice of ½ lemon, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Pound the cutlets thin and season.
  2. Dredge in flour, dip in egg and coat in breadcrumbs.
  3. Fry in hot oil 2–3 minutes per side until golden; drain on paper towels.
  4. Simmer the cream with capers and lemon juice for 3–4 minutes until slightly thick; season.
  5. Serve the schnitzel with the sauce on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Pan-fry in a thin layer of oil or bake on a rack at 220 °C instead of deep-frying.
  • Serve the sauce on the side and use a spoonful.
  • Pair with salad or vegetables instead of fries.

History

Pork schnitzel is a staple of German home cooking, and versions with cream sauce are common in German restaurants today.
