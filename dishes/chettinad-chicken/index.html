🍛 Chettinad chicken

Origin: India · Tamil Nadu

Chettinad chicken is a fiery South Indian curry in which chicken is braised in a masala of freshly roasted and ground spices — fennel, star anise, black pepper and poppy seeds — with grated coconut. The sauce is thick, dark and aromatic, with heat from pepper and a rounded nutty sweetness from the coconut. It is served with steamed rice or flatbreads.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g chicken pieces
  • 1 tbsp fennel seeds
  • 2 star anise
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns
  • 1 tbsp poppy seeds
  • 60 g grated coconut
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste, 1 tsp turmeric, 4 dried red chillies
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt, curry leaves

Method

  1. Dry-roast fennel, star anise, peppercorns, poppy seeds, chillies and coconut until fragrant; grind to a paste with a little water.
  2. Heat the oil, fry the curry leaves and onions until golden, then add ginger-garlic paste.
  3. Add tomatoes and turmeric and cook until soft.
  4. Stir in the spice paste and fry 2–3 minutes.
  5. Add the chicken, salt and 200 ml water; cover and simmer 25–30 minutes until tender and the sauce is thick.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken pieces to cut fat without changing the flavour.
  • Halve the coconut; the spices carry most of the taste.
  • Serve with a measured cup of rice and a vegetable side rather than several flatbreads.

History

The dish comes from Chettinad, a region of Tamil Nadu home to the Chettiar merchant community, whose cooking is known for its generous use of spices. Chettinad cooks typically dry-roast whole spices before grinding them, and the chicken curry is the region's best-known dish on restaurant menus.
