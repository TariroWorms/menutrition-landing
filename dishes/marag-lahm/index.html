Marag is the everyday lamb stew of the Gulf: bone-in lamb simmered with tomato, onion, potato and zucchini in a broth spiced with baharat and dried lime. The meat turns tender, the vegetables soften and the soupy red sauce is ladled over white rice. Dried lime gives it a gentle sour, slightly smoky note.

Recipe

4 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g lamb on the bone, in chunks
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, grated, and 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 potatoes, cut into chunks
  • 2 zucchini, cut into chunks
  • 2 tsp baharat
  • 2 dried limes, pierced
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, salt
  • 1.2 litres water

Method

  1. Brown the onion in the oil, add the lamb and baharat and sear for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the tomatoes, tomato paste, dried limes, salt and water; simmer covered for 60 minutes.
  3. Add the potatoes and cook 15 minutes, then the zucchini for another 10 minutes.
  4. Adjust the salt and serve the stew over rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the stew and lift off the solidified fat before reheating.
  • Use lean lamb leg instead of fatty cuts and add more zucchini.
  • Measure the rice: the stew itself is light, most calories come from a large rice portion.

History

Marag (also spelled maraq, from the Arabic word for broth) is a staple of home cooking across Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, the Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman, with each family adjusting the vegetables and spice mix.
