Leche frita, 'fried milk', is a thick milk custard flavoured with lemon zest and cinnamon, set in a tray, cut into squares, coated in flour and egg and fried. The outside turns crisp and golden while the inside stays soft and creamy. The squares are rolled in sugar and cinnamon and served warm or at room temperature.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml milk
  • 100 g sugar (70 g for the custard, 30 g for coating)
  • 60 g flour, plus 40 g for coating
  • 2 eggs (1 for coating)
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 1 cinnamon stick and 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Heat the milk with the lemon zest and cinnamon stick, then let it infuse for 10 minutes and remove both.
  2. Whisk 70 g sugar, 60 g flour and 1 egg yolk with a little cold milk, add to the warm milk and cook, stirring, until very thick.
  3. Pour into an oiled dish about 2 cm deep, cool and chill for 3 hours until firm.
  4. Cut into squares, dip in flour, then in beaten egg, and fry in hot oil until golden on both sides.
  5. Drain and roll in the remaining sugar mixed with ground cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Two small squares are a portion; frying adds a lot of the energy.
  • Shallow-fry in a non-stick pan with a thin film of oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Skip the sugar coating and dust with cinnamon only.

History

Leche frita is a traditional Spanish home dessert claimed by several northern regions, including Castile and León, Cantabria and the Basque Country. It is especially associated with Easter and is still common in Spanish homes and old-style restaurants.
