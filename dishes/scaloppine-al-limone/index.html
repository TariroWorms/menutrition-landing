Veal scaloppine with lemon

Origin: Italy · northern Italy

Scaloppine al limone, also called veal piccata, are thin veal escalopes dusted in flour, browned in butter and finished with a bright pan sauce of lemon juice and parsley. The light flour coating thickens the sauce slightly so it clings to the meat. It is a quick weeknight dish served with vegetables, potatoes or bread.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g veal escalopes, pounded thin
  • 40 g plain flour
  • 40 g butter
  • Juice of 1½ lemons, plus lemon slices
  • 100 ml water or light stock
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Season the veal and dust it lightly with flour, shaking off the excess.
  2. Melt half the butter in a large pan and brown the veal for 1–2 minutes per side, then set aside.
  3. Add the lemon juice and water to the pan and scrape up the browned bits.
  4. Whisk in the remaining butter and simmer until slightly thickened.
  5. Return the veal and lemon slices, turn to coat, sprinkle with parsley and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter in the sauce — 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal.
  • Shake off as much flour as possible so the coating stays thin.
  • Serve with a large green salad or steamed green beans instead of fries.

History

Scaloppine — thin escalopes of veal pounded flat and cooked quickly in a pan — are a staple of Italian home and trattoria cooking. They are especially associated with northern and central Italy, though the dish is now served throughout the country and in Italian restaurants abroad.
