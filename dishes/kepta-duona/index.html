Kepta duona (Lithuanian fried rye bread)

Origin: Lithuania

Kepta duona is dark rye bread cut into sticks, fried in oil until crisp and rubbed with raw garlic and salt. It is crunchy outside, chewy inside and full of garlic flavour. It is a classic bar snack served with beer, often with melted cheese or a garlic cheese dip.

What it is made of

breadgarlicoilcheesesalt

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g dark rye bread
  • 80 ml oil for frying
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 100 g grated cheese
  • 60 ml cream

Method

  1. Cut the bread into sticks about 1 cm thick.
  2. Fry in hot oil until crisp on all sides and drain on paper.
  3. Rub immediately with a cut garlic clove and sprinkle with salt.
  4. Melt cheese with cream into a smooth dip.
  5. Serve the sticks warm with the dip.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the sticks on a tray with a spoon of oil instead of frying.
  • Choose a garlic yogurt dip instead of cheese sauce.
  • Watch the salt, especially if served with other salty snacks.

History

It is one of the best-known Lithuanian snacks and a symbol of the country's pub culture. It uses dark rye bread, the staple of the national table.
