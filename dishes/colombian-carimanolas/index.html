Carimañolas (Colombian stuffed yuca fritters)

Origin: Colombia · Caribbean coast

Carimañolas are oval fritters made from boiled, mashed yuca wrapped around a filling of seasoned ground beef or salty white cheese. Deep-frying gives them a thin, crackly shell and a chewy, soft inside. On the Caribbean coast they are a classic breakfast or street snack, often eaten with suero costeño (a tangy cultured cream) or a spicy ají sauce.

Recipe

6 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g yuca, peeled
  • 250 g ground beef
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tomato, grated
  • 100 g crumbly white cheese (optional filling)
  • 1 tsp cumin, salt and pepper
  • Oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Boil the yuca in salted water for 25-30 minutes until very soft, remove the fibrous core and mash into a smooth dough.
  2. Fry the onion and tomato, add the beef and cumin, and cook until dry; season and let cool.
  3. Take a ball of yuca dough, flatten it in your palm and place a spoonful of filling in the middle.
  4. Close and shape into a torpedo, sealing well.
  5. Deep-fry at 175 °C for 4-5 minutes until golden.
  6. Drain on paper and serve warm with ají.

How to make it lighter

  • Air-fry or bake them brushed with a little oil for a much lighter version.
  • Use lean beef or shredded chicken in the filling.
  • Pair one or two with a fresh salad rather than eating them as a whole meal.

History

They belong to the fritter tradition of Colombia's Caribbean coast, where yuca is a daily staple, and are also popular in Panama. They are sold from street stalls in cities like Cartagena and Barranquilla.
