☕ Arabic coffee (gahwa)

Arabic coffee, or gahwa, is lightly roasted coffee boiled in water with crushed cardamom and a few threads of saffron. It comes out pale golden, fragrant and lightly bitter, with no sugar or milk. It is poured from a dallah pot into small handleless cups, a few sips at a time, and usually served with dates.

What it is made of

coffeecardamomsaffronwater

Recipe

6 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml water
  • 3 tbsp lightly roasted coffee, ground medium-coarse
  • 1 tbsp green cardamom pods, lightly crushed
  • A pinch of saffron threads

Method

  1. Bring the water to the boil in a pot or dallah, stir in the coffee and simmer gently for 10–12 minutes.
  2. Take it off the heat for a minute so the grounds settle.
  3. Put the cardamom and saffron in a thermos or serving pot and carefully pour the coffee over, leaving the grounds behind. Let it steep 5 minutes.
  4. Pour a small amount into each cup and serve with dates.

How to make it lighter

  • Plain gahwa is only a few kcal per cup; the dates served alongside are what count — each date is roughly 20–65 kcal depending on size and variety.
  • Stick to two or three dates per round of coffee rather than reaching for the plate between sips.
  • Keep it unsweetened, as it is traditionally served, so the cup stays almost calorie-free.

History

Coffee drinking spread across the Arabian Peninsula from Yemen from around the 15th century, and serving coffee to guests became a central part of hospitality in the region. In 2015 UNESCO added Arabic coffee as a symbol of generosity to its list of intangible cultural heritage, in a joint nomination by the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Oman and Qatar.
