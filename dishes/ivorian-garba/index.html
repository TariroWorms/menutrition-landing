Garba (attiéké with fried tuna)

Origin: Ivory Coast · Abidjan

Garba is the beloved Abidjan lunch of attiéké, a couscous-like fermented cassava, served with a fried tuna steak. It comes with chopped onion, tomato and a fiery pepper sauce, and is usually eaten by hand. It is cheap, filling and full of flavor.

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g steamed attiéké
  • 2 tuna steaks (300 g)
  • 200 ml oil
  • 1 onion (100 g)
  • 2 tomatoes (200 g)
  • 1 hot pepper, salt

Method

  1. Season the tuna with salt and pepper.
  2. Fry the tuna in hot oil until crisp outside and cooked through.
  3. Warm the attiéké and fluff it with a fork.
  4. Chop onion, tomato and pepper and mix with a spoon of the frying oil.
  5. Serve the fish on the attiéké with the fresh salad.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or pan-sear the fish with little oil.
  • Add extra tomato and cucumber for fiber.
  • Use smaller portions of attiéké if watching carbs.

History

Garba is tied to the street canteens of Abidjan, often run by informal vendors near markets and schools. Attiéké itself is a traditional staple of the Ebrié people of the lagoon coast.
