Kare raisu, or curry rice, is Japan's everyday curry: tender pork, potatoes, carrots and onions simmered in a thick, glossy, mildly spiced sauce and ladled beside a mound of short-grain rice. The sauce is gently sweet and savoury rather than fiery. Pork is the most common meat in eastern Japan, and the dish is a favourite family dinner and school lunch.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 350 g pork shoulder or loin, sliced
  • 2 potatoes and 1 carrot, cut into chunks
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 100 g Japanese curry roux
  • 700 ml water
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 600 g cooked short-grain rice, to serve

Method

  1. Fry the onion in the oil until soft and lightly golden, then add the pork and cook until it changes colour.
  2. Add the potato and carrot, pour in the water and bring to the boil, skimming any foam.
  3. Simmer for 15–20 minutes until the vegetables are tender.
  4. Turn off the heat, add the curry roux and stir until dissolved, then simmer 5 minutes until thick.
  5. Serve beside the rice on a wide plate.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean pork loin and trim visible fat.
  • Fill half the plate with curry vegetables and keep the rice portion small.
  • Thin the sauce with a little more water and use less roux to lower the calories per spoonful.

History

Curry arrived in Japan from Britain in the late nineteenth century and was adapted into a thick, roux-based sauce served with rice. Ready-made roux blocks, sold since the mid-twentieth century, turned curry rice into one of the most cooked dishes in Japanese homes.
