Pachamanca (Andean earth-oven feast)

Origin: Peru · Andes

Pachamanca is less a single recipe than a celebration. Stones are heated in a fire, then layered in a pit with potatoes, sweet potatoes, corn, fava beans and meats marinated in a green paste of huacatay, chincho, chile and garlic. Everything is covered with leaves, cloth and earth and left to cook in the trapped heat. The food comes out tender, smoky and fragrant with herbs, and is shared at family gatherings and festivals.

Recipe

8 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg mixed meat (lamb, pork, chicken) in large pieces
  • 1 kg potatoes
  • 500 g sweet potatoes
  • 4 corn cobs
  • 300 g fava beans in pods
  • 1 bunch huacatay (40 g)
  • 2 tbsp ají amarillo paste
  • 6 garlic cloves, 2 tbsp oil, cumin, salt

Method

  1. Blend huacatay, ají paste, garlic, cumin, oil and salt; marinate the meat for several hours.
  2. Heat stones in a fire for about 1 hour (at home, use a large covered roasting tin in the oven at 190 °C).
  3. Layer potatoes and sweet potatoes on the hottest stones, then the meat, then corn and beans.
  4. Cover with banana or herb leaves, damp cloth and earth (or foil).
  5. Cook for about 1 hour 15 minutes.
  6. Uncover, let the steam escape and serve everything together.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose chicken and lean lamb and remove the skin.
  • Fill most of the plate with vegetables, corn and beans.
  • Leftover potatoes and meat make a good next-day salad.

History

The name comes from Quechua: pacha (earth) and manca (pot). Cooking in earth ovens is an old practice in the Andes, and pachamanca is especially associated with the central highlands, such as Junín and Huánuco.
