Lutefisk (Norwegian lye-cured cod)

Origin: Norway

Lutefisk is dried cod that has been soaked in a lye solution until it turns soft and jelly-like. It is boiled or baked and served with bacon, mushy peas, potatoes and melted butter. The taste is very mild and the texture is delicate, which is why strong sides are common. It is a Christmas-season tradition in Norway.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g lutefisk, soaked and rinsed
  • 200 g bacon
  • 300 g green peas
  • 800 g potatoes
  • 60 g butter
  • salt and pepper

Method

  1. Rinse the soaked lutefisk well in cold water several times.
  2. Peel and boil the potatoes in salted water until tender.
  3. Place the fish in a pan with a little salted water or wrap it in foil and cook at 200 °C for 25 minutes.
  4. Fry the bacon until crisp and warm the peas, lightly mashed.
  5. Plate the fish with potatoes, peas and bacon and pour over melted butter.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less bacon and butter to cut the fat noticeably.
  • Serve with extra peas and boiled vegetables for more fibre.
  • Rinse the fish thoroughly so no taste of lye remains.

History

Lutefisk grew out of the old Nordic practice of preserving stockfish. It is now mostly eaten in the weeks before Christmas, and Norwegian emigrants carried the tradition to the American Midwest.
