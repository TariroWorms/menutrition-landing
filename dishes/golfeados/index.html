Golfeados (papelón and cheese sticky rolls)

Origin: Venezuela

Golfeados are soft yeast rolls shaped into spirals around a filling of grated papelón, anise seeds and crumbled salty white cheese. As they bake the sugar melts and caramelises, and they are brushed with more syrup so the tops are glossy and sticky. The mix of sweet, aniseed and salty is distinctive. They are eaten warm with a slice of fresh white cheese and coffee.

Recipe

12 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml warm milk
  • 60 g butter
  • 1 egg
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 250 g grated papelón
  • 150 g grated salty white cheese
  • 1 tbsp anise seeds

Method

  1. Make a dough with flour, yeast, milk, egg, butter and a pinch of salt; knead and rise 1 hour.
  2. Roll into a large rectangle and sprinkle with papelón, cheese and anise.
  3. Roll up tightly and cut into 12 slices.
  4. Place in a buttered tin, rise 30 minutes and bake at 180 °C for 25 minutes.
  5. Brush with a syrup of a little papelón melted in water and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the papelón in the filling and skip the extra syrup.
  • Use a lighter fresh cheese.
  • Enjoy one roll with fruit rather than a second.

History

Golfeados are traditionally associated with Los Teques and the Altos Mirandinos near Caracas. They are a classic of Venezuelan bakeries and are often compared to cinnamon rolls.
