🥟 Mandu

Origin: Korea

Mandu are Korean dumplings with a thin wheat wrapper and a juicy filling of minced pork, crumbled tofu, chopped kimchi and glass noodles. Steamed mandu are soft and savoury with a gentle tang from the kimchi. They are eaten as a snack or side, dipped in a little soy sauce and vinegar, and also added to soups.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g minced pork
  • 200 g firm tofu, pressed and crumbled
  • 150 g kimchi, finely chopped and squeezed
  • 50 g glass noodles, soaked and chopped
  • 2 spring onions, chopped
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 30 round dumpling wrappers
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Mix the pork, tofu, kimchi, glass noodles, spring onions and garlic; season with salt and pepper.
  2. Place 1 heaped teaspoon of filling in the centre of each wrapper.
  3. Wet the edge with water, fold into a half-moon and press to seal.
  4. Steam the mandu over boiling water for 10–12 minutes until the filling is cooked.
  5. Serve hot with a dip of soy sauce and rice vinegar.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced pork or replace half of it with extra tofu.
  • Steam rather than pan-fry — frying adds oil to every dumpling.
  • Count the dumplings: a portion of 6–8 with a bowl of vegetable soup is a balanced meal.

History

Dumplings are often said to have reached Korea from Central Asia and China around the Goryeo period, and mandu have long been a fixture of Korean home cooking. Many families make them in large batches for the Lunar New Year, when mandu soup is a common dish.
