🥧 Cheese burek (sirnica)

Origin: Balkans · Bosnia and Serbia

Burek sa sirom, known in Bosnia as sirnica, is a Balkan pie of phyllo layers filled with crumbled fresh white cheese mixed with eggs. It bakes into a flaky, golden crust around a soft, salty and slightly tangy filling. It is a favourite breakfast, usually eaten warm from the bakery.

What it is made of

phyllo pastrycheeseegg

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g phyllo sheets
  • 500 g fresh white cheese (such as a crumbly cow's cheese)
  • 3 eggs
  • 60 ml vegetable oil, divided
  • A pinch of salt

Method

  1. Crumble the cheese and mix it with the eggs and a pinch of salt.
  2. Oil a 28 cm round baking tin. Layer sheets of phyllo, brushing each lightly with oil, then spread the filling and roll or fold into a spiral or layered pie.
  3. Brush the top with a little oil and bake at 200 °C for 35–40 minutes until deep golden and crisp.
  4. Rest for 10 minutes, then cut into wedges and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a lower-fat fresh cheese for the filling; it keeps the texture with fewer calories.
  • Brush the phyllo with oil instead of pouring — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Cut the pie into 8 smaller wedges and eat one with a vegetable salad.

History

Burek came to the Balkans with Ottoman cuisine and is related to the Turkish börek. In Bosnia and Herzegovina only the meat-filled version is strictly called burek, while the others are known as pita; elsewhere in the former Yugoslavia every version is simply burek. Today it is a standard breakfast from bakeries across the region.
