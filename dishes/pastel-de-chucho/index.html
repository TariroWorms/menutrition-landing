Pastel de chucho (stingray and plantain bake)

Origin: Venezuela · Margarita

Pastel de chucho is a layered casserole, a bit like a tropical lasagne, built from slices of sweet ripe plantain, a stew of shredded stingray and grated white cheese. Eggs bind the layers as it bakes. The contrast of sweet plantain and savoury, slightly smoky fish is what makes it memorable. It is cut into squares and eaten as a main course, often at weekend lunches.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g stingray meat (or salted stingray, soaked to remove the salt)
  • 4 very ripe plantains (800 g)
  • 200 g grated white cheese
  • 1 onion (150 g)
  • 1 bell pepper (150 g)
  • 2 tomatoes (200 g)
  • 3 eggs
  • 2 tbsp oil (30 ml)

Method

  1. Boil the stingray 10 minutes, cool and shred it, discarding cartilage.
  2. Sauté onion, pepper and tomato in 1 tbsp oil, add the fish and cook until thick.
  3. Slice the plantains lengthwise and brown them in a lightly oiled pan.
  4. Layer plantain, fish and cheese in a greased dish, repeating twice and ending with plantain.
  5. Beat the eggs and pour them over so they seep between layers; top with cheese.
  6. Bake at 180 °C for 30 minutes until golden; rest 10 minutes before cutting.

How to make it lighter

  • Roast the plantain slices in the oven instead of frying them.
  • Use a lighter fresh cheese and less of it on top.
  • Serve a small square with a big green salad.

History

Stingray, locally called chucho, has long been caught by Margarita fishermen and is usually salted or stewed. Pastel de chucho is regarded as one of the island’s emblematic dishes and appears on most local restaurant menus.
