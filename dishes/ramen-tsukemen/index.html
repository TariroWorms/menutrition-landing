🍜 Tsukemen (Dipping Ramen)

Origin: Japan · Tokyo

Tsukemen is a style of ramen where thick, chewy noodles are served cold or warm on a separate plate with a small bowl of hot, concentrated broth for dipping. Toppings typically include slices of braised pork belly, a soft-boiled marinated egg and bamboo shoots. The broth is richer and saltier than regular ramen soup because it coats the noodles rather than being drunk.

Recipe

2 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g fresh thick ramen noodles
  • 300 g pork belly
  • 600 ml rich pork broth
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 eggs
  • 100 g bamboo shoots (menma)
  • 2 spring onions, sliced

Method

  1. Simmer the pork belly in water with 2 tbsp soy sauce for 2 hours, then slice.
  2. Boil the eggs for 6½ minutes, cool, peel and marinate in the pork cooking liquid.
  3. Reduce the broth by about half with the remaining soy sauce to make a concentrated dip.
  4. Cook the noodles, rinse under cold water and drain well.
  5. Serve noodles on a plate with pork, halved eggs, bamboo shoots and spring onions, and the hot broth in a bowl for dipping.

How to make it lighter

  • Dip the noodles lightly and leave most of the concentrated broth in the bowl.
  • Choose fewer slices of pork belly and add extra bamboo shoots and spring onions.
  • Ask for a regular rather than large noodle portion — tsukemen servings are often generous.

History

Tsukemen is usually credited to Kazuo Yamagishi of the Tokyo shop Taishoken, who began serving it in the 1950s. It spread widely across Japan in the 2000s and is now a ramen category of its own.
