Canja de galinha (Brazilian chicken and rice soup)

Origin: Brazil

Canja is a simple, soothing soup of chicken simmered with onion, garlic and carrot, then thickened with rice that cooks right in the broth. The chicken is shredded back into the pot and the soup is finished with parsley. In Brazilian homes it is what you make for someone who is ill, for new mothers, or on a cold evening.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g chicken thighs or breast
  • 100 g rice
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 2 litres water
  • Chopped parsley
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Soften the onion and garlic in the oil in a large pot.
  2. Add the chicken and water, bring to the boil and skim.
  3. Simmer gently for 30 minutes until the chicken is cooked.
  4. Lift out the chicken, add the rice and carrot and cook for 15 to 20 minutes.
  5. Shred the chicken and return it to the pot.
  6. Season, scatter with parsley and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken breast for the leanest soup.
  • Add courgette or spinach for more vegetables.
  • Use brown rice and cook it a little longer for more fibre.

History

Canja came to Brazil from Portugal, where a similar chicken and rice soup is a long-standing home remedy. It is often said to have been a favourite of the Brazilian emperor Pedro II, and it remains one of the most common soups in the country.
