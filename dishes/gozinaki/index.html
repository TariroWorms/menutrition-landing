Gozinaki (honey walnut brittle)

Origin: Georgia

Gozinaki is a simple brittle of chopped walnuts bound with hot honey and cut into diamonds. It is crunchy, fragrant and intensely sweet, with a slight bitterness from the toasted nuts. Small pieces are eaten with tea or offered to guests.

Recipe

12 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g walnuts
  • 300 g honey
  • 1 tbsp sugar

Method

  1. Toast the walnuts in a dry pan until fragrant, then chop coarsely.
  2. Bring the honey and sugar to a boil and simmer until it darkens slightly, about 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in the walnuts and cook on low heat for 8–10 minutes, stirring constantly.
  4. Spread the hot mixture 1 cm thick on a wet board.
  5. Cut into diamonds while warm and leave to set completely.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small pieces: 30–40 g is a satisfying portion.
  • Leave out the sugar; honey alone sets the brittle.
  • Mix in some pumpkin seeds instead of part of the walnuts for variety.

History

Gozinaki is closely tied to New Year in Georgia, when it appears on almost every festive table. Traditionally only walnuts and honey are used, and the pieces are often set on leaves or a wet board.
