Llapingachos (cheese-stuffed potato patties)

Origin: Ecuador

Llapingachos are thick patties of mashed potato coloured with achiote, filled with fresh cheese and onion and cooked on a griddle until crisp. Inside, the cheese turns soft and stringy. They are served with a mild peanut sauce, a fried egg, avocado, chorizo or hornado and a crunchy salad. They make a filling lunch or market snack.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg floury potatoes
  • 150 g fresh cheese, grated
  • 1/2 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp achiote oil
  • For the sauce: 60 g peanut butter, 150 ml milk, 1/4 onion
  • 4 eggs, lettuce, tomato
  • salt

Method

  1. Boil the potatoes, mash them smooth with achiote oil and salt, and let cool.
  2. Mix the cheese with the onion.
  3. Shape balls of potato, press a hollow, fill with cheese and flatten into thick patties.
  4. Chill for 30 minutes so they hold together.
  5. Cook on a lightly oiled griddle for 4 minutes per side until golden.
  6. Simmer peanut butter, milk and onion into a sauce and serve with fried eggs and salad.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook them in a non-stick pan with just a brush of oil.
  • Use a light cheese and keep the peanut sauce on the side.
  • Swap the chorizo for extra salad and avocado.

History

The name comes from Kichwa and refers to something flattened or pressed. Llapingachos are strongly associated with Ambato and the central highlands. They are the usual companion to hornado.
