🍝 Ciceri e tria

Origin: Italy · Salento, Apulia

Ciceri e tria is a rustic pasta dish from Salento: soft stewed chickpeas tossed with fresh flat pasta ribbons, garlic and plenty of olive oil. The trick is that part of the pasta is fried until crunchy and scattered on top, so the plate mixes creamy chickpeas, tender noodles and crisp golden bits. It is served warm as a hearty main course.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g cooked chickpeas plus 300 ml of their cooking liquid
  • 300 g fresh flat pasta ribbons (tagliatelle-style)
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 4 tbsp olive oil, plus oil for frying
  • 1 bay leaf
  • Salt, black pepper, chopped parsley

Method

  1. Set aside about a third of the pasta. Fry it in 1 cm of oil until golden and crunchy, then drain on paper towels.
  2. Warm 4 tbsp olive oil with the garlic and bay leaf, add the chickpeas and their liquid and simmer for 10 minutes, mashing a few chickpeas to thicken.
  3. Boil the remaining pasta in salted water for 2–3 minutes and drain.
  4. Stir the pasta into the chickpeas with a splash of cooking water and season.
  5. Serve topped with the fried pasta, parsley and black pepper.

How to make it lighter

  • Fry only a small handful of pasta for crunch, or toast it in a dry pan instead of frying.
  • Measure the olive oil — each tablespoon is about 120 kcal.
  • Make the chickpeas the larger share of the bowl and the pasta the smaller one.

History

The dish belongs to the traditional cooking of Salento in southern Apulia. The word tria is usually traced to an Arabic term for dried pasta, often cited as a sign of Arab influence on southern Italian food. It is still cooked at home and served in trattorie across Lecce and the surrounding area.
