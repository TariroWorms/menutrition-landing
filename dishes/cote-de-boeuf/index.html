Côte de bœuf is a thick, bone-in rib steak, usually around 1 kg and meant for two or more people. It is seared hard in a pan or over coals, then finished gently so the inside stays pink and juicy, and rested before carving. The meat is well marbled, rich and tender, and it is sliced at the table.

What it is made of

beef prime rib

Recipe

3 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in beef rib steak, about 1 kg, 5 cm thick
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 3 sprigs thyme
  • Coarse salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Take the steak out of the fridge 45 minutes before cooking and heat the oven to 200 °C.
  2. Pat dry, oil lightly and season generously with salt.
  3. Sear in a very hot ovenproof pan for 3–4 minutes per side, including the fat edge.
  4. Add garlic and thyme, transfer to the oven for 10–15 minutes until 52–54 °C inside for medium-rare.
  5. Rest 10 minutes, slice off the bone and season with pepper.

How to make it lighter

  • One côte feeds two to three — weigh your share rather than eating half.
  • Trim the outer fat cap before eating.
  • Serve with green beans or a salad instead of fries.

History

Côte de bœuf is a classic of French butchers and bistros: a thick bone-in beef rib steak, similar to what English speakers call a bone-in rib steak. It is usually cooked for two or more people to share.
