Koose (Ghanaian bean fritters)

Origin: Ghana

Koose is a fritter of ground black-eyed peas whipped with onion and hot pepper, then deep-fried until deep golden. It is crisp outside, light and a little grainy within, with a warm peppery bite. In northern Ghana it is a standard breakfast, eaten with koko (millet porridge) or tucked into bread with a spicy sauce.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g black-eyed peas, soaked
  • 80 g onion
  • 5 g hot pepper
  • 5 g salt
  • 300 ml vegetable oil for frying

Method

  1. Soak and peel the peas, then drain well
  2. Blend with onion, pepper and salt, whisking until light and fluffy
  3. Heat the oil to about 180 °C
  4. Drop in spoonfuls and fry 3-4 minutes, turning, until golden
  5. Drain on paper and serve hot

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the shaped fritters to cut the oil a lot
  • Whisk the batter well so it absorbs less fat
  • Serve with fresh tomato and cucumber instead of white bread

History

Koose belongs to the same West African family as akara and accra, and in Ghana it is closely tied to the Hausa and northern communities. Street sellers fry it fresh at dawn, which is how most people still buy it.
