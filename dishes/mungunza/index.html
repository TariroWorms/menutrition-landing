Mungunzá (hominy in coconut milk)

Origin: Brazil · Northeast

Mungunzá is a thick, sweet stew of white hominy kernels cooked slowly in coconut milk with sugar, cloves and cinnamon. The kernels stay pleasantly chewy in a fragrant, creamy liquid. It is eaten warm for breakfast or as an afternoon treat, and is a favourite during the June festivals in the Northeast.

What it is made of

hominycoconut milksugarclovescinnamon

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g dried white hominy, soaked overnight
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 500 ml water
  • 130 g sugar
  • 5 cloves
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1 pinch salt

Method

  1. Drain the hominy and cook in fresh water until tender, about 1 hour.
  2. Drain and return to the pan with 500 ml water, cloves and cinnamon.
  3. Add sugar and salt and simmer for 15 minutes.
  4. Pour in the coconut milk and cook gently 15 minutes more, stirring, until creamy.
  5. Remove the cloves and cinnamon and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk and less sugar; the spices carry the flavour.
  • It is naturally vegan, unlike many Brazilian sweets.
  • A small bowl is filling thanks to the fibre in the hominy.

History

The name comes from a Kimbundu word for cooked corn, reflecting African influence on Northeastern cooking. It is the Northeastern counterpart of the dish called canjica in the Southeast, and some cooks add peanuts or cow's milk.
