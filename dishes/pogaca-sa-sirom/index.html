🍞 Pogača sa sirom (Serbian cheese bread)

Origin: Serbia · Balkans

Pogača sa sirom is a round Serbian bread enriched with yogurt, egg and butter and studded with crumbled white cheese. It bakes up tall and fluffy with a golden crust, and is torn into pieces at the table. It is served warm at breakfast, at family gatherings and celebrations, often alongside cured meats, ajvar and fresh vegetables.

Recipe

8 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 7 g dried yeast and 1 tsp sugar
  • 150 ml warm water
  • 200 g plain yogurt
  • 1 egg plus 1 yolk for brushing
  • 50 g butter, melted
  • 200 g white cheese, crumbled
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, sugar and salt; add the water, yogurt, egg and butter and knead to a soft dough.
  2. Cover and leave to rise for about 1 hour.
  3. Knead in most of the cheese and shape into a round loaf in a greased tin.
  4. Rise 20 minutes, brush with yolk and scatter the remaining cheese on top.
  5. Bake at 190 °C for 30–35 minutes until golden; cool slightly before tearing.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the loaf into 8 pieces and count one piece as a portion.
  • Use a low-fat yogurt and a lighter white cheese.
  • Serve it with plenty of fresh tomatoes, peppers and cucumber.

History

Pogača is a traditional festive bread across the Balkans; the name shares its roots with the Italian focaccia. Today pogača sa sirom is a common home-baked bread in Serbia and neighbouring countries.
