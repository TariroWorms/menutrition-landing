🥞 Chapssal hotteok (sticky-rice sweet pancake)

Origin: South Korea

This hotteok is made with glutinous rice powder, which gives a very chewy, stretchy pancake. The dough is wrapped around brown sugar, cinnamon and a handful of seeds, then pan-fried until the outside is golden and the sugar melts into a gooey syrup. It is eaten hot as a sweet snack.

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g glutinous rice powder
  • 200 ml hot water
  • A pinch of salt
  • 60 g brown sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 2 tbsp sunflower or pumpkin seeds, chopped
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil for frying

Method

  1. Mix the rice powder and salt, then gradually add the hot water and knead into a smooth, soft dough.
  2. Mix the brown sugar, cinnamon and seeds.
  3. Divide the dough into 6 balls, flatten each, fill with a spoonful of the sugar mix and seal well.
  4. Fry in a lightly oiled pan over medium-low heat, pressing flat, for 3–4 minutes per side until golden.
  5. Cool for a minute before eating — the filling gets very hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the brown sugar in half; the cinnamon still gives plenty of flavour.
  • Use a non-stick pan with only a thin film of oil.
  • Have one pancake with tea or fruit rather than a pair.

History

Hotteok is a popular street snack across South Korea, and versions made partly or wholly with glutinous rice flour, known as chapssal hotteok, are sold for their extra-chewy texture.
