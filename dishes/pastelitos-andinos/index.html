Pastelitos andinos

Origin: Venezuela · Andes

Pastelitos andinos are the breakfast turnovers of Mérida, Táchira and Trujillo in the Venezuelan Andes. Unlike lowland empanadas they use a thin wheat dough, which fries into a light, blistered, flaky-looking crust. Classic fillings are minced beef mixed with rice, or salty white cheese, sometimes with guava or chicken. They are eaten hot with a coffee or a cup of pisca andina.

Recipe

8 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g wheat flour
  • 1 egg, 40 g butter, 15 g sugar, salt
  • 180 ml cold water
  • 300 g minced beef cooked with onion and pepper
  • 150 g cooked white rice
  • or 300 g grated white cheese
  • oil for frying

Method

  1. Mix flour, sugar and salt, rub in the butter, add egg and water and knead into a smooth dough; rest 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the cooked beef with the rice and season well.
  3. Roll the dough very thin and cut into 10–12 cm squares or rounds.
  4. Place a spoonful of filling on one, cover with another and press the edges firmly with a fork.
  5. Fry at 180 °C for 3–4 minutes until golden and blistered; drain and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake brushed with egg at 210 °C for a lighter pastry.
  • Use lean beef and more rice and vegetables in the filling.
  • Have one or two with fruit rather than a large plate.

History

Wheat has long been grown in the cool Andean valleys of Venezuela, which explains why the region's turnovers are made with wheat rather than corn. Pastelito stands are a fixture of mornings in Andean towns.
