🥪 Sandwich de pernil

Sandwich de pernil is a classic Chilean street-food sandwich: slices of juicy roast pork leg piled into a soft bun and topped with fresh tomato. It is often finished with a spoonful of pebre or green chili sauce. Simple, warm and filling, it is a staple of sandwich shops and a favourite at family gatherings.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg boneless pork leg
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, salt and pepper
  • 4 soft bread rolls
  • 2 ripe tomatoes, sliced
  • Pebre or green chili sauce to serve

Method

  1. Rub the pork with garlic, cumin, oregano, oil, salt and pepper and leave it for at least 1 hour.
  2. Place it in a roasting dish with 100 ml of water, cover with foil and roast at 180 °C for about 1 hour 30 minutes.
  3. Uncover and roast for another 20 minutes to brown the outside.
  4. Rest the meat for 10 minutes, then slice it thinly and moisten with a little of the pan juices.
  5. Warm the rolls, fill with the pork and tomato slices and add pebre or chili sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim the visible fat from the pork before slicing: the meat stays juicy and the sandwich gets lighter.
  • Use a smaller roll or open-faced bread and add extra tomato and lettuce to make it filling for fewer calories.
  • Choose pebre or chili sauce instead of mayonnaise, which adds around 90 kcal per tablespoon.

History

Roast pork leg, called pernil in Chile, is a traditional centrepiece of festive tables, and the leftovers naturally ended up between two pieces of bread. Over time the sandwich became a fixture of Chilean fuentes de soda and street stalls. It is typically served with a simple topping so that the flavour of the roast pork stays in front.
