🍚 Arroz doce (rice pudding)

Origin: Portugal · Brazil

Arroz doce is the Portuguese-language world's sweet rice: short-grain rice simmered slowly in milk with sugar until thick and creamy, enriched at the end with egg yolks and dusted with ground cinnamon. It is soft, rich and gently spiced, and is eaten warm or cold, often from a wide dish with cinnamon drawn on top in patterns.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g short-grain rice
  • 1 litre milk
  • 500 ml water
  • 150 g sugar
  • 3 egg yolks
  • 1 cinnamon stick, a strip of lemon peel and a pinch of salt
  • Ground cinnamon to dust

Method

  1. Cook the rice in the water with the salt, lemon peel and cinnamon stick until the water is almost absorbed, about 10 minutes.
  2. Add the hot milk gradually, stirring often, and simmer on low heat for 30–35 minutes until creamy.
  3. Stir in the sugar and cook for 5 minutes, then remove from the heat and discard the peel and cinnamon stick.
  4. Mix the egg yolks with a ladle of the hot rice, stir them back in and cook on very low heat for 2 minutes without boiling.
  5. Pour into a dish or bowls and dust with ground cinnamon. Serve warm or chilled.

How to make it lighter

  • Use semi-skimmed milk instead of whole milk for a lighter pudding with the same texture.
  • Cut the sugar by a third — the cinnamon and lemon still carry the flavour.
  • Serve in small bowls of about 120–150 g; it is filling, and a little goes a long way.

History

Arroz doce is one of the oldest and most common desserts of Portugal, a fixture of festivals, weddings and Christmas tables. Portuguese settlers took it to Brazil, where arroz doce is also a much-loved everyday sweet, especially during the June festivals.
