🍆 Imam bayildi (with parsley)

Origin: Turkey · Ottoman cuisine

This version of imam bayildi is whole eggplant slit open and filled with onion slowly softened in olive oil, garlic, tomato and plenty of fresh parsley, then baked until meltingly soft. It tastes sweet from the onions and rich from the oil. It is served at room temperature with the cooking juices spooned over.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 4 medium eggplants
  • 3 onions, thinly sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 3 ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 80 ml olive oil
  • 1 tsp sugar, salt and pepper
  • A handful of flat-leaf parsley, chopped

Method

  1. Peel the eggplants in stripes, slit each lengthwise without cutting through, salt and leave for 20 minutes; rinse and pat dry.
  2. Fry the eggplants briefly in 2 tbsp of the oil until lightly golden, then set them in a baking dish.
  3. Soften the onions in the remaining oil for 10 minutes, add the garlic, tomatoes, sugar, salt and pepper and cook for 5 minutes; stir in the herbs.
  4. Open the slits and pack the filling inside. Add 150 ml water to the dish.
  5. Cover and bake at 180 °C for 40–45 minutes until very soft. Cool and serve at room temperature or cold.

How to make it lighter

  • Most calories come from olive oil: 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal, so roast the eggplants instead of frying them.
  • Cut the oil in the filling to 2–3 tbsp and add a splash more water.
  • Serve one eggplant with a large salad as a light main course.

History

The Turkish name means 'the imam fainted'; the story goes that an imam swooned either at the taste of the dish or at how much olive oil it used. It belongs to the Ottoman tradition of zeytinyağlı — vegetables cooked in olive oil and eaten at room temperature.
