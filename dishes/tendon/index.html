🍤 Tendon (Tempura Rice Bowl)

Origin: Japan · Tokyo

Tendon is a bowl of steamed rice topped with shrimp and vegetable tempura and drizzled with tare, a sweet-savory sauce of soy sauce, mirin and sugar. The sauce soaks into the batter and the rice beneath, so every bite is crisp, sticky and glossy at once. It is a hearty, inexpensive lunch served at dedicated tendon shops and tempura restaurants.

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g cooked short-grain rice
  • 4 shrimp tempura
  • 4 pieces vegetable tempura (pumpkin, eggplant, green pepper)
  • For tare: 40 ml soy sauce, 40 ml mirin, 15 g sugar, 40 ml water

Method

  1. Simmer soy sauce, mirin, sugar and water for 2–3 minutes until slightly thickened.
  2. Fry or reheat the tempura so it is hot and crisp.
  3. Divide the hot rice between two bowls and drizzle a spoonful of tare over it.
  4. Dip each tempura piece briefly in the tare and arrange on the rice.
  5. Spoon over a little more sauce and serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • Ask for less tare — the sauce is where most of the sugar and salt sit.
  • Make the bowl with more vegetable tempura and less rice, or swap half the rice for steamed greens.
  • Share one bowl and add a clear soup or salad on the side.

History

The name is a contraction of tenpura donburi, a tempura rice bowl. Tendon is generally said to have appeared in Tokyo in the 19th century, and today it is sold everywhere from old tempura houses to fast-food tendon chains.
