🐙 Pulpo a Feira

Origin: Spain · Galicia

Pulpo a feira is boiled octopus cut into rounds with scissors and laid on boiled potato slices, then dressed with olive oil, coarse salt and smoked paprika. The octopus is tender with a slight bite, and the paprika gives a smoky, gently spicy finish. It is traditionally served on a wooden plate and eaten with toothpicks.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg octopus, cleaned (previously frozen is best)
  • 4 medium waxy potatoes
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika (sweet or hot)
  • Coarse sea salt

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Dip the octopus in and out three times, then submerge it.
  2. Simmer for 40–50 minutes until a knife slides in easily; leave it in the water for 10 minutes.
  3. Boil the potatoes in the octopus water until tender, then slice into rounds.
  4. Cut the octopus into rounds with scissors and arrange over the potatoes.
  5. Drizzle with olive oil and sprinkle with coarse salt and smoked paprika.

How to make it lighter

  • Measure the olive oil rather than pouring freely; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Use fewer potato slices and more octopus for a higher-protein plate.
  • Add a side of green salad to fill up.

History

The name means 'fair-style octopus' and refers to the Galician tradition of pulpeiras cooking octopus in copper pots at village fairs. The dish is strongly associated with inland Galician towns such as O Carballiño, which holds a well-known octopus festival.
