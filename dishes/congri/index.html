Congrí (Cuban red bean rice)

Origin: Cuba

Congrí is the eastern Cuban cousin of moros y cristianos, made with red beans instead of black. The beans and rice cook together in a base of onion, pepper, garlic and pork, so every grain is tinted pink-brown and rich in flavor. It is a standard side with roast pork or fried chicken.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g dried red kidney beans, soaked
  • 250 g long-grain rice
  • 100 g pork belly, diced
  • 1 onion and 1 green pepper, chopped (220 g)
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp oregano
  • 1 bay leaf, salt

Method

  1. Boil the beans with the bay leaf until nearly tender, about 50 minutes.
  2. Brown the pork in a heavy pot and render its fat.
  3. Add onion, pepper and garlic and cook until soft.
  4. Stir in cumin, oregano, the beans and 500 ml of bean liquid.
  5. Add the rice, salt, cover and cook on low for 20 minutes.
  6. Rest five minutes and fluff with a fork.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork or smoked turkey to cut fat.
  • Add extra peppers and onion for fiber.
  • Serve with grilled meat and a tomato salad.

History

The dish is strongly tied to Oriente, the eastern provinces of Cuba, where red beans are favored. The word congrí is often linked to Haitian influence in the region, though the exact origin of the name is debated. Families cook it for weekend meals and celebrations.
