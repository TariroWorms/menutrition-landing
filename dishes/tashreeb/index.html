🥘 Tashreeb (tashreeb laham)

Origin: Iraq & Gulf · Arabian Peninsula

Tashreeb is a homely dish of torn flatbread soaked in a rich lamb broth with chickpeas, tomato, onion and dried lime, then crowned with the tender meat. The bread drinks up the tangy, aromatic broth and turns soft and spoonable. It is served in a deep shared dish, especially in winter and during Ramadan.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g lamb on the bone
  • 200 g cooked chickpeas
  • 2 onions, quartered
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 dried limes, pierced
  • 1.5 l broth or water
  • 2–3 rounds of khubz (Arabic flatbread)
  • Salt, black pepper, baharat

Method

  1. Put the lamb, onions, dried limes and spices in a pot with the broth and bring to a boil, skimming the foam.
  2. Simmer for 1.5 hours until the lamb is tender.
  3. Add tomatoes and chickpeas and cook 15 minutes more.
  4. Tear the khubz into a deep dish and ladle over the hot broth until soaked.
  5. Top with the lamb and chickpeas and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the broth and lift off the fat before reheating.
  • Use less bread and more chickpeas and broth per bowl.
  • Choose lean cuts and trim visible fat before cooking.

History

Bread soaked in meat broth is one of the oldest dishes of the Arab world, often linked to tharid, mentioned in early Islamic sources. Tashreeb is the name used today in Iraq and the Gulf, where it remains a classic Ramadan and family dish.
