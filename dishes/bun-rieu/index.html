Bún riêu is a Vietnamese rice-vermicelli soup with a tangy tomato broth and soft, fluffy clusters of crab paste that float to the surface. Fried tofu and fine rice vermicelli fill the bowl, and it is served with fresh herbs, lime and chili. The flavour is sour, savoury and delicate, lighter than most meat-based rice-vermicelli soups.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g crab paste (pounded paddy crab or crab meat)
  • 400 g fresh rice vermicelli
  • 4 tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 200 g firm tofu, cubed and pan-fried
  • 1 shallot, sliced, 1 tbsp oil
  • 1.5 l water, salt, a little tamarind or vinegar
  • Herbs, lime and chili to serve

Method

  1. Soften the shallot in oil, add the tomatoes and cook until they start to break down.
  2. Pour in the water, bring to a simmer and season with salt and tamarind.
  3. Spoon the crab paste gently into the simmering broth and wait for it to rise in clusters; do not stir.
  4. Add the fried tofu and simmer for 5 minutes.
  5. Put vermicelli in bowls, ladle over broth, crab and tofu; serve with herbs, lime and chili.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook the tofu cubes with a light brush of oil, or add them unfried.
  • Add more tomato and leafy greens to the broth for a bigger bowl.
  • Use a smaller nest of vermicelli and more broth.

History

Bún riêu cua comes from the rice-growing north of Vietnam, where it was traditionally made with small freshwater paddy crabs pounded with their shells. It is now eaten across the country, from Hanoi street stalls to Saigon markets.
