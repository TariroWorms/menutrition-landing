🍚 Chicken congee

Origin: China · Southern China

Congee is a thick, silky rice porridge simmered for a long time until the grains break down completely. This version is cooked with chicken and plenty of fresh ginger, then finished with sliced scallions and a few drops of soy sauce. It is mild, warming and comforting, eaten for breakfast or as a light meal across China and South-East Asia.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g jasmine rice
  • 2 chicken thighs or 300 g chicken breast
  • 2 litres water
  • 30 g fresh ginger, half sliced and half cut into thin strips
  • 3 scallions, sliced
  • 1–2 tbsp soy sauce
  • Salt, white pepper

Method

  1. Rinse the rice and put it in a large pot with the water, chicken and sliced ginger.
  2. Bring to the boil, then simmer gently for about 1¼ hours, stirring often, until the rice has broken down into a thick porridge.
  3. Lift out the chicken, shred the meat and return it to the pot. Season with salt and white pepper.
  4. Serve topped with the ginger strips, scallions and a few drops of soy sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken breast for the leanest bowl.
  • Congee is mostly water, so a big bowl is filling for its calories — avoid fried dough sticks on the side.
  • Add extra greens such as bok choy or spinach for more volume.

History

Rice porridge has been eaten in China for many centuries and is especially associated with Cantonese cooking, where it is known as jook. Today congee is a breakfast and late-night staple from southern China and Hong Kong to Singapore and Malaysia.
