🧀 Knafeh Nabulsieh

Origin: Palestine · Nablus

Knafeh nabulsieh is the Nablus style of knafeh: a thin layer of fine shredded pastry baked over a thick bed of melted white akkawi or nabulsi-style cheese, turned out bright orange and soaked with sugar syrup. Pistachios are scattered on top. It is served hot, so the cheese stretches in long strings, and cut from a large round tray.

Recipe

8 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g shredded phyllo (kataifi), finely chopped
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil
  • 450 g akkawi cheese, soaked to remove salt and sliced
  • 200 g sugar and 150 ml water for syrup
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • 30 g pistachios, ground

Method

  1. Boil the sugar and water 8 minutes with the lemon juice and leave to cool.
  2. Rub the pastry with the oil and press a thin, even layer into a round tray.
  3. Cover with the cheese and bake at 200 °C for 20–25 minutes until the base is golden.
  4. Invert onto a plate, pour over the syrup and sprinkle with pistachios; serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Pour on half the syrup and taste before adding more.
  • Keep portions to about 100–120 g and serve on small plates.
  • Skip extra toppings such as clotted cream; the cheese already makes it rich.

History

The city of Nablus in the West Bank is widely regarded as the home of this cheese-filled knafeh and is famous for its sweet shops. It is now made throughout Palestine, Jordan, Lebanon and Syria and in Arab bakeries worldwide.
