Mie goreng is Indonesian fried noodles: yellow wheat noodles stir-fried over high heat with chicken, egg, cabbage, garlic and chili, then glazed with kecap manis, the thick sweet soy sauce. The result is chewy, smoky and sweet-savoury with a gentle heat. It is sold by street hawkers and served in homes and restaurants, often topped with crisp fried shallots and a wedge of lime.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g dried yellow wheat noodles
  • 150 g chicken breast, thinly sliced
  • 2 eggs
  • 150 g white cabbage, shredded
  • 3 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1–2 red chilies, sliced
  • 3 tbsp kecap manis (sweet soy sauce)
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, salt
  • 1 spring onion and lime wedges to serve

Method

  1. Cook the noodles according to the packet, drain and rinse with cold water.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok, fry the garlic and chili for 30 seconds, add the chicken and stir-fry for 3–4 minutes.
  3. Push everything to one side, crack in the eggs and scramble them.
  4. Add the cabbage and stir-fry for 2 minutes until just wilted.
  5. Add the noodles and kecap manis and toss over high heat for 2 minutes until glossy; season with salt.
  6. Serve topped with spring onion and lime wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use more cabbage and less noodles — vegetables add volume for few calories.
  • Kecap manis is full of sugar, so measure it rather than pouring from the bottle.
  • Use one tablespoon of oil in a hot wok; each extra tablespoon adds about 120 kcal.

History

Mie goreng grew out of the Chinese-Indonesian community, whose noodle cooking blended with local ingredients such as kecap manis and chili. Over time it became one of the most common dishes in Indonesia, cooked at street carts across Java and beyond. It is also popular in Malaysia and Singapore, where the spelling mee goreng is common.
