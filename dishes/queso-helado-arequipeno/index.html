Queso helado (Arequipa frozen milk dessert)

Origin: Peru · Arequipa

Despite its name, "frozen cheese" contains no cheese. It is a frozen custard of milk, evaporated and condensed milk and egg yolks, perfumed with cinnamon and cloves and enriched with grated coconut. Traditionally it is frozen in a metal drum turned by hand over ice and salt, then sliced and sold sprinkled with ground cinnamon. It is less airy than ice cream, with a dense, slightly icy and very milky texture.

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 litre whole milk
  • 400 ml evaporated milk
  • 395 g condensed milk
  • 4 egg yolks
  • 80 g grated coconut
  • 2 cinnamon sticks, 6 cloves, ground cinnamon to serve

Method

  1. Heat the milk with cinnamon and cloves for 15 minutes; strain.
  2. Whisk the yolks with the condensed milk and add the hot milk slowly.
  3. Cook gently, stirring, until it lightly coats a spoon; do not boil.
  4. Stir in the evaporated milk and coconut and cool completely.
  5. Churn or freeze in a loaf tin, stirring several times; slice and dust with cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Use semi-skimmed milk for part of the whole milk.
  • Cut down the condensed milk and add a little cinnamon for flavour.
  • Serve one thin slice per person.

History

Queso helado is the emblematic sweet of Arequipa in southern Peru, where vendors sell it in the plazas and markets. The name is usually explained by the way it is cut into slices like a fresh cheese.
