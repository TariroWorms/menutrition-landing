Mojarra frita (Colombian fried tilapia)

Origin: Colombia · Caribbean coast

Mojarra frita is the classic plate of Colombia's beaches and riverside restaurants: a whole tilapia, scored on both sides, rubbed with lime, garlic and salt, lightly dusted with flour and deep-fried until the skin and fins are crackling crisp. The flesh inside stays white and juicy. It comes with patacones (twice-fried green plantain), coconut rice, a simple tomato and onion salad and lime wedges.

What it is made of

fishlimegarlicflouroilplantain

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 whole tilapia (about 400 g each), cleaned
  • 2 limes
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 60 g flour
  • Salt and oil for frying
  • 2 green plantains for patacones

Method

  1. Score the fish deeply on both sides and rub with lime, garlic and salt; rest 20 minutes.
  2. Pat dry and dust lightly with flour.
  3. Fry in plenty of oil at 180 °C for 6–8 minutes per side until crisp and golden.
  4. Meanwhile fry thick plantain slices, smash them flat and fry again for patacones.
  5. Drain everything on paper and serve with coconut rice, salad and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or grill the fish instead of frying for a much leaner dish.
  • Swap patacones for boiled yuca or a big salad.
  • Skip the flour coating so the fish absorbs less oil.

History

Tilapia is widely farmed in Colombia, and fried mojarra became a standard dish along the Caribbean coast and around rivers and reservoirs inland. It is a typical weekend lunch by the sea.
