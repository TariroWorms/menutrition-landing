Espresso is a short, concentrated coffee made by forcing hot water under pressure through finely ground coffee. A single shot is about 30 ml, with a dense body, intense aroma and a layer of golden-brown crema on top. It is drunk quickly, on its own or with a little sugar, and forms the base of many other coffee drinks.

What it is made of

espresso

Recipe

1 servings3 min

Ingredients

  • 18 g finely ground coffee
  • 40 ml water
  • Sugar (optional)

Method

  1. Fill the portafilter with the ground coffee and tamp it evenly.
  2. Lock it into the machine and place a warmed cup underneath.
  3. Extract for 25–30 seconds to get about 30 ml of espresso.
  4. Serve immediately, with sugar on the side if you like.

How to make it lighter

  • Plain espresso is almost calorie-free — it is the additions that count.
  • 1 tsp sugar ≈ 16 kcal; try cutting down gradually.
  • Order it instead of large sweetened coffee drinks when you want a lighter option.

History

Espresso developed in Italy in the early 20th century with the first commercial machines that brewed coffee under steam pressure. The modern style with thick crema spread after lever machines appeared in the 1940s, and the espresso bar became part of everyday Italian life.
