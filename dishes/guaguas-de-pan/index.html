Guaguas de pan (bread babies)

Origin: Ecuador

Guaguas de pan are soft, brioche-like breads shaped into little swaddled figures, "guagua" meaning baby in Kichwa. Many are filled with guava jam or sweet cream and decorated with colored icing faces and patterns. They are made for the Day of the Dead and dipped into a cup of colada morada.

Recipe

10 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 10 g dry yeast
  • 80 g sugar
  • 80 g butter, softened
  • 2 eggs plus 1 yolk for brushing
  • 200 ml warm milk, pinch of salt
  • 200 g guava jam
  • Icing sugar and food coloring to decorate

Method

  1. Mix flour, yeast, sugar and salt, add milk and eggs and knead 10 minutes; work in the butter.
  2. Let rise covered until doubled, about 1 hour.
  3. Divide into 10 pieces, flatten each, add a spoon of jam and roll into an oval body with a small head.
  4. Proof 30 minutes, brush with yolk and bake at 180 °C for 20–25 minutes.
  5. Cool and decorate faces and blankets with colored icing.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half whole-wheat flour for more fiber.
  • Fill with fresh fruit purée instead of sugary jam.
  • Decorate with a little icing only; one small guagua is plenty with colada.

History

Baking bread figures for All Souls is a practice that blends Catholic customs with older Andean rituals honoring ancestors. In Ecuador, families and bakeries prepare guaguas every year in the days before 2 November.
