🐟 Shui Zhu Yu (Sichuan Boiled Fish)

Origin: China · Sichuan

Shui zhu yu, literally 'water-boiled fish', is a fiery Sichuan dish of tender white fish slices poached and laid over bean sprouts and celery, then covered with dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns. Hot oil is poured over the top to release the aromas. The result is soft, flaky fish with a numbing, spicy heat, eaten with plain rice.

Recipe

3 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g white fish fillet, thinly sliced
  • 200 g bean sprouts
  • 2 celery stalks, sliced
  • 10 dried chilies, cut into pieces
  • 1 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 600 ml water
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Season the fish slices with a pinch of salt.
  2. Blanch the bean sprouts and celery in boiling salted water for 1 minute and put them in a deep bowl.
  3. Bring the water to a simmer with garlic and salt, slide in the fish and poach for 2–3 minutes until just opaque.
  4. Lift the fish and some of the liquid over the vegetables.
  5. Top with chilies and Sichuan peppercorns, heat the oil until shimmering and pour it over to sizzle; serve straight away.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less finishing oil: 1 tbsp oil is about 120 kcal, and most of the flavour comes from the spices.
  • Leave the oil at the bottom of the bowl — lift out the fish and vegetables with a slotted spoon.
  • Double the bean sprouts and celery to make the bowl bigger without many extra calories.

History

Shui zhu yu belongs to the 'water-boiled' family of Sichuan dishes, which is often linked to the salt-producing city of Zigong. It became a favourite of Sichuan restaurants across China in recent decades.
