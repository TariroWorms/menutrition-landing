Tapioca (Brazilian cassava crepe)

Origin: Brazil · Northeast

Brazilian tapioca is a thin round crepe made only from moistened cassava starch sifted onto a hot pan. The grains fuse into a soft, slightly chewy disc in a minute, with no oil, eggs or flour. It is folded around fillings such as queijo coalho, grated coconut, butter, carne de sol or condensed milk. People eat it for breakfast or as an afternoon snack, often from street stalls.

What it is made of

tapioca starchqueijo coalho cheesecoconutbutter

Recipe

2 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g hydrated tapioca starch (goma)
  • 60 g queijo coalho, grated
  • 2 tbsp grated fresh coconut
  • 10 g butter
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Rub the goma between your fingers and pass it through a sieve.
  2. Heat a non-stick pan over medium heat without oil.
  3. Sift a thin, even layer of starch to cover the base.
  4. Cook about 1 minute until the disc holds together and lifts easily.
  5. Scatter cheese and coconut on one half.
  6. Flip, fold in half and add a little butter on top.
  7. Serve immediately while soft.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the butter; the crepe needs no fat to cook.
  • Fill with eggs, tomato or chicken for more protein.
  • Keep sweet fillings small, as the crepe itself is mostly starch.

History

Cassava and its starch were used by Indigenous peoples of Brazil long before colonisation, and the tapioca crepe grew from that tradition. It became a symbol of the Northeast, especially Olinda and Recife, and spread across the country as a gluten-free snack.
