🥩 Lomo a lo pobre

Lomo a lo pobre is a hearty Chilean plate: a grilled beef steak served on a bed of French fries, topped with sweet fried onions and one or two fried eggs. The egg yolk runs into the fries and onions and makes a natural sauce. It is a filling, calorie-dense main course found in restaurants and family kitchens across Chile.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 4 beef sirloin steaks, about 180 g each
  • 800 g potatoes, cut into fries
  • 2 large onions, sliced into thin rings
  • 4 eggs
  • Vegetable oil for frying
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Peel the potatoes, cut them into fries, rinse and dry them well.
  2. Fry the potatoes in hot oil until golden and crisp, drain on paper and salt them.
  3. In a large pan, cook the onion rings in 2 tablespoons of oil over medium heat for 10–12 minutes until soft and golden.
  4. Season the steaks with salt and pepper and grill or pan-sear them for 3–4 minutes per side, then rest them for 3 minutes.
  5. Fry the eggs in a little oil until the whites are set and the yolks are still soft.
  6. Pile the fries on plates, lay a steak on top, cover with the onions and finish with a fried egg.

How to make it lighter

  • The fries add most of the fat: oven-bake them with a tablespoon of oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Choose a lean cut, trim visible fat, and use one egg per plate instead of two.
  • Cook the onions slowly in just a little oil and add a green salad or tomato salad to fill the plate.

History

The name means “poor man’s loin”, which is ironic, because the plate is anything but modest. It is a classic of Chilean home-style and restaurant cooking, built from simple everyday ingredients: beef, potatoes, eggs and onions. Variations add a pork or chicken cut, or more eggs, and it is often shared at family lunches and informal gatherings.
