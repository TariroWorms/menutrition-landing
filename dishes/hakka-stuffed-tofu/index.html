Hakka stuffed tofu (yong tau foo)

Origin: China · Guangdong
300kcal

Squares of firm tofu are hollowed out and filled with a bouncy mixture of minced pork and fish paste. They are fried until golden, then braised briefly in a light savory sauce. The result is soft outside, springy inside, and a good example of Hakka home cooking.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g firm tofu
  • 200 g minced pork
  • 100 g fish paste
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Cut the tofu into squares and scoop a small hollow in each.
  2. Mix pork, fish paste, scallion, half the soy sauce and cornstarch until sticky.
  3. Fill each tofu piece, pressing the stuffing in slightly proud of the surface.
  4. Pan-fry stuffed side down in oil until golden, then flip briefly.
  5. Add garlic, remaining soy sauce and a ladle of water and cover.
  6. Braise for 8 minutes and thicken the sauce slightly.

How to make it lighter

  • Steam the tofu instead of frying to reduce the fat.
  • Use lean pork or more fish paste.
  • Serve with greens to bulk up the meal.

History

Stuffed tofu is a signature of Hakka cuisine, said to have grown from the wish to recreate dumplings with locally available ingredients. It later became popular in Hong Kong and Southeast Asia in many forms.
