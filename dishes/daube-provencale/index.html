Daube provençale is a slow braise of beef chunks marinated and cooked in red wine with black olives, strips of orange zest and herbes de Provence. After hours over low heat the meat turns fork-tender and the sauce becomes dark, glossy and fragrant with citrus and thyme. It is served hot as a hearty main, often with boiled potatoes or polenta on the side.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg beef chuck, cut into 5 cm chunks
  • 750 ml red wine
  • 150 g black olives
  • 2 strips orange zest
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 carrots, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp herbes de Provence
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Marinate the beef with the wine, onions, carrots, garlic, orange zest and herbs overnight in the fridge.
  2. Lift out the meat, pat dry and brown it in batches in the olive oil in a heavy pot.
  3. Return the meat with the marinade, season and bring to a gentle simmer.
  4. Cover and cook on very low heat for 3–4 hours until the beef is tender.
  5. Add the olives for the last 30 minutes, adjust seasoning and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the beef before marinating; leaner cuts like round work with longer cooking.
  • Chill the finished stew and lift off the solidified fat from the surface before reheating.
  • Serve with steamed green vegetables instead of potatoes to keep the plate lighter.

History

Daube is a traditional Provençal stew whose name is usually linked to the daubière, a pot-bellied earthenware braising pot. It was long a dish for Sundays and festive meals in Provence, and leftovers are traditionally used in other local dishes.
