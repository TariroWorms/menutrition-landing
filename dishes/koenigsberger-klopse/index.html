🍲 Königsberger Klopse (meatballs in caper sauce)

Origin: Germany · East Prussia

Königsberger Klopse are tender veal meatballs poached in broth and served in a creamy white sauce sharpened with lemon and capers. A little anchovy in the meat mixture gives depth without tasting fishy. The result is mild, tangy and delicate, and the dish is traditionally served with boiled rice or potatoes and beetroot.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g minced veal
  • 1 stale bread roll, soaked in water and squeezed
  • 1 egg
  • 2 anchovy fillets, finely chopped
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 litre light broth
  • 2 tbsp flour, 100 ml cream
  • 3 tbsp capers, juice of ½ lemon
  • 200 g rice, cooked, to serve

Method

  1. Mix the veal, bread, egg, anchovy, onion, salt and pepper and shape into 12 balls.
  2. Simmer the meatballs gently in the broth for 15 minutes, then lift them out.
  3. Whisk the flour with a little cold broth, stir into the hot broth and simmer for 5 minutes until thickened.
  4. Stir in the cream, capers and lemon juice and season.
  5. Return the meatballs to the sauce to warm through and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Replace half of the cream with extra broth for a lighter sauce.
  • Serve with a smaller portion of rice and a side of beetroot or salad.
  • Three meatballs with sauce make a satisfying portion.

History

The dish is named after Königsberg, the former capital of East Prussia (today Kaliningrad, Russia). It became a classic of German home cooking and is still widely served in Berlin and eastern Germany.
