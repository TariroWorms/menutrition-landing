Matambre arrollado (rolled stuffed flank)

Origin: Argentina

Matambre arrollado is a thin sheet of beef flank spread with garlic and herbs, layered with carrots, peppers and hard-boiled eggs, then rolled tightly and tied. It is simmered in broth or baked, pressed under weight and chilled. Cut cold, every slice shows a colourful spiral of meat, egg and vegetables. It is a typical cold starter at family celebrations and holidays.

What it is made of

beef flankeggcarrotbell peppergarlicparsley

Recipe

8 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg beef flank (matambre), trimmed
  • 4 hard-boiled eggs
  • 2 carrots, cut into sticks
  • 1 red bell pepper, in strips
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 2 l beef broth
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Lay the flank flat, trim excess fat and season with salt, pepper, garlic, parsley and oregano.
  2. Arrange carrots, pepper strips and whole eggs in rows across the meat.
  3. Roll tightly with the grain running along the roll and tie firmly with kitchen string.
  4. Simmer in broth for about 2 hours, until tender.
  5. Cool under a weight, chill overnight, then slice and serve cold.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim all visible fat before rolling for a much leaner result.
  • Add more vegetables such as spinach or peas and fewer eggs.
  • Serve with a Russian salad made with yogurt instead of mayonnaise.

History

Matambre is a thin cut between the hide and the ribs; the name is often explained as "mata hambre", hunger-killer. The rolled version has long been a staple of Argentine holiday tables, especially at Christmas and New Year.
