🐖 Carnitas (Michoacán-style)

Origin: Mexico · Michoacán

Michoacán-style carnitas are chunks of pork slowly cooked in their own lard with orange, garlic, cumin and a splash of milk, which helps the meat brown. The pork becomes tender and juicy inside with crisp, caramelised edges. It is chopped and served with warm tortillas, onion, cilantro, lime and salsa, or eaten by weight from the market stall.

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg pork shoulder, cut into large chunks
  • 200 g lard
  • 1 orange, juiced, plus the squeezed halves
  • 120 ml milk
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 bay leaves, salt

Method

  1. Melt the lard in a heavy pot and add the pork, salt, garlic, cumin and bay leaves.
  2. Add the orange juice and squeezed halves and enough water to come halfway up the meat.
  3. Simmer uncovered for about 2 hours, turning occasionally, until the water has evaporated and the pork is tender.
  4. Pour in the milk and keep cooking, turning the meat, until it fries in the fat and turns golden and crisp at the edges.
  5. Lift out the pork, drain, chop roughly and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Drain the carnitas well and leave the fat in the pot rather than on the plate.
  • Use a leaner cut, or trim the pork, and braise with less lard.
  • Serve a measured portion of meat with plenty of onion, cilantro, salsa and lime.

History

Carnitas are strongly associated with the state of Michoacán, and the town of Quiroga in particular is famous for them. Large copper cauldrons of carnitas are a familiar sight at markets across central Mexico.
