🥧 Engadine walnut tart

Origin: Switzerland · Graubünden

Engadiner Nusstorte is a sturdy Swiss tart of buttery shortcrust pastry enclosing a dense filling of chopped walnuts cooked in caramel and cream. It is chewy, nutty and very sweet, with a crisp, crumbly crust on all sides. Cut into thin wedges, it is served with coffee and keeps well for weeks, which made it a popular gift.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g plain flour
  • 200 g butter, cold
  • 120 g sugar for the pastry
  • 1 egg
  • 250 g sugar for the caramel
  • 250 g walnuts, roughly chopped
  • 200 ml cream
  • 1 tbsp honey

Method

  1. Rub flour and butter together, add sugar and egg, knead briefly and chill 30 minutes.
  2. Melt the sugar to a golden caramel, add the cream carefully, then the honey and walnuts; cook 2 minutes and cool.
  3. Line a 24 cm tin with two thirds of the pastry, fill with the walnut mixture and cover with the rest, sealing the edges.
  4. Prick the top and bake at 180 °C for 35–40 minutes; cool fully before cutting.

How to make it lighter

  • It is very rich: a thin 50 g wedge is plenty with coffee.
  • Bake in a smaller tin and cut 16 pieces instead of 12.
  • Share a slice instead of having it as a dessert after a full meal.

History

The tart is closely linked to the Engadine valley in the canton of Graubünden, and today it is one of Switzerland's best-known regional pastries.
