🥩 Smoked Brisket

Smoked brisket is the centrepiece of Texas barbecue: a whole beef brisket rubbed with just coarse salt and black pepper, then smoked low and slow over oak for many hours. It develops a dark, peppery bark outside and a smoke ring within, while the meat turns tender and juicy. It is sliced to order and served simply on butcher paper.

Recipe

10 servings720 min

Ingredients

  • 4–5 kg whole beef brisket
  • 3 tbsp coarse salt
  • 3 tbsp coarse black pepper
  • Oak wood for smoking

Method

  1. Trim the hard fat, leaving about 5 mm on the fat cap.
  2. Mix salt and pepper and season the brisket evenly on all sides.
  3. Smoke at 110–120 °C over oak until the internal temperature reaches about 70 °C and a dark bark forms.
  4. Wrap in butcher paper and continue cooking until about 93–95 °C and probe-tender, 10–12 hours in total.
  5. Rest wrapped for at least 1 hour, then slice against the grain.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose slices from the lean 'flat' rather than the fattier 'point'.
  • Trim off the outer fat layer on your plate.
  • Pair with pickles, onions and a vegetable side instead of heavy sides.

History

Brisket barbecue is usually traced to Central Texas, where meat markets run by German and Czech immigrants smoked meats in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The salt-and-pepper style over post oak remains the hallmark of Central Texas barbecue.
