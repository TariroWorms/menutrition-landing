🦐 Arroz de marisco (Portuguese seafood rice)

Origin: Portugal · Atlantic coast

Arroz de marisco is Portugal's soupy seafood rice: rice simmered in a tomato broth with shrimp, clams, mussels and squid until it is loose and spoonable, then finished with a big handful of fresh cilantro. It is brothier than paella, tastes deeply of the sea, and is brought to the table in the pot so everyone can ladle their own.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g medium-grain rice
  • 300 g shrimp, shells on
  • 500 g clams, purged of sand
  • 500 g mussels, cleaned
  • 250 g squid, cut into rings
  • 3 ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion, 1 green bell pepper and 3 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 3 tbsp olive oil, 1 bay leaf, chilli flakes, salt
  • 1.2 litres water
  • 1 large bunch cilantro, chopped

Method

  1. Peel the shrimp and simmer the heads and shells in the water for 15 minutes; strain to make a quick broth.
  2. Open the clams and mussels in a covered pan with a little of the broth, then strain their liquid back into the broth.
  3. In a wide pot, soften the onion, pepper and garlic in the olive oil, add the tomatoes, bay leaf and chilli and cook for 8 minutes.
  4. Add the squid and rice, stir, pour in the hot broth and simmer for 15 minutes, stirring now and then; the rice should stay loose and soupy.
  5. Add the shrimp for the last 3 minutes, then the clams, mussels and cilantro. Serve at once, straight from the pot.

How to make it lighter

  • This is one of the lighter rice dishes because much of each ladle is broth — a generous bowl still fits most budgets.
  • Use 1–2 tablespoons of olive oil instead of 3; each tablespoon is about 120 kcal.
  • Ask for more shellfish and less rice when ordering, or add extra tomatoes and peppers at home.

History

Arroz de marisco is a classic of seafood restaurants along the Portuguese coast, where rice dishes are traditionally served wet and soupy, a style known as malandrinho. Cilantro, rare in the rest of Europe, is a signature herb of Portuguese cooking and is what makes this dish taste distinctly Portuguese.
