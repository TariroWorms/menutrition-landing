Pepitoria (Santander goat offal and blood rice)

Origin: Colombia · Santander

Pepitoria is a dark, intensely flavored rice dish from Santander made with the chopped liver, heart, kidneys and other offal of young goat, cooked together with rice, peas, onion, garlic and cumin, and bound with the animal's blood. The blood gives the rice its characteristic brown color and a rich, slightly iron-like taste. It is usually served as a side to cabrito (roast kid) with yuca and arepa.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g goat offal (liver, heart, kidneys), cleaned and finely chopped
  • 250 ml fresh goat blood, cooked until set and crumbled
  • 300 g rice
  • 150 g peas
  • 1 onion, 4 spring onions, 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin, salt and pepper
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Simmer the chopped offal in salted water for 30 minutes; drain and keep the broth.
  2. Fry the onions and garlic in the oil with cumin.
  3. Add the offal and crumbled cooked blood and fry 5 minutes.
  4. Add the rice and stir, then pour in about 600 ml of the broth.
  5. Add the peas, cover and cook on low heat 20 minutes until the rice is tender.
  6. Season and serve hot with roast kid and yuca.

How to make it lighter

  • Offal is very rich in iron and vitamin B12, so a small portion goes a long way.
  • Add extra vegetables like carrot to the rice.
  • People who need to limit cholesterol or purines should eat it only occasionally.

History

Goat herding is common in the dry canyons of Santander, and pepitoria is a way of using the whole animal. It is a traditional regional dish offered in restaurants specializing in cabrito around Bucaramanga and Barichara.
