Karak tea is the Gulf's favourite street drink: strong black tea boiled with cardamom, sugar and evaporated milk, often with a few threads of saffron. The long boil makes it dark, creamy and intensely spiced. It is handed through car windows at roadside cafeterias in small cups and is a fixture of evenings out across the region.

Recipe

2 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml water
  • 2 tbsp loose black tea
  • 4 cardamom pods, crushed
  • A few saffron threads
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 150 ml evaporated milk

Method

  1. Boil the water with the cardamom and saffron.
  2. Add the tea and sugar and boil for 3–4 minutes until dark.
  3. Pour in the evaporated milk, bring back to the boil and let it rise two or three times.
  4. Strain into small cups and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the sugar by half; each teaspoon is about 16 kcal.
  • Use light evaporated milk or a splash more water.
  • Choose the small cup — a standard serving is around 200 ml.

History

Karak tea grew out of the spiced milk teas brought to the Gulf by South Asian communities, and the name is usually explained as meaning 'strong'. It has since become part of everyday life in the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman.
