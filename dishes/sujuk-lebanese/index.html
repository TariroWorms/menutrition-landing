🌭 Sujuk (Lebanese Spicy Sausage)

Lebanese sujuk is a spicy, garlicky beef sausage that is sliced and pan-fried with tomato, garlic and olive oil until juicy and slightly caramelised. The sausage is heavily seasoned with cumin, paprika, chili and allspice, giving it a deep red colour and bold flavour. It is served hot as a mezze dish or for breakfast, often with fresh vegetables on the side.

Recipe

3 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g beef sujuk, sliced
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/2 tsp chili flakes
  • Juice of 1/2 lemon
  • Chopped parsley to serve

Method

  1. Heat the olive oil in a pan over medium heat.
  2. Add the sujuk slices and fry for 3–4 minutes until browned.
  3. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Stir in the tomatoes, cumin and chili and cook for 5 minutes until the tomatoes soften into a sauce.
  5. Finish with lemon juice and parsley and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Drain off the fat the sujuk releases before adding the tomatoes.
  • Double the tomatoes and add sliced peppers so a smaller amount of sausage fills the plate.
  • 1 tbsp olive oil ≈ 120 kcal — the sausage fat is often enough, so the oil can be reduced to a teaspoon.

History

Sujuk is closely related to Turkish sucuk, and in Lebanon it is often associated with the Armenian community. Today pan-fried sujuk with tomato is a familiar item on mezze tables and in sandwich shops across Lebanon.
