🫓 Lángos

Lángos is a Hungarian flatbread of yeasted dough stretched by hand and deep-fried until puffy, golden and crisp at the edges. The classic topping is garlic rubbed or brushed over the hot bread, then a thick layer of sour cream and a heap of grated cheese. It is eaten hot from the hand at markets, beaches and festivals.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g wheat flour
  • 7 g dried yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar, 1.5 tsp salt
  • 320 ml lukewarm water
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed with 2 tbsp water
  • 200 g sour cream
  • 150 g grated hard cheese

Method

  1. Mix flour, yeast, sugar, salt and water into a soft dough, knead 5 minutes and leave to rise 1 hour.
  2. Divide into 4 balls and stretch each by hand into a thin round with oiled fingers.
  3. Deep-fry at 180 °C for 1–2 minutes per side until puffed and golden; drain on paper.
  4. Brush with the garlic water, spread with sour cream and scatter cheese.
  5. Serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Share one lángos between two — a whole one is a full meal.
  • Use light sour cream and a smaller handful of cheese.
  • Drain well on paper towels and keep the oil hot (180 °C) so the dough absorbs less fat.

History

Lángos is a classic of Hungarian street food, and it is often said to have begun as a way to use up leftover bread dough on baking day. Today it is sold at markets, fairs and beaches, especially around Lake Balaton, and is popular across Central Europe.
