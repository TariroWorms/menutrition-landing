🫘 Msabaha

Msabaha is a warm Levantine dish of whole cooked chickpeas in a loose tahini and lemon sauce with garlic and cumin, finished with olive oil and parsley. It is chunky and creamy at the same time, like a rustic, unblended hummus. It is served warm, often for breakfast, as part of a mezze spread.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g cooked chickpeas, with 100 ml of their cooking liquid
  • 4 tbsp tahini
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt
  • Chopped parsley

Method

  1. Warm the chickpeas in their cooking liquid for 5 minutes.
  2. Mix the tahini, lemon juice, garlic and salt with a few spoons of warm liquid into a pourable sauce.
  3. Drain the chickpeas, keeping a little liquid, and lightly mash a third of them.
  4. Fold the chickpeas into the tahini sauce.
  5. Spread on plates, sprinkle with cumin, drizzle with olive oil and scatter with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Tahini is calorie-dense: 1 tbsp ≈ 90 kcal, so measure it and thin the sauce with lemon and water.
  • Keep the olive oil drizzle small — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Pair it with sliced tomatoes, cucumber and onion to make a smaller portion more filling.

History

Msabaha is closely related to hummus and is eaten across Palestine, Lebanon, Syria and Jordan today, especially in hummus shops at breakfast time.
