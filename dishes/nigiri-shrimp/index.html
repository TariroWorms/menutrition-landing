🍤 Shrimp nigiri

Shrimp nigiri is a small block of vinegared sushi rice topped with a cooked, butterflied shrimp and a dab of wasabi. The shrimp is firm, sweet and mild, and the rice gently sour. It is one of the most familiar pieces in a sushi set and a good choice for anyone who wants sushi without raw fish.

Recipe

2 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g Japanese short-grain rice
  • 240 ml water
  • 3 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 10 large shrimp, shell on
  • 1 tsp wasabi paste

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then cook it with the water and let it rest covered for 10 minutes.
  2. Warm the vinegar with the sugar and salt until dissolved, fold it into the hot rice with a cutting motion and let it cool to body temperature.
  3. Skewer each shrimp lengthways so it stays straight, boil for 2–3 minutes, chill in ice water, then peel and cut along the belly to open it flat.
  4. With wet hands, shape about 20 g of rice into a small oblong, dab a little wasabi on the underside of the topping and press it gently onto the rice.
  5. Serve right away, 2 pieces per person as a snack or 8–10 as a meal.

How to make it lighter

  • Count pieces: the rice makes up most of the calories, so 2–4 pieces alongside a salad is lighter than a full sushi platter.
  • Shrimp is lean protein; plain shrimp nigiri is lighter than rolls with fried shrimp or creamy sauces.
  • Ask for smaller rice balls or order the same topping as sashimi to skip the rice entirely.

History

Nigiri is the hand-pressed style of sushi that took shape in Edo, today's Tokyo, in the early 19th century, when street stalls sold quick bites of vinegared rice topped with fish from Edo Bay. Its invention is usually credited to the sushi maker Hanaya Yohei, though the story is hard to verify. Before refrigeration, many toppings were cured, simmered or marinated, and raw toppings became common only in the 20th century. Cooked shrimp has been a sushi topping since the Edo period and is still on nearly every sushi menu.
