🫘 Feijoada à transmontana

Origin: Portugal · Trás-os-Montes

Feijoada à transmontana is the hearty bean stew of north-eastern Portugal: red beans slowly simmered with pork, smoked chouriço, blood sausage (morcela) and cabbage. The sausages give the broth a deep, smoky flavour, the beans turn soft and creamy, and the cabbage adds a little freshness. It is served steaming hot in a deep plate, usually as a filling winter main course.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g dried red beans, soaked overnight
  • 400 g pork shoulder or belly, cubed
  • 1 chouriço (about 200 g), sliced
  • 1 morcela blood sausage (about 150 g), sliced
  • 300 g cabbage, shredded
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 bay leaf, 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Boil the soaked beans with the bay leaf in fresh water until tender, about 1 hour.
  2. Brown the pork in the oil, then add the onion and garlic and cook until soft.
  3. Add the chouriço, paprika and a ladle of bean water; simmer 30 minutes.
  4. Stir in the beans with some of their liquid and the cabbage; simmer 20 minutes.
  5. Add the morcela for the last 10 minutes, season and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork shoulder instead of belly and skim the fat from the surface before serving.
  • Double the cabbage and beans and halve the sausages for a lighter, still smoky plate.
  • 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal: brown the meat in just a teaspoon and let the sausages release their own fat.

History

The dish belongs to the mountainous Trás-os-Montes region, where pig slaughter in winter supplied cured sausages and salted pork for the rest of the year. It is one of many regional feijoadas in Portugal, each built around local beans and meats. Today it is a fixture of tascas and family lunches in the north, especially in the cold months.
