Esquites are a Mexican street snack of corn kernels cooked until tender, then mixed with mayonnaise, lime juice, crumbled cotija cheese and chili powder. The result is creamy, tangy, salty and gently smoky. They are sold warm in cups from street carts and eaten with a spoon.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g corn kernels (fresh or frozen)
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 1 lime
  • 40 g cotija cheese, crumbled
  • ½ tsp chili powder
  • 1 tbsp chopped cilantro
  • Salt

Method

  1. Boil the corn in salted water for 5–7 minutes until tender, then drain.
  2. While still warm, stir in the mayonnaise and the juice of half the lime.
  3. Divide into cups and top with cotija, chili powder and cilantro.
  4. Serve with lime wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tbsp mayonnaise instead of 2 — 1 tbsp mayonnaise ≈ 90 kcal.
  • Add extra lime and chili for flavour without extra calories.
  • Keep it to one cup as a side rather than a full meal.

History

Esquites are a close relative of elote, corn on the cob with similar toppings, and are typical of central Mexico. The name is generally traced to the Nahuatl word for toasted corn, and today they are a fixture of street stalls across the country.
