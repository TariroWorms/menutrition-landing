Cachapa (Venezuelan corn pancake)

Origin: Venezuela

A cachapa is a large, thick pancake made from fresh sweet corn ground to a rough batter, cooked on a griddle until the edges are lacy and brown. It is soft, sweet and tastes of fresh corn. The classic way to eat it is folded over a thick slice of queso de mano, a mild, stretchy hand-made cheese that warms and softens inside. Roadside stalls sell cachapas with butter, cheese and sometimes fried pork.

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g fresh corn kernels (about 5 cobs)
  • 100 ml milk
  • 40 g sugar, a pinch of salt
  • 40 g flour or cornmeal if the batter is thin
  • 20 g butter
  • 300 g queso de mano or fresh mozzarella, sliced

Method

  1. Blend the corn kernels with milk, sugar and salt into a thick, slightly rough batter.
  2. Add a little flour if it is too runny; it should pour slowly.
  3. Heat a griddle or heavy pan and grease lightly with butter.
  4. Pour a ladle of batter into a 15–18 cm round and cook 3–4 minutes until bubbles form and the edges brown.
  5. Flip, cook 2–3 minutes more, then top half with cheese, fold and serve with a little butter.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the extra sugar if the corn is very sweet.
  • Use a light fresh cheese and only 40–50 g per cachapa.
  • Cook on a non-stick pan with just a brush of oil.

History

Cachapas are an old corn dish of Venezuela, made when fresh corn is in season. Roadside cachapa stands, especially on the highways around the central states, are a well-known stop for travellers.
