Chicken harees is a variation of the classic Gulf Ramadan porridge made with chicken instead of lamb. Whole wheat and chicken are simmered for hours, then beaten until they merge into a smooth, thick, almost elastic porridge, seasoned simply with salt and cinnamon and finished with melted butter. It is milder and lighter than the lamb version and is eaten with a spoon from a shared bowl.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g whole wheat (hulled wheat berries), soaked overnight
  • 600 g chicken on the bone (thighs or half a chicken)
  • 1 cinnamon stick, plus ground cinnamon to serve
  • About 2 litres water
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • 30 g butter, melted, to serve

Method

  1. Drain the wheat and put it in a large pot with the chicken, cinnamon stick and water.
  2. Bring to the boil, skim, then cover and simmer on very low heat for 3 hours, stirring now and then and adding water if it dries out.
  3. Remove the chicken, discard skin and bones, shred the meat and return it to the pot with the salt.
  4. Beat vigorously with a wooden spoon or blend briefly until smooth and stretchy, cooking 20–30 minutes more.
  5. Serve in bowls with a little melted butter and a pinch of ground cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Use only a teaspoon of butter per bowl, or skip it — the porridge is already rich in texture.
  • Choose skinless chicken breast to make it leaner still.
  • Harees is dense and filling; a small bowl with salad is a full meal at iftar.

History

Harees is one of the oldest dishes of the Arabian Peninsula and is especially associated with Ramadan iftar and Eid in the Gulf. Using chicken instead of lamb is a common modern home variation.
