Chopped chili fish head (Duo jiao yu tou)

A large fish head is split open, covered with a thick blanket of salted red chilies and steamed until the flesh turns silky. The juices mix with the chili, garlic and ginger to make a bright, salty, fiery sauce. It is brought to the table in the steaming dish and eaten with rice or noodles stirred into the leftover sauce.

Recipe

3 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 1 fish head, about 900 g (carp or bighead)
  • 80 g salted chopped red chilies
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 30 g ginger, shredded
  • 2 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 2 scallions, chopped

Method

  1. Split the fish head in half, rinse and pat dry.
  2. Rub it with a little soy sauce and ginger and lay it in a deep dish.
  3. Spread garlic, ginger and the chopped chilies thickly over the top.
  4. Steam over high heat for 12 to 15 minutes until the flesh is just opaque.
  5. Heat the oil until smoking, pour it over the chilies and scatter scallions.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less salted chili and add fresh pepper to cut sodium.
  • Steam rather than fry the fish, as in the original.
  • Serve with plain rice and a green vegetable.

History

This dish is tied to Hunan home and restaurant cooking, where salted chopped chilies are a staple condiment. Locals often say it grew out of a way to make the cheaper fish head taste special. It remains one of the best-known Hunan dishes across China.
