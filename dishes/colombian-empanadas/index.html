Colombian empanadas

Origin: Colombia

Colombian empanadas are made with yellow corn dough rather than wheat, so they fry into a thin, glassy, very crunchy shell. The filling is usually a mix of mashed potato and shredded or ground meat cooked with hogao, tinted with achiote. They are eaten hot as a snack at any time of day, always with a spoonful of ají — a fresh sauce of chopped onion, cilantro, chili and vinegar — and a squeeze of lime.

Recipe

8 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g precooked yellow cornmeal
  • 500 ml warm water with 1 tsp achiote
  • 400 g potatoes, boiled and mashed
  • 250 g ground beef or pork
  • 1 tomato, 2 scallions, 1 garlic clove, 1 tsp cumin
  • Oil for frying
  • Ají: onion, cilantro, chili, vinegar

Method

  1. Sauté tomato, scallion and garlic to make hogao; add the meat and cook until done.
  2. Mix the meat with the mashed potato and season with cumin and salt.
  3. Knead the cornmeal with the achiote water and a pinch of salt into a smooth dough.
  4. Press small balls of dough into thin rounds between plastic, add a spoonful of filling, fold and seal.
  5. Fry in hot oil until crisp and golden; drain and serve with ají and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry them brushed with a little oil to cut the fat dramatically.
  • Make them small and limit yourself to two with a salad.
  • Use lean meat or a bean filling.

History

Empanadas arrived with the Spanish but in Colombia they took on the native corn dough. They are sold everywhere, from street corners and bakeries to football matches.
