🥐 Apple strudel

Origin: Austria · Vienna

Apfelstrudel is a roll of paper-thin pastry wrapped around sliced apples, raisins, sugar and cinnamon, brushed with melted butter and baked until the layers turn crisp and golden. Inside, the apples soften into a warm, fragrant filling. It is cut into thick slices and served warm, often dusted with icing sugar.

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 8 sheets filo pastry
  • 1 kg tart apples, peeled and thinly sliced
  • 60 g raisins
  • 80 g sugar
  • 2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 70 g butter, melted

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 190 °C and line a tray with baking paper.
  2. Toss the apples with the raisins, sugar, cinnamon and lemon juice.
  3. Lay out the filo sheets on a clean cloth, overlapping them slightly, brushing each layer lightly with butter.
  4. Spread the apple mixture along one long edge, leaving a border, then use the cloth to roll the pastry up tightly. Tuck in the ends.
  5. Place seam-side down on the tray, brush with the remaining butter and bake for 35–40 minutes until deep golden. Cool for 10 minutes before slicing.

How to make it lighter

  • A 150 g slice is about 390 kcal; a thinner 100 g slice saves around 130 kcal.
  • Brush the filo sparingly — 1 tbsp of butter is about 100 kcal, and a light coat still crisps the layers.
  • Use very sweet apples and cut the added sugar by a third; the raisins add sweetness too.

History

Layered pastries of this kind are usually linked to the Ottoman and Hungarian kitchens, from where the technique spread into the Habsburg lands. A handwritten recipe for a milk-cream strudel from 1696, kept in the Vienna City Library, is often cited as the oldest known strudel recipe. Apple strudel became a fixture of Viennese coffee houses and is now considered one of Austria's national desserts.
