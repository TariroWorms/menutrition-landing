🥩 Dongpo pork

Origin: China · Hangzhou, Zhejiang

Dongpo pork is thick squares of pork belly braised slowly in soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar, ginger and scallion until the fat turns soft and jiggly and the skin glossy. The sauce reduces to a sweet, savoury, deep red glaze. It is served one square per person, usually with plain rice and green vegetables.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g skin-on pork belly in one piece
  • 300 ml Shaoxing wine
  • 4 tbsp light soy sauce and 2 tbsp dark soy sauce
  • 40 g rock sugar or white sugar
  • 50 g ginger, sliced
  • 6 scallions, in long lengths

Method

  1. Blanch the pork in boiling water for 5 minutes, rinse and cut into 5 cm squares.
  2. Line a heavy pot with the scallions and ginger and set the pork on top, skin side down.
  3. Add the wine, soy sauces and sugar, bring to the boil, cover and simmer on very low heat for 90 minutes.
  4. Turn the pieces skin side up and simmer 30 minutes more, then reduce the sauce until glossy and spoon it over the pork.

How to make it lighter

  • Treat one square as the whole meat portion and fill the rest of the plate with greens.
  • Chill the braising liquid and lift off the set fat before reducing it.
  • Pair it with plenty of steamed vegetables and a small bowl of rice.

History

The dish is named after the Song dynasty poet and official Su Shi, also known as Su Dongpo, who wrote a poem about slow-cooking pork. Legend has it that while governing Hangzhou he gave braised pork to the workers who dredged West Lake. It remains one of the signature dishes of Hangzhou cuisine.
