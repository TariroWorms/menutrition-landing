Gumbo is a thick Louisiana stew built on a dark roux of flour and fat cooked until the colour of chocolate, then simmered with chicken, smoky pork sausage, okra and the 'holy trinity' of onion, bell pepper and celery. The result is rich, savoury and gently spiced, with okra helping to thicken the broth. It is ladled over a scoop of white rice.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 60 ml oil
  • 60 g flour
  • 600 g chicken thighs, cut into pieces
  • 250 g smoked pork sausage, sliced
  • 300 g okra, sliced
  • 1 onion, 1 bell pepper and 2 celery sticks, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, 1.5 l water, 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp paprika, ½ tsp cayenne, thyme, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Brown the sausage and chicken in a heavy pot and set aside.
  2. Add the oil and flour and stir constantly over medium-low heat for 25–35 minutes until the roux is dark brown.
  3. Add the onion, pepper, celery and garlic and cook 5 minutes.
  4. Gradually whisk in the water, add the spices, bay leaves, chicken and sausage, and simmer for 45 minutes.
  5. Add the okra and simmer 20 minutes more. Season and serve over rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken breast and less sausage to cut the fat.
  • Make a smaller roux — 2 tbsp each of oil and flour still gives flavour; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve with a smaller scoop of rice and extra okra in the bowl.

History

Gumbo developed in 18th- and 19th-century Louisiana and draws on West African, French, Spanish and Native American cooking. The name is generally traced to an African word for okra. Countless family and regional versions exist, and it is now an emblem of Creole and Cajun cuisine.
