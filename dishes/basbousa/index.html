Basbousa is a soft semolina cake soaked in sugar syrup, beloved across Egypt and the wider Arab world. The batter of semolina, yogurt, melted butter and desiccated coconut bakes into a golden, slightly grainy cake that is drenched in syrup while hot, turning it moist and sweet. It is cut into diamonds or squares and served with tea or coffee, especially during Ramadan and celebrations.

Recipe

12 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g coarse semolina
  • 50 g desiccated coconut
  • 100 g sugar
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 250 g plain yogurt
  • 120 g melted butter
  • Syrup: 250 g sugar, 200 ml water, 1 tsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Make the syrup: boil the sugar and water for 8–10 minutes, add the lemon juice and leave to cool.
  2. Mix the semolina, coconut, sugar and baking powder, then stir in the yogurt and melted butter to a thick batter. Rest for 15 minutes.
  3. Spread the batter in a greased 25 cm tin, smooth the top and score it into diamonds with a knife.
  4. Bake at 180 °C for 30–35 minutes until deep golden.
  5. Pour the cool syrup over the hot cake straight from the oven and let it soak for at least 30 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small pieces: basbousa is dense and sweet, and a 50 g square is often enough.
  • Use less syrup and a lighter-sugar syrup; most of the sweetness soaks in from it.
  • Make it with low-fat yogurt and reduce the butter slightly; the cake still stays moist from the syrup.

History

Syrup-soaked semolina cakes are eaten across the Middle East, North Africa and the eastern Mediterranean under many names, such as harissa or hareeseh in the Levant and revani in Turkey and Greece. Basbousa is the Egyptian name, and it is now one of the most popular sweets in Egyptian bakeries and homes.
