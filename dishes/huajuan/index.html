Huajuan (flower rolls)

Origin: China · northern China

Huajuan are fluffy steamed buns rolled up with a thin layer of scallion-flavoured oil and twisted so the layers show as petals. The crumb is soft and slightly chewy, with a gentle savory onion aroma. They are eaten warm beside stir-fries and soups, or torn apart as a snack.

Recipe

8 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 5 g dry yeast
  • 250 ml warm water
  • 40 ml oil
  • 4 chopped scallions
  • 5 g salt

Method

  1. Mix flour, yeast and warm water into a smooth dough and let it rise until doubled, about 1 hour.
  2. Roll the dough into a thin rectangle.
  3. Brush with oil and sprinkle with salt and scallions.
  4. Roll up like a log, cut into 8 pieces and pinch and twist each into a flower shape.
  5. Rest 20 minutes, then steam over boiling water for 18 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half whole wheat flour for more fibre.
  • Use less oil and more scallions for fewer calories.
  • Serve with vegetable dishes instead of rice to keep the meal balanced.

History

Steamed wheat breads are a staple of northern Chinese home cooking, where wheat grows better than rice. Huajuan is one of the most common shaped versions and is often served at family meals.
