Miyan kuka (baobab leaf soup)

Origin: Nigeria · Hausa

Miyan kuka is a slightly slimy, earthy soup made from powdered dried baobab leaves, with dried fish, onion, pepper and often groundnut oil or dawadawa. It has a deep savory taste with a touch of sourness. It is eaten with tuwo shinkafa or other swallows.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 4 tbsp baobab leaf powder (kuka)
  • 100 g dried fish
  • 1 onion
  • 2 tomatoes, 2 chilies
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp dawadawa (fermented locust bean)
  • 500 ml water
  • salt, stock cube

Method

  1. Blend tomatoes, chilies and onion and cook the paste in oil for 10 minutes.
  2. Add water, dried fish, dawadawa and seasoning and simmer.
  3. Mix the kuka powder with a little water into a paste.
  4. Stir it into the pot gradually to avoid lumps.
  5. Cook 10-15 minutes until slightly thick.
  6. Serve hot with tuwo.

How to make it lighter

  • Use little oil and plenty of vegetables.
  • Choose fish for protein instead of fatty meat.
  • Pair with a modest portion of swallow.

History

Miyan kuka is a traditional Hausa soup of northern Nigeria and the Sahel, where baobab trees grow. The dried leaf powder is sold in markets and keeps well.
