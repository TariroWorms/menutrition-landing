🐌 Escargots

Origin: France

Escargots are cooked land snails baked with a generous amount of butter mixed with garlic and parsley. The snails are tender and slightly chewy, and the hot herb butter is fragrant and savoury. They are served as a starter, often six or twelve to a plate, in special dishes with small indentations.

What it is made of

snailsbuttergarlicparsley

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 12 cooked snails (canned, drained)
  • 40 g soft butter
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped parsley
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 220 °C.
  2. Mash the butter with the garlic, parsley, a pinch of salt and pepper.
  3. Rinse and dry the snails and place one in each hollow of an escargot dish or a small baking dish.
  4. Top each snail with a little herb butter.
  5. Bake for 8–10 minutes until the butter is bubbling and serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter and add extra parsley and garlic for flavour — 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal.
  • Count them as a starter and follow with a light main course.
  • The snails themselves are lean; most calories come from the butter left in the dish.

History

Snails have been eaten in Europe since antiquity, and they became a celebrated part of French cuisine, especially in Burgundy. Today escargots are a typical festive starter in France, particularly around Christmas.
