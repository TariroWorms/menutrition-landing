🥪 Francesinha

Origin: Portugal · Porto

Francesinha is Porto's famous oversized sandwich: layers of bread filled with steak, sausage and ham, covered completely in melted cheese, topped with a fried egg and drowned in a hot, spicy tomato and beer sauce. It is rich, saucy and eaten with a knife and fork. In Porto it is usually served in a deep plate so the sauce pools around it.

Recipe

2 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thick slices white bread
  • 2 thin beef steaks (about 100 g each)
  • 2 fresh sausages, halved lengthwise
  • 4 slices ham
  • 8 slices melting cheese
  • 2 eggs
  • For the sauce: 330 ml beer, 400 g crushed tomatoes, 1 onion, chilli flakes, 1 bay leaf

Method

  1. Make the sauce: soften the chopped onion in a little oil, add the tomatoes, beer, bay leaf and chilli, and simmer for 25 minutes; blend until smooth.
  2. Sear the steaks and the sausages in a hot pan until cooked through.
  3. Build each sandwich: bread, ham, steak, sausage, more ham, then a second slice of bread.
  4. Cover each sandwich completely with cheese slices and bake at 200 °C for 5–8 minutes until melted.
  5. Fry the eggs, place one on each sandwich and pour the hot sauce around and over.

How to make it lighter

  • Francesinha is very rich; sharing one between two people with a large salad is a realistic way to fit it into a day.
  • Ask for less cheese on top, or use fewer slices at home — the cheese layer adds a large share of the calories.
  • Pour the sauce over the sandwich rather than letting it soak in a deep pool, and skip extra bread for mopping it up.

History

The francesinha ("little Frenchie") is usually credited to Daniel da Silva, a returning emigrant who is said to have adapted the French croque-monsieur at a Porto restaurant in the 1950s or 1960s. It has since become the signature dish of Porto, and recipes for the sauce are closely guarded by local cafés.
