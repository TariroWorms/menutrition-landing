Mala crayfish (mala xiaolongxia)

Origin: China · Hubei

Mala xiaolongxia is a messy, hands-on feast. Whole crayfish are fried briefly and then stewed with garlic, ginger, dried chilies, Sichuan pepper, star anise and doubanjiang until coated in a deeply red, oily sauce. People peel the shells with gloved hands and dip the tails into the sauce. It is a favorite summer night meal in China, served in big piles.

Recipe

3 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 1000 g live crayfish, cleaned
  • 4 garlic cloves, 2 star anise
  • 20 g fresh ginger, 2 scallions
  • 20 g dried chilies, oil for frying
  • 2 tbsp doubanjiang, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 2 tbsp rice wine
  • 1 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns, salt

Method

  1. Scrub the crayfish well, remove the intestinal line and drain.
  2. Fry the crayfish in hot oil for 1-2 minutes until red and set aside.
  3. Fry doubanjiang, garlic, ginger, chilies, star anise and Sichuan pepper until the oil is red.
  4. Return the crayfish with a splash of wine and a little water and simmer for 8-10 minutes.
  5. Season with soy sauce and salt and serve with scallions.

How to make it lighter

  • Peel the crayfish and eat only the meat to avoid the oily sauce.
  • Use less oil and doubanjiang to lower fat and sodium.
  • Serve with corn and vegetable sides instead of extra rice.

History

Crayfish became a popular street food in China in recent decades, with Hubei province, especially Qianjiang, famous for it. The mala version blends this trend with Sichuan flavors.
