🌮 Nachos

Origin: Mexico · Coahuila

Nachos are crisp tortilla chips layered with seasoned ground beef and plenty of cheese, baked until the cheese melts, then topped with sliced jalapeños, salsa and spoonfuls of sour cream. Every bite mixes crunch, melted cheese, heat and cool creaminess. They are a shared appetiser or game-day snack, eaten with the fingers straight from the tray.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g tortilla chips
  • 300 g ground beef
  • 1 tsp ground cumin and 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 150 g grated cheese
  • 2 jalapeños, sliced
  • 200 g tomato salsa
  • 100 g sour cream

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200 °C.
  2. Brown the beef with onion, cumin, paprika and salt; drain the fat.
  3. Spread the chips on a baking tray and scatter over the beef and cheese.
  4. Bake 6–8 minutes until the cheese has melted.
  5. Top with jalapeños, salsa and sour cream and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean ground beef and drain all the fat after browning.
  • Halve the cheese and add more salsa and jalapeños for flavour.
  • Portion the nachos onto a small plate instead of eating from the shared tray.

History

Nachos are usually credited to Ignacio “Nacho” Anaya, a restaurant maître d' in Piedras Negras, Mexico, who is said to have improvised them for visiting guests in the 1940s. The dish spread across Texas and the United States and is now a standard bar, cinema and stadium snack.
