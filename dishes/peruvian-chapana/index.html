Chapana (Peruvian sweet cassava in banana leaf)

Origin: Peru · Cañete

Chapana is a rustic sweet of finely grated cassava mixed with melted chancaca and a little anise. The dough is wrapped in banana leaves, tied and steamed or boiled until it sets into a dense, chewy, amber-coloured loaf. It is unwrapped and eaten in slices, warm or cold, with a deep caramel and leafy aroma.

What it is made of

cassavachancaca (raw cane sugar)anise seedbanana leaf

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg fresh cassava
  • 300 g chancaca or dark panela
  • 100 ml water
  • 1 tsp anise seeds
  • 1 pinch salt
  • Banana leaves for wrapping

Method

  1. Peel the cassava, remove the woody core and grate it finely.
  2. Melt the chancaca in the water with the anise and let it cool slightly.
  3. Mix the syrup into the grated cassava with the salt to form a thick, sticky dough.
  4. Pass the banana leaves briefly over a flame to make them pliable.
  5. Spoon portions of dough onto the leaves, fold into rectangular parcels and tie with string.
  6. Steam or simmer the parcels for about 1 hour until the dough is firm and translucent.
  7. Let rest 15 minutes, then unwrap and slice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 200 g of chancaca instead of 300 g for a less sweet, more cassava-forward result.
  • Pair a slice with fresh fruit or plain yogurt to balance the starch.
  • Never eat cassava raw; make sure the parcels are fully cooked through.

History

Chapana is a traditional sweet of Afro-Peruvian communities in Cañete and Chincha on the southern coast. It uses cassava and cane sugar, staples of the region's former plantations, and is still made by local families and sold at fairs.
