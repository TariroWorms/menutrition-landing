🐟 Teriyaki Salmon

Teriyaki salmon is a fillet of salmon seared in a hot pan until the skin crisps, then glazed with soy sauce, mirin, sugar and ginger reduced to a shiny, sticky coat. The rich, flaky fish balances the sweet-salty sauce. It is served with steamed rice, greens or in a bento, and makes a quick weeknight dinner.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 2 salmon fillets (about 150 g each)
  • 30 ml soy sauce
  • 30 ml mirin
  • 10 g sugar
  • 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
  • 1 tsp oil

Method

  1. Stir together soy sauce, mirin, sugar and ginger.
  2. Pat the salmon dry and season lightly with salt.
  3. Sear skin-side down in the oiled pan over medium-high heat for 4 minutes, then turn and cook 2 minutes.
  4. Pour in the sauce, lower the heat and spoon it over the fish for 1–2 minutes until glossy.
  5. Serve with rice and steamed vegetables.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a nonstick pan and only a teaspoon of oil — salmon releases its own fat.
  • Brush on the glaze at the end instead of pouring it all, using less sauce overall.
  • Pair with plenty of greens and a modest portion of rice.

History

Teriyaki is a Japanese glazing technique — teri for shine, yaki for grilling — traditionally used on fish such as yellowtail. Salmon teriyaki became a fixture of Japanese restaurant menus abroad as salmon grew popular from the late 20th century.
