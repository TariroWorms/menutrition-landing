🍪 Polvorones

Polvorones are small, crumbly Spanish shortbread cookies made from toasted flour, lard, ground almonds and sugar, flavoured with cinnamon. They are so tender they dissolve into powder in the mouth, which gives them their name (from 'polvo', dust). They are dusted with powdered sugar, wrapped in paper and eaten mainly at Christmas.

Recipe

20 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g plain flour
  • 100 g ground almonds
  • 125 g lard, softened
  • 100 g powdered sugar, plus extra for dusting
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 pinch salt

Method

  1. Toast the flour on a baking tray at 160 °C for 15–20 minutes, stirring now and then, until lightly golden; let it cool completely.
  2. Toast the ground almonds in a dry pan for 2–3 minutes until fragrant.
  3. Beat the lard with the powdered sugar until creamy, then mix in the flour, almonds, cinnamon and salt to a crumbly dough.
  4. Press the dough to 1.5 cm thick between sheets of baking paper and cut out small rounds or ovals.
  5. Bake at 170 °C for 12–15 minutes; they should stay pale.
  6. Cool fully on the tray before moving them, then dust with powdered sugar.

How to make it lighter

  • Polvorones are dense in energy, so one or two cookies is a sensible serving with coffee or tea.
  • Cut them smaller (about 15 g each) so a treat costs fewer calories.
  • Skip the extra powdered sugar on top to save a little added sugar.

History

Polvorones are closely associated with Andalusia, especially the town of Estepa near Seville, which is known for large-scale production of these Christmas sweets. They belong to the wider family of Spanish mantecados, cookies made with lard.
