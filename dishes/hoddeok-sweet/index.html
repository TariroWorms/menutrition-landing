🥞 Hotteok (sweet Korean pancake)

Origin: South Korea

Hotteok is a Korean street pancake made from a soft yeasted wheat dough wrapped around a filling of brown sugar, cinnamon, chopped peanuts and sesame seeds. It is pressed flat and pan-fried until the outside is crisp and the sugar inside melts into a hot, runny syrup. It is sold from street carts in winter and eaten straight from a paper cup.

Recipe

8 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g plain flour
  • 5 g dry yeast
  • 1 tbsp sugar and ½ tsp salt
  • 220 ml lukewarm water
  • 80 g brown sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 30 g roasted peanuts, chopped
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil for frying

Method

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, sugar, salt and water into a sticky dough, cover and leave for 1 hour to double.
  2. Mix the brown sugar, cinnamon, peanuts and sesame for the filling.
  3. With oiled hands, divide the dough into 8 pieces, flatten each, add a spoonful of filling and pinch closed.
  4. Place seam-side down in a lightly oiled pan over medium heat, cook for 1 minute, then press flat.
  5. Cook for 2–3 minutes per side until golden and crisp. Let them cool slightly — the filling is very hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a non-stick pan and just a film of oil instead of shallow-frying.
  • Make smaller pancakes and use less sugar in the filling.
  • Peanuts and sesame are calorie-dense: a tablespoon of each is enough for flavour.

History

Hotteok is generally believed to have been introduced by Chinese merchants who settled in Korea in the late 19th century, which is why the name contains ho, a word once used for things from China. It has since become one of South Korea's best-known winter street snacks.
