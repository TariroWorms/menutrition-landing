Shekerbura (Azerbaijani crescent nut pastry)

Origin: Azerbaijan

Shekerbura is a crescent-shaped pastry with a tender, buttery dough and a sweet filling of ground almonds or walnuts, sugar and cardamom. Before baking, its top is pinched with special tweezers called maggash into a herringbone pattern. It bakes pale with a golden edge and is eaten with tea, especially during Novruz.

Recipe

4 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 150 g butter, softened
  • 100 ml milk
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 5 g dry yeast
  • Filling: 250 g ground almonds or walnuts, 200 g sugar, 1 tsp ground cardamom

Method

  1. Rub butter into the flour, add milk, yolks and yeast and knead a soft dough; rest for 40 minutes.
  2. Mix the ground nuts, sugar and cardamom.
  3. Roll small balls of dough into thin discs about 10 cm wide.
  4. Place a spoonful of filling, fold into a half-moon and seal the edge with a crimped twist.
  5. Pinch the top into a pattern with tweezers or a fork.
  6. Bake at 180 °C for 18–20 minutes until the edges are just golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Make smaller pastries so one serving is about 30 g.
  • Use less sugar in the filling; ground almonds are naturally sweet.
  • Enjoy with unsweetened tea to balance the sweetness.

History

Shekerbura is one of the three classic Novruz sweets in Azerbaijan, alongside pakhlava and gogal. Its half-moon shape is commonly described as a symbol of the moon in the holiday's imagery.
