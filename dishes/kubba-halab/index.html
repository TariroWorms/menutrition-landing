🧆 Kubba halab (rice kibbeh)

Origin: Iraq · Baghdad

Kubba halab is an Iraqi croquette with a shell made from rice tinted golden with turmeric, wrapped around a filling of minced beef, onion and parsley. The torpedo-shaped pieces are deep-fried until the outside is thin and crisp while the inside stays soft and juicy. They are served hot as a snack or starter, often with pickles and fresh herbs.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g short-grain rice
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 300 g minced beef
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • A handful of parsley, chopped
  • 1 tsp baharat, salt and black pepper
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Cook the rice with the turmeric and a little salt in plenty of water until very soft, drain well and mash or blitz into a smooth, sticky dough.
  2. Fry the onion in 1 tbsp oil, add the beef and cook until browned. Season with baharat, salt and pepper, stir in the parsley and let cool.
  3. With wet hands, flatten a small ball (about 40 g) of rice dough, place a spoon of filling in the centre and close it into an oval.
  4. Deep-fry the kubba in hot oil at 175 °C for 4–5 minutes until golden, then drain on paper towels and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the kubba brushed with a little oil at 200 °C instead of deep-frying to cut the absorbed fat.
  • Use lean minced beef and drain the fat after browning the filling.
  • Count the pieces: two kubba with a large salad make a lighter meal than a full plate.

History

Despite the name, which means 'Aleppo kubba', the rice version is closely associated with Iraqi and especially Baghdadi home cooking. It is a popular dish at family gatherings and during Ramadan iftars in Iraq today.
