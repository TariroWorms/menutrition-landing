Chimarrão

Origin: Brazil · Rio Grande do Sul

Chimarrão is finely ground green yerba mate packed into a hollow gourd (cuia) and topped up again and again with hot water. It is sipped through a metal straw with a filter, the bomba. The taste is grassy and bitter, and the gourd is passed around a circle of friends or family.

What it is made of

yerba mate

Recipe

1 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g green yerba mate for chimarrão
  • about 1 l hot water at 70–80 °C
  • 1 cuia (gourd) and bomba

Method

  1. Fill the gourd about two-thirds with mate.
  2. Tilt the gourd so the mate forms a slope on one side.
  3. Pour a little lukewarm water into the empty side and let it absorb.
  4. Insert the bomba into the moist part, thumb over the top.
  5. Pour hot (not boiling) water next to the bomba, sip until empty, and refill.

How to make it lighter

  • Never use boiling water; it scorches the leaves and makes it harsher.
  • It has essentially no calories, a good replacement for sweet drinks.
  • Mate is caffeinated; limit it in the evening.

History

The habit of drinking mate comes from the Guaraní peoples and spread among gaúchos of the southern pampas. In Rio Grande do Sul chimarrão is a strong symbol of regional identity and is drunk at home, at work and outdoors.
