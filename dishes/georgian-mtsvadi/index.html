Mtsvadi (Georgian grilled skewers)

Origin: Georgia

Mtsvadi is Georgia's answer to shashlik: large chunks of pork or lamb threaded on skewers and cooked over glowing grapevine embers. The meat stays juicy inside with a smoky, lightly charred crust. It is served with thin onion rings, sometimes pomegranate seeds, and tkemali sauce, usually with bread and fresh herbs at a supra feast.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork neck or shoulder
  • 2 onions
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 50 g pomegranate seeds
  • 100 ml tkemali sauce

Method

  1. Cut the pork into 4–5 cm chunks and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Mix in one sliced onion, squeeze lightly and leave for at least 30 minutes.
  3. Prepare embers from vine cuttings or charcoal until they glow without flames.
  4. Thread the meat onto skewers, leaving small gaps between pieces.
  5. Grill for 15–20 minutes, turning often, until browned and just cooked through.
  6. Serve with thin rings of the second onion, pomegranate seeds and tkemali.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lamb leg or pork loin instead of fatty neck to cut the fat.
  • Fill half the plate with tomatoes, cucumbers and herbs.
  • Use tkemali rather than mayonnaise-based sauces for flavour with few calories.

History

Grilling meat over vine cuttings is closely tied to Georgia's ancient wine-growing culture, since pruned vines provide an aromatic fuel. Mtsvadi is a fixture of outdoor gatherings, holidays and the traditional supra.
