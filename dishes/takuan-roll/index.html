🍣 Takuan Roll

A takuan roll, or oshinko maki, is a thin sushi roll of seasoned rice and a strip of takuan, the bright yellow pickled daikon radish, wrapped in nori. The pickle gives a crunchy bite and a sweet, tangy flavour against the soft vinegared rice. It is usually cut into six small pieces and served as a simple vegetarian item on sushi menus.

What it is made of

sushi ricepickled daikonnori

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g cooked sushi rice
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 2 sheets nori, halved
  • 80 g takuan (pickled daikon), cut into long thin strips

Method

  1. Mix the vinegar, sugar and salt and fold into the warm rice; let it cool to room temperature.
  2. Place half a nori sheet, shiny side down, on a bamboo mat.
  3. Spread a thin layer of rice over the nori with wet hands, leaving 1 cm free at the far edge.
  4. Lay a strip of takuan along the middle and roll tightly with the mat.
  5. Cut each roll into 6 pieces with a wet sharp knife.

How to make it lighter

  • Spread the rice thinly: rice provides most of the calories in a roll.
  • Pair the roll with a clear soup or salad to make a fuller meal.
  • Pickled daikon is already salty, so the roll needs little extra seasoning.

History

Takuan is a traditional Japanese pickle of dried daikon, and thin rolls with pickles are a long-standing part of sushi shops' simple maki. Takuan rolls are commonly found on sushi menus in Japan and abroad today.
