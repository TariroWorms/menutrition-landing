🍤 Pad Thai

Origin: Thailand

Pad Thai is Thailand's best-known noodle dish: thin rice noodles stir-fried with shrimp, egg and bean sprouts in a sweet-sour tamarind sauce. It is finished with crushed roasted peanuts and a squeeze of lime, giving a mix of chewy noodles, crunchy nuts and fresh sprouts. It is usually served with extra lime, chilli flakes and sugar so each diner can adjust the taste.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g dried flat rice noodles (3–5 mm)
  • 200 g raw shrimp, peeled
  • 2 eggs
  • 150 g bean sprouts
  • 3 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 40 g roasted peanuts, crushed
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • ½ tsp salt

Method

  1. Soak the noodles in warm water for 20–30 minutes until pliable, then drain.
  2. Mix the tamarind paste, sugar, salt and 3 tbsp water into a sauce.
  3. Heat the oil in a wok over high heat and cook the shrimp for 1–2 minutes; push aside and scramble the eggs.
  4. Add the noodles and sauce and toss for 2–3 minutes until the noodles are soft and glossy.
  5. Fold in most of the bean sprouts, then plate and top with peanuts, the remaining sprouts and lime wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Peanuts are calorie-dense: 1 tbsp chopped peanuts ≈ 50 kcal, so sprinkle rather than pile them.
  • Bulk up the plate with extra bean sprouts and use a smaller portion of noodles.
  • Cut the sugar in the sauce and lean on lime for brightness.

History

Pad Thai is widely credited to a campaign of the Thai government in the late 1930s and 1940s under Prime Minister Plaek Phibunsongkhram, which promoted rice noodles as a national dish. It has since become a symbol of Thai cuisine and a staple on Thai menus abroad.
