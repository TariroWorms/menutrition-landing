Kabobi zamini (Tajik braised lamb with potatoes)

Origin: Tajikistan

Kabobi zamini, literally 'earth kabob', is a homely Tajik dish of lamb braised with potatoes. The meat is first browned in a kazan with onion, then tomato and spices are added and potatoes simmer in the thick sauce until they absorb its flavour. The result is a hearty, mildly spiced stew with tender meat. It is eaten with flatbread and a fresh salad.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g lamb shoulder, in pieces
  • 800 g potatoes, in large chunks
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp cumin, salt, pepper
  • cilantro

Method

  1. Brown the lamb in hot oil in a heavy pot.
  2. Add the onion and fry until golden.
  3. Stir in the tomatoes and spices and cook 5 minutes.
  4. Add a glass of hot water, cover and braise 45 minutes.
  5. Add the potatoes and cook covered 25-30 minutes until tender; garnish with cilantro.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean lamb leg and skim the fat from the sauce.
  • Swap part of the potatoes for carrots or peppers.
  • Serve with salad rather than a lot of bread.

History

'Zamini' comes from sibzamini, the Tajik word for potato, literally 'apple of the earth'. Potatoes became a common crop in Tajikistan during the twentieth century and quickly entered everyday cooking. This dish is a staple of family dinners and canteens.
