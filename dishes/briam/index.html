🍆 Briam (Greek roasted vegetables)

Briam is a Greek oven dish of sliced potatoes, courgettes, aubergine, tomato and onion, roasted slowly in olive oil with garlic and herbs until soft, sweet and slightly caramelised at the edges. It belongs to the Greek family of ladera, vegetable dishes cooked in olive oil. It is eaten warm or at room temperature as a main course with bread or as a side.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g potatoes, thinly sliced
  • 2 courgettes, sliced
  • 1 aubergine, sliced
  • 2 red onions, sliced
  • 4 ripe tomatoes, grated, plus 2 sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, chopped parsley, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 190 °C.
  2. Toss the potatoes, courgettes, aubergine and onions with the garlic, oregano, salt, pepper and 3 tbsp of the olive oil.
  3. Spread in a large baking dish, pour over the grated tomato and lay the tomato slices on top.
  4. Drizzle with the remaining oil, add 100 ml water, cover with foil and bake for 45 minutes.
  5. Uncover and bake for another 30 minutes until the vegetables are soft and browned at the edges. Sprinkle with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Olive oil is the main calorie source — 2–3 tbsp for the whole dish instead of the generous traditional pour still works (1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal).
  • Use more courgette and aubergine and fewer potatoes for a lighter tray.
  • Add cooked chickpeas or beans to turn it into a more filling main.

History

Briam is a summer dish of Greek home cooking, made when vegetables are at their cheapest and best. Similar oven-baked vegetable dishes are made in Turkey and the Balkans. Today it is a standard in Greek tavernas and a favourite meal during meatless fasting periods.
