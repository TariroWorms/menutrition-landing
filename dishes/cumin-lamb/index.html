🍖 Cumin lamb

Origin: China · Xinjiang and Sichuan

Cumin lamb is a fiery stir-fry of thinly sliced lamb tossed in a wok with whole and ground cumin, dried chili, onion and scallion. The meat comes out browned at the edges and tender inside, with a smoky, earthy aroma and a lingering heat. It is served hot with steamed rice as part of a shared spread.

What it is made of

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g boneless lamb leg or shoulder, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp cumin seeds and 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1–2 tsp chili flakes and 6 dried chilies
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 3 scallions, cut into 4 cm lengths
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt

Method

  1. Season the lamb with salt and ground cumin and leave for 10 minutes.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok until very hot and sear the lamb in batches for 1–2 minutes until browned; set aside.
  3. Fry the cumin seeds, dried chilies and garlic for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  4. Add the onion and stir-fry for 2 minutes, keeping it slightly crisp.
  5. Return the lamb, add the chili flakes and scallions, toss for 1 minute and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before slicing, or use lean leg instead of shoulder.
  • Sear in a well-heated pan so 1 tbsp oil is enough; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Bulk up the plate with extra onion and scallion, or serve it with a small portion of rice and a side of greens.

History

The pairing of cumin and lamb comes from the Uyghur cooking of Xinjiang, known for its street skewers. As a wok stir-fry, cumin lamb is a fixture of Sichuan and Hunan restaurant menus in China and abroad.
