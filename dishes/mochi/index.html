🍡 Mochi

Origin: Japan

Mochi is a Japanese sweet made from glutinous rice powder (mochiko) mixed with water and sugar, steamed into a soft, stretchy dough and wrapped around a filling of sweet red bean paste. The outside is smooth and chewy, the inside dense and sweet. Small rounds are dusted with starch and served with green tea as a snack or dessert.

What it is made of

glutinous rice floursugarred bean paste

Recipe

8 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g glutinous rice powder (mochiko)
  • 50 g sugar
  • 180 ml water
  • 200 g sweet red bean paste
  • Potato or corn starch, for dusting

Method

  1. Divide the bean paste into 8 balls.
  2. Mix rice powder, sugar and water into a smooth batter in a heatproof bowl.
  3. Steam for 15–20 minutes, stirring halfway, until thick and translucent.
  4. Turn onto a starch-dusted surface, divide into 8 pieces and flatten each.
  5. Wrap each around a bean ball, pinch closed and dust off excess starch.

How to make it lighter

  • One piece is a sensible portion; mochi is dense and sweet.
  • Cut the sugar in the dough, since the bean paste already brings sweetness.
  • Pair it with unsweetened green tea instead of a sweet drink.

History

Rice cakes pounded from glutinous rice have been made in Japan for many centuries and are closely tied to New Year and other celebrations. Sweet filled versions made with rice powder are now sold everywhere from traditional confectioners to convenience stores.
