Palow (Turkmen pilaf)

Origin: Turkmenistan

Palow is the festive centrepiece of Turkmen cooking: rice steamed in a heavy cauldron over a base of mutton, onion and sweet yellow carrots cut into long strips. The rice absorbs the fat and juices, so each grain is glossy and full of flavour. It is served on a large shared platter with the meat on top, usually with green tea and fresh salad.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g mutton or lamb, in pieces
  • 500 g long-grain rice
  • 600 g carrots, in thin strips
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 120 ml vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 head garlic
  • salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Soak the rice in salted water for 30 minutes.
  2. Heat oil in a heavy pot and brown the meat well, then add onion.
  3. Add carrots and fry until soft, season with cumin, salt and pepper.
  4. Pour in water to cover by 1 cm and simmer 30 minutes (this is the zirvak).
  5. Drain the rice, spread it on top, add water to cover by 1.5 cm and push in the garlic head.
  6. Cook until the water is absorbed, then cover tightly and steam on low heat 25 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the oil; the meat releases plenty of fat.
  • Use more carrots and less meat.
  • Serve a big tomato and cucumber salad alongside to balance the meal.

History

Pilaf is cooked throughout Central Asia, and in Turkmenistan palow is essential at weddings, holidays and big family meals. Making it in a kazan for many guests is traditionally a man's job and a point of pride.
