Shito (Ghanaian black pepper sauce)

Origin: Ghana

Shito is a dark, oily, intensely savoury pepper sauce made of chili, onion, ginger and dried fish and shrimp cooked slowly in oil. It is hot, salty and slightly smoky with a deep umami taste. A spoonful goes a long way on rice, kenkey, waakye, fried yam or even bread. It keeps for weeks in a jar.

Recipe

20 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g scotch bonnet pepper
  • 50 g dried fish powder
  • 30 g dried shrimp
  • 300 g vegetable oil
  • 150 g onion
  • 30 g fresh ginger

Method

  1. Blend onion, ginger and chili into a coarse paste.
  2. Heat oil and fry the paste on low heat for 20 minutes.
  3. Add dried fish and shrimp powder, tomato paste and spices.
  4. Cook gently for 25 minutes, stirring until dark and the oil separates.
  5. Cool and store in a clean jar.

How to make it lighter

  • Use it as a condiment by the teaspoon, not as a main sauce.
  • Strain some oil off the surface after cooking.
  • Pair with plain boiled foods to balance the strong salt.

History

Shito is an essential condiment in Ghana, and its name is simply the word for pepper in the Ga language. Each family and cook has a personal recipe.
