🍳 Eggs with tomato (baid tamat)

Origin: Gulf · Arabian Peninsula

Baid tamat is an everyday Gulf breakfast of eggs cooked down with tomatoes and onions. The onion is softened in a little butter, the tomatoes are cooked until jammy, and then the eggs are stirred in until just set, seasoned with black pepper and often a pinch of turmeric or cumin. It is soft, savoury and slightly tangy, served hot straight from the pan and scooped up with flatbread.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs
  • 3 ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 15 g butter
  • ½ tsp black pepper, ¼ tsp turmeric, salt
  • 1 green chili, chopped (optional), and chopped coriander to serve

Method

  1. Melt the butter in a frying pan and cook the onion over medium heat until soft and golden, about 5 minutes.
  2. Add the tomatoes, chili, turmeric, black pepper and salt and cook for 8–10 minutes, stirring, until the tomatoes break down into a thick sauce.
  3. Lower the heat, crack in the eggs and stir gently until just set and still soft.
  4. Sprinkle with coriander and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter, or a teaspoon of oil, and let the tomatoes cook in their own juice.
  • Make it with 3 eggs and an extra tomato and onion for a bigger, lighter pan.
  • Serve it with cucumber, extra tomato and fresh herbs on the side for a fuller plate without many extra calories.

History

Eggs cooked with tomato are a common home breakfast across the Arabian Peninsula, from Kuwait and Bahrain to the UAE and Oman, and are known by the Gulf dialect name baid tamat.
