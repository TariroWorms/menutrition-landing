Torta negra (Venezuelan dark fruitcake)

Origin: Venezuela

Torta negra is a heavy, almost black cake made with melted papelón, butter, eggs and a large amount of dried fruit such as raisins, prunes and candied peel, plus warm spices. Many families soak the fruit in advance to deepen the flavour. Its crumb is moist, dense and intensely sweet. It is a Christmas and wedding cake, eaten in thin slices.

Recipe

16 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 250 g papelón or dark brown sugar
  • 200 g butter
  • 5 eggs
  • 500 g mixed raisins, prunes and candied fruit
  • 1 tsp each cinnamon, nutmeg and clove
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 150 ml strong black coffee or fruit juice for soaking

Method

  1. Soak the chopped dried fruit in warm coffee or juice overnight.
  2. Melt the papelón with a splash of water into a dark syrup and let it cool.
  3. Cream the butter, beat in the eggs one by one, then the syrup.
  4. Fold in flour, baking powder, spices and the drained fruit.
  5. Bake in a lined tin at 160 °C for 70–80 minutes until a skewer comes out clean; cool before slicing.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thin slices: a little goes a long way.
  • Use more prunes and fewer candied fruits to reduce added sugar.
  • Replace part of the butter with unsweetened apple purée.

History

It descends from the British and Spanish tradition of rich fruit cakes and became a fixture of Venezuelan Christmas alongside hallacas and pan de jamón. Its dark colour comes mainly from papelón and caramelised sugar.
