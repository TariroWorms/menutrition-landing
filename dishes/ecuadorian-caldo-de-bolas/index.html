Caldo de bolas (plantain dumpling soup)

Origin: Ecuador · Coast

Caldo de bolas is a coastal soup in which grated green plantain is shaped into balls the size of an orange and filled with a stew of minced beef, peas, egg and peanuts. They are poached in a beef broth with corn, cassava and squash. The dumplings are dense and satisfying, the broth light and aromatic. One bowl is a whole meal.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 6 green plantains
  • 500 g beef shank
  • 300 g minced beef
  • 2 eggs, hard-boiled and chopped
  • 50 g ground peanuts
  • 100 g peas
  • 2 corn cobs, 300 g cassava
  • onion, garlic, cumin, achiote, cilantro, salt

Method

  1. Simmer the beef shank with onion and garlic for about an hour to make a broth.
  2. Cook the minced beef with onion, cumin, peas and peanuts, then mix in the chopped egg.
  3. Grate the peeled plantains and knead with a little achiote oil and broth into a dough.
  4. Flatten portions of dough, fill with the meat and close into large balls.
  5. Add corn and cassava to the broth, then the balls, and simmer for 25 minutes.
  6. Serve each bowl with one or two balls, vegetables and cilantro.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef and skim the fat from the broth.
  • One dumpling per person with extra vegetables is plenty.
  • Reduce the peanuts if you need fewer calories.

History

Green plantain is a staple of Ecuador's coast, where it replaces bread and potatoes in many dishes. Caldo de bolas is a traditional Sunday lunch in Guayaquil and Manabí. Making the dumplings is often a family job.
