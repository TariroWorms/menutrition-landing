A lollipop is a hard candy on a stick, made by boiling sugar and corn syrup until it reaches the hard-crack stage, then adding flavouring and colour and moulding it into discs, spheres or spirals. It is glassy, brittle and sweet, and is meant to be licked or sucked slowly. Lollipops come in every fruit flavour imaginable and are a classic children's treat.

Recipe

12 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g sugar
  • 100 ml corn syrup
  • 100 ml water
  • 1 tsp fruit flavouring
  • A few drops of food colouring
  • 12 lollipop sticks
  • Neutral oil for greasing

Method

  1. Lightly oil a tray or lollipop moulds and lay out the sticks.
  2. Heat sugar, corn syrup and water, stirring until dissolved, then boil without stirring.
  3. Cook until the syrup reaches 150 °C on a sugar thermometer.
  4. Take off the heat, quickly stir in the flavouring and colouring.
  5. Pour small rounds over the stick ends, let them harden for 20 minutes and wrap.

How to make it lighter

  • A small lollipop is a contained treat — one is usually enough to satisfy a sweet craving.
  • Make mini lollipops so each portion is smaller.
  • Savour it slowly instead of crunching it; it lasts longer and you are less likely to reach for a second.

History

Lollipops are sold and eaten all over the world today, from simple fruit discs at supermarket counters to large swirled candies at fairs and sweet shops.
