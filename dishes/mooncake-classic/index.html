🥮 Lotus seed mooncake

Origin: China · Guangdong

The classic Cantonese mooncake is a round baked pastry with a thin, glossy golden crust pressed with decorative patterns. Inside is a dense, smooth and sweet lotus seed paste around one or two salted egg yolks, which add a rich, savoury contrast and look like a full moon when sliced. Mooncakes are cut into small wedges and shared with tea.

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g plain flour
  • 130 g golden syrup
  • 50 ml vegetable oil, 1/2 tsp alkaline water
  • 600 g lotus seed paste
  • 8 salted egg yolks
  • 1 egg yolk for glazing

Method

  1. Mix the syrup, oil and alkaline water, then stir in the flour to a soft dough; rest for 1 hour.
  2. Divide the lotus paste into 8 balls and wrap each around a salted yolk.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 pieces, flatten each and wrap around a filling ball.
  4. Press into a mooncake mould and turn out onto a lined tray.
  5. Bake at 180 °C for 5 minutes, brush with egg yolk and bake 12–15 minutes more until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • A whole mooncake is a lot; cut it into 4–8 wedges and share it.
  • Choose versions without the egg yolk or with a single yolk.
  • Pair a small slice with unsweetened tea instead of a sweet drink.

History

Mooncakes are eaten and given as gifts during the Mid-Autumn Festival, when families gather to admire the full moon. The Cantonese style with lotus paste and salted yolk is the best known version today; the popular story that messages hidden in mooncakes helped an uprising against Mongol rule is a legend.
