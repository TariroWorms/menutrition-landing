Arroz a la tumbada (Veracruz seafood rice)

Origin: Mexico · Veracruz

Arroz a la tumbada is a loose, brothy rice from the Veracruz coast, cooked in a sofrito of tomato, onion, garlic and pepper with mixed seafood. It is wetter than a paella, closer to a thick soup, and fragrant with cilantro, epazote or mint. It is served in deep plates with lime and is eaten with a spoon.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g long-grain rice
  • 300 g shrimp, peeled
  • 200 g squid, in rings
  • 2 crabs, cleaned and halved
  • 3 tomatoes, blended
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1.2 l fish broth
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Cilantro, lime

Method

  1. Fry the onion, garlic and pepper in oil until soft.
  2. Add the blended tomato and cook 8 minutes to a thick sofrito.
  3. Stir in the rice for 2 minutes, then pour in the hot broth and add the crab.
  4. Simmer 12 minutes, then add the squid and shrimp and cook 5 more minutes.
  5. Turn off the heat while still soupy, add cilantro and serve with lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Use more seafood and less rice for extra protein.
  • Use a wide non-stick pot so the sofrito needs only a little oil.
  • Add diced zucchini or peas for more vegetables.

History

The dish reflects the Spanish rice tradition adapted by the fishermen and port cooks of Veracruz, Alvarado and Boca del Río. Its name is usually explained by the rice being "tumbado", thrown into the pot of broth.
