Quipe (Dominican fried bulgur croquettes)

Origin: Dominican Republic

Quipe is a torpedo-shaped croquette with a bulgur shell filled with seasoned ground beef, onion and herbs. Deep-fried, it has a crunchy crust and a soft, spiced interior. It is a favourite street snack and party appetizer, often eaten with hot sauce or lime.

Recipe

6 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g fine bulgur
  • 300 g ground beef
  • 1 onion, minced
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp dried mint
  • 1 tbsp flour
  • oil for frying

Method

  1. Soak the bulgur in warm water for 30 minutes, drain and squeeze dry.
  2. Knead with a little salt and flour into a firm dough.
  3. Cook the beef with onion, garlic, cumin and mint until dry.
  4. Form a small ball of dough, hollow it, fill with beef and close into a torpedo shape.
  5. Heat oil to 175 C.
  6. Fry the quipes 4-5 minutes until deep golden and drain.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake at 200 C with a brush of oil instead of frying.
  • Use lean beef or add chopped vegetables to the filling.
  • Serve with a fresh salad and yogurt sauce.

History

Quipe is the Dominican version of Lebanese and Syrian kibbeh, brought by Arab immigrants in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It became so popular that it is now a staple of Dominican snack stands and birthday tables.
