🍓 Gruyère meringues with double cream

Origin: Switzerland · Gruyère, Fribourg

Gruyère meringues are large, crisp meringue shells served with the region's famously thick double cream and fresh berries. The meringue shatters into a sweet, airy crunch, while the cream is rich and almost spoonable. It is a simple dessert served in mountain restaurants and farm inns, the cream often spooned generously over the top.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 3 egg whites
  • 180 g caster sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 200 ml double cream
  • 250 g mixed fresh berries

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 100 °C and line a tray with baking paper.
  2. Whisk the egg whites with the salt to soft peaks, then add the sugar a spoonful at a time and whisk to stiff, glossy peaks.
  3. Spoon 8 large ovals onto the tray and bake for about 2 hours, until crisp and dry; cool in the oven.
  4. Serve two meringues per person with a spoonful of double cream and the berries.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a smaller spoonful of cream — it carries most of the fat.
  • Make the berries the bulk of the plate and serve one meringue instead of two.
  • Share one dessert portion between two people after a large meal.

History

The Gruyère district in the Swiss canton of Fribourg is known for its dairy, and its thick 'crème double' is a local speciality. Meringues served with this cream have become the area's signature dessert and are a standard in restaurants around the town of Gruyères today.
