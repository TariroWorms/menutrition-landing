🍲 Rasam

Origin: India · Tamil Nadu

Rasam is a thin, tangy South Indian soup made from tamarind and tomato, sharpened with black pepper, cumin and garlic and finished with a tempering of mustard seeds and curry leaves. It is light, sour and peppery, with a warming kick. Rasam is poured over rice or sipped from a cup as part of a South Indian meal.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 30 g tamarind, soaked in 250 ml warm water
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 tsp black pepper, coarsely ground
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 10 curry leaves
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 600 ml water

Method

  1. Squeeze the tamarind and strain the liquid.
  2. Lightly crush the pepper, cumin and garlic together.
  3. Simmer the tamarind water, tomatoes, crushed spices, salt and water for 10–15 minutes until frothy.
  4. Heat the oil, add the mustard seeds and curry leaves and let them splutter.
  5. Pour the tempering into the rasam and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Sip a cup of rasam before the main course to start the meal with a warm, light soup.
  • Keep the tempering to 1 tbsp oil for the whole pot — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Eat it as a soup rather than over large portions of rice.

History

Rasam is a staple of Tamil, Karnataka, Andhra and Kerala home cooking, where it is served as a course of the traditional meal. Many families make it daily, with versions based on pepper, lemon, tomato or garlic.
