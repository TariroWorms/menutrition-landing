Oha soup

Origin: Nigeria · Igbo

Oha is a classic Igbo soup. Tender oha leaves are added to a base thickened with cocoyam, cooked with palm oil, meat, stockfish and crayfish. It is earthy, fragrant and slightly viscous. It is served with pounded yam, fufu or garri.

Recipe

6 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g oha (or spinach) leaves
  • 150 g cocoyam or yam paste
  • 100 g palm oil
  • 100 g stockfish
  • 500 g assorted meats (beef, tripe)
  • 40 g ground dried crayfish
  • 2 g scotch bonnet peppers
  • 150 g onions

Method

  1. Boil the meats and stockfish with onion and seasoning until tender.
  2. Add the cocoyam paste in small lumps and let it dissolve into the stock.
  3. Add palm oil, crayfish and pepper and simmer 15 minutes.
  4. Stir in the shredded oha leaves.
  5. Cook 5 minutes and adjust the salt.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less palm oil and skim the surface to cut saturated fat.
  • Choose lean meat or fish and add extra leafy greens.
  • Season with spices and herbs to reduce salt and stock cubes.

History

Oha leaves are traditionally tied to Igbo cooking in the southeast and the soup is common at feasts and for visitors.
