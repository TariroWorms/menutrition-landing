Ewa agoyin (mashed beans with pepper sauce)

Origin: Nigeria · Lagos
480kcal

Ewa agoyin is a dish of beans boiled until very soft and lightly mashed, served with a dark, slow-cooked sauce of fried dried peppers, onion and palm oil. It is earthy, smoky and fiery. It is eaten with soft bread such as agege bread.

What it is made of

beansred palm oilonionchili peppersalt

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g brown beans
  • 150 ml palm oil
  • 3 onions
  • 8 dried chili peppers, ground
  • 1 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 pinch baking soda
  • bread to serve

Method

  1. Boil the beans with a pinch of baking soda and salt until very soft.
  2. Drain most of the water and mash roughly.
  3. Heat palm oil until it darkens, without smoking.
  4. Fry sliced onions until dark brown.
  5. Stir in ground chili and crayfish and fry slowly 20 minutes until deep dark.
  6. Serve the beans topped with the sauce, with bread.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less oil in the sauce for a lighter dish.
  • Eat with wholemeal bread.
  • Add a salad to increase vegetables.

History

The dish is closely linked with Lagos, where street vendors sell it with fresh bread. Its name is often explained by Beninese (Agoyin) roots, and it has become a staple of Lagos street food.
