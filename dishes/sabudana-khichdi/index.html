Sabudana khichdi

Origin: India · Maharashtra

Sabudana khichdi is a popular Maharashtrian dish, especially on fasting days. Tapioca pearls are soaked until soft, then tossed with roasted peanuts, potato, cumin and green chilli. Done right, each pearl stays separate and translucent, with a pleasant chew and a nutty crunch.

Recipe

3 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g sabudana (tapioca pearls)
  • 80 g roasted peanuts, coarsely crushed
  • 1 medium potato, diced
  • 2 tbsp ghee or oil
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 green chillies, chopped
  • Salt, sugar pinch, lemon juice, fresh coriander

Method

  1. Rinse the sabudana until the water runs clear, then soak with just enough water to cover for 4-6 hours.
  2. Drain well and mix with crushed peanuts, salt and a pinch of sugar.
  3. Heat the ghee and splutter the cumin and green chillies.
  4. Fry the potato until tender and lightly browned.
  5. Add the sabudana mixture and stir gently on medium heat for 5-6 minutes until translucent.
  6. Finish with lemon juice and coriander.

How to make it lighter

  • Do not over-soak the pearls or they turn gluey; they should be soft but separate.
  • Use less ghee and add more potato or vegetables to lower the calories.
  • Serve with plain yogurt for extra protein.

History

Sabudana is made from the starch of the cassava root, which became an important crop in India in the nineteenth century. Because it is a root-based starch, it is widely eaten during religious fasts such as Navratri and Ekadashi. The dish is also a common breakfast and tiffin item in Maharashtra.
