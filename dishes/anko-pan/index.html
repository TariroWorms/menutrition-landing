🍞 Anpan

Origin: Japan · Tokyo

Anpan is a soft, round Japanese sweet bun filled with anko, a smooth or chunky paste of red azuki beans cooked with sugar. The bread is light and slightly sweet, with a glossy brown top, and the filling is dense and earthy-sweet. It is sold in bakeries and convenience stores across Japan and eaten as a snack or quick breakfast.

Recipe

8 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 350 g strong bread flour
  • 40 g sugar, 5 g salt
  • 6 g dried yeast
  • 200 ml lukewarm water
  • 25 ml vegetable oil
  • 400 g sweet red bean paste (anko)
  • 1 tbsp sugar dissolved in 2 tbsp water for glazing

Method

  1. Mix the flour, sugar, salt and yeast, add the water and oil and knead for 10 minutes into a smooth, soft dough. Cover and leave to rise for 1 hour, until doubled.
  2. Divide the dough into 8 pieces and the bean paste into 8 balls of 50 g.
  3. Flatten each piece of dough, wrap it around a ball of bean paste, pinch to seal and set seam-side down on a lined tray. Press the tops gently to flatten.
  4. Leave to rise for 40 minutes, brush with sugar water and bake at 180 °C for 13–15 minutes until golden brown.
  5. Brush once more with sugar water while hot and cool on a rack.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 35 g of bean paste per bun instead of 50 g — the sweet filling carries much of the energy.
  • Make smaller buns from the same dough: 12 instead of 8.
  • Skip the second sugar glaze; a plain water brush still gives a soft crust.

History

Anpan is usually credited to Kimura Yasubei, a former samurai who opened a bakery in Tokyo and created the bun in the 1870s by putting a familiar Japanese filling inside Western-style bread. The story goes that it was presented to Emperor Meiji in 1875. Anpan is still one of the most popular bakery items in Japan.
