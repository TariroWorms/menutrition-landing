Kuchmachi (offal with walnuts and pomegranate)

Origin: Georgia · Western Georgia

Kuchmachi is a warm dish of hearts, livers, gizzards or beef tripe and lungs that are boiled, chopped and fried with onion. Ground walnuts, garlic, coriander, blue fenugreek and hot pepper are added, and the dish is finished with a scattering of bright pomegranate seeds. It is savoury, spicy and slightly sweet-sour, eaten hot with bread or as part of a feast.

Recipe

4 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g chicken hearts, livers and gizzards
  • 2 onions (200 g)
  • 100 g walnuts
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp ground coriander, blue fenugreek and chili
  • 1 pomegranate (seeds, about 120 g)
  • 2 tbsp oil (25 ml)
  • small bunch of cilantro

Method

  1. Clean the offal, boil it in salted water until tender (30–40 minutes for chicken) and drain.
  2. Chop it into small pieces.
  3. Fry the onion in oil, add the offal and cook until lightly browned.
  4. Stir in walnuts ground with garlic and the spices, add a little broth and cook 5–10 minutes.
  5. Serve hot, topped with pomegranate seeds and cilantro.

How to make it lighter

  • Offal is rich in iron and B12, so a modest portion goes a long way.
  • Use less oil and fewer walnuts for a lighter result.
  • Serve with a fresh salad rather than fried potatoes.

History

Kuchmachi comes from western Georgia, where walnut-and-spice combinations are typical. Using every part of the animal is a long-standing habit in Georgian village cooking.
