🌿 Kuku sabzi

Kuku sabzi is a Persian herb frittata in which the herbs are the main ingredient and the eggs simply bind them. Large amounts of parsley, coriander, dill and spring onion are chopped and mixed with eggs, then studded with walnuts and tart barberries. Cooked slowly in a pan or baked, it turns deep green, dense and fragrant, and is served warm or cold in wedges.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 6 eggs
  • 100 g parsley, finely chopped
  • 80 g coriander, finely chopped
  • 40 g dill, finely chopped
  • 4 spring onions, finely chopped
  • 40 g walnuts, chopped
  • 2 tbsp dried barberries, rinsed
  • ½ tsp turmeric, salt and pepper
  • 2 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180 °C. Beat the eggs with turmeric, salt and pepper.
  2. Stir in all the herbs and spring onion — the mixture should be thick and very green — then add the walnuts and barberries.
  3. Heat the oil in an ovenproof pan, pour in the mixture and cook on low heat for 5 minutes.
  4. Bake for 20–25 minutes until set in the middle, then cool slightly and cut into wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tbsp oil in a non-stick pan instead of 2 — each tablespoon is about 120 kcal.
  • Halve the walnuts or chop them finely so a smaller amount spreads further.
  • Serve with a large salad or vegetables to make a fuller plate.

History

Kuku sabzi is a traditional part of the Nowruz (Persian New Year) table, where the green herbs are associated with spring and renewal. It is eaten across Iran today as a starter, a light meal or picnic food.
