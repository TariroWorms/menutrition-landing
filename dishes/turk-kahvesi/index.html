☕ Türk kahvesi

Türk kahvesi is coffee ground to a fine powder and simmered with cold water, and sugar if wanted, in a small long-handled pot called a cezve. Heated slowly, it rises into a thick foam and is poured unfiltered into small cups, so the grounds settle at the bottom. It is strong, intense and velvety, and is traditionally served with a glass of water and often a small sweet.

Recipe

1 servings5 min

Ingredients

  • 70 ml cold water
  • 7 g very finely ground coffee (1 heaped tsp)
  • Sugar to taste (0–1 tsp)

Method

  1. Put the cold water, coffee and sugar into a cezve and stir once to combine.
  2. Heat slowly over low heat without stirring until a thick foam rises to the rim.
  3. Spoon some foam into the cup, then pour in the coffee gently.
  4. Let it rest for a minute so the grounds settle, and drink without stirring.

How to make it lighter

  • Order it plain (sade) — without sugar the cup has almost no calories.
  • If you take it sweet, 1 tsp sugar ≈ 16 kcal; medium (orta) usually means about one teaspoon, sweet (şekerli) about two.
  • The sweet served alongside often has more calories than the coffee — enjoy it or skip it consciously.

History

Coffee reached Istanbul in the 16th century, and the Ottoman way of brewing it in a cezve spread across the empire. Turkish coffee culture and tradition were inscribed by UNESCO on its list of intangible cultural heritage in 2013.
