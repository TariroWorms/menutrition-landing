🍗 Ayam penyet (smashed fried chicken)

Origin: Indonesia · East Java

Ayam penyet is fried chicken smashed with a stone pestle onto a mortar of fiery sambal, so the crisp, flattened meat soaks up the chili. It comes as a full plate: steamed rice, fried tofu and tempeh, a heap of sambal and lalapan — raw cucumber, cabbage, basil and long beans that cool the heat. It is crunchy, spicy and very filling.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces, simmered for 25 minutes in spiced water (garlic, turmeric, coriander, salt)
  • 200 g firm tofu and 200 g tempeh, cut into thick slices
  • Sambal: 10 red chilies, 5 bird's-eye chilies, 4 shallots, 2 garlic cloves, 2 tomatoes, 1 tsp shrimp paste (terasi), 1 tsp sugar, salt
  • 4 portions steamed rice (about 600 g cooked)
  • Lalapan: cucumber, white cabbage, long beans, Thai basil
  • Vegetable oil for frying

Method

  1. Fry the chilies, shallots, garlic and tomatoes in a little oil until soft, then pound them in a mortar with the toasted shrimp paste, sugar and salt into a rough sambal.
  2. Pat the simmered chicken dry and deep-fry at 180 °C until crisp and golden. Fry the tofu and tempeh until golden.
  3. Place each piece of chicken on the sambal in the mortar and press it firmly with the pestle to flatten it slightly.
  4. Serve with rice, tofu, tempeh, extra sambal and a pile of raw lalapan vegetables.

How to make it lighter

  • Fried tofu and tempeh add plenty of protein — choose them over a second piece of chicken.
  • Take half the rice and twice the lalapan: raw vegetables add volume for almost no calories.
  • Ask for skinless breast, or at home pan-fry with 1 tbsp oil (≈120 kcal) instead of deep-frying.

History

Penyet means “pressed” or “smashed” in Javanese, after the way the chicken is flattened into the sambal. The dish is usually linked to East Java, and it has since spread across Indonesia and on to Malaysia and Singapore through restaurants that specialise in it.
