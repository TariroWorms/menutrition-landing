Morcilla (grilled blood sausage)

Origin: Argentina

Morcilla is a dark sausage of pork blood, fat and onion, sometimes with orange peel, nuts or spices depending on the maker. At the asado it is one of the first things to come off the grill, eaten as a starter. The skin blisters and crackles while the filling becomes soft and almost spreadable. Many people smear it straight onto bread.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 4 morcilla sausages (about 500 g)
  • Crusty bread to serve

Method

  1. Prepare medium-low embers.
  2. Place the morcilla on the grill without pricking the skin.
  3. Grill 10–12 minutes, turning gently once.
  4. Take them off as soon as the skin is crisp and the inside is hot.
  5. Cut into pieces and serve with bread as a starter.

How to make it lighter

  • Morcilla is high in fat; treat it as a tasting starter, about half a sausage each.
  • It is rich in iron, which is a real plus in small portions.
  • Skip the bread and pair with a fresh tomato salad.

History

Blood sausages arrived in Argentina with Spanish and other European immigrants. In the Río de la Plata they became a standard part of the achuras, the sausages and offal served before the main cuts.
