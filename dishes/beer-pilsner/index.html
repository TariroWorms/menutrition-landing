Pilsner is a pale golden lager with a bright, clean taste: a light malt body, lively carbonation and a crisp, floral hop bitterness that leaves a dry, refreshing finish. Czech pilsners are a little fuller and softer, German pils drier and more bitter. It is served well chilled in a tall, tapered glass under a dense white head.

Recipe

1 servings2 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml pilsner beer
  • A tall tapered pilsner glass
  • Well chilled, around 4–7 °C

Method

  1. Rinse the glass with cold water.
  2. Tilt the glass slightly and pour steadily, straightening it halfway.
  3. Finish pouring into the centre to build a thick, creamy head of two to three fingers. Serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • A 500 ml glass of pilsner is about 200 kcal.
  • A 300 ml glass instead of a half-litre saves around 80 kcal per round.
  • Alternate each beer with sparkling water to slow the pace over an evening.

History

Pilsner is named after the Bohemian city of Plzeň (Pilsen in German), where the town's brewery produced a clear golden lager in 1842, brewed by the Bavarian brewer Josef Groll. Its clear golden colour, soft local water and Saaz hops made it hugely popular, and it became the model for most pale lagers brewed around the world.
