🍞 Pain d'Épices

Origin: France · Burgundy & Alsace

Pain d'épices is a French honey spice bread made with rye flour, honey and warm spices such as cinnamon, ginger, anise and cloves, enriched with egg and a little butter. It bakes into a dense, moist loaf with a dark crust and a fragrant, gently sweet crumb that improves after a day or two. It is sliced thin and eaten at breakfast or with tea, especially around Christmas.

What it is made of

Recipe

10 servings80 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g honey
  • 250 g rye flour
  • 2 tsp mixed spices (cinnamon, ginger, anise, cloves)
  • 1 egg
  • 40 g butter
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 100 ml water
  • 1 pinch salt

Method

  1. Warm the honey, butter and water in a pan until melted; let cool slightly.
  2. Mix the rye flour, spices, baking soda and salt in a bowl.
  3. Pour in the honey mixture, add the egg and stir into a smooth, thick batter.
  4. Pour into a lined loaf tin and bake at 160 °C for 50–60 minutes until a skewer comes out clean.
  5. Cool completely and wrap; it slices best the next day.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thin slices of about 40–50 g and enjoy them with tea rather than spread with extras.
  • It is sweet and dense, so one slice can replace a dessert.
  • Pair a slice with fruit for a more filling snack.

History

Honey spice breads were made across medieval Europe, and in France the towns of Reims and Dijon became known for pain d'épices; Dijon is still closely associated with it. In Alsace it is a traditional part of Christmas markets.
