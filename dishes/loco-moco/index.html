🍳 Loco moco

Origin: United States · Hawaii

Loco moco is a Hawaiian plate of white rice topped with a grilled hamburger patty, a fried egg and plenty of brown gravy, often with sautéed onions. The runny yolk mixes into the gravy and rice, making it rich, savoury and filling. It is a classic of Hawaiian diners and plate-lunch counters, eaten for breakfast, lunch or late at night.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g minced beef, shaped into 4 patties
  • 600 g cooked white rice
  • 4 eggs
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 500 ml beef stock
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt and pepper
  • Sliced spring onion to serve

Method

  1. Season the patties and fry them in 1 tbsp oil for 4–5 minutes per side; set aside.
  2. In the same pan soften the onion, sprinkle in the flour and stir for 1 minute.
  3. Whisk in the stock and simmer until the gravy thickens; season with salt and pepper.
  4. Fry the eggs in the remaining oil, keeping the yolks runny.
  5. Spoon rice onto plates, add a patty, a fried egg and a generous ladle of gravy; finish with spring onion.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef and drain the fat from the pan before making the gravy.
  • Serve a smaller scoop of rice with a side of steamed vegetables.
  • Fry the egg in a non-stick pan with a spray of oil — each tablespoon of oil is about 120 kcal.

History

Loco moco is usually credited to the Lincoln Grill in Hilo, on Hawaii's Big Island, around 1949, where it is said to have been created as a cheap, filling meal for local teenagers. It has since become one of the best-known dishes of Hawaiian local food and appears in many variations.
