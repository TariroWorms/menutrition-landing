🍝 Bucatini all'amatriciana

Origin: Italy · Lazio (Amatrice)

Bucatini all'amatriciana is a Roman classic of thick, hollow spaghetti in a tomato sauce built on crisp guanciale, the cured pork cheek. A little chili gives warmth and grated pecorino romano adds a sharp, salty finish. The sauce clings inside and around each strand, making the dish savoury, tangy and filling. It is served straight from the pan with extra pecorino.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g bucatini
  • 150 g guanciale, cut into strips
  • 400 g canned peeled tomatoes, crushed
  • 60 g pecorino romano, grated
  • 1 small dried chili, crumbled
  • Salt

Method

  1. Cook the guanciale in a cold dry pan over medium heat until the fat renders and the edges crisp.
  2. Add the chili, then the tomatoes and a pinch of salt; simmer for 12–15 minutes.
  3. Boil the bucatini in salted water until al dente.
  4. Drain the pasta, toss it in the sauce with a splash of cooking water.
  5. Take off the heat, stir in most of the pecorino and serve with the rest on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 100 g guanciale for four and pour off part of the rendered fat — 1 tbsp of fat is about 120 kcal.
  • Serve 80 g of dry pasta per person and add a green salad on the side.
  • Grate pecorino finely: a small amount covers more pasta and still gives full flavour.

History

The sauce takes its name from Amatrice, a mountain town in Lazio, and is often said to have reached Rome with shepherds from the area. The tomato-free pasta alla gricia is usually described as its older relative. Bucatini is the shape most associated with it in Roman trattorias.
