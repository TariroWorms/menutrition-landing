🐟 Feseekh (fermented mullet)

Origin: Egypt

Feseekh is grey mullet that has been dried in the sun, then packed in salt and left to ferment for weeks until the flesh turns soft and intensely salty. Its smell is famously strong. It is eaten in small amounts as an appetizer, opened up and dressed with lemon juice, sliced onion and a little oil, and is a fixture of the Egyptian spring holiday Sham el-Nessim.

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 1 ready-made feseekh (about 400 g) from a trusted producer
  • 2 lemons, juiced
  • 2 onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 bunch spring onions
  • ground cumin and chilli flakes, to taste

Method

  1. Rinse the feseekh briefly, then open it, remove the skin and the bones.
  2. Cut the flesh into pieces and place on a serving plate.
  3. Pour over the lemon juice and oil, sprinkle with cumin and chilli.
  4. Top with sliced onion and serve with spring onions and plenty of lemon.

How to make it lighter

  • Feseekh is very salty: keep the portion small and drink plenty of water with it.
  • Serve it with a large plate of fresh vegetables such as lettuce, cucumber and radish to make the meal more filling.
  • Use just a teaspoon of oil in the dressing — the fish is already rich; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Salting and fermenting fish along the Nile is often traced back to ancient Egypt, and feseekh is closely tied to Sham el-Nessim, a spring festival with ancient roots. Making it is a specialist craft, and authorities in Egypt regularly warn to buy it only from trusted producers because badly prepared fish can be dangerous. Today it remains a much-loved, once-a-year treat for many Egyptian families.
