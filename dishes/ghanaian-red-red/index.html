Red red (Ghanaian bean stew with fried plantain)

Origin: Ghana

Red red is a bean stew named for its two reds: the red palm oil and the red tomato sauce. Black-eyed peas simmer until creamy in a spiced base of onion, ginger and pepper. It tastes earthy, smoky and a little sweet next to golden fried ripe plantain. It is often eaten at lunch and sold in street stalls.

Recipe

4 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g black-eyed peas
  • 70 g red palm oil
  • 400 g ripe plantain
  • 300 g tomatoes
  • 150 g onion
  • 5 g scotch bonnet pepper

Method

  1. Soak black-eyed peas for 4 hours, then boil 40 minutes until soft.
  2. Heat palm oil, fry onion, ginger and pepper for 8 minutes.
  3. Add tomato and cook until thick.
  4. Stir in the beans and simmer for 15 minutes, mashing a few.
  5. Fry ripe plantain slices and serve alongside.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake plantain slices instead of deep-frying.
  • Reduce the palm oil by a third.
  • Add gari for more fibre and a crunchy contrast.

History

Red red is a popular Ghanaian dish eaten across the country and is easily made vegan. The beans, called ewa in some regional cooking, are a staple protein for many families.
