Siserapur (Armenian chickpea soup)

Origin: Armenia

Siserapur is a simple, nourishing soup of chickpeas cooked until soft with onion, carrot and tomato. Part of the chickpeas are often mashed to give a creamy body. It is finished with lots of chopped parsley, cilantro and sometimes tarragon, and eaten with lavash, especially during Lent.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g dried chickpeas
  • 1 onion (120 g)
  • 1 carrot (100 g)
  • 2 tomatoes (200 g) or 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 20 g butter or oil
  • 1.8 l water
  • a bunch each of parsley and cilantro
  • salt, pepper, dried mint

Method

  1. Soak the chickpeas overnight, then drain.
  2. Cover with the water and simmer for 1–1.5 hours until very soft.
  3. Fry the onion and grated carrot in the butter until soft, then add the chopped tomato.
  4. Add the vegetables to the chickpeas and simmer 10 minutes more.
  5. Mash about a third of the chickpeas with a spoon to thicken the soup.
  6. Season, stir in the chopped herbs and a pinch of dried mint, and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use olive oil instead of butter to make it fully vegan for Lent.
  • Chickpeas are rich in fibre and protein, so a bowl keeps you full for a long time.
  • Add spinach or chard at the end for extra vegetables.

History

Chickpeas have been grown in the Armenian Highlands since ancient times, and legume soups are a mainstay of the Armenian Lenten table. Siserapur is one of the simplest of them.
