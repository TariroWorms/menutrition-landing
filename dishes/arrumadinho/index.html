Arrumadinho (layered beans and carne de sol)

Origin: Brazil · Pernambuco

Arrumadinho, "the neatly arranged one", is a plate from Pernambuco built in tidy rows. Seasoned black-eyed beans are laid next to cubes of fried carne de sol, a fresh vinaigrette of tomato, onion and pepper, and golden farofa. Each forkful mixes soft beans, salty meat and the sharp, juicy salad. It is common in bars and restaurants as a shared dish or a full lunch.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g cooked black-eyed peas
  • 400 g carne de sol, desalted and cubed
  • 2 tomatoes, 1 onion, 1 green pepper, finely diced
  • 2 tbsp vinegar and 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 150 g cassava flour toasted with 20 g butter
  • garlic, cilantro, spring onion, salt

Method

  1. Season the cooked beans with garlic, a little onion and cilantro; keep them firm.
  2. Fry the carne de sol cubes in a hot pan until browned.
  3. Mix the tomato, onion and pepper with vinegar, oil and salt for the vinaigrette.
  4. Toast the cassava flour in butter until golden to make farofa.
  5. Arrange beans, meat, vinaigrette and farofa side by side on a platter and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Increase the beans and vinaigrette and reduce the farofa.
  • Fry the meat with just a teaspoon of oil in a non-stick pan.
  • Desalt the meat well to control sodium.

History

Arrumadinho is associated with Recife and the interior of Pernambuco, built from staples of the northeastern table. The name describes the way the ingredients are arranged side by side rather than mixed.
