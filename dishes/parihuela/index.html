Parihuela (Peruvian seafood soup)

Origin: Peru

Parihuela is a robust coastal soup packed with fish and shellfish. The broth is coloured deep red by ají panca and tomato and gets a kick from fresh chile. It tastes of the sea, a little smoky and quite spicy. Peruvians eat it hot with a squeeze of lime, often as a hearty lunch.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g white fish, in pieces
  • 300 g shrimp
  • 400 g mussels
  • 200 g squid rings
  • 2 tomatoes (250 g)
  • 2 tbsp ají panca paste (30 g)
  • 1 onion (150 g)
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1.5 l fish stock
  • 1 tbsp oil, cilantro, lime

Method

  1. Fry chopped onion and garlic in the oil until soft.
  2. Stir in ají panca and grated tomato and cook for 8 minutes.
  3. Pour in the stock and simmer for 10 minutes.
  4. Add the fish and simmer for 5 minutes.
  5. Add shrimp, squid and mussels and cook until the mussels open.
  6. Season, scatter cilantro and serve with lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Use little oil for the base; the chile paste gives plenty of flavour.
  • Add extra fish and less shellfish to lower cholesterol.
  • Serve without bread or rice for a light, high-protein meal.

History

Parihuela comes from the fishing communities of the Peruvian coast, where cooks used whatever the boats brought in. It is widely seen as a restorative dish and is a fixture of cevicherías.
