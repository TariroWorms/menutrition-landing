🧁 Financiers

Financiers are small French almond cakes baked in rectangular moulds. The batter is made from ground almonds, sugar, egg whites and melted butter, which gives a crisp golden edge and a moist, nutty crumb inside. They are sold in French pastry shops and served with coffee or tea, usually two or three small cakes at a time.

Recipe

12 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g ground almonds
  • 130 g sugar
  • 4 egg whites (about 120 g)
  • 100 g butter
  • A pinch of salt

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200 °C and butter a financier or mini-muffin tray lightly.
  2. Melt the butter in a small pan and cook it until it smells nutty and turns light brown; let it cool slightly.
  3. Mix the ground almonds, sugar and salt, then stir in the egg whites until smooth.
  4. Stir in the warm butter, fill the moulds about three-quarters full.
  5. Bake for 12–14 minutes until the edges are golden; cool for 5 minutes before turning out.

How to make it lighter

  • One small financier is a full dessert portion — pair it with an unsweetened coffee.
  • Bake in mini moulds so each cake is smaller.
  • Cut the sugar by a quarter; the browned butter keeps the flavour rich.

History

Financiers are usually credited to a Paris pastry cook working near the stock exchange in the late 19th century, and the gold-bar shape is said to explain the name. Earlier, similar almond cakes were made by nuns and known as visitandines.
