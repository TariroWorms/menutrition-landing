🍲 Bündner barley soup

Origin: Switzerland · Graubünden

Bündner Gerstensuppe is a hearty barley soup from the Swiss Alps, simmered with beef, smoky bacon, cabbage, carrot and celery until the barley turns soft and creamy. A splash of cream at the end makes it rich and smooth. It is a warming winter meal, served in deep bowls straight from the pot.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g pearl barley
  • 300 g beef shin or brisket, diced
  • 100 g smoked bacon, diced
  • 1 onion and 1 leek, chopped
  • 2 carrots and 2 celery sticks, diced
  • 200 g white cabbage, shredded
  • 2 litres water
  • 100 ml single cream
  • Salt, pepper, 1 bay leaf, chopped parsley

Method

  1. Fry the bacon in a large pot until the fat runs, then add the onion and leek and cook for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the beef, barley, bay leaf and water, bring to the boil and skim, then simmer covered for 1 hour.
  3. Add the carrots, celery and cabbage and simmer for 30–40 minutes more until the barley and vegetables are tender.
  4. Stir in the cream, season with salt and pepper and sprinkle with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the bacon and use a lean cut of beef; the smoky flavour still comes through.
  • Replace part of the cream with extra vegetables blended into the broth for a creamy texture.
  • Serve one bowl as a full meal — barley and beef make it filling without extra bread.

History

The soup takes its name from the canton of Graubünden (Bünden) in eastern Switzerland, where barley was a staple grain of mountain farming. Today it is served in Alpine restaurants and mountain huts across Switzerland, especially in the ski season.
