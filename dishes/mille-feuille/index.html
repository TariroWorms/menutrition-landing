🍰 Mille-feuille

Mille-feuille is a French pastry of three crisp layers of baked puff pastry sandwiched with thick vanilla pastry cream. The top is dusted with icing sugar or glazed with white icing and feathered with chocolate. Each bite combines flaky, buttery shards with cool, creamy filling. It is sold as rectangular slices in pâtisseries and eaten with coffee or as a dessert.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g ready-rolled puff pastry
  • 500 ml milk
  • 4 egg yolks
  • 90 g sugar
  • 40 g cornflour
  • 1 vanilla pod or 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 20 g butter
  • 2 tbsp icing sugar for dusting

Method

  1. Heat the milk with the vanilla. Whisk the yolks, sugar and cornflour, pour in the hot milk, return to the pan and cook, whisking, until thick. Stir in the butter, cover and chill for at least 1 hour.
  2. Cut the pastry into three equal rectangles, prick them all over and bake between two trays at 200 °C for 15–20 minutes until golden and flat. Cool.
  3. Spread half the cream on one pastry layer, cover with a second layer, spread the remaining cream and top with the third layer.
  4. Dust generously with icing sugar, chill for 30 minutes and cut with a serrated knife.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thin slices — a standard pâtisserie slice is already a rich dessert.
  • Use a lighter pastry cream with less butter, or fold in fresh berries to replace part of it.
  • Share one slice between two with coffee or tea.

History

The mille-feuille, meaning “a thousand leaves”, took shape in French pastry kitchens in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the pastry chef Marie-Antoine Carême is often credited with popularising it. Puff pastry itself is much older. Today it is sold in pâtisseries across France and far beyond, and is known as a Napoleon in parts of the English-speaking world.
