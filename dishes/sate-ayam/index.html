Sate ayam is Indonesian chicken satay: small pieces of chicken marinated in kecap manis, grilled on bamboo skewers and served under a coarse peanut sauce. It is finished with more sweet soy sauce, raw shallots and a squeeze of lime, so each bite is sweet, nutty, smoky and fresh. It is one of the most common street foods in Indonesia.

Recipe

4 servings80 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g chicken thighs, cut into 2–3 cm pieces
  • 5 tbsp kecap manis (sweet soy sauce)
  • 4 shallots, sliced
  • 120 g roasted peanuts
  • 1 lime
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, salt

Method

  1. Toss the chicken with 2 tbsp kecap manis, a little oil and salt and marinate for 30 minutes.
  2. Thread onto soaked bamboo skewers.
  3. Grill over hot charcoal for 6–8 minutes, turning, until charred at the edges and cooked through.
  4. Grind the peanuts with the garlic, then simmer with 150 ml water, 2 tbsp kecap manis and salt for 5 minutes until thick.
  5. Pour the peanut sauce over the skewers, drizzle with the remaining kecap manis, scatter over the shallots and serve with lime wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Peanut sauce is energy-dense: use a few spoonfuls for dipping instead of covering the skewers.
  • Kecap manis is rich in sugar, so brush on a thin layer rather than a thick glaze.
  • Choose skinless chicken breast for a leaner skewer, and add cucumber or raw vegetables on the side.

History

Satay is most closely associated with Java, where kambing (goat or lamb) and ayam (chicken) skewers glazed with kecap manis are classic street food; it is often suggested that satay developed in the 19th century under the influence of kebabs brought by Indian Muslim and Arab traders. Madura island off East Java is famous for its chicken satay.
