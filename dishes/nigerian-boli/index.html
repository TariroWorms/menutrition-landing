Boli (roasted plantain with fish and pepper sauce)

Origin: Nigeria · Port Harcourt

Boli is plantain roasted on a charcoal grill until the skin is blackened and the flesh smoky and soft, then peeled and served with spicy groundnut or pepper sauce and often grilled fish. It is sweet, smoky and hot together. It is a favourite street meal in Port Harcourt and across southern Nigeria.

What it is made of

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 plantains (about 800 g)
  • 2 grilled mackerel or tilapia (about 400 g)
  • 2 tbsp palm oil
  • 2 onions
  • 3 chili peppers
  • 1 tsp salt
  • pepper sauce

Method

  1. Grill the unpeeled plantains over charcoal or under a grill, turning, for 20-25 minutes.
  2. Season and grill the fish until cooked through.
  3. Blend onion and chili with salt and palm oil into a rough sauce.
  4. Peel the plantains and cut into pieces.
  5. Plate the plantain with the fish.
  6. Spoon the pepper sauce over.

How to make it lighter

  • Roast rather than fry, which suits lighter eating.
  • Use less palm oil in the sauce.
  • Add a salad with tomatoes and cucumber.

History

Boli is strongly associated with Port Harcourt in Rivers State, where roadside grills sell it in the evenings. Different degrees of ripeness give a firmer, starchy or softer, sweeter result.
