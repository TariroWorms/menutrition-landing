🐟 Chicharrón de pescado

Origin: Peru · Pacific coast

Chicharrón de pescado is bite-sized pieces of white fish marinated with garlic and lime, dredged in flour and deep-fried until crisp and golden. The coating crackles while the fish inside stays moist and flaky. It is served piled on a plate with lime wedges, often with a sharp red onion salad, as a snack or a seafood lunch.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g firm white fish fillet, cut into 3 cm pieces
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • Juice of 2 limes, plus wedges to serve
  • 100 g plain flour
  • Oil for frying
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 1 red onion, thinly sliced (optional salad)

Method

  1. Season the fish with garlic, juice of 1 lime, salt and pepper; leave 10 minutes.
  2. Heat oil to 180 °C.
  3. Dredge the fish in flour and shake off the excess.
  4. Fry in batches for 3–4 minutes until golden and crisp; drain on paper.
  5. Serve hot with lime wedges and red onion dressed with the remaining lime juice.

How to make it lighter

  • Air-fry or oven-bake the floured fish at 220 °C instead of deep-frying.
  • Keep the flour coating thin; thick coatings soak up more oil.
  • Fill up on the onion salad and lime rather than extra fried sides.

History

Fried fish chicharrón is a staple of cevicherías along Peru's Pacific coast, where it is ordered alongside ceviche. Similar dishes are found across Latin America; reliable details on when the Peruvian version first appeared are scarce.
