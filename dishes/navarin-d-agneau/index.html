Navarin d'agneau is a French lamb stew in which shoulder or neck of lamb is browned and slowly braised with onion, then finished with young spring vegetables — turnips, carrots, small potatoes and peas. The meat turns meltingly tender and the vegetables stay bright, in a light, savoury sauce. It is served on its own as a one-pot meal.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g lamb shoulder, cubed
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 300 g small turnips
  • 300 g carrots
  • 400 g small potatoes
  • 150 g peas
  • Thyme, bay leaf, salt, pepper
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Brown the lamb in batches in the oil and set aside; soften the onion and garlic in the same pot.
  2. Return the lamb, add tomatoes, herbs and enough water to just cover, and simmer covered for 1 hour.
  3. Add the turnips, carrots and potatoes and cook 25 minutes more.
  4. Stir in the peas for the last 5 minutes and season to taste.
  5. Skim off excess fat and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb and skim the surface fat after braising.
  • Use more turnips and peas and fewer potatoes for a lighter bowl.
  • Brown the meat in 1 tsp oil in a heavy pot — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Navarin is a classic of French home and bistro cooking, traditionally made in spring, when it is also called 'navarin printanier'. The origin of the name is uncertain; it is often linked to 'navet', the French word for turnip.
