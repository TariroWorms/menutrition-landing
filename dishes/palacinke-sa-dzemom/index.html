🥞 Palačinke with Jam

Origin: Balkans · Southeastern Europe

Palačinke are thin Balkan crepes made from a simple batter of flour, eggs and milk, cooked in a hot pan until lightly golden. This classic version is spread with apricot jam, rolled or folded, and finished with a sprinkle of sugar. They are soft and tender with a sweet, fruity filling, and are eaten warm as a dessert or an afternoon treat.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g plain flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 300 ml milk
  • 1 pinch salt
  • 1 tsp oil for the pan
  • 120 g apricot jam
  • 1 tbsp sugar

Method

  1. Whisk flour, eggs, milk and salt into a smooth, thin batter; rest 10 minutes.
  2. Heat a lightly oiled pan and pour in a thin layer of batter, tilting to cover the base.
  3. Cook about 1 minute per side until lightly golden; repeat with the rest of the batter.
  4. Spread each crepe with jam, roll or fold, and sprinkle with sugar.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a thin layer of jam (about 1 tbsp per crepe) and skip the sugar on top.
  • Brush the pan with oil instead of pouring it in; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Add fresh sliced apricots or berries for volume and serve two crepes instead of three.

History

Thin crepes known as palačinke are a fixture of home cooking across Croatia, Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and neighbouring countries, a tradition shared with Central European cuisines. Apricot jam is one of the most popular fillings.
