🍚 Yangzhou fried rice

Origin: China · Jiangsu, Yangzhou

Yangzhou fried rice is the best-known Chinese fried rice: day-old rice tossed in a very hot wok with scrambled egg, shrimp, diced char siu barbecue pork and scallion. Each grain should be separate, lightly coated and fragrant from the wok, with small colourful pieces of meat and seafood throughout. It is served as a main dish or shared alongside other plates at Cantonese and Chinese restaurants worldwide.

What it is made of

Recipe

3 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g cooked long-grain rice, chilled overnight
  • 150 g peeled shrimp
  • 150 g char siu (Chinese barbecue pork), diced
  • 3 eggs, beaten
  • 3 scallions, sliced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt, white pepper

Method

  1. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a wok, scramble the eggs softly and set aside.
  2. Add the remaining oil, stir-fry the shrimp for 2 minutes until pink, then add the char siu.
  3. Add the cold rice, breaking up clumps, and toss over high heat for 4–5 minutes.
  4. Return the egg, season with salt and white pepper, add the scallions and toss once more before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tbsp oil and a non-stick wok — 1 tbsp oil is about 120 kcal.
  • Double the shrimp and scallion and cut back on the rice for a lighter, higher-protein bowl.
  • Measure a portion of about 300 g instead of eating from the shared plate.

History

The dish is named after the city of Yangzhou in Jiangsu province, which has a long tradition of fried rice. The version with shrimp and barbecue pork now known worldwide was largely shaped by Cantonese restaurants in southern China and Hong Kong, which spread it abroad.
