Hakka salt-baked chicken (yan ju ji)

Origin: China · Guangdong

A whole chicken is rubbed with ginger and shallot, wrapped in paper and buried in hot coarse salt. The salt gives even heat and the meat comes out moist, delicately seasoned and lightly fragrant. It is torn by hand and eaten warm or cold with a ginger-scallion dip.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1 chicken, about 1.4 kg
  • 2 kg coarse salt
  • 30 g ginger, sliced
  • 3 shallots
  • 1 tbsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp white pepper
  • Baking parchment, kitchen paper and ginger-scallion dip to serve

Method

  1. Pat the chicken dry and rub the inside with ginger, shallot and pepper.
  2. Brush the skin lightly with sesame oil and wrap in parchment, then kitchen paper.
  3. Heat the coarse salt in a heavy pot until very hot.
  4. Bury the wrapped chicken in half of the salt, cover with the rest and close the lid.
  5. Cook on low heat for about 45 minutes, turning once, until the juices run clear.
  6. Unwrap, rest for 10 minutes, tear into pieces and serve with the ginger-scallion dip.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin to cut the fat noticeably.
  • Serve with steamed greens instead of rice.
  • Use a ginger dip rather than oil-based sauces.

History

The dish is associated with the Hakka people of Guangdong, whose cooking relied on simple preserving staples such as salt. The original method used heated salt in a wok, and it later spread across Cantonese restaurants.
