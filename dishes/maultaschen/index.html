🥟 Maultaschen

Origin: Germany · Swabia

Maultaschen are large Swabian pasta pockets, a little like oversized ravioli, filled with minced pork, spinach, onion, breadcrumbs and a hint of nutmeg. The thin dough turns pillowy when boiled, and the filling is soft and savoury. They are served in clear broth, sliced and pan-fried, or topped with soft fried onions.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g pasta dough (or fresh pasta sheets)
  • 300 g minced pork
  • 200 g spinach, blanched and chopped
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 40 g breadcrumbs
  • ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1.5 l water or clear vegetable stock, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Soften the onion in the oil for 5 minutes and let it cool.
  2. Mix the pork, spinach, onion, breadcrumbs, nutmeg, salt and pepper into a smooth filling.
  3. Roll the dough thin, spread the filling over half, fold over and press firmly into rectangles about 8 × 10 cm.
  4. Simmer the pockets in salted water or stock for 12–15 minutes until they float and the filling is cooked through.
  5. Serve in the broth with chopped chives, or slice and brown in a dry non-stick pan.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve them in broth instead of pan-fried in fat to keep the plate lighter.
  • Use lean minced pork and more spinach in the filling to cut fat.
  • Two pockets with a side salad are a filling portion; log each pocket separately in the app.

History

Maultaschen come from Swabia in south-western Germany, and their invention is often credited, in a popular legend, to Maulbronn monks hiding meat inside pasta during Lent. The story is folklore rather than documented history. In 2009 the EU registered 'Schwäbische Maultaschen' as a protected regional speciality.
