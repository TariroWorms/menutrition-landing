🍆 Makdous

Origin: Syria · Levant

Makdous are small eggplants that are boiled, salted, stuffed with a paste of walnuts, red pepper and garlic, and then preserved in olive oil. After a week or two in the jar they turn tangy, soft and nutty. They are a staple of the Levantine breakfast and mezze table, eaten whole alongside olives and fresh vegetables.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg baby eggplants
  • 120 g walnuts, chopped
  • 2 red peppers, finely chopped
  • 6 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tsp chili flakes
  • 2 tsp salt
  • About 500 ml olive oil to cover

Method

  1. Remove the stems and boil the eggplants for 8–10 minutes until just tender.
  2. Cut a slit in each one, salt the inside and press them in a colander under a weight overnight to drain.
  3. Mix the walnuts, red pepper, garlic, chili and a pinch of salt.
  4. Stuff each eggplant with the filling and pack them tightly into a sterilised jar.
  5. Cover completely with olive oil, close and leave in a cool place for 7–10 days before eating.

How to make it lighter

  • Lift each eggplant out of the jar and let the oil drip off — the oil is where most of the calories are.
  • One or two pieces with fresh vegetables is a good portion; walnuts make makdous dense in energy.
  • Every extra tablespoon of oil adds about 120 kcal, so leave the jar oil behind.

History

Makdous is a traditional mouneh, the late-summer pantry preserves made across Syria and Lebanon to last through winter. Families still make large batches when baby eggplants are in season and keep the jars for months.
