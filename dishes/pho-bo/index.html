🍜 Pho bo (beef pho)

Pho bo is the classic beef version of Vietnam's noodle soup. Beef bones simmer for hours with charred onion and ginger, star anise and cinnamon, and the broth is seasoned with fish sauce for depth. It is ladled over flat rice noodles and sliced beef and served with bean sprouts, Thai basil and lime, so every bowl can be adjusted at the table.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg beef bones and 500 g beef brisket
  • 300 g beef sirloin, very thinly sliced
  • 400 g flat rice noodles
  • 1 onion and a 5 cm piece of ginger, charred
  • 3 star anise and 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce, salt and a little sugar
  • Bean sprouts, Thai basil and lime wedges to serve

Method

  1. Blanch the bones for 5 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Simmer the bones and brisket in 3 litres of water with the charred onion and ginger, star anise and cinnamon for 3 hours, skimming often.
  3. Slice the brisket, strain the broth and season with fish sauce, salt and a little sugar.
  4. Divide the cooked noodles, brisket and raw sirloin between bowls.
  5. Pour over boiling broth and serve with sprouts, basil and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat from the chilled broth before serving.
  • Fish sauce is salty — add it gradually and taste.
  • Swap part of the noodles for extra bean sprouts and herbs.

History

Pho emerged in northern Vietnam, around Hanoi and Nam Định, in the early 20th century. Its exact origins are debated, with influences often traced to Vietnamese rice-noodle soups and to French and Chinese cooking of the period. After 1954 it spread south and later around the world with the Vietnamese diaspora.
