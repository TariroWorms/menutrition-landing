🥟 Taro puffs

Origin: China · Guangdong (Cantonese dim sum)

Taro puffs are a Cantonese dim sum snack: an oval of mashed taro pastry wrapped around a savoury filling of pork and shrimp, then deep-fried. The outside sets into a lacy, flaky, very crisp shell while the inside stays soft and earthy. They are served hot, usually three to a plate, at dim sum brunch.

What it is made of

taroporkshrimppuff pastry

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g taro, peeled and diced
  • 250 g ready-made puff pastry
  • 150 g minced pork
  • 100 g raw shrimp, chopped
  • 1 spring onion, chopped
  • 1 tsp grated ginger, salt, white pepper
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Steam the taro for 20 minutes until soft, then mash with a pinch of salt.
  2. Stir-fry the pork, shrimp, ginger and spring onion until just cooked; season and cool.
  3. Roll out the pastry and cut it into 12 squares; spread each with mashed taro.
  4. Spoon the filling into the centre and fold into an oval, pressing the edges closed.
  5. Deep-fry at 180 °C for 3–4 minutes until golden and crisp, then drain on paper.

How to make it lighter

  • Deep-fried pastry is the main calorie source, so have two puffs and fill up on steamed dishes.
  • Bake them at 200 °C for about 20 minutes instead of frying for a lighter version.
  • Drain well on paper towels to remove surface oil.

History

Taro puffs, known in Cantonese as wu gok, belong to the dim sum tradition of Guangdong and Hong Kong teahouses. Traditionally their lacy, honeycomb crust comes from a taro dough fried in very hot oil; this home version uses ready-made pastry instead. Today they appear on dim sum menus wherever Cantonese communities live.
