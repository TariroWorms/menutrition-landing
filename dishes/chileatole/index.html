Chileatole (spicy corn atole)

Origin: Mexico · Puebla

Chileatole is a savoury cousin of the sweet atole: a thick, warm corn drink that sits halfway between a beverage and a soup. Fresh corn kernels and masa give it body, while green chile and epazote make it sharp and herbal. It is sipped from a cup or eaten with a spoon, often on cool mornings or at market stalls.

What it is made of

corn masacornchili pepperepazoteonion

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g fresh masa
  • 300 g corn kernels
  • 2 serrano or poblano chiles
  • 1 sprig epazote
  • 1/2 onion (60 g)
  • 1.5 l water, salt

Method

  1. Simmer the corn kernels in 1 l of water for 15 minutes.
  2. Blend the chiles, onion and epazote with a little water until smooth.
  3. Dissolve the masa in 500 ml of water and strain it into the pot.
  4. Stir in the chile purée and keep stirring as it thickens, about 15 minutes.
  5. Season with salt and serve hot, adding a squeeze of lime if you like.

How to make it lighter

  • Made with water only, it is naturally low in fat.
  • Use more corn kernels and less masa for extra fibre.
  • Remove the chile seeds for a milder version.

History

Atoles made from corn and water have been part of central Mexican eating since pre-Hispanic times. The chile version is especially associated with Puebla and neighbouring Tlaxcala, where it is still sold by street vendors.
