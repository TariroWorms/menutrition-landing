🥟 Chebureki (Ciborek)

Origin: Crimea · former Soviet Union and Eastern Europe

Ciborek, better known as chebureki, is a large half-moon turnover of thin unleavened dough filled with juicy minced beef and onion and deep-fried until blistered and crisp. The raw filling steams inside the sealed pastry, so each bite releases hot, peppery meat juices. It is eaten hot from the fryer as street food, often in hand with a napkin.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g plain flour
  • 160 ml water
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil for the dough, plus oil for frying
  • 400 g minced beef
  • 2 onions, very finely chopped or grated
  • 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 2–3 tbsp cold water for the filling

Method

  1. Mix the flour, water, 1 tbsp oil and a pinch of salt into a firm, smooth dough. Cover and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the beef with the onion, salt, pepper and cold water until loose and juicy.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 pieces and roll each very thinly into a 18 cm circle.
  4. Spread filling over one half, fold over and press the edges firmly, sealing with a fork.
  5. Fry in 2–3 cm of oil at 175 °C for 2–3 minutes per side until golden and blistered, then drain on paper towels.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef: the pastry already takes up plenty of frying oil.
  • One cheburek with a large tomato and cucumber salad makes a full meal.
  • Drain well and blot with paper towels to remove surface oil.

History

Chebureki are a Crimean Tatar dish that spread across the former Soviet Union and Eastern Europe, where they are sold today at street stalls, markets and seaside resorts.
