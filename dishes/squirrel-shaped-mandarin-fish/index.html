Squirrel-shaped mandarin fish (song shu gui yu)

Origin: China · Jiangsu

A whole boned fish is scored in a crisscross pattern, dusted with starch and deep-fried so the flesh fans out like a bushy squirrel tail. A glossy sweet and sour sauce is poured over it at the table, with a sizzle. It has a crisp surface, tender flesh and bright tangy flavor.

What it is made of

mandarin fishcornstarchketchupsugarvinegarpeas

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 1 whole mandarin fish or sea bass, 700 g
  • 60 g cornstarch
  • 3 tbsp ketchup
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 50 g peas
  • Oil for deep frying
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Fillet the fish keeping the tail and head attached, and score the flesh diagonally in both directions.
  2. Season with salt and dust with cornstarch, pressing into the cuts.
  3. Deep-fry at 180 °C until crisp and golden and the flesh opens.
  4. Heat ketchup, sugar, vinegar, peas and a little cornstarch slurry until glossy.
  5. Place the fish on a plate.
  6. Pour the hot sauce over and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or shallow-fry for less oil.
  • Use less sugar and ketchup in the sauce.
  • Serve with steamed vegetables and a small rice portion.

History

The dish is a famous specialty of Suzhou in Jiangsu, said to date back to the Qing dynasty. It is usually served at banquets and restaurants as a showpiece dish.
