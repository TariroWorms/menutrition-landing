🐚 Linguine alle vongole (with clams)

Linguine alle vongole is a classic Italian pasta with fresh clams steamed open in white wine, garlic and olive oil, then tossed with the linguine and parsley. The clams release their juices into a light, briny sauce that soaks into the pasta. It is served immediately, often still with the clams in their shells.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 360 g linguine
  • 1 kg fresh clams, cleaned
  • 100 ml dry white wine
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Large handful of parsley, chopped
  • Pinch of chili flakes (optional)

Method

  1. Soak the clams in salted water for 30 minutes and rinse; discard any that stay open.
  2. Cook the linguine in salted water until 1–2 minutes short of al dente.
  3. Warm the oil with garlic, add the clams and wine, cover and steam 3–4 minutes until they open; discard any that stay closed.
  4. Add the pasta and some cooking liquid to the pan and toss for 1–2 minutes until the sauce clings.
  5. Finish with parsley and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Clams are lean; most of the energy comes from pasta and oil, so 70–80 g dry pasta per person is a good portion.
  • 1 tbsp olive oil ≈ 120 kcal — the clam juices mean you need very little.
  • Serve with a large green salad instead of bread.

History

Pasta with clams is usually linked to Naples and the coast of Campania, where it is a traditional dish for Christmas Eve and summer by the sea. Both the 'white' version with wine and a version with tomato are cooked across Italy today.
