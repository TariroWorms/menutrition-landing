🍝 Linguine aglio e olio

Linguine aglio e olio is one of the simplest Italian pastas: linguine tossed with olive oil gently infused with sliced garlic and chili, then finished with parsley. The garlic turns golden and fragrant, and a splash of pasta water makes the oil cling to the strands. It is served hot, straight after tossing, as a quick midnight or weeknight meal.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 360 g linguine
  • 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled, or ½ tsp chili flakes
  • Handful of parsley, chopped
  • Salt

Method

  1. Cook the linguine in well-salted water until al dente; keep a cup of the water.
  2. Warm the oil over low heat with garlic and chili until the garlic is pale golden, about 3 minutes.
  3. Add the pasta and 4–5 tbsp pasta water and toss over the heat for 1 minute until glossy.
  4. Stir in parsley and serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • The oil is most of the fat: 2 tbsp instead of 4 saves about 240 kcal for the pan; pasta water keeps it silky.
  • Stick to 70–80 g dry pasta per person and add sautéed vegetables for volume.
  • Toss in cherry tomatoes or spinach at the end to bulk up the plate for few calories.

History

Spaghetti or linguine aglio, olio e peperoncino is usually associated with Naples and the cooking of Campania, where it grew out of everyday pantry ingredients. It is now cooked in homes all over Italy as a classic late-night pasta.
