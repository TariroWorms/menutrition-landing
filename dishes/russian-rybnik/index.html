Rybnik (Russian fish pie)

Origin: Russia · North

Rybnik is a closed pie in which a whole gutted fish, or thick fillets, is wrapped in rye or mixed-flour dough and baked until the crust is firm. Inside, the fish steams in its own juices, staying moist and delicately flavoured. To serve, the top crust is cut away and the fish eaten with pieces of the juicy bottom crust.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g rye flour
  • 200 g wheat flour
  • 300 ml water or kefir
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 kg whole fish or thick fillets (pike-perch, perch, salmon)
  • 1 onion
  • 50 g butter
  • 2 tbsp sour cream
  • salt, pepper

Method

  1. Knead a firm dough from both flours, liquid and salt and rest it for 30 minutes.
  2. Clean the fish, remove large bones and season inside and out with salt and pepper.
  3. Roll out the dough into an oval about 1 cm thick and place the fish in the centre with sliced onion and a few pieces of butter.
  4. Fold the dough over the fish and pinch the edges tightly, leaving a small hole on top.
  5. Bake at 200 °C for 20 minutes, then at 170 °C for another 40–50 minutes, brushing with butter at the end.
  6. Rest 15 minutes, cut off the top crust and serve the fish with the soaked bottom crust and sour cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean white fish like pike-perch or perch to keep the fat low.
  • Skip the extra butter on top and brush with a little milk instead.
  • Serve with a large green salad and eat less of the crust.

History

Rybnik is traditional in Karelia, the Arkhangelsk region and other northern lands rich in lakes and rivers. Hosts cut it open at the table, and the fish-soaked crust is considered the best part.
