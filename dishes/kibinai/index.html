Kibinai (Trakai mutton pastries)

Origin: Lithuania · Trakai

Kibinai are crescent-shaped baked pastries with a firm, buttery crust and a juicy filling of hand-chopped mutton, onion and pepper. They are savoury and rich, with a crimped edge and a golden top. They are eaten warm, usually with a cup of broth, in Trakai and across Lithuania.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 200 g cold butter
  • 1 egg (60 g)
  • 100 ml sour cream
  • 400 g mutton or lamb, hand-chopped
  • 150 g onion, chopped
  • salt, pepper

Method

  1. Rub butter into flour, add egg, sour cream and salt and knead into a smooth dough; chill 30 minutes.
  2. Mix chopped meat with onion, salt and pepper and a spoon of water.
  3. Roll the dough thin and cut rounds about 12 cm across.
  4. Place filling on one half, fold and crimp the edge.
  5. Brush with egg and bake at 200 C for 25 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean lamb and trim the fat before chopping.
  • Serve with a vegetable salad instead of more pastry.
  • Use a lighter pastry with olive oil for fewer saturated fats.

History

Kibinai are associated with the Karaite community of Trakai, whose ancestors settled there centuries ago, and they are now regarded as a Lithuanian national dish. Traditionally the meat is chopped, not minced.
