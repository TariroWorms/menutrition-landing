Abará (steamed black-eyed pea cake)

Origin: Brazil · Bahia

Abará is the steamed sister of acarajé. The same black-eyed pea paste is mixed with ground dried shrimp, onion and dendê oil, wrapped in banana leaves and steamed until firm. Unwrapped, it is soft, dense and orange, with a gentle shrimp flavour and a hint of leaf. Like acarajé it is often split and topped with vatapá, caruru and pepper sauce.

What it is made of

black-eyed peasdried shrimpdendê (red palm) oiloniongingerbanana leaf

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g dried black-eyed peas
  • 80 g dried shrimp, ground
  • 4 tbsp dendê oil (50 ml)
  • 1 onion (150 g)
  • 10 g fresh ginger
  • banana leaves, salt

Method

  1. Soak the peas overnight and remove the skins.
  2. Grind them with onion, ginger and the dried shrimp into a smooth paste.
  3. Beat in the dendê oil and salt until the paste is evenly orange.
  4. Wrap portions in softened banana leaf squares and tie or fold tightly.
  5. Steam for 40–45 minutes until firm; serve plain or with vatapá and caruru.

How to make it lighter

  • Steamed abará is a lighter choice than fried acarajé.
  • Cut the dendê oil to 2 tbsp; it still sets well.
  • Serve with a tomato and onion salad instead of vatapá.

History

Abará belongs to the Afro-Bahian tradition of bean dishes linked to the Yoruba and is sold by the same baianas who make acarajé. It also has a place among the foods offered in Candomblé.
