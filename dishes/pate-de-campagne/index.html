🍖 Pâté de Campagne (Country Pâté)

Pâté de campagne is a rustic French country pâté of coarsely chopped pork and pork liver, seasoned with herbs, garlic and a splash of brandy, then baked in a terrine and chilled. It has a firm, coarse texture and a rich, savoury flavour. It is sliced thick and served cold with sharp cornichons as a starter or picnic dish.

Recipe

10 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork shoulder, coarsely minced
  • 300 g pork liver, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley and thyme
  • 3 tbsp brandy
  • 1 tsp salt, ½ tsp pepper, pinch of allspice
  • Bay leaves for the top
  • Cornichons to serve

Method

  1. Mix the pork, liver, garlic, herbs, brandy and seasoning thoroughly; leave to marinate for a few hours or overnight.
  2. Pack into a terrine dish, smooth the top and lay bay leaves on it.
  3. Cover and bake in a water bath at 160 °C for about 1½ hours, until the centre reaches 70 °C.
  4. Weigh down the top and chill for at least 24 hours.
  5. Slice and serve cold with cornichons.

How to make it lighter

  • A 50–60 g slice is a typical starter portion; the pâté is rich in fat.
  • Serve with plenty of cornichons, radishes and salad leaves.
  • Use leaner pork shoulder and trim excess fat before mincing.

History

Country pâtés grew out of the French farmhouse tradition of using every part of the pig after the annual slaughter. Today pâté de campagne is a fixture of charcuteries, bistros and markets throughout France.
