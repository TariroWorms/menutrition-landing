🥮 Bougatsa with cream (bougatsa me krema)

Origin: Greece · Thessaloniki

Bougatsa me krema is the sweet custard version of the famous northern Greek pastry: a vanilla semolina cream baked between layers of butter-brushed phyllo until the top shatters. In bougatsa shops it is chopped into small pieces with a large knife and served on a plate under a snowfall of icing sugar and cinnamon. It is best eaten warm, straight from the oven.

Recipe

8 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 10 sheets phyllo pastry
  • 100 g butter, melted
  • 800 ml milk
  • 100 g fine semolina
  • 120 g sugar
  • 2 eggs
  • Grated zest of 1 lemon or 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Icing sugar and ground cinnamon to serve

Method

  1. Warm the milk with the sugar and lemon zest, whisk in the semolina and cook, stirring, until thick, about 5 minutes. Off the heat, beat in the eggs and 20 g of the butter.
  2. Heat the oven to 180 °C. Butter a 25 × 35 cm tray and layer 5 phyllo sheets, brushing each with butter.
  3. Pour in the warm cream, level it and cover with the remaining phyllo, brushing each sheet.
  4. Sprinkle the top with a little water, score and bake for about 40 minutes until deep golden.
  5. Rest for 10 minutes, chop into small pieces and serve dusted with icing sugar and cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Brush the phyllo thinly; butter is about 100 kcal per tablespoon and adds up quickly over 10 sheets.
  • Share a portion or cut smaller squares — it is as rich as most cakes.
  • Use cinnamon generously and keep the icing sugar to a light dusting.

History

Bougatsa is most closely associated with Thessaloniki, and its spread there is usually credited to Greek refugees from Asia Minor in the 1920s. Alongside cheese and meat fillings, the cream-filled version is the most popular, typically eaten for breakfast or as a late-night snack.
