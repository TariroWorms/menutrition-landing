Canjeero (Somali pancake)

Origin: Somalia

Canjeero is a soft spongy pancake with a honeycomb of tiny holes on top, cooked on one side only. The batter is lightly fermented, which gives a mild tang. Somalis eat it for breakfast with sesame oil and sugar, or with stew.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g flour
  • 100 g sorghum flour
  • 500 ml warm water
  • 1 tsp yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1/2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Whisk all ingredients into a thin smooth batter.
  2. Cover and ferment 8 to 12 hours.
  3. Stir the batter and thin with water if needed.
  4. Heat a nonstick pan lightly oiled.
  5. Pour a thin layer and cook covered until holes form and the top dries.
  6. Do not flip; stack and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use whole-grain flour to add fiber.
  • Serve with eggs or beans for more protein.
  • Use less sugar and top with fruit.

History

It is the Somali relative of the East African and Ethiopian fermented flatbreads. It is made across Somalia and the diaspora, often at dawn. The name varies, and it is also called laxoox in some places.
