🫘 Pasulj (Serbian Bean Soup)

Origin: Serbia · Balkans

Pasulj is a thick Serbian bean soup-stew of white beans simmered with smoked meat, onion, carrot and sweet paprika. The beans turn creamy, the broth takes on a deep smoky flavour, and the paprika gives it a warm red colour. It is served hot in deep bowls as a hearty everyday meal, especially in winter.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g dried white beans, soaked overnight
  • 400 g smoked meat on the bone
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tbsp sweet paprika
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Drain the soaked beans, cover with fresh water, bring to the boil for 5 minutes and drain again.
  2. Cover the beans with fresh water, add the smoked meat and bay leaves and simmer for about 1 hour.
  3. Soften the onions and carrots in oil, then stir in the paprika off the heat.
  4. Add the vegetables to the pot and simmer for another 30–45 minutes until the beans are creamy.
  5. Remove the meat, cut it into pieces, return it to the pot and season to taste.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a smaller amount of lean smoked meat and trim visible fat.
  • Skim the fat from the surface after cooking.
  • Beans are filling: one bowl of about 350 g makes a complete meal on its own.

History

Bean stews are a staple across the Balkans, and pasulj is one of the most widely eaten home dishes in Serbia, also popular in neighbouring countries. It is a regular feature of canteens, family tables and outdoor cooking competitions.
