Gosh-e fil (Afghan elephant-ear pastry)

Origin: Afghanistan

Gosh-e fil means elephant ear, named for the broad, curved shape of this fried pastry. The dough is thin and crisp, with bubbles and folds, and it is dusted with powdered sugar, cardamom and ground pistachios. It is served at weddings, Eid and with tea.

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 100 ml milk
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 300 ml oil for frying
  • 60 g powdered sugar, 40 g ground pistachios

Method

  1. Mix flour, eggs, milk and cardamom into a smooth dough and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Divide into balls and roll each very thin.
  3. Pinch the center and fold into a bow shape.
  4. Fry in hot oil until golden and blistered, 1-2 minutes per side.
  5. Drain on paper towels.
  6. Dust with sugar and pistachios.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake at 190 °C for a lighter version.
  • Use half the sugar.
  • Enjoy a single piece with unsweetened tea.

History

Fried sweet pastries are festive foods across Afghanistan and Central Asia. Gosh-e fil is among the best known and its shape gives it its name.
