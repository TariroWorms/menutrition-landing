🍛 Iraqi Biryani

Origin: Iraq · Baghdad

Iraqi biryani is a festive rice dish quite different from its South Asian cousins: fragrant rice cooked with baharat and mixed with chicken, fried potato cubes, green peas, toasted vermicelli, plump raisins and fried almonds. It is mildly spiced, slightly sweet and full of contrasting textures. It is piled on a large platter and served for family gatherings and celebrations.

Recipe

6 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g basmati rice, soaked 30 minutes
  • 600 g chicken pieces
  • 2 medium potatoes, diced
  • 150 g green peas
  • 50 g vermicelli, broken
  • 50 g raisins
  • 40 g almonds
  • 2 tsp baharat
  • 4 tbsp oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in water with salt and half the baharat until cooked, then shred or cut it and keep the broth.
  2. Fry the potato cubes in some of the oil until golden; set aside. Toast the almonds briefly in the pan, then the raisins.
  3. Toast the vermicelli in a little oil until golden.
  4. Add the rice, vermicelli, remaining baharat and enough chicken broth to cover by 1 cm; cook covered on low heat for 15 minutes.
  5. Fold in the chicken, potatoes and peas, cover and steam for 10 more minutes.
  6. Serve on a platter topped with almonds and raisins.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the potato cubes with a light brush of oil instead of frying them.
  • Use skinless chicken breast to cut fat.
  • Keep the almonds and raisins as a garnish rather than a thick layer; they are calorie-dense.

History

Biryani is generally thought to have come to Iraq from the Indian subcontinent and Persia, and Iraqi cooks made it their own: milder, with vermicelli, nuts and raisins. Today it is a celebratory dish in Iraqi homes.
