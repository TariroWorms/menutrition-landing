🥬 Spinach fatayer

Origin: Lebanon · Levant

Spinach fatayer are triangular baked pastries filled with chopped spinach and onion, sharpened with lemon juice and sumac and studded with pine nuts. The filling is tart and herby, and the sealed triangle keeps it moist while the dough bakes golden. They are eaten warm or at room temperature as a snack, a mezze dish or a lunchbox staple, and they contain no animal products.

Recipe

4 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour, 1 tsp dried yeast, ½ tsp salt
  • 180 ml warm water, 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 500 g spinach, chopped
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 1 tbsp sumac
  • 30 g pine nuts

Method

  1. Make a soft dough from flour, yeast, salt, water and 1 tbsp oil and let it rise for 40 minutes.
  2. Rub the spinach with salt, leave 10 minutes and squeeze out all the liquid.
  3. Mix spinach, onion, lemon juice, sumac, pine nuts and 2 tbsp oil.
  4. Roll the dough thin, cut 10 cm circles, add filling and pinch three sides together into triangles.
  5. Bake at 220 °C for 15 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tbsp oil in the filling instead of 2 — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Roll the dough thin so the filling makes up more of each pastry.
  • Pair with a bowl of vegetable soup or salad to make a lighter meal of two or three pieces.

History

Spinach fatayer are among the best-known Levantine pastries and are especially popular during Lent and other meat-free periods. Today they are sold in bakeries across Lebanon, Syria, Palestine and Jordan and throughout the Arab diaspora.
