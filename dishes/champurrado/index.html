Champurrado (chocolate masa drink)

Origin: Mexico

Champurrado is a chocolate atole: corn masa is whisked into hot milk or water with Mexican chocolate, piloncillo and cinnamon until it thickens like a pourable pudding. It tastes of cocoa and toasted corn with a light spice. It is sipped hot for breakfast or supper, usually with tamales or sweet bread.

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 80 g corn masa (or masa harina mixed with water)
  • 1 l milk or half milk, half water
  • 90 g Mexican chocolate
  • 60 g piloncillo
  • 1 cinnamon stick

Method

  1. Heat the milk with the cinnamon and piloncillo until the sugar melts.
  2. Add the chopped chocolate and stir until dissolved.
  3. Blend the masa with 250 ml water until smooth and pour it into the pot through a sieve.
  4. Simmer, whisking constantly, for 10–15 minutes until thick and glossy.
  5. Remove the cinnamon and serve hot, frothing it with a whisk if you like.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half water, half milk to lighten it.
  • Halve the piloncillo; the chocolate already brings sweetness.
  • Drink a smaller cup with a tamale instead of also adding sweet bread.

History

Atoles made from corn go back to pre-Hispanic times, and chocolate was combined with them long before the Spanish arrived. Champurrado is especially popular in cold months and around the Day of the Dead and Christmas posadas.
