🥮 Znoud el Sit

Origin: Lebanon · Levant

Znoud el sit are crisp fried rolls of phyllo pastry filled with ashta, a thick Levantine clotted cream, then soaked in syrup perfumed with orange blossom water and sprinkled with ground pistachios. The shell shatters around a cool, creamy centre. They are a favourite in Lebanese and Syrian sweet shops, especially during Ramadan.

What it is made of

Recipe

12 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 12 sheets phyllo pastry
  • 500 g ashta (Levantine clotted cream)
  • Oil for frying
  • 300 g sugar and 150 ml water for syrup
  • 1 tbsp orange blossom water
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • 30 g pistachios, finely ground

Method

  1. Simmer the sugar, water and lemon juice for 10 minutes, add the orange blossom water and cool the syrup.
  2. Cut the phyllo into rectangles, place a line of ashta along one edge, fold in the sides and roll up tightly.
  3. Fry the rolls in oil at 175 °C until golden and crisp.
  4. Drain briefly and dip in the cool syrup.
  5. Dip the ends in ground pistachios and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Have one roll and enjoy it slowly; two make a substantial dessert.
  • Bake the rolls brushed lightly with oil instead of deep-frying them.
  • Drizzle a little syrup instead of soaking the rolls.

History

The name means 'ladies' upper arms' in Arabic, a playful reference to the rolls' plump shape. The sweet is part of the Levantine tradition of ashta-filled pastries and is made across Lebanon, Syria and neighbouring countries.
