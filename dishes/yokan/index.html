🍡 Yokan (red bean jelly)

Origin: Japan

Yokan is a Japanese confection made from sweet red bean paste set with agar into a firm, smooth block. It is sliced into thin rectangles and has a dense, slightly chewy texture and a deep, sweet bean flavour. Yokan is traditionally served in small pieces with green tea, and keeps well, which makes it a popular gift.

What it is made of

red beanagar-agarsugar

Recipe

12 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 g agar powder
  • 400 ml water
  • 250 g smooth sweet red bean paste
  • 100 g sugar
  • A pinch of salt

Method

  1. Dissolve the agar in the water, bring to the boil and simmer for 2 minutes, stirring.
  2. Add the sugar and stir until dissolved, then add the bean paste and salt.
  3. Cook over low heat, stirring constantly, for 10 minutes until thick and glossy.
  4. Pour into a rectangular mould, smooth the top and leave to set at room temperature, then chill.
  5. Turn out and slice into thin pieces.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thin slices of about 25 g and enjoy one with tea.
  • Reduce the sugar by a quarter; the bean flavour still comes through.
  • Treat yokan as the whole dessert rather than an extra after a large meal.

History

Yokan is usually traced to China, where the name originally referred to a mutton broth; Buddhist monks are credited with bringing a meatless version made with beans to Japan. Over the centuries it became a sweet, and the agar-set neri-yokan known today became widespread in the Edo period.
