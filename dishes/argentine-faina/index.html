Fainá (chickpea flatbread)

Origin: Argentina

Fainá is a thin flatbread made only from chickpea flour, water, oil and salt, baked in a very hot round pan. The edges turn crisp while the middle stays soft and almost custardy, with a nutty, earthy flavour. In Buenos Aires pizzerias it is sold by the slice and often placed right on top of a slice of mozzarella pizza, a combination called a caballo. A crack of black pepper finishes it.

What it is made of

chickpea flourolive oilsaltblack pepper

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g chickpea flour
  • 750 ml water
  • 60 ml olive oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • black pepper

Method

  1. Whisk the flour with the water and salt until smooth.
  2. Let the batter rest at least 2 hours, skimming off any foam.
  3. Heat the oven to 250 °C with a large round pan inside.
  4. Pour the oil into the hot pan, then the batter, and stir lightly to spread the oil.
  5. Bake for 15–20 minutes until set with crisp golden edges.
  6. Season with pepper and cut into wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less oil in the pan and a non-stick tray.
  • Eat it on its own rather than on top of pizza for a lighter snack.
  • Add chopped rosemary or onion for flavour without extra fat.

History

Fainá descends from Ligurian farinata, brought to the Río de la Plata by Genoese immigrants. In Argentina and Uruguay it became inseparable from pizzeria culture. In Uruguay it is also a tradition to eat it on the 29th of each month alongside gnocchi in some homes.
