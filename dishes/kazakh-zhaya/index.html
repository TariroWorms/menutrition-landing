Zhaya (salted smoked horse hip)

Origin: Kazakhstan

Zhaya is cut from the hip of a horse, a thick piece of meat with a layer of fat on top. It is rubbed with salt, pepper and garlic, left to cure, then dried and smoked. Boiled and cooled, it is sliced thin and served on the meat platter, where it tastes smoky, salty and deeply meaty.

What it is made of

horse meathorse fatsaltblack peppergarlic

Recipe

8 servings300 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg horse hip with fat layer
  • 40 g salt
  • 4 garlic cloves (20 g)
  • 1 tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Trim the meat into an even block, keeping the fat layer.
  2. Rub all over with salt, crushed garlic and pepper.
  3. Leave to cure in the cold for 2–3 days, turning daily.
  4. Dry in an airy place for a day, then cold-smoke for several hours.
  5. Simmer the zhaya in water for about 2 hours.
  6. Cool completely and slice thinly across the grain.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim some of the fat layer before eating.
  • Soak briefly before boiling to reduce the salt.
  • Serve thin slices with fresh vegetables rather than bread alone.

History

Zhaya is one of the classic horse-meat preserves of the Kazakh winter slaughter, alongside kazy and karta. Curing and smoking let herders keep meat through the cold months, and today it is a favourite festive cold cut.
