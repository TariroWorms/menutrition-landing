Šakotis (Lithuanian spit cake)

Origin: Lithuania

Šakotis is a tall, hollow cake with pointed branches, made by pouring an egg-and-butter batter in layers onto a rotating spit over a fire or in a special oven. The result is golden, light and crisp, with a sweet vanilla and buttery flavour. It is the centrepiece of weddings and holidays and is broken into pieces and eaten with tea.

What it is made of

Recipe

20 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 10 eggs (600 g)
  • 300 g butter
  • 300 g sugar
  • 300 g flour
  • 200 ml sour cream
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • a pinch of salt

Method

  1. Cream the butter with the sugar until pale.
  2. Beat in the eggs one at a time and add vanilla and sour cream.
  3. Fold in the flour and salt to a thick pourable batter.
  4. Pour a thin layer onto the rotating spit over heat and bake until golden.
  5. Repeat layer by layer, letting the dripping batter form branches.
  6. Cool and slide off the spit.

How to make it lighter

  • Slice thinly: this is a rich cake and a small piece is enough.
  • Reduce sugar by a quarter without hurting structure.
  • Pair with unsweetened tea or berries.

History

Šakotis is considered a Lithuanian national dessert and is traditionally served at weddings and other major celebrations. The branches form as drops of batter run down the spit while it turns.
