Chuchvara

Origin: Uzbekistan

Chuchvara are tiny dumplings, often no bigger than a thumbnail, filled with minced meat and onion. They are boiled and served either floating in a clear broth with herbs or drained and topped with yogurt or sour cream. The thin dough and small size make them delicate and quick to eat. Black pepper and a dash of vinegar are common at the table.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour, 150 ml water, 1 egg, salt
  • 350 g minced beef or lamb
  • 2 onions, grated
  • 1.2 l meat broth
  • Black pepper, salt
  • Fresh dill, yogurt to serve

Method

  1. Make a firm dough and rest it 30 minutes.
  2. Mix minced meat with onion, salt, pepper and a splash of water.
  3. Roll the dough very thin, cut into 4 cm squares, fill and fold into small rings.
  4. Boil in salted water or broth for 5-6 minutes after they float.
  5. Serve in hot broth with dill, or drained with yogurt.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve in broth rather than with sour cream to keep it light.
  • Use lean minced beef.
  • Add chopped greens and tomato to the broth for extra vegetables.

History

Chuchvara are part of the wide Central Asian family of small boiled dumplings, related to Russian pelmeni and Uyghur chüchüre. Shaping them is a shared family task, and skilled cooks are proud of how small they can make them.
