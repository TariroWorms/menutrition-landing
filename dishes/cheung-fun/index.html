🍤 Cheung fun (shrimp rice noodle rolls)

Origin: China · Guangdong

Cheung fun are silky sheets of steamed rice noodle rolled around plump shrimp, cut into lengths and doused with a sweetened soy sauce and a few drops of sesame oil. The rolls are soft, slippery and slightly chewy, with the shrimp giving a sweet, springy bite. They are a dim sum classic, served hot in small portions alongside tea.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 120 g rice flour
  • 30 g tapioca starch
  • 350 ml water
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 250 g peeled shrimp
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 2 spring onions, chopped

Method

  1. Whisk rice flour, tapioca starch, water and oil into a thin batter; rest 15 minutes.
  2. Lightly oil a flat tray, pour in a thin layer of batter, scatter a few shrimp and spring onion and steam 3–4 minutes until set.
  3. Scrape the sheet into a roll with a spatula and repeat with the remaining batter.
  4. Simmer soy sauce, sugar and 3 tbsp water until the sugar dissolves; stir in sesame oil.
  5. Cut the rolls into pieces and pour the sauce over just before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Drizzle the sauce lightly — most of the sugar and salt sit in it.
  • Use only a few drops of sesame oil; 1 tbsp of any oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Pair a plate of cheung fun with steamed greens instead of fried dim sum.

History

Rice noodle rolls are a Cantonese specialty usually traced to Guangdong province, where they became a breakfast and street-food staple in the twentieth century. The name means 'intestine noodle', after the rolls' shape, and shrimp-filled cheung fun are a fixture of dim sum restaurants in Guangzhou, Hong Kong and Chinese communities worldwide.
