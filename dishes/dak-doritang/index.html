🍲 Spicy braised chicken (dak-dori-tang)

Origin: South Korea

Dak-dori-tang, also called dakbokkeumtang, is chicken on the bone braised with chunky potatoes, carrots and onion in a red, spicy sauce of Korean chili flakes, soy sauce and garlic. The potatoes soak up the sauce and thicken it, and the chicken falls off the bone. It is a hearty family dish served in a shared pot with rice.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces on the bone
  • 3 potatoes, cut into large chunks
  • 1 carrot, cut into chunks
  • 1 onion, cut into wedges
  • 3 tbsp gochugaru (Korean chili flakes)
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 5 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 500 ml water
  • 2 scallions, sliced

Method

  1. Blanch the chicken in boiling water for 3 minutes, drain and rinse.
  2. Mix the gochugaru, soy sauce, garlic and sugar into a paste.
  3. Put the chicken, paste and water in a pot, bring to the boil and simmer covered for 15 minutes.
  4. Add the potatoes, carrot and onion and simmer for 20–25 minutes until the potatoes are soft and the sauce has thickened.
  5. Top with scallions and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the chicken skin before braising and skim fat from the sauce.
  • Swap one potato for more carrot or onion.
  • Serve a smaller scoop of rice, since the potatoes already add starch.

History

Dak-dori-tang is a long-standing Korean home dish. The origin of the word “dori” is debated, and the National Institute of Korean Language has recommended the alternative name dakbokkeumtang. It remains a comfort food across South Korea.
