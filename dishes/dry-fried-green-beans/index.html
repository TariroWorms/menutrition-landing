🫘 Dry-fried green beans

Origin: China · Sichuan

Dry-fried green beans are a Sichuan classic in which whole green beans are fried until their skins blister and wrinkle, then tossed with savoury ground pork, garlic, dried chili and chopped preserved vegetable. The beans are tender inside and slightly chewy outside, with a deep, salty and spicy flavour. They are served as a side dish with rice.

Recipe

3 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 450 g green beans, trimmed
  • 120 g ground pork
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 4 dried chilies, cut
  • 2 tbsp preserved vegetable (ya cai), chopped
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Dry the beans thoroughly.
  2. Fry them in the oil over medium-high heat, turning, for 8–10 minutes until blistered and wrinkled, then remove.
  3. In the same pan fry the pork until browned and crumbly.
  4. Add garlic, chili and preserved vegetable and stir-fry 1 minute.
  5. Return the beans, toss for 1–2 minutes and season with salt.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean ground pork or halve the amount to cut the fat.
  • Blister the beans in a hot dry pan or under the grill with 1 tsp oil instead of shallow-frying.
  • Add more beans and less pork to keep the plate large and the calories low.

History

Gan bian si ji dou, or dry-fried green beans, comes from Sichuan cuisine, where 'dry-frying' means cooking ingredients in a little oil until their moisture evaporates and they shrivel. The preserved vegetable is traditionally Sichuan ya cai. The dish is now a standard on Sichuan restaurant menus worldwide.
