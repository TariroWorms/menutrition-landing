Pamonha (Brazilian corn husk parcel)

Origin: Brazil

Pamonha is a soft parcel of freshly grated green corn mixed with milk and sugar, or with cheese for a savoury version, then tied in corn husks and boiled. The texture is tender and slightly springy, and the flavour is pure fresh corn. It is eaten warm as a snack or breakfast and sold by street vendors and roadside stalls.

Recipe

10 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 8 ears fresh corn with husks
  • 300 ml milk
  • 100 g sugar (or 150 g cheese for savoury)
  • 40 g butter, melted
  • 1 pinch salt

Method

  1. Remove the husks carefully and keep the best ones; cut off the kernels.
  2. Blend the kernels with the milk into a thick paste.
  3. Mix in sugar, butter and salt (or crumbled cheese).
  4. Fold two husks into a pouch, fill with paste and tie with husk strips.
  5. Boil in plenty of water for 40-50 minutes until firm.
  6. Drain and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Make the savoury version with less butter and a light cheese.
  • Fresh corn is naturally sweet, so reduce the sugar.
  • Pamonhas freeze well; boil a batch and reheat for quick snacks.

History

Pamonha comes from Indigenous corn cookery and the name has Tupi roots. It is especially loved in Goiás, São Paulo and the Northeast, and is one of the essential foods of the June festivals.
