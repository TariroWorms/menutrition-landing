🫔 Pork Tamales

Tamales are parcels of corn masa dough wrapped around a filling and steamed inside dried corn husks. This classic version is filled with tender shredded pork simmered in red chili sauce. The masa turns soft and fluffy while the filling stays juicy and gently spicy. They are unwrapped at the table and eaten warm, often at breakfast, holidays and family gatherings.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g masa harina (nixtamalised corn)
  • 600 ml warm stock or water
  • 80 ml neutral oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 500 g pork shoulder
  • 300 ml red chili sauce
  • 18 dried corn husks

Method

  1. Soak the corn husks in hot water for 30 minutes until pliable.
  2. Simmer the pork in salted water for about 1.5 hours until tender, then shred it and mix with the chili sauce.
  3. Beat the masa harina with oil, salt, baking powder and warm stock into a soft, spreadable dough.
  4. Spread 2–3 tbsp of masa on each husk, add a spoonful of pork, fold the sides in and the bottom up.
  5. Stand the tamales upright in a steamer and steam for 60–75 minutes, until the dough pulls away from the husk.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the pork before simmering to keep the filling leaner.
  • Use less oil in the masa: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Pair one or two tamales with a fresh salad or salsa instead of rice and beans.

History

Tamales date back to pre-Columbian Mesoamerica, where corn dough cooked in leaves was eaten long before the Spanish arrived. Pork became a common filling after the conquest, and today tamales are a festive staple across Mexico, especially around Christmas and Candelaria in early February.
