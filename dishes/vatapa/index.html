Vatapá (Bahian bread and peanut cream)

Origin: Brazil · Bahia

Vatapá is a velvety orange cream that sits somewhere between a sauce and a purée. Soaked bread is cooked with coconut milk, ground peanuts and cashews, dried shrimp, ginger and dendê oil until it thickens and pulls from the pan. It tastes nutty, gently sweet and rich, with a briny shrimp note. It fills acarajé and is served with rice and chicken or fish.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g stale bread
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 100 g roasted peanuts and 50 g cashews
  • 80 g dried shrimp
  • 3 tbsp dendê oil (40 ml)
  • 1 onion, 15 g ginger, cilantro, salt

Method

  1. Soak the bread in half the coconut milk and some water until soft.
  2. Blend the bread with peanuts, cashews, dried shrimp, onion and ginger into a smooth paste.
  3. Cook the paste in a heavy pan with the rest of the coconut milk, stirring constantly.
  4. After 15–20 minutes, when thick and glossy, stir in the dendê oil and cilantro.
  5. Season and serve warm with rice or as a filling for acarajé.

How to make it lighter

  • Use it as a sauce in small amounts; it is calorie-dense.
  • Replace part of the coconut milk with fish stock for a lighter cream.
  • Serve with plenty of vegetables and lean fish to balance the plate.

History

Vatapá is one of the core dishes of Afro-Bahian cooking and is closely tied to the baianas de acarajé. In northern Brazil, especially Pará, a version thickened with flour is eaten at Círio de Nazaré feasts.
