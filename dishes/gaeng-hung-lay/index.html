🍛 Gaeng hung lay (Northern Thai pork curry)

Origin: Thailand · Chiang Mai

Gaeng hung lay is a rich, dark pork belly curry from northern Thailand. The meat is braised slowly with a curry paste spiced in the Burmese style, plenty of fresh ginger, sour tamarind and whole peanuts until it is tender and the sauce is sweet, sour and deeply savoury. It contains no coconut milk and is eaten with sticky rice.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork belly, cut into 4 cm cubes
  • 4 tbsp massaman or hung lay curry paste
  • 60 g fresh ginger, cut into thin strips
  • 3 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 60 g roasted peanuts
  • 2 tbsp palm or brown sugar
  • 6 garlic cloves, peeled whole
  • Salt
  • 500 ml water

Method

  1. Rub the pork with the curry paste and leave to marinate for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Fry the pork in a heavy pot over medium heat until it releases some fat and browns.
  3. Add the water, tamarind and sugar, cover and simmer for 1 hour.
  4. Add the ginger, garlic and peanuts and simmer for another 30 minutes until the pork is very tender.
  5. Season with salt and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Pork belly is fatty: chill the curry and lift off the solid fat before reheating.
  • Swap part of the belly for lean pork shoulder to cut the energy per portion.
  • Serve a small portion of the curry with plenty of steamed vegetables and less rice.

History

The curry is a speciality of Chiang Mai and the old Lanna kingdom, and is generally said to have come to northern Thailand with Burmese and Shan communities; it is related to the Burmese hinlay. It is often cooked for festivals and temple fairs in the north.
