Moqueca baiana (Bahian fish stew)

Origin: Brazil · Bahia

Moqueca baiana is the signature fish stew of Bahia. Firm white fish is marinated in lime and garlic, then layered with tomato, onion and sweet pepper and gently cooked in coconut milk and dendê oil. The sauce turns a deep orange, silky and slightly sweet, with a fresh lift from cilantro. It is served bubbling in its clay pot with white rice, farofa and pirão.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g firm white fish steaks
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 3 tbsp dendê oil (40 ml)
  • 3 tomatoes (300 g), sliced
  • 1 onion (150 g), sliced
  • 1 red and 1 green bell pepper (250 g), sliced
  • juice of 2 limes, 3 garlic cloves, 1 bunch cilantro

Method

  1. Rub the fish with lime juice, crushed garlic and salt and leave for 20 minutes.
  2. Cover the base of a wide clay pot or pan with half the onion, tomato and pepper slices.
  3. Lay the fish on top and cover with the remaining vegetables and half the cilantro.
  4. Pour in the coconut milk and drizzle with dendê oil.
  5. Cover and simmer gently for 15–20 minutes without stirring, shaking the pot now and then.
  6. Scatter with the rest of the cilantro and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk and halve the dendê oil to cut the fat noticeably.
  • Serve with extra vegetables or a salad instead of farofa to keep the meal lighter.
  • Choose a firm fish like hake or cod so it holds together during the simmer.

History

Fish stews cooked in clay pots existed among Indigenous peoples of the Brazilian coast. The Bahian version took its defining ingredients, dendê oil and coconut milk, from Afro-Brazilian cooking in Salvador. Today it is one of the best-known dishes of Bahia.
