Kuy teav (Cambodian pork noodle soup)

Origin: Cambodia

A clear, gently sweet pork-bone broth poured over rice noodles with sliced pork, meatballs and sometimes shrimp. It is topped with fried garlic, scallions and herbs, and each diner adds lime, chili and sauce at the table. It is a classic Cambodian breakfast.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork bones
  • 3 l water
  • 300 g rice noodles
  • 200 g sliced pork
  • 100 g bean sprouts
  • 3 scallions
  • fried garlic, lime, chili and fish sauce to serve

Method

  1. Blanch the bones, then simmer them in fresh water for 1.5 hours.
  2. Season the strained broth with a little salt, sugar and fish sauce.
  3. Poach the sliced pork briefly in the broth.
  4. Soften the noodles in hot water and divide into bowls.
  5. Add pork, sprouts, scallions and hot broth, then top with fried garlic.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim fat from the broth.
  • Add extra greens and sprouts to lighten the bowl.
  • Go easy on the added sauces.

History

Kuy teav is eaten across Cambodia in the morning, in homes and at market stalls. The noodle soup tradition is shared with neighbouring Vietnam and southern China, but the Khmer version has its own seasonings and garnishes.
