Tamales panameños (Panamanian corn tamales)

Origin: Panama

Panamanian tamales are soft, moist corn-dough parcels filled with seasoned stewed chicken or pork, with olives, raisins and sometimes capers, boiled in banana leaves. They taste savory with small sweet and salty accents. They are eaten at Christmas and on celebration days with coffee or a hot drink.

Recipe

10 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g corn masa
  • 500 g chicken (cooked and shredded)
  • 80 ml cooking broth
  • 2 tbsp achiote oil
  • 1 onion
  • 1 sweet pepper
  • 50 g green olives
  • 40 g raisins
  • banana leaves and string

Method

  1. Cook a seasoned filling of chicken, onion, pepper and achiote with a little broth.
  2. Mix the masa with broth, oil and salt into a soft dough.
  3. Spread dough on a banana leaf and add a spoon of filling, olives and raisins.
  4. Fold the leaf into a parcel and tie with string.
  5. Boil the tamales in water for about 1 hour and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean chicken breast for the filling.
  • Use less oil in the dough.
  • Serve with salad instead of rice.

History

Tamales exist throughout Latin America, and Panama's version is wrapped in banana leaf and cooked in water, unlike dry-husk tamales of Mexico. They are a festive classic, usually made in large batches by families.
