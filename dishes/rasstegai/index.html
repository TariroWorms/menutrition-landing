Rasstegai (open-top Russian fish pies)

Origin: Russia

Rasstegai are small boat-shaped pies made of soft yeast dough, pinched at both ends but left open in the centre. They are typically filled with minced or diced fish, rice and onion, and a piece of salmon is often placed on top. Traditionally a spoonful of hot fish broth is poured into the opening before eating, making them very juicy.

Recipe

10 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • Dough: 500 g flour, 250 ml warm milk, 1 egg, 10 g dry yeast, 50 g butter, 1 tsp sugar, salt
  • 400 g white fish fillet (pike perch or cod)
  • 150 g salmon fillet
  • 100 g cooked rice
  • 1 onion (100 g)
  • 1 egg for glazing
  • 100 ml fish broth (optional)

Method

  1. Make a soft yeast dough from the dough ingredients and let it rise for 1 hour.
  2. Fry the chopped onion, then mix with diced white fish and rice; season.
  3. Divide the dough into 10 balls and roll each into an oval.
  4. Put filling in the centre, pinch the ends together and leave the middle open.
  5. Place a slice of salmon in each opening, rest the pies 15 minutes and brush with egg.
  6. Bake at 200 °C for 20-25 minutes until golden.
  7. Pour a spoonful of hot broth into each pie before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean white fish only and skip the salmon slice on top.
  • Glaze with milk instead of egg and use less butter in the dough.
  • Eat one pie with a bowl of clear soup or salad as a full meal.

History

Rasstegai were a well-known pastry of old Moscow taverns and were often served with fish soup. The name comes from the Russian word for 'unbuttoned', a reference to the open top.
