🥟 Shish Barak

Origin: Levant · Lebanon and Syria

Shish barak are small dumplings of thin dough filled with spiced ground lamb, folded into little hats and simmered in a warm yogurt sauce. The sauce is flavoured with garlic and dried mint, and the dish is often served with plain rice on the side. The result is soft, tangy and comforting, with tender pasta and savoury meat in every spoonful.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g dough (flour and water)
  • 250 g ground lamb
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 l plain yogurt
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp dried mint
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Fry the onion in a little oil, add the lamb, season and cook until browned; cool.
  2. Roll the dough thin, cut 5 cm rounds, fill each with a little meat, fold into half-moons and pinch the tips together.
  3. Bake or pan-toast the dumplings briefly until lightly set.
  4. Whisk the yogurt with a little water and salt, heat gently while stirring until it simmers, then add the dumplings and cook 10–15 minutes.
  5. Fry the garlic and mint in the oil for a few seconds and pour over the pot. Serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean ground lamb and drain the fat after browning.
  • Make the yogurt sauce with low-fat yogurt; stir constantly so it does not split.
  • Serve a smaller bowl and skip the rice side to cut the portion's energy.

History

Shish barak is a home dish of Lebanon, Syria and Palestine, with relatives in the meat dumplings of Turkey and the Caucasus. It is considered a labour of love because every dumpling is shaped by hand, and families often make large batches together.
