Arepa pelúa

Origin: Venezuela

Arepa pelúa means "hairy arepa", a joke about the strands of grated yellow cheese that stick out over the filling. Inside is carne mechada, beef stewed with tomato, onion and peppers and pulled into threads. The hot meat melts the cheese a little, so every bite is juicy, salty and stringy. It is one of the most ordered fillings at Venezuelan areperas.

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g precooked white corn flour
  • 380 ml warm water
  • 400 g carne mechada (stewed shredded beef)
  • 150 g yellow cheese (gouda or cheddar), grated
  • salt

Method

  1. Knead the corn flour with water and salt into a soft dough; shape 6 discs.
  2. Cook on a hot griddle for 6–7 minutes per side, then finish in a 200 °C oven for 10 minutes.
  3. Reheat the shredded beef with a little of its sauce.
  4. Split the arepas and fill with hot beef.
  5. Pile grated cheese on top so it hangs over the edges and starts to melt.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a lean cut like flank for the beef and skim the fat from the stew.
  • Cut the cheese to 15 g per arepa; a stronger cheese gives flavour with less.
  • Serve with a tomato or cabbage salad instead of fries.

History

The pelúa grew out of two arepera staples, shredded beef and yellow cheese, and its nickname is typical of the humorous way Venezuelans name their arepas. It is now found wherever Venezuelans have opened arepa shops abroad.
