South Indian filter coffee (kaapi)

Origin: India · Tamil Nadu

Filter coffee is made by dripping hot water through ground coffee and chicory in a metal filter to get a dark decoction. A spoonful of that decoction is mixed with plenty of hot boiled milk and sugar. It is frothy, strong and sweet. It is served in a steel tumbler and davara and cooled by pouring from a height.

Recipe

1 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp ground coffee with chicory (25 g)
  • 150 ml boiling water for the decoction
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 2 tsp sugar (10 g)
  • pinch of cardamom (optional)
  • steel tumbler and davara

Method

  1. Put the ground coffee in the upper chamber of the filter and pour in the boiling water.
  2. Cover and let the decoction drip slowly for 15-20 minutes.
  3. Boil the milk until frothy.
  4. Put the sugar and 3 tbsp of decoction into a tumbler.
  5. Add hot milk and pour between tumbler and davara a few times to froth.

How to make it lighter

  • Use semi-skimmed milk and cut the sugar to one teaspoon.
  • Make the decoction stronger and add more milk to dilute it.
  • Skip the afternoon cup if caffeine keeps you awake.

History

Coffee reached southern India centuries ago and became a daily habit in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. Mixing in roasted chicory is a long-standing local practice that makes the brew thicker and slightly bitter.
