🍮 Sticky toffee pudding

Origin: United Kingdom · England

Sticky toffee pudding is a soft, moist sponge made with chopped dates, baked and then soaked in a warm toffee sauce. The dates melt into the batter and give it a deep caramel flavour, while the sauce makes the top glossy and sticky. It is served warm with a pour of cream.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g pitted dates, chopped
  • 250 ml boiling water
  • 175 g self-raising flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 80 g butter, softened
  • 120 g brown sugar
  • For the toffee sauce: 100 g brown sugar, 60 g butter, 150 ml cream
  • Cream to serve

Method

  1. Soak the dates in the boiling water for 10 minutes, then mash lightly.
  2. Beat the butter and sugar, then beat in the eggs one by one.
  3. Fold in the flour and the dates with their liquid.
  4. Bake in a greased dish at 180 °C for 30–35 minutes.
  5. Melt the sauce ingredients together and simmer 3 minutes.
  6. Pour half the sauce over the warm pudding and serve with the rest and a little cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small squares: a 100 g piece is already a full dessert.
  • Use half the toffee sauce and skip the extra cream on top.
  • Share one portion between two after a filling meal.

History

The pudding is usually credited to English country hotels in the Lake District in the 1970s, though several places claim it. It became a fixture of British pub and restaurant dessert menus in the following decades.
