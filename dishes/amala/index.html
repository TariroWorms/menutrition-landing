Amala (yam flour swallow)

Origin: Nigeria · Yoruba

Amala is a soft, brown-black dough made from dried yam flour. The flour is stirred into boiling water with a wooden spoon until smooth and elastic. It has a mild earthy taste and is never eaten alone. Pieces are pinched off by hand, dipped in soups like ewedu, gbegiri or egusi and swallowed.

What it is made of

yam flour

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g yam flour (elubo)
  • 900 g water

Method

  1. Boil about 900 ml of water in a pot.
  2. Reduce the heat and sprinkle in a third of the flour while stirring to avoid lumps.
  3. Cover and cook 3 minutes.
  4. Add the remaining flour gradually, beating firmly with a wooden spoon.
  5. Cover 2 minutes, beat again until smooth and elastic, then shape into balls.

How to make it lighter

  • Keep the swallow portion modest and let the vegetable soup fill the plate.
  • Pair with a broth-based soup rather than a very oily one.
  • Eat slowly with plenty of leafy greens and lean protein.

History

Amala is a staple of the Yoruba people of southwest Nigeria and is sold in many local restaurants called amala joints. The dark colour comes from the dried, peeled yam used for the flour.
