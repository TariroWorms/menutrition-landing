Karelian pasty (Karjalanpiirakka)

Origin: Finland · Karelia

Karjalanpiirakka is an oval open pasty with a thin, crisp rye crust and a filling of soft milky rice porridge. The edges are pinched into a decorative frill. It is brushed with melted butter and traditionally eaten with munavoi, a spread of chopped boiled egg mixed with butter.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g rice
  • 500 ml milk
  • 200 g rye flour
  • 50 g wheat flour
  • 60 g butter
  • 2 pcs hard-boiled eggs

Method

  1. Cook the rice in milk and water with salt until it becomes a thick, creamy porridge and cool it.
  2. Mix rye flour, a little wheat flour, water and salt into a firm dough.
  3. Roll the dough into a very thin sheet and cut into circles.
  4. Spoon the porridge onto the center of each circle and fold and pinch the edges into a frill.
  5. Bake at 250 C for 10 to 15 minutes until the crust is dark in places.
  6. Brush with melted butter and serve with the egg butter.

How to make it lighter

  • Reduce the butter on top and use less egg butter.
  • Use wholegrain rye for more fibre.
  • Fill with mashed carrot or potato for a different filling.

History

It comes from Karelia, the region shared by Finland and Russia, and has long been a staple of eastern Finnish homes. Karelian pasties are recognised as a protected traditional specialty in the European Union.
