Sajji (Balochi roast stuffed lamb or chicken)

Origin: Pakistan · Balochistan

Sajji is the great roast of Balochistan: a whole lamb or large chicken, rubbed only with salt, sometimes stuffed with rice, raisins and nuts, and set upright on skewers beside glowing embers. It cooks slowly for hours, so the meat turns soft and juicy while the skin stays smoky. The flavour is clean and meaty rather than hot. It is carved at the table and eaten with flatbread or the stuffing rice.

What it is made of

chickenriceblack peppersaltraisinsalmonds

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken (1.5 kg)
  • 200 g basmati rice, cooked
  • 50 g raisins
  • 40 g almonds
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 30 g butter

Method

  1. Rub the chicken inside and out with salt and pepper and rest 1 hour.
  2. Mix the cooked rice with raisins and chopped almonds.
  3. Stuff the cavity loosely and close it with a skewer or string.
  4. Roast at 170 C for about 2 hours, basting with butter, until the skin is deep golden.
  5. Rest 10 minutes, carve and serve with the rice and flatbread.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin before eating to cut a good share of the fat.
  • Use less rice stuffing and serve with a big salad.
  • Choose chicken instead of lamb for a leaner version.

History

Sajji comes from the nomadic and mountain communities of Balochistan, where a whole animal was roasted in the open for gatherings. It later spread to Quetta, Karachi and Lahore restaurants.
