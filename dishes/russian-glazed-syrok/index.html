Glazed syrok (chocolate-coated curd bar)

Origin: Russia

A glazed syrok is a small bar of sweetened, whipped farmer's cheese covered in a thin shell of chocolate. The cold, tangy-sweet curd filling contrasts with the snap of the glaze, much like a mini cheesecake bar. It is sold in the dairy aisle and eaten straight from the fridge as a snack or with tea.

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g full-fat tvorog (farmer's cheese)
  • 80 g powdered sugar
  • 60 g soft butter
  • 1 tsp vanilla sugar
  • 200 g dark or milk chocolate
  • 1 tsp coconut or vegetable oil

Method

  1. Press the tvorog through a sieve or blend until perfectly smooth.
  2. Beat in the powdered sugar, butter and vanilla.
  3. Shape the mixture into small bars about 2 × 7 cm and freeze for 30 minutes until firm.
  4. Melt the chocolate with the oil over a water bath.
  5. Dip each bar in the chocolate, let the excess drip off and place on baking paper.
  6. Chill until the glaze sets and keep refrigerated.

How to make it lighter

  • Use low-fat tvorog and skip the butter, adding a spoon of Greek yogurt for creaminess.
  • Reduce the sugar and flavour with vanilla or lemon zest.
  • Coat only the top in dark chocolate to cut sugar and fat.

History

Glazed curd bars appeared in the Soviet Union in the mid-twentieth century and quickly became a favourite treat for children and adults. Today they come in many flavours and fillings, from vanilla to condensed milk.
