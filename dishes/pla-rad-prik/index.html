🐟 Pla rad prik (fried fish with chili sauce)

Pla rad prik is a whole fish deep-fried until the skin is crackling and the edges are crisp, then smothered in a glossy sauce of chilies, garlic, fish sauce and palm sugar. The sauce is sweet, salty and spicy, soaking into the crunchy fish. It is a centrepiece dish in Thai restaurants, shared with steamed jasmine rice.

Recipe

3 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 1 whole fish such as sea bass or snapper (about 800 g), cleaned
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • 6 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 5 red chilies, chopped
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 3 tbsp palm or brown sugar
  • 3 tbsp tamarind water or lime juice
  • 4 tbsp water

Method

  1. Score the fish deeply on both sides and pat it very dry.
  2. Deep-fry in hot oil (180 °C) for 6–8 minutes per side until crisp and golden; drain on paper.
  3. In 1 tbsp oil fry the garlic and chilies for 1 minute.
  4. Add fish sauce, sugar, tamarind and water and simmer until slightly syrupy.
  5. Pour the sauce over the fish and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the fish instead of deep-frying to save a lot of oil.
  • Halve the sugar in the sauce: 1 tbsp sugar ≈ 50 kcal.
  • Eat the flesh and leave the oily, crisp skin.

History

Rad prik means “topped with chili” in Thai, and fried fish served this way is a staple of Thai restaurants and family meals; no specific origin is recorded.
