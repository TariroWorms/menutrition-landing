🍚 Cơm tấm sườn (broken rice with grilled pork chop)

Origin: Vietnam · Saigon (Ho Chi Minh City)

Cơm tấm is Saigon's broken-rice plate: fluffy fragments of rice topped with a sweet-savoury grilled pork chop marinated in fish sauce, garlic and sugar, with a fried egg, a spoonful of scallion oil and tangy pickled vegetables. Smoky, caramelised pork meets soft rice and bright pickles. Everything is drizzled with nước chấm, a sweet-sour fish-sauce dipping sauce.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thin bone-in pork chops (about 600 g)
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce, 1 tbsp sugar, 2 garlic cloves and 1 shallot, minced (marinade)
  • 400 g broken rice
  • 4 eggs
  • Pickled carrot and daikon (do chua) to serve
  • Dipping sauce: 3 tbsp fish sauce, 2 tbsp sugar, 2 tbsp lime juice, 6 tbsp water, 1 chilli
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 3 scallions, sliced, warmed in 2 tbsp oil (scallion oil)

Method

  1. Mix the marinade, coat the pork chops and leave for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Rinse the broken rice and cook it with about 500 ml water until fluffy.
  3. Grill the chops over high heat or under a hot grill for 4–5 minutes per side until charred and cooked through.
  4. Fry the eggs in the oil.
  5. Stir the dipping sauce ingredients until the sugar dissolves.
  6. Plate the rice with a chop, an egg and pickles and spoon scallion oil over the rice; pour over the dipping sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Pick a lean chop and trim the fat before marinating.
  • Take a smaller scoop of rice and extra pickles or cucumber and tomato.
  • Cook the egg with a light film of oil and use the scallion oil sparingly — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Broken rice, the fragments left over from milling, was long a cheap staple in southern Vietnam. In Saigon it became a much-loved street food, and today cơm tấm with grilled pork chop is eaten at all hours, from roadside stalls to restaurants.
