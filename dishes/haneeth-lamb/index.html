🍖 Lamb haneeth

Haneeth is Yemen's celebrated slow-roasted lamb: pieces of young lamb rubbed with cumin, coriander, cardamom and turmeric, cooked for hours in a sealed pit or oven until the meat falls off the bone. It is served on a bed of fragrant spiced rice that catches the rich juices. The meat is tender and aromatic rather than spicy-hot.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg lamb shoulder or leg pieces, on the bone
  • 500 g basmati rice, rinsed and soaked 30 minutes
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1½ tsp turmeric
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Mix the cumin, coriander, cardamom, 1 tsp turmeric, salt, pepper and 1 tbsp oil and rub over the lamb; marinate at least 2 hours.
  2. Place the lamb in a roasting dish with 100 ml water, seal tightly with foil and roast at 160 °C for 3 hours, until falling off the bone.
  3. Uncover, raise to 220 °C and roast 15 minutes to brown.
  4. Meanwhile soften the onion in the remaining oil, add the rice, remaining turmeric, salt and 850 ml water; cover and cook on low heat 20 minutes.
  5. Serve the lamb on the rice with the skimmed juices poured over.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before marinating.
  • Skim the fat off the roasting juices before pouring them on the rice.
  • Keep the rice to one modest scoop and fill up with salad and salsa.

History

Haneeth comes from Yemen and the south-west of the Arabian Peninsula, where meat was traditionally slow-roasted in a sealed pit oven; it is close kin to mandi. Lamb haneeth is a festive dish in Yemen, Saudi Arabia and the Gulf today.
