Tolma (stuffed grape leaves)

Origin: Georgia

Georgian tolma consists of small parcels of grape leaves, or cabbage leaves in winter, filled with minced beef and pork, rice, onion and plenty of herbs such as cilantro, dill, mint and basil. The rolls are packed tightly into a pot and simmered in broth until the leaves soften and the filling cooks through. They are served warm with a garlicky yogurt or matsoni sauce.

Recipe

6 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 40 grape leaves (about 200 g)
  • 400 g minced beef
  • 200 g minced pork
  • 100 g round rice
  • 2 onions (200 g)
  • a bunch of cilantro, dill and mint (60 g)
  • 700 ml broth
  • 300 g matsoni with 2 garlic cloves

Method

  1. Blanch the grape leaves for 1–2 minutes and drain.
  2. Mix the minced meat with raw rice, chopped onion, herbs, salt and pepper.
  3. Place a spoonful of filling on each leaf, fold in the sides and roll tightly.
  4. Pack the rolls seam side down in a pot, cover with a plate and add hot broth to just cover.
  5. Simmer gently for 50–60 minutes and serve with garlic matsoni.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and skip the pork for a lighter filling.
  • Serve with plain yogurt sauce instead of sour cream.
  • Add more herbs and less rice for fewer carbohydrates.

History

Stuffed leaves are found from the Balkans to Iran, and the Georgian name tolma is shared with Armenia and Azerbaijan. Grape leaf tolma is made in early summer, when young leaves are tender.
