Asado negro (Caracas black roast)

Origin: Venezuela · Caracas

Asado negro is a whole piece of beef eye of round browned in sugar that has been cooked to a dark caramel, then braised slowly with onion, garlic, peppers and tomato. The meat comes out almost black on the outside, sliced into tender rounds and covered with a glossy, sweet-and-savoury sauce. It is a Sunday and special-occasion dish in Caracas, usually served with white rice and fried sweet plantain.

Recipe

8 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg beef eye of round (muchacho redondo)
  • 80 g panela (papelón) or dark brown sugar
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 2 onions, 1 red pepper, 6 garlic cloves, 2 tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, black pepper, salt
  • 500 ml water or beef broth

Method

  1. Season the beef with garlic, salt, pepper and Worcestershire sauce and leave 2 hours or overnight.
  2. Melt the panela in the oil in a heavy pot until it turns dark brown, without letting it burn.
  3. Add the beef and turn it in the caramel until dark all over.
  4. Add chopped onion, pepper, garlic and tomato, pour in the water and cover.
  5. Braise on low heat for about 2.5 hours, turning occasionally, until tender.
  6. Slice the meat thinly, blend or reduce the sauce and pour it over.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the panela; the sauce is still dark and flavourful.
  • Eye of round is lean – trim any outer fat before cooking.
  • Serve with vegetables and a small portion of rice instead of fried plantain.

History

Asado negro is closely associated with Caracas and its home cooking. The dark crust comes from caramelising papelón, the Venezuelan raw cane sugar, before the meat is added.
