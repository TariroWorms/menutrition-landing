🍱 Tonkatsu

Tonkatsu is a thick pork cutlet coated in flour, beaten egg and coarse breadcrumbs, then deep-fried until the crust is shatteringly crisp and the meat stays juicy. It is sliced into strips and served with finely shredded raw cabbage and a thick, fruity tonkatsu sauce, usually with rice and miso soup on the side.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 2 pork loin chops, about 150 g each
  • 30 g flour
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 60 g coarse breadcrumbs
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • 200 g white cabbage, finely shredded
  • 4 tbsp tonkatsu sauce
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Trim the pork, nick the fat edge to stop curling and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Dust with flour, dip in egg and press into the breadcrumbs.
  3. Fry in oil at 170 °C for 5–6 minutes, turning once, until golden and cooked through.
  4. Drain on a rack, rest 2 minutes and slice.
  5. Serve with the shredded cabbage and sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the breaded cutlet instead of deep-frying to save a lot of oil.
  • Use lean loin and trim the fat edge.
  • Pile up the cabbage and go easy on the sauce, which contains sugar.

History

Tonkatsu grew out of Western-style cutlets introduced to Japan in the late 19th century; a Tokyo restaurant is usually credited with the thick, deep-fried pork version in the early 20th century. It is now a staple of Japanese set-meal restaurants.
