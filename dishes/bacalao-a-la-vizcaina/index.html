🐟 Bacalao a la vizcaína

Bacalao a la vizcaína is a Basque dish of desalted salt cod braised in a deep red sauce made from dried choricero peppers, slowly cooked onions, garlic, olive oil and tomato. The sauce is sweet, earthy and only mildly spicy, and the cod stays in large, juicy flakes. It is served hot in an earthenware dish, usually as a main course with boiled potatoes.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g salt cod loins, desalted for 36–48 hours
  • 8 dried choricero peppers (or 2 tbsp choricero pepper paste)
  • 3 onions, thinly sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 6 tbsp olive oil, salt if needed

Method

  1. Soak the cod in cold water in the fridge for 36–48 hours, changing the water every 8 hours. Soak the dried peppers in warm water for 1 hour.
  2. Cook the onions and garlic in 4 tbsp olive oil over very low heat for 45 minutes until soft and sweet. Add the tomatoes and cook 15 minutes more.
  3. Scrape the flesh from the soaked peppers and add it with a ladle of their soaking water. Simmer 10 minutes, then blend and sieve into a smooth sauce.
  4. Cut the cod into portions and fry skin side up in 2 tbsp oil for 2 minutes per side.
  5. Lay the cod in the sauce, cover and simmer gently for 10 minutes. Taste for salt before seasoning and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook the onions in 2 tablespoons of oil instead of 4 — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal — and add a splash of water if they catch.
  • Skip frying the cod and poach it directly in the sauce.
  • Serve with steamed green beans or a salad instead of potatoes for a lighter plate.

History

The dish is named after Biscay (Vizcaya), the Basque province around Bilbao, where salt cod has been a staple since Basque fishermen sailed to the North Atlantic cod grounds. Its signature sauce relies on choricero peppers, which arrived in Spain after the voyages to the Americas. Today it is a classic of Basque home cooking, especially during Lent and at Christmas.
