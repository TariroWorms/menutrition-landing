🍝 Spaghetti alla Nerano

Origin: Italy · Campania, Sorrento peninsula

Spaghetti alla Nerano is a summer pasta from the Sorrento peninsula: slices of zucchini fried until soft and golden, then tossed with spaghetti, provolone (traditionally provolone del Monaco), butter and fresh basil. Part of the zucchini melts into a creamy, pale green sauce. It tastes gentle, sweet and cheesy, with a fresh basil finish.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g spaghetti
  • 600 g zucchini, thinly sliced
  • 4 tbsp olive oil for frying
  • 80 g provolone, finely grated
  • 20 g butter
  • A handful of basil leaves
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Fry the zucchini slices in the oil in batches until soft and golden; drain and salt them.
  2. Set aside a few slices and lightly mash the rest in the pan.
  3. Cook the spaghetti until a little short of al dente; keep a cup of the cooking water.
  4. Add the pasta to the zucchini with a ladle of cooking water and cook for 1–2 minutes.
  5. Off the heat, stir in the butter, provolone and torn basil, tossing until creamy. Top with the reserved zucchini and pepper.

How to make it lighter

  • Roast the zucchini in the oven with 1 tbsp of oil instead of frying them; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Skip the butter — the cheese and cooking water still make it creamy.
  • Use more zucchini and less pasta for a lighter, larger plate.

History

The dish is named after Nerano, a fishing village on the Sorrento peninsula near Massa Lubrense. Local accounts usually credit a restaurant there with creating it in the mid-twentieth century, and it has since become a signature pasta of the Amalfi and Sorrento coast.
