Chakapuli (lamb and tarragon stew)

Origin: Georgia

Chakapuli is a bright green spring stew of lamb or veal cooked with masses of tarragon, green onions, coriander and sour green plums or tkemali. A splash of dry white wine adds acidity, and the broth turns fragrant and slightly sour. It is eaten hot with bread to mop up the herb-laden juices.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg lamb shoulder on the bone
  • 150 g tarragon
  • 100 g green onions
  • 50 g coriander
  • 200 ml dry white wine
  • 150 g sour green plums or 100 ml tkemali
  • 2 garlic cloves, salt

Method

  1. Cut the lamb into pieces and place in a heavy pot.
  2. Chop the tarragon, green onions and coriander roughly.
  3. Layer the herbs over the meat, add salt and pour in the wine.
  4. Cover and simmer gently for about 1.5 hours, adding a little water if needed.
  5. Add the plums or tkemali and cook 15 more minutes.
  6. Stir in crushed garlic and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before cooking.
  • Use veal for a leaner version.
  • Serve with a salad instead of a lot of bread.

History

The dish is traditionally linked with spring and Easter, when young lamb and fresh tarragon are in season. It is especially associated with eastern Georgia, where white wine is used in the braise.
