Gyoza

Origin: Japan

Gyoza are Japanese pan-fried dumplings: thin wheat wrappers folded around a juicy filling of minced pork, finely chopped cabbage, garlic and ginger. They are fried on one side until golden and crisp, then steamed in the same pan so the tops stay soft and tender. They are served hot with a dipping sauce, often of rice vinegar and chili oil, as a snack, side dish or with rice.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 24 round gyoza wrappers
  • 250 g minced pork
  • 150 g cabbage, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, grated
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • Salt, white pepper
  • 1 tbsp oil and 80 ml water for cooking
  • Dipping sauce: soy sauce, rice vinegar and chili oil

Method

  1. Salt the cabbage, leave for 10 minutes and squeeze out the water.
  2. Mix the pork, cabbage, garlic, ginger, salt and pepper until sticky.
  3. Place about 1 tbsp of filling in each wrapper, wet the edge and fold with small pleats to seal.
  4. Fry the gyoza flat side down in the oil for 2–3 minutes until golden.
  5. Add the water, cover and steam for 5 minutes, then uncover and cook until the bottoms are crisp again. Serve with the dipping sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced pork and more cabbage in the filling.
  • Use a non-stick pan and just 1 tsp oil per batch; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Count them — five or six gyoza with a vegetable side is a sensible portion.

History

Gyoza are adapted from Chinese jiaozi, and they became widely popular in Japan after the Second World War, when people returning from northeastern China brought the dumplings home. Cities such as Utsunomiya and Hamamatsu are now famous for them.
