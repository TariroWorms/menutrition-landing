🦞 Lobster bisque

Lobster bisque is a smooth, velvety French soup made by simmering lobster shells and meat with tomato, aromatic vegetables and a splash of cognac, then blending, straining and finishing it with cream and butter. It has a deep coral colour and an intense, sweet shellfish flavour. It is served hot in small bowls as a starter, often garnished with pieces of lobster meat.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 2 cooked lobsters (about 600 g each)
  • 30 g butter
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot and 1 celery stalk, diced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste and 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 50 ml cognac
  • 1 litre water, 1 bay leaf, a few thyme sprigs
  • 40 g white rice
  • 150 ml single cream
  • Salt, pepper and a pinch of cayenne

Method

  1. Remove the meat from the lobsters, dice it and keep in the fridge; crush the shells.
  2. Melt the butter, fry the shells and vegetables for 8 minutes, then stir in the tomato paste.
  3. Pour in the cognac and let it bubble for 1 minute, then add the tomatoes, water, herbs and rice.
  4. Simmer for 40 minutes, remove the bay leaf, blend thoroughly and strain through a fine sieve, pressing hard.
  5. Return to the pan, stir in the cream, season with salt, pepper and cayenne and heat without boiling.
  6. Serve in warm bowls topped with the lobster meat.

How to make it lighter

  • Cream and butter carry most of the fat: halve the cream and the soup stays rich thanks to the shells.
  • The rice thickens the bisque, so no extra cream is needed for body.
  • Serve it as a small starter cup of about 200 ml rather than a large main bowl.

History

Bisques have been part of French cooking since at least the 17th century, when the word appears in French cookbooks; today the name is used mostly for smooth shellfish soups. The origin of the word is uncertain; one popular theory links it to the Bay of Biscay. Lobster bisque remains a signature starter in French restaurants and on festive menus.
