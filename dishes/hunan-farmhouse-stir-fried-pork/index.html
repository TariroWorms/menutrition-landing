Hunan farmhouse stir-fried pork (Nongjia xiaochaorou)

Origin: China · Hunan

Thin slices of pork belly are seared in their own fat until the edges curl, then stir-fried with green chilies, garlic and fermented black beans. The result is smoky, savory and sharply hot, with a little sweetness from the pork fat. It is a daily rice-companion dish across Hunan.

Recipe

3 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g pork belly, thinly sliced
  • 200 g long green chilies, sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1 tbsp fermented black beans
  • 1 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 1 tsp dark soy sauce
  • 1 tsp oil

Method

  1. Heat a dry wok with a little oil over high heat.
  2. Add pork slices and sear until the fat renders and edges curl.
  3. Push the pork aside, add garlic and black beans and fry until fragrant.
  4. Add chilies and stir-fry until blistered.
  5. Season with both soy sauces and toss for a minute before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a leaner pork cut and drain excess fat.
  • Add more chilies and less soy to lower sodium.
  • Pair with steamed rice and a vegetable dish.

History

The name means "farmhouse little stir-fry" and points to simple country cooking built on pork and fresh chilies. It is a benchmark dish that Hunan cooks are often judged by.
