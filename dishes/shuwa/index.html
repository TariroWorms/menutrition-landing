🍖 Shuwa (Omani Slow-Roasted Lamb)

Origin: Oman

Shuwa is Oman's celebratory dish of lamb rubbed with a paste of garlic, cumin, coriander, chili and vinegar, wrapped in banana leaves and slow-cooked in an underground sand pit for a day or more. The meat comes out smoky and so tender it falls from the bone. It is shared at Eid gatherings, usually with rice.

Recipe

6 servings300 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg lamb shoulder or leg, cut into large pieces
  • 8 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp ground coriander
  • 1 tbsp chili powder
  • 4 tbsp vinegar
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Banana leaves for wrapping
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Pound the garlic with cumin, coriander, chili, vinegar, oil and salt into a paste.
  2. Make deep cuts in the lamb and rub the paste in well; marinate for at least 4 hours or overnight.
  3. Wrap the meat tightly in banana leaves, then in foil.
  4. Cook in a covered dish at 150 °C for 4–5 hours (or in a traditional pit) until the meat falls apart.
  5. Unwrap and serve with the cooking juices.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before marinating.
  • Choose leaner leg meat over shoulder for a lower-fat portion.
  • Serve with a large salad and a modest scoop of rice to balance the plate.

History

Shuwa is a festive tradition in Oman, where families and villages prepare it together, especially for Eid al-Adha, often cooking the meat in a communal pit. The method of sealing spiced meat in leaves and burying it with hot coals is passed down as part of Omani holiday custom.
