Hunan smoked pork with bamboo shoots (La rou chao sun)

Origin: China · Hunan

Cured and smoked pork belly is sliced and stir-fried with bamboo shoots, green chilies and garlic. The smoky, salty meat contrasts with the crunchy, mild bamboo, and the chilies add a sharp heat. It is a winter classic eaten with rice.

Recipe

3 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g smoked cured pork belly
  • 200 g bamboo shoots, sliced
  • 2 green chilies, sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1 tbsp fermented black beans
  • 1 tsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Steam or simmer the cured pork for 15 minutes to soften, then slice thinly.
  2. Blanch the bamboo shoots for 2 minutes and drain.
  3. Fry the pork in a dry wok until the fat turns translucent.
  4. Add garlic, black beans and chilies and stir-fry.
  5. Add bamboo and soy sauce and toss over high heat for 2 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Boil the cured pork first to remove some salt.
  • Use more bamboo and less meat.
  • Skip extra soy sauce.

History

Smoking pork over the kitchen fire for winter storage is a long-standing tradition in rural Hunan. The cured meat, called la rou, is cooked with seasonal vegetables. Bamboo shoots or garlic leaves are common partners.
