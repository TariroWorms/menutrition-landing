Thiébou yapp (Senegalese meat and rice)

Origin: Senegal
700kcal

Thiébou yapp is the meat version of thiéboudienne: lamb or beef browned with onions, simmered with tomato and vegetables, then used to cook broken rice that picks up all the flavour. The rice is soft and reddish, with tender meat on top. It is popular at everyday lunches and for big family gatherings.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings110 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g lamb, cubed
  • 500 g broken rice
  • 200 g onion
  • 60 g tomato paste
  • 200 g carrots
  • 100 ml vegetable oil for frying
  • 10 g garlic
  • 5 g black pepper, salt, water as needed

Method

  1. Brown the meat in oil with onion and garlic until well coloured
  2. Stir in tomato paste, pepper, salt and water and simmer 40 minutes
  3. Add carrot and any other vegetables and cook until tender, then lift out meat and vegetables
  4. Add the rice to the stock and cook covered on low heat about 25 minutes
  5. Serve the rice topped with the meat and vegetables

How to make it lighter

  • Trim the fat from the meat before cooking
  • Use less oil and more vegetables
  • Choose a smaller rice portion with a salad on the side

History

The word yapp means meat in Wolof. It follows the same one-pot rice method as other Senegalese rice dishes, which spread through the country as rice became a staple in the 20th century.
