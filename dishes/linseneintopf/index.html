🍲 German lentil stew (Linseneintopf)

Linseneintopf is a hearty German lentil stew of brown lentils simmered with carrot, celery and potato, with slices of smoked sausage cooked in. It is thick, earthy and filling, and a splash of vinegar at the end gives it its typical tangy finish. It is served in deep bowls as a complete one-pot meal.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g brown lentils, rinsed
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery sticks, diced
  • 300 g potatoes, diced
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 200 g smoked sausage, sliced
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1.2 litres water, 1 bay leaf, salt, pepper
  • 2 tbsp vinegar

Method

  1. Soften the onion in the oil for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the lentils, carrot, celery, bay leaf and water, bring to the boil and simmer 20 minutes.
  3. Add the potatoes and sausage and simmer 20 more minutes until everything is tender.
  4. Season with salt and pepper and stir in the vinegar just before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the sausage and add more lentils and vegetables — the stew stays just as filling.
  • Choose a lower-fat smoked sausage; it is the main source of fat in the pot.
  • A 400 g bowl is a full meal on its own — no side dish needed.

History

Lentil stews have long been everyday food across Germany, and in Swabia lentils are a regional staple. Linseneintopf with sausage remains a classic of home kitchens and canteens throughout the country.
