🍝 Tagliatelle with Neapolitan Ragù

Origin: Italy · Campania

This dish pairs tagliatelle with Neapolitan ragù, a sauce in which whole pieces of beef and pork braise for hours in tomato, onion and red wine. Unlike the minced ragù of Bologna, the meat stays in large chunks and the sauce becomes dark, glossy and intensely tomatoey. The sauce dresses the pasta, and the meat is often served separately as a second course.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g dried tagliatelle
  • 400 g beef chuck, in large pieces
  • 300 g pork ribs or shoulder
  • 800 g tomato passata
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 150 ml red wine
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and basil

Method

  1. Brown the beef and pork in olive oil in a heavy pot, then set aside.
  2. Soften the onion in the same pot, return the meat and add the wine; let it reduce by half.
  3. Stir in the passata, cover partly and simmer on the lowest heat for 3 hours, stirring now and then.
  4. Lift out the meat to serve separately; season the sauce and add basil.
  5. Cook the tagliatelle until al dente and toss with the ragù.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean cuts and skim the fat from the surface of the sauce before serving.
  • Dress the pasta with sauce only and keep the meat for a separate lean portion with vegetables.
  • Weigh 80 g dry pasta per person to keep the plate within budget.

History

Ragù napoletano is a traditional Sunday dish in Naples, where it is famously left to simmer slowly for many hours. In Naples it is typically served with short or tubular pasta, while pairing it with tagliatelle is a common variation outside the city.
