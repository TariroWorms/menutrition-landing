🥜 Cashew chicken (gai pad med mamuang)

Gai pad med mamuang is a Thai stir-fry of chicken with roasted cashew nuts, onion, bell pepper and crisp dried chilies, glazed in a savoury-sweet oyster sauce. It is cooked fast in a very hot wok so the vegetables keep their crunch and the cashews stay crisp. Mild by Thai standards, it is a popular order with jasmine rice.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g chicken breast or thigh, sliced
  • 100 g roasted unsalted cashews
  • 1 onion, cut into wedges
  • 1 red and 1 green bell pepper, cut into chunks
  • 4–6 dried red chilies, cut into pieces
  • 3 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 3 tbsp oyster sauce
  • 1 tsp sugar, 2 tbsp water
  • 2 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Fry the dried chilies briefly in the hot oil until crisp, then lift them out.
  2. Add the garlic, then the chicken, and stir-fry over high heat until just cooked.
  3. Add the onion and peppers and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
  4. Stir in the oyster sauce, sugar and water and toss until glossy.
  5. Add the cashews and chilies, toss once more and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Cashews are energy-dense: use 15–20 g per portion instead of a large handful.
  • Use chicken breast and only 1 tbsp oil in a non-stick wok — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Double the peppers and onion to make a larger plate with fewer calories per bite.

History

Cashew chicken is a staple of Thai restaurants and food stalls and reflects the strong Chinese influence on Thai stir-frying. Cashew trees are grown in southern Thailand, and the dish is now one of the most familiar Thai plates abroad.
