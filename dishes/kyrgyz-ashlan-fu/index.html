Ashlan-fu (cold Karakol noodle soup)

Origin: Kyrgyzstan · Karakol

Ashlan-fu is a chilled bowl of soft starch jelly strips and thick wheat noodles in a sharp, vinegary broth, topped with a stir-fried sauce of peppers, tomato, garlic and chili and often a thin egg omelette. It is cold, sour and spicy all at once, very refreshing on a hot day. It is eaten as a quick lunch, often together with a fried potato or meat pastry.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g starch jelly, cut in strips
  • 300 g thick wheat noodles
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 2 tomatoes, 1 red pepper, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp chili oil
  • 4 tbsp vinegar
  • 1 l cold vegetable or meat broth

Method

  1. Cook the noodles, rinse in cold water and drain.
  2. Fry the eggs as a thin omelette, cool and slice into strips.
  3. Stir-fry pepper, tomato and garlic with chili oil for 5 minutes to make a sauce.
  4. Season the cold broth with vinegar and salt.
  5. Layer jelly and noodles in bowls, add sauce and egg, and pour over the cold broth.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less chili oil and more fresh peppers.
  • Add extra vegetables such as cucumber or cabbage.
  • Pick a vegetable broth for a lighter version.

History

Ashlan-fu was brought to the Issyk-Kul region by Dungans, Chinese-speaking Muslims who settled there in the late nineteenth century. Karakol is famous for it, and whole streets of small cafes serve little else.
