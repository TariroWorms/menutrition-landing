Origin: United States · the South

Cornbread is a quick bread made from cornmeal, buttermilk, eggs, butter and a little sugar, baked in a hot pan or skillet. It has a golden crust, a crumbly, slightly sweet interior and a distinct corn flavour. It is served warm in squares or wedges alongside chili, barbecue, beans or stews.

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g cornmeal
  • 300 ml buttermilk
  • 2 eggs
  • 60 g butter, melted
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 220 °C and put a greased skillet or baking tin inside to heat.
  2. Mix the cornmeal, sugar, baking powder and salt.
  3. Whisk the buttermilk, eggs and melted butter, then stir into the dry mix until just combined.
  4. Pour into the hot pan and bake for 20–25 minutes until golden and a skewer comes out clean.
  5. Cool for 5 minutes and cut into 8 pieces.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the bread into 8 even pieces so one portion is easy to count.
  • Skip the extra butter on top — 1 tbsp butter is about 100 kcal.
  • Pair a piece with a bean or vegetable chili rather than with fatty barbecue.

History

Cornbread grew out of the cornmeal breads of Native American peoples, which European settlers adopted because maize grew far more easily than wheat. It became a staple of Southern cooking, where it is traditionally less sweet than the cake-like versions popular in the North.
