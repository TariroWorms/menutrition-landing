🍚 Plain White Rice (Ruz Abyad)

Origin: Gulf · Middle East

Ruz abyad is plain white basmati rice, rinsed, boiled and steamed with a little salt and oil until every grain is separate and fluffy. It has a clean, mild flavour and a light, airy texture that soaks up sauces. Across the Gulf it is the everyday base for stews, curries and grilled meat, served on a large platter or in individual bowls.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g basmati rice
  • 600 ml water
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Rinse the rice several times until the water runs clear, then soak for 15 minutes and drain.
  2. Bring the water, salt and oil to a boil in a pot.
  3. Add the rice, return to a boil, then cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 minutes.
  4. Turn off the heat and leave covered for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork.

How to make it lighter

  • Measure the oil: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal, and the rice cooks well with less.
  • Fill half the plate with vegetables or salad and keep rice to a quarter.
  • Weigh cooked rice once to learn what a 180 g portion looks like.

History

Rice became a staple in the Gulf through centuries of trade with India and Persia, and long-grain basmati remains the preferred variety. Plain white rice is served daily in homes and restaurants across the region alongside saloona, marag and grills.
