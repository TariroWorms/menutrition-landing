Hallacas (Venezuelan Christmas tamales)

Origin: Venezuela

Hallacas are the centrepiece of Christmas in Venezuela. A thin layer of corn dough coloured with annatto is spread on a plantain leaf, filled with a rich stew of beef, pork and chicken, then topped with olives, raisins, capers and strips of pepper before being folded, tied and boiled. Unwrapped, they are soft, savoury and a little sweet. Families make dozens at once in a shared kitchen session and eat them through the holidays.

Recipe

12 servings300 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g precooked corn flour
  • 1 l chicken broth, 80 ml oil with annatto
  • 400 g beef, 300 g pork, 300 g chicken, diced
  • 2 onions, 1 red pepper, 4 garlic cloves, 2 tomatoes, 50 g panela
  • 80 g olives, 50 g raisins, 30 g capers, strips of red pepper
  • plantain leaves, kitchen string, salt

Method

  1. Cook the diced meats with onion, pepper, garlic, tomato and panela for about 1.5 hours into a thick stew (guiso); cool overnight.
  2. Knead the corn flour with warm broth, annatto oil and salt into a soft dough.
  3. Clean and wipe the plantain leaves and pass them over a flame to soften.
  4. Spread a thin round of dough on an oiled leaf, add 2–3 tablespoons of guiso and top with olives, raisins, capers and pepper.
  5. Fold the leaf into a rectangle, wrap in a second leaf and tie with string like a parcel.
  6. Boil the hallacas for 1 hour, drain and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use mostly chicken and lean pork in the stew to reduce fat.
  • Spread the dough as thin as possible – less starch per parcel.
  • Serve one hallaca with a fresh salad rather than with bread and pork.

History

Hallacas are related to the tamales of the wider Americas, with European ingredients such as olives, capers and raisins added in colonial times. Making them together in December is one of the strongest family traditions in Venezuela, and every household defends its own recipe.
