Cuchuco de trigo (cracked-wheat soup with pork ribs)

Origin: Colombia · Boyacá

Cuchuco is a rustic, porridge-like soup from the Boyacá and Cundinamarca highlands. Coarsely cracked wheat is cooked slowly with pork ribs until it thickens the broth, then potatoes, fresh peas, fava beans and cabbage are added. It is filling, earthy and gently seasoned with hogao and cumin, the kind of soup served at midday on farms.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g cracked wheat
  • 600 g pork ribs
  • 400 g potatoes
  • 150 g fresh peas
  • 150 g fresh fava beans
  • 200 g cabbage, shredded
  • 1 onion, 1 tomato, 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin

Method

  1. Soak the cracked wheat 1 hour and rinse.
  2. Simmer the ribs in 3 l water with onion and garlic for 40 minutes.
  3. Add the wheat and cook 30 minutes, stirring occasionally so it does not stick.
  4. Add the potatoes, peas, fava beans and cabbage plus a quick hogao of tomato and cumin.
  5. Simmer 25 minutes more until thick; season and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim the fat from the ribs or use lean pork loin.
  • Increase the vegetables and reduce the meat for a lighter bowl.
  • Cracked wheat is rich in fibre, so keep it as the base of the soup.

History

Wheat was brought to the Andean highlands by the Spanish and adapted into local soups alongside native potatoes. Cuchuco, also made with corn or barley, remains a symbol of peasant cooking in Boyacá.
