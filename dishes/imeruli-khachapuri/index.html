Imeretian khachapuri

Origin: Georgia · Imereti

Imeretian khachapuri is the everyday cheese bread of Georgia: a soft round of dough wrapped around a thick layer of brined cheese. It is cooked until golden, then cut into wedges like a pie. The crumb is light, the filling salty and milky. It sits on almost every Georgian table, from breakfast to feasts.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml warm milk
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 1 egg
  • 400 g Imeruli cheese, grated
  • 150 g sulguni, grated
  • 20 g butter
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the warm milk, add the egg, salt and flour and knead a soft dough.
  2. Cover and leave to rise for about an hour.
  3. Mix both cheeses; if dry, add a spoon of milk.
  4. Divide the dough into four, flatten each round and put a ball of cheese in the middle.
  5. Gather the edges over the filling, pinch shut and gently roll out flat.
  6. Bake at 220 °C for 15–18 minutes or cook in a dry pan on both sides.
  7. Brush with butter and cut into wedges while hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use part low-fat feta or cottage cheese to reduce salt and fat.
  • Skip the butter on top; the bread is moist enough without it.
  • Serve a smaller wedge with a large tomato-cucumber salad.

History

The bread takes its name from Imereti in western Georgia, where it is considered the classic form of khachapuri. Each region has its own version, and the Imeretian one is the simplest and most widespread.
