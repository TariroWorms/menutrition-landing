Terrine de campagne, or country terrine, is a coarse French pâté of minced pork and chicken liver seasoned with cognac, herbs and cracked peppercorns, packed into a mold and baked slowly in a water bath. Once chilled and pressed, it slices into dense, rustic, savory slabs. It is served cold as a starter with cornichons and mustard.

Recipe

8 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork shoulder, coarsely minced
  • 200 g pork belly, minced
  • 250 g chicken livers, cleaned and chopped
  • 40 ml cognac
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 tbsp chopped thyme and parsley
  • 1 tsp cracked black peppercorns
  • 12 g salt

Method

  1. Mix the pork, pork belly, chicken livers, cognac, garlic, herbs, peppercorns and salt; chill for 1 hour.
  2. Pack firmly into a 1 l terrine dish and cover with a lid or foil.
  3. Bake in a water bath at 160 °C for about 1 hour 30 minutes, until the center reaches 70 °C.
  4. Cool, then press with a weight and refrigerate overnight.
  5. Slice and serve cold with pickles and mustard.

How to make it lighter

  • Keep slices thin — a 50–80 g slice is a typical starter portion.
  • Serve with plenty of pickles and a green salad rather than extra slices.
  • Use leaner pork shoulder in place of part of the belly if you make it at home.

History

Terrines take their name from the earthenware dish they are cooked in, and pâté de campagne is a staple of French charcuterie shops and bistro menus. Recipes vary by region and by charcutier rather than coming from one recorded origin.
