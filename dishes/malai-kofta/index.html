🍛 Malai kofta

Malai kofta are soft dumplings of paneer and mashed potato, deep-fried until golden and served in a rich, creamy tomato gravy thickened with cashews. The gravy is mildly spiced with garam masala, so the dish tastes rich rather than hot. It is a vegetarian centrepiece of North Indian restaurant menus and is eaten with rice or flatbread.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g paneer, grated
  • 300 g potatoes, boiled and mashed
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • 4 tomatoes, puréed
  • 50 g cashews, soaked in hot water
  • 100 ml cream
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 1 tsp garam masala, 1/2 tsp chili powder, salt

Method

  1. Mix the paneer, potato and a pinch of salt and shape into 12 balls.
  2. Deep-fry the balls in hot oil until golden and drain on paper.
  3. Fry the onion, garlic and ginger in 1 tbsp oil, add the tomatoes and spices and cook for 10 minutes.
  4. Blend with the cashews until smooth, return to the pan, stir in the cream and simmer for 5 minutes.
  5. Place the kofta in the gravy just before serving so they stay soft but intact.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the kofta instead of deep-frying them.
  • Use half the cream and a little more cashew-tomato purée for the gravy.
  • Eat two or three kofta with plenty of vegetables rather than a full bowl.

History

Malai kofta is associated with North Indian and Mughlai-style cooking, where creamy, nut-thickened gravies are common. Today it is one of the best-known vegetarian dishes on Indian restaurant menus worldwide.
