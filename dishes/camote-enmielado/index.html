🍠 Camote enmielado (candied sweet potato)

Origin: Mexico

Camote enmielado is a traditional Mexican dessert of sweet potato cooked in a dark syrup of piloncillo (unrefined cane sugar), cinnamon and orange until glossy and sticky. The flesh turns soft and almost jammy, with notes of caramel, spice and citrus. It is served warm or at room temperature, on its own or as a sweet side.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg sweet potatoes, peeled and cut into thick slices
  • 200 g piloncillo, chopped (or dark brown sugar)
  • 2 cinnamon sticks
  • Zest and juice of 1 orange
  • 500 ml water

Method

  1. Put the water, piloncillo, cinnamon and orange zest in a wide pan and heat until the sugar dissolves.
  2. Add the sweet potato in one layer and pour over the orange juice.
  3. Simmer uncovered for 40–50 minutes, turning gently, until the potatoes are tender and the syrup is thick. Alternatively bake covered at 180 °C for about an hour.
  4. Let cool slightly and serve with spoonfuls of syrup.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the piloncillo — the sweet potato is already naturally sweet.
  • Serve a few slices with little syrup rather than a full bowl.
  • Leftover slices are good mashed with cinnamon as a lighter breakfast side.

History

Sweet potatoes were grown in Mexico long before the Spanish arrived, and cooking them in piloncillo syrup became a common home-style sweet. Camote enmielado is especially associated with autumn and the Day of the Dead, when it is placed on altars and shared with family. It is still a typical seasonal dessert in Mexican homes and markets.
