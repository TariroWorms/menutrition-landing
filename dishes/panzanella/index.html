Panzanella is a Tuscan summer salad that turns stale bread into the main ingredient. Chunks of day-old bread soak up the juice of ripe tomatoes, olive oil and vinegar, then are tossed with cucumber, red onion and torn basil. The result is crunchy at the edges, soft in the middle and pleasantly tangy. It is served at room temperature as a starter or a light lunch.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g stale rustic bread, cut into cubes
  • 500 g ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 cucumber, sliced
  • 1 small red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 bunch basil
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tbsp vinegar
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Soak the sliced onion in cold water for 10 minutes to soften its bite, then drain.
  2. Toss the bread cubes with the tomatoes and a pinch of salt so the bread absorbs the juices.
  3. Add the cucumber, onion and torn basil.
  4. Whisk the oil and vinegar, pour over the salad and toss well.
  5. Rest for 10–15 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 2 tbsp oil instead of 4 and let the tomato juice do the work — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Increase the tomatoes and cucumber and reduce the bread for a lighter bowl.
  • Serve it as a side to grilled chicken or beans rather than as a full meal of bread.

History

Panzanella grew out of the peasant habit in Tuscany and central Italy of never wasting bread. Early versions were mostly soaked bread with onion and herbs; tomatoes became standard later, once they were widely grown in Italy. Today it is a classic of Italian summer cooking.
