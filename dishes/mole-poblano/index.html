🍗 Mole poblano

Origin: Mexico · Puebla

Mole poblano is a rich, dark reddish-brown sauce from Puebla made from several dried chilies, spices, peanuts, sesame seeds and a touch of chocolate, served over braised chicken. It is complex rather than sweet: smoky, nutty and mildly hot. It is traditionally sprinkled with toasted sesame seeds and served with rice and warm tortillas at weddings, baptisms and holidays.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g chicken pieces
  • 3 ancho, 3 mulato and 2 pasilla dried chilies
  • 50 g peanuts, 30 g sesame seeds
  • 2 tomatoes, 1 onion, 3 garlic cloves
  • 40 g dark chocolate
  • 1/2 tsp cinnamon, 2 cloves, 1/2 tsp black pepper, 1/2 tsp anise seed
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in salted water for 35 minutes; keep the broth.
  2. Toast the chilies and soak them in hot water; toast the peanuts and most of the sesame seeds.
  3. Char the tomatoes, onion and garlic and blend everything with the spices and some broth until smooth.
  4. Fry the paste in oil for 10 minutes, add the chocolate and broth and simmer 30 minutes until thick.
  5. Add the chicken, heat through and sprinkle with the remaining sesame seeds.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken and keep the sauce to about 4 tbsp per plate.
  • Peanuts and sesame make the sauce calorie-dense; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal, so fry the paste with less oil.
  • Serve with vegetables or a small portion of rice rather than both rice and tortillas.

History

Mole poblano comes from the city of Puebla and is often called Mexico's national dish. The popular story credits nuns of the Santa Rosa convent with inventing it for a visiting official, but this is a legend; the sauce grew from older Mesoamerican chili sauces combined with spices introduced in colonial times.
