Oromo (Kyrgyz steamed meat roll)

Origin: Kyrgyzstan

Oromo is a large roll of thinly rolled dough spread with chopped meat, onion and often pumpkin, then cooked in a multi-tier steamer. Once done it is cut into thick rounds that show the spiral of filling. The taste is close to manti but the shape is quicker to make. It is usually served with sour cream or a tomato-garlic sauce.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 400 g lamb or beef, finely chopped
  • 300 g pumpkin, grated
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 50 g lamb fat or butter
  • black pepper

Method

  1. Knead a firm dough from flour, water and salt and rest it 30 minutes.
  2. Mix the meat, onion, pumpkin, fat, salt and pepper.
  3. Roll the dough into a very thin large sheet.
  4. Spread the filling evenly, leaving a border, and roll it up tightly.
  5. Coil the roll onto an oiled steamer tray and steam for 40-45 minutes.
  6. Cut into rounds and serve with sour cream or sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Use more pumpkin and less fat for a lighter filling.
  • Lean beef can replace lamb.
  • Serve with yogurt instead of sour cream to cut calories.

History

Oromo belongs to the family of steamed dough dishes shared across Central Asia, alongside manti and khanum. The name comes from a Kyrgyz word meaning 'to wrap' or 'to roll'. Pumpkin versions are especially common in autumn.
