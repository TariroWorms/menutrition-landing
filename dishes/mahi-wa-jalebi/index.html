Mahi wa jalebi (Kabul fried fish with jalebi)

Origin: Afghanistan · Kabul

Mahi wa jalebi pairs crunchy fried fish in a spiced batter with golden spirals of sweet jalebi. The fish is savoury and peppery, the jalebi sticky and syrupy, and the contrast is the whole point. It is a cold-weather snack sold from street stalls in Kabul.

What it is made of

fishfloursugarturmericcuminoil

Recipe

4 servings80 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g white fish fillets
  • 120 g flour and about 150 ml water for the fish batter
  • 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp cumin, salt
  • 300 g flour, 200 g yogurt and about 150 ml water for the jalebi batter (about 8 jalebi)
  • 200 g sugar and 200 ml water for syrup
  • 500 ml oil for frying

Method

  1. Whisk the jalebi flour, yogurt and water into a thick batter and rest it overnight.
  2. Mix the fish flour, turmeric, cumin, salt and water into a thick batter and coat the fish.
  3. Fry the fish in hot oil until golden and crisp, then drain.
  4. Pipe spirals of jalebi batter into hot oil and fry until crisp.
  5. Boil the sugar and 200 ml water into a syrup, then soak the spirals in it briefly while still warm.
  6. Serve the fish with the jalebi on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the fish instead of frying it.
  • Eat only one or two jalebi; they are very sweet.
  • Add a lemon wedge and a raw onion salad.

History

Fried river fish became a Kabul speciality, often eaten in winter from stalls and small restaurants. Jalebi arrived with the shared sweet traditions of South and Central Asia, and the combination is a local custom.
