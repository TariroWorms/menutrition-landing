Mathrooba is a thick, creamy Gulf porridge of chicken, rice and flour cooked with onion, tomato and baharat, then beaten vigorously until smooth. Its name comes from the Arabic for 'beaten'. Finished with a spoon of ghee, it is warm, savoury and comforting, and is a popular dish during Ramadan.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g chicken pieces
  • 150 g rice
  • 60 g flour
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 tsp baharat
  • 1 tbsp ghee
  • 1½ l water

Method

  1. Simmer chicken, onion, tomato, baharat and salt in the water for 40 minutes; remove the chicken and shred it.
  2. Add rice to the broth and cook until very soft, about 30 minutes.
  3. Whisk the flour with a little cold water, stir into the pot and cook 10 minutes, stirring.
  4. Return the chicken and beat the mixture with a whisk or wooden spoon until smooth and thick.
  5. Serve topped with the ghee.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tsp ghee per bowl or skip it; 1 tbsp ghee ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Use skinless chicken and skim the broth.
  • Add extra tomato and onion for volume without many calories.

History

Mathrooba is a traditional dish of Bahrain and Qatar and is eaten across the Gulf, particularly at iftar during Ramadan. Its exact origin is not documented.
