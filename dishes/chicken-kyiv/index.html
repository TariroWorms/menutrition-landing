🍗 Chicken Kyiv

Origin: Ukraine · Kyiv

Chicken Kyiv is a chicken breast pounded thin, rolled around a cold log of butter mixed with garlic and fresh herbs, then coated in egg and breadcrumbs and deep-fried. When cut open, the melted garlic butter gushes out over the plate. The outside is crisp and golden, the meat juicy. It is traditionally served with mashed or fried potatoes and green peas.

Recipe

2 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 2 chicken breasts (about 180 g each)
  • 40 g butter, softened
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 tbsp chopped dill and parsley
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 60 g breadcrumbs
  • Salt, pepper and oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Mix the butter with the garlic and herbs, shape it into two small logs and freeze for 20 minutes.
  2. Butterfly and flatten the chicken breasts, season them and wrap each tightly around a butter log, sealing the edges.
  3. Dust with flour, dip in egg and roll in breadcrumbs; repeat the egg and crumbs for a sealed coat.
  4. Deep-fry at 170 °C for 5–6 minutes until golden, then finish in a 190 °C oven for 8 minutes until cooked through.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter in the filling and add more herbs and garlic for flavour.
  • Bake the breaded rolls at 200 °C for about 25 minutes instead of deep-frying.
  • Serve with green vegetables or salad instead of fried potatoes.

History

The origin of chicken Kyiv is disputed. Similar stuffed cutlets appear in nineteenth-century Russian and French cooking, while the name became firmly attached to the dish in the Soviet era through a Kyiv restaurant connection that is often told but hard to verify. Today it is regarded as a classic of Ukrainian restaurant cooking and is popular well beyond Ukraine.
