Glorias (goat milk pecan candies)

Origin: Mexico · Northern Mexico

Glorias are small, chewy candies made from goat milk cooked down with sugar until it becomes a thick caramel, then rolled with chopped pecans. They taste deep and milky with a toasty nut crunch. Each one is twisted in red cellophane and sold as a sweet treat, a gift or a souvenir.

What it is made of

goat milksugarpecansvanillacinnamon

Recipe

20 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 1 l goat milk
  • 300 g sugar
  • 1/4 tsp baking soda
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 150 g pecans, chopped

Method

  1. Bring the milk, sugar and cinnamon to a simmer in a wide heavy pot.
  2. Stir in the baking soda dissolved in a spoon of water; it will foam.
  3. Cook on medium-low, stirring constantly, for about 50 minutes until very thick and amber.
  4. Remove the cinnamon, add vanilla and half the pecans, and let cool until it can be handled.
  5. Roll walnut-sized balls, coat in the remaining pecans and wrap each in cellophane.

How to make it lighter

  • Make the balls smaller, about 15 g, to keep one treat under 70 kcal.
  • Toast the pecans first: their flavor gets stronger, so you can use fewer.
  • Swap half the sugar for piloncillo for a rounder taste, but treat it as a dessert either way.

History

The candy comes from Linares in Nuevo León, a town known for its goat milk sweets. It grew out of the northern tradition of cajeta-style milk caramels and is now one of the best-known candies of the region.
