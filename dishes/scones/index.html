Scones are small, quick-baked breads made from flour, butter, sugar, a little egg and cream, mixed only briefly so they stay light and crumbly. They rise tall in a hot oven and split easily in half. Lightly sweet rather than cake-like, they are the centrepiece of afternoon tea, eaten warm with jam and clotted cream or simply with butter.

Recipe

8 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 350 g plain flour
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 80 g cold butter, cubed
  • 40 g sugar
  • 1 egg
  • 150 ml single cream
  • A pinch of salt

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 220 °C and line a tray with baking paper.
  2. Rub the butter into the flour, baking powder and salt until it looks like breadcrumbs, then stir in the sugar.
  3. Beat the egg with the cream, keep 1 tbsp for glazing and mix the rest in quickly to a soft dough.
  4. Pat out 3 cm thick, cut rounds with a floured cutter without twisting and place them on the tray.
  5. Brush the tops with the reserved egg mix and bake for 10–12 minutes until risen and golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut smaller scones: a 5 cm cutter gives noticeably less per piece than a large café scone.
  • Choose one topping, jam or butter, rather than piling on both.
  • Pair a scone with fresh berries instead of a second one.

History

Scones are usually traced to Scotland, where they were first made as flat rounds cooked on a griddle. Baking powder, which spread in the 19th century, turned them into the risen oven-baked scones known today across Britain, Ireland and the Commonwealth.
