Granita is a Sicilian frozen dessert made from water, sugar and fruit juice, brightened with lemon. It is frozen slowly and scraped or stirred as it sets, giving a texture of fine, soft ice crystals — smoother than shaved ice, coarser than sorbet. It is served very cold in a glass, and in Sicily it is often eaten for breakfast with a soft brioche.

What it is made of

icesugarfruit juicelemon

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml fruit juice (e.g. orange, strawberry or watermelon)
  • 250 ml water
  • 120 g sugar
  • Juice and zest of 1 lemon

Method

  1. Warm the water with the sugar until dissolved, then cool.
  2. Stir in the fruit juice, lemon juice and zest.
  3. Pour into a shallow metal tray and freeze.
  4. Every 30 minutes, scrape and stir with a fork to break up the ice crystals, for about 3–4 hours.
  5. Serve in chilled glasses.

How to make it lighter

  • Reduce the sugar — very ripe fruit juice needs less sweetening.
  • Use a naturally sweet juice such as watermelon and skip part of the added sugar.
  • Serve in a small glass; a 150 g portion is a light dessert.

History

Granita is closely tied to Sicily, where snow from Mount Etna and other mountains was historically stored and used to chill drinks and sweets. Over time the flavoured ice evolved into the granita eaten across the island today, with lemon, almond, coffee and fruit among the classic flavours.
