Pizza al tacho (Montevideo tray pizza)

Origin: Uruguay

Pizza al tacho is a style of pizza from Montevideo, baked in large rectangular metal trays and kept warm in deep containers until it is sold. Each portion is a soft, thick rectangle of dough with tomato sauce and a generous, gooey layer of mozzarella. It is cheap, filling and eaten on the go, often alongside a portion of fainá.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pizza dough
  • 200 ml tomato sauce
  • 400 g mozzarella, grated
  • 1 tsp oregano
  • 2 tbsp olive oil

Method

  1. Oil a large rectangular baking tray.
  2. Stretch the dough to fill the tray and let it rise 40 minutes.
  3. Spread the tomato sauce over the dough.
  4. Bake at 220 °C for 12 minutes.
  5. Cover thickly with mozzarella and bake 10 minutes more.
  6. Sprinkle with oregano and cut into rectangles.

How to make it lighter

  • Spread the cheese more thinly and add sliced tomato.
  • Use a mix of white and whole-wheat flour in the dough.
  • Eat one rectangle with a salad instead of two.

History

Uruguayan pizzerias share their roots with those of Buenos Aires, founded by Italian immigrants. The name refers to the metal containers, or tachos, in which the pizza was stacked and kept hot in shops and at stands. It remains a popular quick meal in Montevideo.
