Pudim de leite condensado (Brazilian flan)

Origin: Brazil

Pudim de leite condensado is Brazil's favourite dessert: a ring-shaped caramel custard that is denser and creamier than a classic crème caramel. Condensed milk, milk and eggs are blended, poured into a caramel-lined tube pan and cooked in a water bath. Turned out, it is glossy with amber caramel running down the sides, and it appears at almost every family Sunday lunch.

What it is made of

condensed milkmilkeggsugar

Recipe

10 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 395 g sweetened condensed milk
  • 400 ml whole milk
  • 3 eggs
  • 150 g sugar for the caramel
  • 50 ml water

Method

  1. Melt the sugar in a pan until amber, add the water carefully and swirl into a syrup.
  2. Pour the caramel into a ring mould and tilt to coat the sides.
  3. Blend the condensed milk, milk and eggs for 1 minute.
  4. Pour into the mould through a sieve and cover with foil.
  5. Bake in a water bath at 180 °C for about 1 hour, or until just set.
  6. Cool, chill for at least 4 hours, then turn out onto a plate.

How to make it lighter

  • Use semi-skimmed milk and light condensed milk.
  • Make less caramel and pour only a thin layer.
  • Serve small slices with fresh berries.

History

Caramel custards came to Brazil from the Iberian tradition, but the condensed milk version took over in the twentieth century as canned condensed milk became a household staple. Its smooth texture and easy recipe made it the default celebration dessert.
