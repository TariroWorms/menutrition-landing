🌺 Agua de jamaica (hibiscus water)

Agua de jamaica is a chilled Mexican drink made by steeping dried hibiscus flowers in hot water, then sweetening the deep ruby infusion with sugar and diluting it with cold water. It tastes tart and floral, a little like cranberry, and is served over ice in tall glasses — one of the classic aguas frescas sold at markets, taquerías and family tables across Mexico.

What it is made of

hibiscussugarwater

Recipe

6 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 40 g dried hibiscus flowers
  • 2 litres water
  • 100 g sugar, or to taste
  • Ice to serve

Method

  1. Bring 500 ml of the water to the boil, add the hibiscus, take off the heat and steep for 15 minutes.
  2. Strain into a jug, pressing the flowers, and stir in the sugar until it dissolves.
  3. Add the remaining 1.5 litres of cold water, taste and adjust the sweetness, and chill.
  4. Serve over plenty of ice.

How to make it lighter

  • Nearly all the calories come from sugar: 1 tsp sugar ≈ 16 kcal, so halving it makes a noticeably lighter glass.
  • Dilute it more and add plenty of ice — the tart flavour still comes through.
  • Count a big glass of 300 ml as part of the day’s energy, the same as other sweet drinks.

History

Hibiscus is not native to Mexico; it is generally thought to have arrived from West Africa via the colonial-era trade routes, and it is now grown widely in states such as Guerrero and Oaxaca. Today agua de jamaica is one of the most common aguas frescas in Mexico, poured alongside everyday meals.
