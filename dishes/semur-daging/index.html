Semur daging (Indonesian sweet soy beef stew)

Origin: Indonesia

Semur is a glossy, mildly sweet beef stew where tender chunks and potatoes sit in a dark sauce of soy, shallot and warm spices. It is gentle rather than hot, so children usually like it. It is served with plain white rice and often a little fried shallot on top.

Recipe

4 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g beef chuck, cubed
  • 3 tbsp kecap manis (sweet soy sauce)
  • 6 shallots and 4 garlic cloves, ground
  • 1/2 tsp grated nutmeg and 3 cloves
  • 2 potatoes (300 g), quartered
  • 2 tomatoes, wedged
  • 500 ml water
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Fry the ground shallot and garlic in oil until fragrant.
  2. Add the beef and brown it on all sides.
  3. Add nutmeg, cloves, kecap manis and water; bring to a boil.
  4. Cover and simmer on low heat for about 1 hour 15 minutes.
  5. Add the potatoes and tomatoes and cook 20 minutes more until the sauce thickens.
  6. Taste, adjust the salt and serve hot with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and skim the fat after cooking.
  • Replace half the beef with more potato and carrot to cut calories.
  • Choose a low-sodium soy sauce and use less kecap manis to limit sugar.

History

The name is generally linked to the Dutch word for stew, and the dish shows how Dutch colonial cooking mixed with Indonesian sweet soy and spices. It is a common home and holiday dish across Java and beyond.
