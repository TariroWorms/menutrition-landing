🐟 Classic Tiradito

Classic tiradito is the original form of the Peruvian dish: thin slices of raw fish laid flat and covered with a simple sauce of lime juice, aji amarillo, ginger and cilantro. The slices are silky, and the citrus gives a fresh, sharp heat without masking the fish. It is served chilled as a light starter in Peruvian cevicherías.

What it is made of

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g very fresh white fish such as sole or sea bass
  • 4 limes, juice
  • 2 tbsp aji amarillo paste
  • 10 g fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 small garlic clove, grated
  • Fresh cilantro leaves
  • Salt

Method

  1. Chill the fish well, then slice it thinly against the grain with a sharp knife.
  2. Arrange the slices flat on a cold plate and season lightly with salt.
  3. Mix the lime juice, aji amarillo paste, ginger and garlic into a smooth sauce.
  4. Pour the sauce over the fish just before serving.
  5. Scatter with cilantro and serve straight away.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean white fish such as sole or sea bass; it is naturally low in fat.
  • Make the sauce without any added oil; the lime and chilli carry the flavour.
  • Serve with a side of salad or cucumber instead of fried corn or chips.

History

Tiradito developed in Peru in the 20th century and is usually credited to the influence of Japanese immigrants, whose sashimi-style slicing met the Peruvian tradition of citrus-cured ceviche. Unlike ceviche, it has no onion, and the sauce is poured over the slices just before serving.
