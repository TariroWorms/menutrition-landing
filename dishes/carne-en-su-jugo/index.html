Carne en su jugo (beef in its own broth)

Origin: Mexico · Jalisco

Carne en su jugo means 'meat in its juice': small pieces of thinly cut beef are cooked quickly in a light broth seasoned with tomatillo and chile. It is served in a deep bowl with pinto beans in their liquid, crumbled crisp bacon, and plenty of chopped onion, cilantro, radish and lime on top. It is hearty yet brothy, and eaten with warm tortillas.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g beef sirloin, thinly sliced
  • 150 g bacon
  • 400 g cooked pinto beans with liquid
  • 300 g tomatillos and 1 serrano chile
  • 1 onion (150 g) and 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 bunch cilantro
  • 1 l beef broth, salt, lime, radishes

Method

  1. Fry the bacon until crisp, crumble it and keep the fat in the pan.
  2. Blend the tomatillos with the chile, a little onion, garlic and broth.
  3. Sear the beef pieces quickly in the bacon fat.
  4. Pour in the tomatillo blend and the broth and simmer 15 minutes.
  5. Serve in bowls with the warm beans, bacon, chopped onion, cilantro, radish and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the bacon and pour off its fat before searing.
  • Choose lean sirloin or round.
  • Load the bowl with radish and onion for crunch and volume.

History

The dish is a signature of Guadalajara, where several long-running restaurants have made it their speciality since the mid-20th century. It is one of the best-known dishes of Jalisco alongside birria and torta ahogada.
