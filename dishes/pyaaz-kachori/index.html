Pyaaz kachori (Rajasthani onion-stuffed fried pastry)

Origin: India · Rajasthan

Pyaaz kachori is a round, flaky pastry filled with spiced onion and fried until puffed and golden. The filling is cooked with fennel, coriander, chilli and gram flour, so it is sweet from onion, with a gentle heat. The shell is crisp and slightly blistered. It is eaten hot as a snack with tamarind or mint chutney, famously in Jodhpur.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g plain flour
  • 50 ml oil in the dough, plus oil for frying
  • 3 onions (300 g), finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp gram flour
  • 1 tsp fennel, 1 tsp coriander powder, 1 tsp chilli powder
  • salt, water

Method

  1. Rub 50 ml oil into the flour with salt and knead with water into a soft dough; rest for 20 minutes.
  2. Cook the onions with spices and gram flour in a little oil for 10 minutes, then cool.
  3. Divide the dough into 8 balls, flatten each, fill with a spoonful of onion mixture and seal.
  4. Press gently into discs.
  5. Fry slowly in medium oil until puffed and golden, 8 to 10 minutes; serve hot with chutney.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake at 200 C instead of frying for a lighter version.
  • Make the shell thinner so there is more filling.
  • Eat one, not three, with a fresh salad.

History

Kachori are a beloved street snack across North India, and the onion version is closely tied to Jodhpur in Rajasthan, where shops fry them fresh through the morning.
