🍲 Carbonnade flamande

Carbonnade flamande is a Belgian beef stew in which chunks of beef and plenty of sliced onions are braised slowly in dark beer. A slice of bread spread with mustard is laid on top and melts into the sauce, thickening it and adding a gentle tang. The result is tender meat in a glossy, slightly sweet and bitter gravy, traditionally served with fries or boiled potatoes.

Recipe

4 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg stewing beef, cut into large cubes
  • 3 onions, sliced
  • 500 ml dark Belgian-style beer
  • 2 slices bread
  • 2 tbsp mustard
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 bay leaves, a few thyme sprigs
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar, 1 tbsp vinegar, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Season the beef and brown it in batches in the oil in a heavy pot; set aside.
  2. Cook the onions in the same pot until soft and golden, about 15 minutes.
  3. Return the beef, add the beer, bay leaves, thyme, sugar and vinegar.
  4. Spread the bread with mustard and lay it on top, mustard side down.
  5. Cover and simmer gently for 2½ hours, stirring occasionally, until the beef is tender and the bread has dissolved into the sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the beef before browning.
  • Brown the meat with only 1 tbsp oil in a non-stick pot: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve with boiled potatoes or a side of vegetables instead of fries.

History

The stew is a classic of Flemish home cooking and Belgian brasseries, and the local beer used varies by region and household. Similar beer-braised beef dishes are found across Belgium and northern France.
