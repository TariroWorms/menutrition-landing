🍖 Lamb Mandi

Lamb mandi is a Yemeni rice dish in which spiced lamb is slow-roasted above the rice so the juices drip down and the whole pot takes on a gentle smokiness. The long-grain rice is scented with saffron, cardamom, cloves and cumin, and the meat comes out tender enough to pull from the bone. It is served on a large shared platter.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg lamb shoulder, in large pieces
  • 600 g basmati rice
  • 1 pinch saffron, soaked in 3 tbsp warm water
  • 5 cardamom pods
  • 4 cloves
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1.2 l water
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Rub the lamb with cumin, salt and pepper and leave for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Simmer the lamb in the water with cardamom and cloves for 1 hour, skimming the surface.
  3. Lift out the meat and roast it at 180 °C for 60–75 minutes until browned and tender.
  4. Rinse the rice and cook it in 900 ml of the strained broth until the liquid is absorbed.
  5. Drizzle the saffron water over the rice and steam it covered for 10 minutes.
  6. Spread the rice on a platter and lay the lamb on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before cooking; the rice absorbs whatever renders out.
  • Keep the rice to a fist-sized mound and let the meat be the main part of the plate.
  • Add a large tomato-and-cucumber salad to fill the plate with fewer calories.

History

Mandi is generally traced to the Hadramawt region of Yemen, where the meat was traditionally cooked in a sealed pit oven dug into the ground. The name is often linked to the Arabic word nada, meaning dew, a nod to the moist texture of the meat. Today mandi is a staple across Saudi Arabia, the Gulf states and Yemeni restaurants worldwide.
