Chicken chaud-froid is a classic cold dish of French cuisine: poached chicken coated in a pale cream sauce set with aspic, then glazed and decorated with tarragon leaves and finely cut vegetables. The sauce sets into a smooth, glossy coat around tender meat. It is served chilled as an elegant starter or buffet dish.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 4 chicken breasts (about 600 g)
  • 1 litre chicken stock
  • 1 carrot, 1 leek, 1 celery stick
  • 12 g gelatin sheets
  • 150 ml cream
  • A small bunch of tarragon
  • Salt, white pepper

Method

  1. Poach the chicken in the stock with the carrot, leek, celery and a tarragon sprig for 20 minutes; cool in the liquid.
  2. Strain the stock and reduce 400 ml of it to 250 ml.
  3. Soak the gelatin, dissolve it in the hot stock, add the cream, season and cool until syrupy.
  4. Skin the chicken, pat dry and set on a rack over a tray.
  5. Spoon over two coats of sauce, chilling between coats; decorate with tarragon leaves and thin carrot and leek shapes.
  6. Chill for 2 hours and serve cold.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half cream and half reduced stock for a lighter coat.
  • Serve the chicken with a large green salad as a main course.
  • Skip the skin entirely — it adds fat without being visible under the sauce.

History

The name means 'hot-cold', because the sauce is made hot and served cold. It belongs to the buffet tradition of 19th-century French haute cuisine. Today it appears mostly in classical cookery training and festive buffets.
