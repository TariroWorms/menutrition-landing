Beirute (Brazilian toasted pita sandwich)

Origin: Brazil · São Paulo

The Beirute is a big sandwich made with round Arabic flatbread instead of a roll. It is filled with roast beef, cheese, tomato, lettuce and often a fried egg, then toasted on a press so the bread turns crisp. It is cut into wedges and shared, sprinkled with za'atar or oregano.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 1 large pita (about 25 cm)
  • 150 g roast beef, thinly sliced
  • 100 g mozzarella
  • 1 tomato, sliced
  • Lettuce leaves
  • 1 egg, fried
  • Za'atar or oregano

Method

  1. Split the pita horizontally into two rounds.
  2. Layer cheese and roast beef on the bottom round.
  3. Toast in a sandwich press or pan until the cheese melts.
  4. Add tomato, lettuce, the fried egg and a pinch of za'atar.
  5. Cover with the top round, toast briefly and cut into wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a wholemeal pita.
  • Skip the egg or use only the white.
  • Add cucumber and extra salad.

History

It was created in São Paulo by Lebanese immigrants and their descendants, who brought pita bread to the city's snack bars; the name honours Beirut. It is now a fixture of lanchonete menus across the state.
