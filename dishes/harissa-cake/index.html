🍰 Harissa (semolina cake)

Origin: Levant · Egypt

This harissa is not the chili paste but a dense, golden semolina cake soaked in sugar syrup, known as basbousa in Egypt and namoura in Lebanon. The batter of semolina, yogurt, coconut and ghee bakes into a moist, slightly grainy cake, cut into diamonds, each topped with an almond. It is a favourite tray sweet in Ramadan and at family gatherings.

Recipe

16 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g coarse semolina
  • 100 g sugar
  • 100 g ghee, melted
  • 250 g plain yogurt
  • 40 g desiccated coconut
  • 16 blanched almonds
  • For the syrup: 250 g sugar, 180 ml water and a squeeze of lemon

Method

  1. Make the syrup: boil the sugar and water for 8 minutes, add the lemon and let it cool.
  2. Mix the semolina, sugar, coconut and melted ghee, then stir in the yogurt to a thick batter. Rest 20 minutes.
  3. Spread in a greased 25 cm tray, smooth the top, cut into diamonds and press an almond into each.
  4. Bake at 190 °C for 30–35 minutes until deep golden.
  5. Pour the cool syrup over the hot cake and let it soak for at least 1 hour before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut small diamonds — this cake is very energy-dense, so one small piece goes a long way.
  • Use less syrup, or a lighter syrup, and the cake stays moist.
  • Enjoy it with unsweetened tea or coffee rather than a sweet drink.

History

Syrup-soaked semolina cakes are eaten across the Levant, Egypt and the wider Arab world under several names. In Syria and Palestine the cake is commonly called harissa, and it is a fixture of bakeries and home kitchens during Ramadan.
