🥓 Grilled pork belly (samgyeopsal)

Origin: South Korea

This is samgyeopsal as served in Korean barbecue restaurants: strips of pork belly cooked on a tabletop grill and cut with scissors as they crisp. Each bite goes into a lettuce wrap with garlic, kimchi and ssamjang, balancing the fatty, smoky meat with crunch and spice. It is a social meal, ordered by the portion and grilled together at the table.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork belly, sliced 5–8 mm thick
  • 1 head of lettuce, leaves separated
  • 8 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 4 tbsp ssamjang (soybean and chilli paste)
  • 200 g kimchi
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Heat a grill pan or tabletop grill until very hot.
  2. Grill the pork slices without oil until golden and crisp on both sides, then cut into bite-size pieces with scissors.
  3. Grill the garlic and kimchi in the rendered fat alongside the meat.
  4. Wrap a piece of pork in a lettuce leaf with a little ssamjang, garlic and kimchi and eat in one bite.

How to make it lighter

  • Grill until the fat renders out and drain it away instead of cooking the kimchi in it.
  • Eat more lettuce wraps per slice of meat — a 150 g portion of pork goes a long way when wrapped.
  • Go easy on the ssamjang: 1 tsp per wrap is plenty of flavour.

History

Samgyeopsal, literally “three-layer flesh” after the stripes of fat and meat in pork belly, became a mass favourite in South Korea in the late 20th century. Grilling it at the table is now a fixture of Korean barbecue restaurants and after-work gatherings.
