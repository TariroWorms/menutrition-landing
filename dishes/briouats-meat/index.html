🥟 Meat briouats

Meat briouats are crisp Moroccan pastry triangles filled with minced beef cooked with onion, fresh cilantro and warm spices such as cumin, paprika and cinnamon. The thin warqa wrapping fries up light and flaky around a juicy, aromatic filling. They are served as a starter, at Ramadan iftar and at family gatherings, often with a wedge of lemon.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g minced beef
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 1 small bunch cilantro, chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp sweet paprika, ½ tsp cinnamon, salt and pepper
  • 1 tbsp oil for the filling
  • 12 sheets warqa (or filo) pastry
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Soften the onion in the oil, add the beef and spices and cook, breaking it up, for 10 minutes until dry. Stir in the cilantro and cool.
  2. Cut the pastry into strips about 8 cm wide, put a spoonful of filling at one end and fold into triangles; seal with a little water.
  3. Fry at 170 °C for 2–3 minutes per batch until golden, then drain on paper towels.
  4. Serve hot with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef (5% fat) and drain off any fat after browning.
  • Bake instead of frying: brush lightly with oil — 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal for the whole tray — and bake at 200 °C for 15 minutes.
  • Serve two or three briouats with a large salad rather than as part of a big mezze spread.

History

Briouats are part of the Moroccan family of filled warqa pastries, made at home and sold in bakeries and street stalls across the country. Savoury versions with spiced meat, chicken or seafood are a regular feature of iftar tables during Ramadan.
