Kuk samsa (Uzbek spring greens pastries)

Origin: Uzbekistan

Kuk samsa ('green samsa') are thin dough pastries stuffed with a generous amount of chopped spring greens such as spinach, green onion, dill, cilantro, sorrel and wild herbs. They are fried or baked until crisp, so the shell crackles while the filling stays moist and fresh-tasting. They are eaten warm with tea or yogurt.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 200 ml water
  • 600 g mixed greens (spinach, green onion, dill, cilantro)
  • 1 onion
  • 3 tbsp oil for filling
  • oil for frying
  • salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Knead flour, water and salt into a soft dough; rest 20 minutes.
  2. Chop the greens and onion, salt lightly and mix with oil and pepper.
  3. Roll the dough thin and cut into squares or circles.
  4. Fill with greens and seal into triangles or half-moons.
  5. Fry in hot oil until golden, or bake at 220 °C for 20 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake instead of deep-frying to cut the fat by half.
  • Add more greens and less onion to the filling.
  • Drain on paper towels and serve with yogurt.

History

Kuk samsa are a seasonal spring food in Uzbekistan, made when the first greens appear around Navruz. Many families gather wild herbs specially to make them.
