Escabeche de pescado (Peruvian fish in vinegar-onion sauce)

Origin: Peru

Escabeche de pescado is fried fish covered with a generous layer of thick-cut red onion softened in vinegar, ají amarillo, ají panca, garlic and cumin. The sauce is tangy and aromatic, and the dish is garnished with boiled egg, black olives, fresh cheese and slices of sweet potato. It is often eaten at room temperature, especially during Lent.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 4 fillets of firm white fish, 150 g each
  • Flour for dusting
  • 3 red onions, in thick strips
  • 150 ml red wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp ají amarillo paste
  • 1 tbsp ají panca paste
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1/2 tsp cumin
  • 2 boiled eggs
  • 1 boiled sweet potato, sliced
  • Black olives
  • 3 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Season the fish, dust lightly with flour and pan-fry until golden; set aside.
  2. In the same pan cook garlic, ají amarillo, ají panca and cumin.
  3. Add the onion and cook just until it softens slightly.
  4. Pour in the vinegar with a splash of water and simmer for 3 minutes.
  5. Spoon the onion sauce over the fish and let it rest for at least 15 minutes.
  6. Garnish with egg, olives and sweet potato and serve warm or at room temperature.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or grill the fish instead of frying.
  • Skip the flour coating for a gluten-free plate.
  • Serve with a green salad instead of extra starch.

History

Escabeche is a Spanish technique of preserving fish or meat in vinegar that came to Peru in colonial times. Peruvian cooks added local chillies and garnishes, making it a classic of Lima’s criollo cooking.
