Alegrías (amaranth and honey bars)

Origin: Mexico

Alegrías, “joys”, are crisp bars or discs made from popped amaranth seeds held together with honey or piloncillo syrup. They are airy and crunchy, with a toasty, slightly nutty taste, and are often topped with peanuts, pumpkin seeds or raisins. They are sold by street vendors and in markets as a snack.

Recipe

12 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g popped amaranth
  • 120 g honey
  • 80 g piloncillo or brown sugar
  • 40 g peanuts
  • 40 g raisins
  • 1 tsp lime juice

Method

  1. Heat honey and piloncillo with the lime juice, stirring, until it reaches a thick syrup (about 5 minutes of bubbling).
  2. Pour over the popped amaranth and mix quickly.
  3. Fold in peanuts and raisins.
  4. Press firmly into a lined tray or ring moulds.
  5. Cool completely, then cut into bars.

How to make it lighter

  • Amaranth adds protein and fibre, but the syrup adds sugar, so keep bars small.
  • Use more nuts and seeds and less syrup.
  • Choose an alegría as a snack instead of candy bars.

History

Amaranth was an important crop in pre-Hispanic Mexico, and the modern sweet developed after the colonial period. Santiago Tulyehualco in Mexico City’s Xochimilco area is well known for its amaranth and holds an annual alegría fair.
