🍗 Nashville Hot Chicken

Origin: USA · Nashville, Tennessee

Nashville hot chicken is deep-fried chicken brushed with a fiery paste of cayenne, brown sugar and hot lard. The crust is crunchy, glossy and red, with a sweet heat that builds with every bite. It is traditionally served on a slice of white bread with dill pickle chips to cool the palate and soak up the spicy fat.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g chicken pieces (thighs and drumsticks)
  • 150 g wheat flour
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • 60 g lard
  • 2–3 tbsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 4 slices white bread
  • Dill pickle chips to serve

Method

  1. Season the chicken with salt and rest for 20 minutes; dip in a little pickle brine or water, then dredge well in seasoned flour.
  2. Deep-fry at 165 °C for 12–15 minutes until golden and cooked through (75 °C inside). Drain on a rack.
  3. Melt the lard and whisk in cayenne, brown sugar, garlic powder and a pinch of salt.
  4. Brush the hot paste generously over the fried chicken.
  5. Serve on white bread topped with pickle chips.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose breast pieces and remove the skin before dredging to cut a large share of the fat.
  • Brush on only a thin layer of the spicy lard — 1 tbsp of fat ≈ 115–120 kcal.
  • Skip the bread underneath or eat half a slice, and add extra pickles for crunch.

History

The dish is closely tied to Nashville, where it has been sold for decades and is usually credited to the Prince family's chicken shack. The story goes that a spurned girlfriend over-spiced a man's chicken as revenge, and he liked it so much he started selling it. Today it is served across the United States, often with a choice of heat levels.
