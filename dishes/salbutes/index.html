Salbutes (Yucatecan puffed tortillas)

Origin: Mexico · Yucatán

Salbutes are close cousins of panuchos but without the bean filling: small masa tortillas are dropped into hot oil, where they puff up and stay soft and slightly chewy rather than crisp. They are topped with shredded turkey or chicken, lettuce, sliced tomato, avocado and pickled red onion. A few drops of fiery habanero salsa finish them off.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g fresh corn masa
  • 2 tbsp flour (optional, for extra puff)
  • 300 g cooked turkey or chicken, shredded
  • shredded lettuce, 1 tomato, 1 avocado
  • 1 red onion pickled in lime
  • oil for frying
  • habanero salsa

Method

  1. Knead the masa with the flour, salt and a little water until smooth.
  2. Press small balls into tortillas about 10 cm wide.
  3. Fry each in 2 cm of hot oil, pressing gently so it puffs, about 1 minute per side.
  4. Drain well on paper.
  5. Top with turkey, lettuce, tomato, avocado and pickled onion.
  6. Serve immediately with habanero salsa.

How to make it lighter

  • Drain on a rack and paper to remove as much oil as possible.
  • Load up on lettuce and tomato and go easy on avocado.
  • Use skinless poultry breast for the topping.

History

Like panuchos, salbutes are a staple of evening antojito stands in Mérida and across the Yucatán Peninsula. The name is often explained as coming from Maya, though the exact origin is debated.
