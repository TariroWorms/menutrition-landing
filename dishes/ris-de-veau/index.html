🍖 Veal Sweetbreads

Ris de veau are veal sweetbreads — the thymus gland of a calf — prized in French cooking for their delicate flavour and creamy, tender texture. After soaking and blanching, they are pan-fried in butter until golden and crisp outside, then finished with lemon, capers and parsley. They are served as an elegant starter or main course.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings200 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g veal sweetbreads
  • 40 g butter
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 2 tbsp capers
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Soak the sweetbreads in cold water for a few hours, changing the water twice.
  2. Blanch them in simmering salted water for 3 minutes, cool, then peel off the membrane.
  3. Press under a weight in the fridge for 1 hour, then slice into thick pieces.
  4. Season and fry in the butter for 3–4 minutes per side until golden.
  5. Add the lemon juice, capers and parsley to the pan, baste and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter and a non-stick pan; the sweetbreads still turn golden.
  • 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal, so measure what goes in the pan.
  • Serve with a large green salad or steamed vegetables.

History

Sweetbreads have long been a delicacy in French haute cuisine and appear in classic cookbooks as a luxurious offal dish. Today they are found mainly on bistro and fine-dining menus in France and elsewhere in Europe.
