Ker sangri (Rajasthani desert beans and berries)

Origin: India · Rajasthan

Ker sangri is a dry-style Rajasthani dish of two wild desert ingredients: ker, small tart berries, and sangri, long thin dried beans from the khejri tree. They are soaked, boiled and cooked with spices, sometimes with yogurt. The flavour is earthy, tangy and spicy, with a chewy texture. It is eaten with bajra roti or plain rice.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g dried sangri beans
  • 50 g dried ker berries
  • 100 g plain yogurt
  • 3 tbsp oil (45 ml)
  • 1 tsp each cumin seeds, coriander powder and chilli powder
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric, 1/2 tsp amchur, 2 dried chillies, salt

Method

  1. Soak the sangri and ker overnight in water, then boil them until tender and drain.
  2. Heat the oil, add cumin and dried chillies, and fry briefly.
  3. Add the sangri and ker with all the spices and sauté for 5 minutes.
  4. Stir in the yogurt and a splash of water, and cook covered on low heat for 15 minutes.
  5. Cook uncovered until dry; serve with bajra roti.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 2 tablespoons of oil.
  • Serve with a large cucumber and onion salad.
  • Choose a smaller portion of roti if watching carbohydrates.

History

Ker and sangri grow in the dry Thar desert and have been preserved by drying for centuries, which made them an important food in times of scarcity. The khejri is a revered tree in Rajasthan.
