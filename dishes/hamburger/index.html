A hamburger is a grilled ground-beef patty served in a soft split bun with crisp lettuce, sliced tomato, onion and ketchup. A good one is juicy and well-seared on the outside, seasoned simply with salt and pepper, and eaten in the hand. It is the best-known fast food in the world, but it is just as easy to make at home on a grill or in a pan.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g ground beef (about 15–20% fat)
  • 4 burger buns
  • 4 lettuce leaves
  • 1 large tomato, sliced
  • 1 small onion, sliced into rings
  • 4 tbsp ketchup
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Divide the beef into 4 portions and shape loosely into patties slightly wider than the buns; press a dimple in the centre.
  2. Season both sides with salt and pepper just before cooking.
  3. Grill or pan-fry over high heat for 3–4 minutes per side.
  4. Toast the cut sides of the buns briefly.
  5. Build the burgers: bun, lettuce, patty, tomato, onion, ketchup, top bun.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean beef (5–10% fat) for a noticeably lighter patty.
  • Swap the top bun for extra lettuce, or eat the burger open-faced.
  • Skip the fries and add a side salad — a medium portion of fries can double the meal's calories.

History

Several American towns and cooks claim to have invented the hamburger in the late 19th or early 20th century, and the true story is disputed. The name points to the German city of Hamburg, whose minced-beef steaks travelled to the United States with immigrants. Burger chains spread the dish worldwide in the second half of the 20th century.
