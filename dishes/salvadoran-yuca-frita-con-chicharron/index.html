Yuca frita con chicharrón (fried cassava with pork crackling)

Origin: El Salvador

Yuca frita con chicharrón pairs crisp fried cassava sticks with crunchy pieces of fried pork. It is topped with curtido and a squeeze of lime, which add freshness to the rich fried base. It is a popular street snack and weekend meal.

What it is made of

cassavaporkcabbagelimeoil

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g cassava, peeled
  • 300 g pork belly, cubed
  • oil for frying
  • 250 g curtido
  • 1 lime
  • salt
  • tomato salsa to serve

Method

  1. Boil the cassava in salted water until just tender, about 20 minutes, then cut into sticks.
  2. Simmer the pork cubes in a little water with salt until the water evaporates, then fry in their own fat until crisp.
  3. Fry the cassava in hot oil until golden.
  4. Drain both on paper.
  5. Pile on a plate, top with curtido and tomato salsa.
  6. Finish with lime juice.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the cassava to reduce oil.
  • Use a leaner pork cut and drain well.
  • Double the curtido for more fibre.

History

Cassava has been a staple root in Central America since long before European contact. Combining it with fried pork and curtido is a classic Salvadoran street-food pairing.
