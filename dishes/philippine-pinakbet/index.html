Pinakbet (Ilocano mixed vegetable stew)

Origin: Philippines · Ilocos

Pinakbet is a vegetable dish from northern Luzon in which squash, eggplant, okra, long beans and bitter melon are cooked together in a little water. Fermented shrimp or fish paste gives it a salty, deeply savory base. Vegetables stay slightly firm, with a mild bitterness balanced by the sweet squash. It is served with rice, often alongside grilled fish or crispy pork.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g kabocha squash, cubed
  • 150 g eggplant, sliced
  • 100 g okra and 100 g long beans
  • 1 small bitter melon, sliced
  • 2 tbsp shrimp paste
  • 2 tomatoes, garlic, onion

Method

  1. Sauté garlic, onion and tomato until soft.
  2. Stir in the shrimp paste for 1 minute.
  3. Add squash and 100 ml water and cook 5 minutes.
  4. Add the remaining vegetables and cover.
  5. Cook 8 to 10 minutes until just tender; do not stir too much.

How to make it lighter

  • Use only a little shrimp paste to limit sodium.
  • Keep vegetables slightly firm to retain nutrients.
  • Skip the fried pork topping for a lighter meal.

History

Pinakbet comes from the Ilocos region and its name derives from the Ilocano word pinakebbet, meaning shrunken. Ilocano settlers carried the recipe to other parts of the Philippines. It reflects a cuisine built on garden vegetables and fermented seasonings.
