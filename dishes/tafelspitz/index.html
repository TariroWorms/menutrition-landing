Tafelspitz is Viennese boiled beef: a lean cut simmered slowly with root vegetables until tender, then sliced and served with its clear broth. The classic accompaniments are grated horseradish mixed with apple (Apfelkren), a creamy chive sauce (Schnittlauchsauce) and boiled or roasted potatoes. The result is mild, delicate and herby, with sharp heat from the horseradish.

Recipe

4 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg beef (tafelspitz or rump cap)
  • 500 g root vegetables: carrot, celery root, parsley root, leek
  • 1 onion, halved and browned in a dry pan
  • Peppercorns, bay leaf, salt
  • 600 g potatoes
  • 2 tbsp freshly grated horseradish and 1 apple, grated
  • 150 ml chive sauce

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of water to the boil, add the beef and skim off the foam.
  2. Add the browned onion, peppercorns and bay leaf and simmer very gently for about 2.5 hours.
  3. Add the root vegetables and salt for the last 45 minutes.
  4. Boil the potatoes separately in salted water.
  5. Mix the grated apple with the horseradish and a squeeze of lemon.
  6. Slice the beef across the grain and serve in a little broth with vegetables, potatoes, apple horseradish and chive sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve the broth as a starter: it fills you up for very few calories.
  • Use the chive sauce sparingly and enjoy more apple horseradish, which is much lighter.
  • Swap some potatoes for extra root vegetables from the pot.

History

Tafelspitz is the best-known dish of Vienna's tradition of boiled beef, which grew up in the 19th century. It is often said to have been a favourite of Emperor Franz Joseph. Today it is a fixture of Viennese restaurants and Sunday family meals.
