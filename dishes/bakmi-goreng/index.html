🍜 Bakmi goreng (Indonesian fried noodles)

Bakmi goreng is Indonesian stir-fried wheat-and-egg noodles tossed with chicken, vegetables such as cabbage and choy sum, and scrambled egg. Sweet soy sauce, kecap manis, coats everything in a glossy, caramel-savoury glaze, balanced by garlic, shallot and chili. It is a favourite from street stalls and home kitchens, served with crisp shallots and pickles.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g fresh wheat-and-egg noodles
  • 200 g chicken breast, thinly sliced
  • 2 eggs
  • 150 g cabbage and choy sum, sliced
  • 1 carrot, cut into strips
  • 3 garlic cloves and 2 shallots, sliced
  • 3 tbsp kecap manis (sweet soy sauce)
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, chili to taste

Method

  1. Blanch the noodles for 1 minute, drain and rinse.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok and fry the garlic and shallots until fragrant.
  3. Add the chicken and stir-fry until cooked; push aside and scramble the eggs.
  4. Add the vegetables and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
  5. Add the noodles and kecap manis and toss over high heat until glossy; season with chili.

How to make it lighter

  • Use more vegetables and fewer noodles to make a bigger, lighter plate.
  • Kecap manis is sweet — a couple of tablespoons is enough for two servings.
  • Stir-fry with one tablespoon of oil (about 120 kcal) instead of a generous pour.

History

Bakmi came to Indonesia with Chinese immigrants; the name comes from Hokkien and is usually explained as "meat noodles". Fried with local kecap manis, it became one of the country's most common everyday dishes.
