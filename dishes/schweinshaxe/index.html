🍖 Schweinshaxe (roast pork knuckle)

Origin: Germany · Bavaria

Schweinshaxe is a whole pork knuckle slow-roasted until the meat is soft enough to pull from the bone and the skin turns into a puffed, crackling crust. It is seasoned simply with caraway, garlic and salt and basted with dark beer, which gives the gravy a malty depth. In Bavaria it is served with potato dumplings or sauerkraut and a spoonful of the pan juices.

Recipe

2 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 2 pork knuckles (about 1.2 kg each), skin on
  • 330 ml dark beer
  • 1 tbsp caraway seeds
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 onions, quartered
  • 2 carrots, chopped
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • 500 ml water

Method

  1. Score the skin in a diamond pattern and rub the knuckles with salt, caraway and garlic.
  2. Put the onions and carrots in a roasting tin, set the knuckles on top and pour in the water.
  3. Roast at 160 °C for about 2 hours, turning once and basting with beer every 30 minutes.
  4. Raise the oven to 220 °C and roast skin-side up for 20–30 minutes until the crackling puffs.
  5. Strain and reduce the pan juices into a thin gravy and serve with the knuckle.

How to make it lighter

  • Most of the fat sits in the skin and under it: eating the lean meat and leaving most of the crackling saves a lot of calories.
  • A whole knuckle easily feeds two; share one and fill up with sauerkraut or a green salad.
  • Skim the fat off the pan juices before reducing them into gravy.

History

Roast pork knuckle is a classic of Bavarian beer halls and inns, where it has long been a hearty dish for markets, fairs and Sunday lunch. Today it is closely associated with Munich and with Oktoberfest menus.
