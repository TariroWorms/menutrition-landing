🍜 Laksa

Origin: Malaysia & Singapore · Peranakan

Laksa is a spicy noodle soup of rice noodles in a rich coconut curry broth, topped with shrimp, cubes of tofu and crunchy bean sprouts. The broth is built on a fragrant curry paste with chili, lemongrass and galangal, softened by coconut milk. It is served piping hot in a deep bowl, often with lime and fresh herbs.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g dried rice noodles
  • 300 g raw shrimp, peeled
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 4 tbsp laksa or red curry paste
  • 200 g firm tofu, cubed
  • 150 g bean sprouts
  • 700 ml water
  • 1 tbsp oil, 1 tsp sugar, salt
  • Lime wedges, fresh coriander and chili to serve

Method

  1. Soak or boil the rice noodles according to the packet, drain and divide between bowls.
  2. Fry the curry paste in the oil for 2 minutes until fragrant.
  3. Add the water, coconut milk, sugar and salt and simmer for 10 minutes.
  4. Add the tofu and shrimp and simmer for 3 minutes until the shrimp turn pink.
  5. Blanch the bean sprouts briefly and add to the bowls.
  6. Ladle the hot broth, shrimp and tofu over the noodles and serve with lime, coriander and chili.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk or half coconut milk and half water for a thinner broth.
  • Fill the bowl with extra bean sprouts and shrimp and use fewer noodles.
  • Leave some of the broth in the bowl — coconut fat is where most calories sit.

History

Laksa is usually credited to Peranakan cooking, the mixed Chinese and Malay culture of the Straits ports such as Penang, Malacca and Singapore. Many regional versions exist, from creamy curry laksa to sour tamarind-based styles. Today it is one of the best-known hawker dishes of Malaysia and Singapore.
