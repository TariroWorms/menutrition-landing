Riñones a la parrilla (grilled kidneys)

Origin: Argentina

Riñones are beef kidneys, a classic though less common member of the achuras at an Argentine asado. They are grilled whole or butterflied over fairly strong heat so they brown outside and stay tender inside. Their flavour is distinct and slightly mineral, which is why they are usually dressed with lemon, garlic and parsley. Fans eat them hot as a starter, straight off the grill.

What it is made of

beef kidneyslemongarlicparsleycoarse salt

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 2 beef kidneys (about 700 g)
  • 2 lemons
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • Coarse salt

Method

  1. Remove the outer membrane and the white core of fat and tubes.
  2. Soak the kidneys in cold water with lemon juice for 1 hour, changing the water once.
  3. Pat dry, butterfly or cut into thick pieces and salt.
  4. Grill over medium-high embers 6–8 minutes per side until just cooked.
  5. Dress with lemon, garlic and parsley and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Kidneys are lean and high in protein, iron and B12.
  • They are high in purines and cholesterol, so people with gout should avoid them.
  • Do not overcook; kidneys turn rubbery when grilled too long.

History

Like other offal, kidneys belong to the old rural custom of grilling every part of the animal. Today they are mostly found at traditional parrillas and home asados.
