🥩 Bavette à l'échalote (flank steak with shallots)

Bavette à l'échalote is a French bistro classic: flank steak seared hard in a hot pan and served rare to medium-rare under a glossy sauce of softened shallots, red wine, butter and thyme. The cut is coarse-grained, beefy and pleasantly chewy, and the sweet-sharp shallot sauce cuts through its richness. It usually comes with fries or a green salad.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 2 flank steaks (bavette), about 180 g each
  • 4 shallots, finely sliced
  • 150 ml red wine
  • 30 g butter
  • 3 sprigs thyme
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper

Method

  1. Take the steaks out of the fridge 20 minutes ahead and season them with salt.
  2. Heat the oil in a heavy pan until very hot and sear the steaks for 2–3 minutes per side for rare to medium-rare. Rest them on a warm plate.
  3. Lower the heat, add half the butter and the shallots with the thyme and cook for 3–4 minutes until soft.
  4. Pour in the wine, scrape the pan and boil until reduced by about two thirds.
  5. Take the pan off the heat and swirl in the remaining butter to make a glossy sauce. Season with pepper.
  6. Slice the steaks across the grain, spoon the shallot sauce over and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 10 g butter in the sauce instead of 30 g — every 10 g of butter is about 75 kcal.
  • Serve it with a big green salad or green beans instead of fries.
  • Flank is a fairly lean cut: trim any outer fat and keep the portion to about 150 g of meat.

History

Bavette is the French butcher's name for cuts from the flank and lower belly of beef, prized for flavour rather than tenderness. Paired with a quick shallot pan sauce, it has long been a standard of Parisian bistros and brasseries, and it remains one of the most common steaks on French menus.
