Tklapi (fruit leather)

Origin: Georgia

Tklapi is a thin, flexible sheet of dried fruit purée, most often made from sour plums. The texture is leathery and chewy, and the taste ranges from sweet to sharply tart. It is eaten as a snack, and sour tklapi is also dissolved into soups such as kharcho.

What it is made of

plumsugar

Recipe

8 servings1440 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg ripe plums
  • 2 tbsp sugar (optional)
  • A little oil for the tray

Method

  1. Halve and pit the plums.
  2. Simmer with a splash of water for 10 minutes until soft.
  3. Purée and press through a sieve; stir in sugar if using.
  4. Spread 3 mm thick on oiled trays or baking paper.
  5. Dry in the sun for 1–2 days or in an oven at 60 °C for 6–8 hours, then roll up.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the sugar if the fruit is ripe and sweet.
  • Mix plums with apples to make a milder version for children.
  • Use sour tklapi to add flavour to soups instead of extra salt.

History

Drying fruit purée in the sun is an old way of preserving the summer harvest in Georgia. Rolls of tklapi in many colours are sold at markets, and the sour plum version is valued as a souring agent in cooking.
