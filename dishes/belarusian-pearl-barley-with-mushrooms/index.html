Pearl barley porridge with mushrooms (perlavaya kasha)

Origin: Belarus

Perlavaya kasha is pearl barley cooked slowly until plump and chewy, then mixed with mushrooms fried with onion and carrot. It has a pleasantly springy bite and a woodsy, savoury taste, especially when made with dried or fresh forest mushrooms. It can be a main dish on fasting days or a side for meat; a sweeter version is cooked with milk.

What it is made of

pearl barleymushroomsonioncarrotbutter

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g pearl barley
  • 750 ml water or stock
  • 300 g mushrooms
  • 1 onion
  • 1 carrot
  • 30 g butter, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Soak the barley in cold water for 3-4 hours or overnight, then drain.
  2. Cover with water or stock, add salt and simmer covered for 40-50 minutes until tender.
  3. Meanwhile fry the chopped onion and grated carrot in butter for 5 minutes.
  4. Add sliced mushrooms and fry until their liquid evaporates, then season.
  5. Stir the mushroom mixture into the barley and warm through for 5 minutes.
  6. Rest covered for 10 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a little oil instead of butter for a vegan dish.
  • Barley is high in beta-glucan fibre and keeps you full for long.
  • Add extra vegetables such as peppers or courgette to lower the calorie density.

History

Barley is one of the oldest grains of the region, and Belarusian forests are famous for mushrooms, so the combination is a natural one. Barley porridge was long an everyday village food and later a fixture of canteens and army kitchens.
