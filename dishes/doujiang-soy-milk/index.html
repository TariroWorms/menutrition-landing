Doujiang (Chinese soy milk)

Origin: China · nationwide

Doujiang is made by soaking, grinding and boiling soybeans, then straining the liquid. It is served hot in the morning, either lightly sweetened or in a savory version with soy sauce, pickles and dried shrimp. It has a creamy body and a gentle beany aroma.

What it is made of

soybeanssugarwater

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g dried soybeans
  • 1.2 liters water
  • 2 tbsp sugar (optional)
  • 1 pinch of salt
  • Soy sauce, pickles and chives for the savory version
  • Optional sesame seeds

Method

  1. Soak the soybeans overnight and drain.
  2. Blend with part of the water until very smooth.
  3. Strain through a fine cloth, pressing out all the liquid.
  4. Boil the soy milk for 10 minutes, skimming the foam and stirring often.
  5. Sweeten or season and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Leave out the sugar for an unsweetened version.
  • Serve with whole-grain bread instead of fried dough.
  • Use the leftover pulp (okara) in other dishes.

History

Soy milk has been a breakfast staple in China for centuries and is traditionally made fresh each day. It is usually eaten with youtiao or steamed buns.
