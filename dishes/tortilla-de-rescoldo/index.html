🫓 Tortilla de rescoldo

Origin: Chile · Central and Southern Chile

Tortilla de rescoldo is a rustic Chilean flat bread baked directly in the embers of a wood fire, which gives it a smoky aroma and a slightly charred crust. Inside it is soft and a little dense, perfect for tearing and eating warm. In the countryside it is served with butter, cheese, pebre or as an accompaniment to stews.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 2 tbsp lard, butter or oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • About 250 ml warm water
  • Optional: a few chicharrones (crisp pork bits) or a spoon of toasted flour

Method

  1. Mix the flour and salt, rub in the fat and gradually add the warm water to make a soft dough.
  2. Knead for 5–8 minutes until smooth, then rest for 20 minutes under a cloth.
  3. Divide into 6 balls and flatten each into a disc about 1.5 cm thick.
  4. Rake the embers into a layer and bury the discs in them, or place them in a 230 °C oven or on a hot cast-iron pan.
  5. Cook for 10–15 minutes, turning once, until golden with a few dark spots and hollow-sounding.
  6. Brush off the ash and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a modest amount of fat in the dough; the bread bakes up well without extra butter.
  • Serve with fresh pebre or a salad instead of heaping butter on it, and use part wholemeal flour for more fibre.
  • Share a disc between two: it is dense, at about 285 kcal per 100 g.

History

Baking in the embers is one of the oldest ways to cook bread, and in rural Chile the tortilla de rescoldo is a traditional country food. The name comes from rescoldo, the glowing embers left after a fire burns down. Today many families still make it for a rustic meal, and it is often sold at country markets and roadside stalls in central and southern Chile.
