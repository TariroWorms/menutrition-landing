Matsoni (Georgian fermented milk)

Origin: Georgia

Matsoni is a set fermented milk, thicker than kefir and with a clean, sour taste similar to yogurt. It is eaten by the spoon for breakfast, often drizzled with honey, and is the base of cold soups and sauces. Many Georgians also drink it thinned with water.

Recipe

4 servings480 min

Ingredients

  • 1 l whole milk
  • 2 tbsp matsoni or natural yogurt as starter
  • Honey to serve (optional)

Method

  1. Bring the milk almost to a boil, then cool to about 42 °C.
  2. Stir the starter into a little warm milk, then mix it back into the pot.
  3. Pour into jars or clay pots and cover.
  4. Wrap in a towel and keep warm for 6–8 hours until set.
  5. Refrigerate for at least 2 hours before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use semi-skimmed milk for a lighter version.
  • Top with fresh berries instead of honey.
  • Mix with water, salt and dill for a refreshing drink after meals.

History

Matsoni has been made in Georgian and Armenian households for a very long time, with each family keeping its own starter. Clay pots of matsoni are still sold by home producers at markets.
