🥕 Gajar ka halwa

Origin: India · Punjab

Gajar ka halwa is a North Indian carrot pudding made by slowly simmering grated carrots in milk until it reduces and thickens. It is finished with sugar, ghee and cardamom, then scattered with chopped almonds. The texture is soft and slightly grainy, rich and fragrant, and it is served warm in small bowls, especially in winter.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g carrots, finely grated
  • 750 ml milk
  • 80 g sugar
  • 2 tbsp ghee
  • 4 cardamom pods, seeds crushed
  • 20 g almonds, chopped

Method

  1. Simmer the grated carrots in the milk in a wide heavy pan, stirring often, for 40–50 minutes until the milk has almost evaporated.
  2. Add the ghee and stir-fry the carrots for 5 minutes until glossy.
  3. Stir in the sugar and cook until the mixture thickens again, about 10 minutes.
  4. Add the cardamom, scatter with almonds and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • A small bowl of 100–120 g is a typical dessert portion; serve in small cups to keep it there.
  • Cut the ghee to 1 tbsp — 1 tbsp ghee is about 120 kcal — and rely on slow cooking for richness.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk and reduce the sugar by a third; the carrots are naturally sweet.

History

The dessert is closely associated with Punjab and North India, where sweet red winter carrots are in season. Today gajar ka halwa is a standard sweet at weddings, Diwali and winter family gatherings.
