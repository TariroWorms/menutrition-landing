Tipakay (Peruvian sweet-and-sour chicken)

Origin: Peru · Chifa

Tipakay is the chifa version of sweet-and-sour chicken: battered chicken pieces fried until crunchy and then coated in a glossy red sauce of sugar, vinegar and tomato with chunks of pineapple and bell pepper. The balance of sweet and tangy makes it a hit with children. It is eaten with white rice or arroz chaufa.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g chicken breast, in chunks
  • 1 egg
  • 80 g cornstarch
  • Oil for frying
  • 200 g pineapple chunks
  • 1 red and 1 green bell pepper
  • 4 tbsp sugar
  • 4 tbsp white vinegar
  • 3 tbsp ketchup or tomato sauce
  • 150 ml water
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce

Method

  1. Coat the chicken in beaten egg and then cornstarch.
  2. Fry at 175 °C until golden and crisp; drain.
  3. Stir-fry the peppers for one minute in a little oil.
  4. Add sugar, vinegar, tomato sauce, soy and water and bring to a simmer.
  5. Thicken with a cornstarch slurry and add the pineapple.
  6. Toss the chicken in the sauce and serve immediately with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the coated chicken instead of frying.
  • Halve the sugar and rely on pineapple for sweetness.
  • Serve a larger share of peppers and a smaller portion of rice.

History

The dish belongs to the Cantonese-influenced chifa repertoire that spread through Lima in the twentieth century. Its name is a local adaptation of Chinese words, and it remains a staple of chifa menus.
