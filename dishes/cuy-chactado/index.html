Cuy chactado (crisp-fried guinea pig)

Origin: Peru · Andes

Cuy chactado is a whole guinea pig that is seasoned with garlic, cumin and salt, pressed flat under a heavy stone (chactado) and fried in plenty of oil. A light coating of corn flour helps the skin turn very crisp. The meat is lean and tastes somewhat like dark poultry or rabbit. It is served in pieces with boiled potatoes, corn and a spicy salsa.

What it is made of

Guinea pigcorn flourgarliccuminoilpotato

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 2 guinea pigs, cleaned (about 1.2 kg)
  • 6 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 60 g corn flour
  • oil for frying
  • 800 g boiled potatoes
  • salt, black pepper, salad and salsa to serve

Method

  1. Open the guinea pigs flat and rub with garlic, cumin, salt and pepper.
  2. Marinate for at least 2 hours.
  3. Dust both sides with corn flour.
  4. Fry in hot oil, pressing down with a heavy flat stone or lid, for about 8 minutes per side.
  5. Drain well, cut into quarters and serve with potatoes, corn and salsa.

How to make it lighter

  • Roast instead of frying for a leaner version.
  • Drain thoroughly on paper to remove excess oil.
  • Serve with plenty of salad and a modest portion of potatoes.

History

Guinea pigs have been raised for food in the Andes for thousands of years and are still kept in many highland homes. Cuy chactado is especially linked to Arequipa, while other regions prefer it roasted or in stews.
