🦐 Gambas al ajillo

Gambas al ajillo are Spanish garlic prawns: peeled shrimp cooked quickly in plenty of olive oil with sliced garlic and dried chili, finished with chopped parsley. They arrive at the table still sizzling in a small clay dish, the oil fragrant with garlic. As a tapa, the shrimp are eaten with a fork and the garlicky oil is the highlight.

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g peeled raw shrimp
  • 6 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 80 ml olive oil
  • 1–2 dried chilies, broken
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • Salt

Method

  1. Pat the shrimp dry and season with salt.
  2. Warm the olive oil gently with the garlic and chili until the garlic turns pale gold.
  3. Raise the heat, add the shrimp and cook 1–2 minutes per side until pink.
  4. Sprinkle with parsley and serve sizzling.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the oil; 1 tbsp olive oil is about 120 kcal and most of it stays in the dish.
  • Serve with a big tomato or green salad to make it a light meal.
  • Leave the extra oil in the pan rather than mopping it up.

History

The dish is a staple of tapas bars across Spain, and is especially associated with Madrid and Andalusia. Cooking “al ajillo” — with garlic and oil — is a traditional Spanish method also used for mushrooms, chicken and rabbit.
