🍝 Koshari with dakka and shatta

This is the full koshari plate as served in Cairo shops: rice, lentils, macaroni and chickpeas under a spiced tomato sauce and crispy fried onions, with dakka, the sharp garlic-and-vinegar dressing, and shatta, a fiery chili oil, on the side. Diners spoon on as much dakka and shatta as they like, so the plate can be mild or very hot.

Recipe

4 servings80 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g rice
  • 200 g brown lentils
  • 200 g small macaroni
  • 200 g cooked chickpeas
  • 400 g tomato passata
  • 3 onions, thinly sliced, and oil for frying
  • Dakka: 60 ml vinegar, 3 crushed garlic cloves, 1 tsp cumin, 60 ml water, salt
  • Shatta: 3 tbsp oil, 1 tbsp chili flakes, 1 crushed garlic clove
  • Salt, cumin, chili flakes

Method

  1. Fry the onions in oil until crisp and brown; drain.
  2. Cook the lentils 20 minutes, add the rice, salt and water to cover by 1 cm, and cook covered 15 minutes.
  3. Boil the macaroni and drain; warm the chickpeas.
  4. Simmer the passata with salt, cumin and chili for 15 minutes.
  5. Mix the dakka; warm the oil with chili and garlic for the shatta.
  6. Layer rice and lentils, macaroni, chickpeas, sauce and onions, and serve the dakka and shatta on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Use dakka freely, but take the shatta by the teaspoon: it is mostly oil.
  • Ask for extra chickpeas and lentils and less macaroni.
  • Keep the fried onions to a small sprinkle.

History

Koshari became a Cairo street staple in the 20th century, and dakka and shatta are the standard condiments set beside it in koshari shops. The dish is usually traced to the rice-and-lentil khichdi of India, with Italian pasta and tomato added in Egypt.
