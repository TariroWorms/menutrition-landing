Mshosh (lentils with dried apricots and walnuts)

Origin: Armenia

Mshosh is a thick, spoonable dish of brown lentils mixed with chopped dried apricots, raisins, toasted walnuts and sweet fried onion. It balances earthy, sweet and nutty flavours and is usually eaten at room temperature with lavash. It is especially popular during Lent, when Armenians avoid animal products.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g brown lentils
  • 100 g dried apricots
  • 60 g raisins
  • 70 g walnuts
  • 2 onions (240 g)
  • 3 tbsp olive or sunflower oil
  • salt and black pepper
  • 800 ml water

Method

  1. Rinse the lentils and simmer them in the water for 25–30 minutes until soft and most of the water is absorbed.
  2. Soak the dried apricots and raisins in warm water for 10 minutes, then chop the apricots.
  3. Fry the sliced onions slowly in the oil until deep golden.
  4. Toast and roughly chop the walnuts.
  5. Mix the lentils with the fruit, onion and most of the walnuts, mashing lightly.
  6. Season, top with the remaining walnuts and serve warm or at room temperature.

How to make it lighter

  • Lentils and walnuts make this a protein-rich plant meal on its own.
  • Cut the oil to one tablespoon and fry the onion with a splash of water.
  • Keep portions moderate: the dried fruit adds natural sugar.

History

Mshosh is a traditional Lenten recipe, often linked to the Armenian communities of historic Western Armenia and Cilicia. Combining legumes with dried fruit and nuts is typical of the old Armenian fasting kitchen.
