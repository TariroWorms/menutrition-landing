Vegetable samboosa are fried pastry triangles filled with a quick stir-fry of shredded cabbage, carrot, onion and sweetcorn seasoned with mild spices. The filling stays light and slightly crunchy inside a crisp, flaky shell. They are a popular meat-free option on the Ramadan iftar spread in the Gulf, served hot as a snack or starter.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g white cabbage, finely shredded
  • 2 carrots, grated
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 100 g sweetcorn kernels
  • 1 tsp mixed spices (cumin, black pepper, paprika)
  • Salt to taste
  • 24 samboosa pastry strips
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Stir-fry the onion in 1 tbsp oil, add the cabbage and carrot and cook on high heat for 4–5 minutes.
  2. Add the corn, spices and salt, cook for 1 more minute and let cool completely.
  3. Fill the pastry strips and fold into triangles, sealing with water.
  4. Fry until golden, drain on paper towels and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake instead of deep-frying: brush with 1 tbsp oil and bake at 200 °C until golden — far less fat is absorbed.
  • Count them: two or three pieces with a large salad make a lighter iftar than a whole plate.
  • Drain fried pieces on paper towels and fry at the right temperature (about 175 °C) so the pastry soaks up less oil.

History

Filled fried pastries of this kind spread across the Middle East, Central Asia and India through medieval trade routes, and the name goes back to the Persian sanbosag. In the Gulf and Saudi Arabia, samboosa is closely tied to Ramadan, when it is made in large batches and served at iftar.
