Murabyan

Origin: Kuwait · Gulf

Murabyan is a Kuwaiti rice dish of shrimp and turmeric-tinted rice, layered with caramelized onions, garlic and plenty of fresh dill. Dried lime gives it a gentle sour, smoky note. The rice stays fluffy and fragrant, and the dish is served on a platter as a family main course.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g shrimp, peeled
  • 350 g basmati rice, soaked 20 minutes
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 dried limes, pierced
  • 20 g fresh dill, chopped
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Fry the onions in the oil until golden; remove half for topping.
  2. Add the garlic, turmeric and shrimp and cook for 2–3 minutes; remove the shrimp.
  3. Add 600 ml water, the dried limes and salt and bring to a boil.
  4. Add the drained rice and half the dill, cover and cook on low heat for 15 minutes.
  5. Layer the shrimp on top, cover and steam 5 more minutes; finish with the remaining onions and dill.

How to make it lighter

  • Shrimp is lean — increase it and reduce the rice for a higher-protein plate.
  • Use 1–2 tbsp oil and soften the onions with a splash of water (1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal).
  • Serve with a cucumber-tomato salad and keep the rice to about 250 g.

History

Shrimp has long been part of Kuwait's seafaring food culture, and murabyan remains one of the country's best-known home dishes.
