Carne asada is thin beef steak marinated in lime juice, garlic, onion and cilantro, then grilled over high heat. The result is smoky, juicy and tangy, with charred edges. It is cut into strips and served with tortillas, salsa, grilled onions and beans, or used as a filling for tacos.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g thin beef steak (skirt, flank or sirloin)
  • Juice of 4 limes
  • 5 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • A handful of cilantro, chopped
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt and black pepper
  • To serve: warm tortillas, salsa, lime wedges, grilled spring onions

Method

  1. Mix the lime juice, garlic, onion, cilantro, cumin, oil, salt and pepper.
  2. Add the steak, turn to coat and marinate in the fridge for 1–2 hours.
  3. Heat a grill or cast-iron pan until very hot.
  4. Grill the steak for 2–4 minutes per side, depending on thickness.
  5. Rest for 5 minutes, then slice thinly across the grain.
  6. Serve with tortillas, salsa and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean cuts such as flank or sirloin and trim visible fat; at about 200 kcal per 100 g this is a high-protein dish.
  • Serve with grilled vegetables, beans and salsa rather than fries or a lot of tortillas.
  • Marinate with citrus rather than oil to keep the flavour and cut the fat.

History

The name simply means “grilled meat”, and the dish is closely tied to the cattle-ranching culture of northern Mexico. There, cooking beef over a fire is a social occasion, with families and friends gathering around the grill at weekends and celebrations. Today carne asada is eaten across the country and in Mexican communities abroad.
