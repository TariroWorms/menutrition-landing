🥟 Pierogi with Potato and Cheese

Origin: Poland · Central and Eastern Europe

Pierogi are half-moon dumplings of soft flour dough filled with mashed potato, cheese and fried onion. They are boiled until they float, then often pan-fried in butter until golden at the edges. Served with more caramelized onion and a spoonful of sour cream, they are soft, savory and comforting.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • about 220 ml warm water, pinch of salt
  • 500 g potatoes, boiled and mashed
  • 200 g farmer's cheese (twaróg)
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 40 g butter
  • Sour cream to serve

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt and warm water into a smooth, elastic dough; rest 30 minutes covered.
  2. Fry the onions in half the butter until golden; mix half into the mashed potato and cheese, season.
  3. Roll the dough thin, cut 8 cm circles, fill with a spoon of filling and pinch closed.
  4. Boil in salted water for 2–3 minutes after they float.
  5. Pan-fry briefly in the remaining butter and serve with the rest of the onion and sour cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve them boiled only, without pan-frying in butter.
  • Top with a small spoon of sour cream rather than a dollop.
  • Have 6–8 pierogi with a large salad instead of a full plate.

History

Filled dumplings like pierogi have been eaten across Poland and neighboring countries for centuries and are a centerpiece of Polish cooking, including the Christmas Eve table. Today they are also popular in North America, especially in communities with Polish and Ukrainian roots.
