🐟 Pla nueng manao (steamed fish with lime)

Origin: Thailand

Pla nueng manao is a whole sea bass steamed and then bathed in a sharp, spicy broth of lime juice, fish sauce, garlic and fresh chilies, finished with cilantro. The fish stays soft and juicy, while the sauce is sour, salty and hot all at once. It is usually served in a fish-shaped pan over a burner to keep it hot, shared with steamed rice.

What it is made of

Recipe

2 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 1 whole sea bass (about 700 g), cleaned
  • 4 tbsp lime juice
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 6 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 4–6 bird’s eye chilies, chopped
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 100 ml water
  • A handful of cilantro

Method

  1. Score the fish on both sides and place it on a heatproof plate.
  2. Steam over boiling water for 12–15 minutes until the flesh flakes.
  3. Meanwhile mix lime juice, fish sauce, garlic, chilies, sugar and water.
  4. Pour the sauce over the hot fish, scatter with cilantro and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • The dish is naturally lean; keep it that way by serving it with plain steamed rice, not fried rice.
  • Go easy on the sauce if you watch salt — fish sauce is very salty.
  • Add steamed greens on the side for a fuller plate.

History

Steamed fish with lime is a fixture of Thai seafood restaurants and home cooking, especially in central Thailand; no specific origin is recorded.
