Chicken machboos is a Gulf one-pot dish of basmati rice cooked in a spiced broth with chicken, tomato, onion and dried lime. The baharat spice mix gives warmth, while the loomi adds a sour, slightly smoky tang. The rice comes out fluffy and orange-gold, and it is served on a large platter with the chicken on top, often with a spicy tomato sauce called daqoos.

Recipe

4 servings80 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces
  • 400 g basmati rice, soaked 30 minutes
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 dried limes (loomi), pierced
  • 2 tsp baharat and ½ tsp turmeric
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt
  • Chopped coriander to serve

Method

  1. Fry the onions in the oil until golden, then add the garlic and the chicken and brown on all sides.
  2. Add the tomatoes, loomi, baharat, turmeric, salt and enough water to cover; simmer covered for 40 minutes until tender.
  3. Lift out the chicken and measure about 750 ml of the broth back into the pot.
  4. Add the drained rice, bring to the boil, then cover and cook on low heat for 20 minutes.
  5. Brown the chicken briefly in a hot oven or pan, then serve it on top of the rice with coriander.

How to make it lighter

  • Keep the rice to about one cup cooked per plate and add extra salad or daqoos.
  • Remove the chicken skin and measure the oil: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Choose a larger share of chicken and a smaller share of rice for a more filling plate.

History

Machboos is one of the best-known rice dishes of the Gulf and is closely related to Saudi kabsa. It is flavoured with spices and dried lime (loomi), and it is widely regarded as a national dish in Bahrain, Kuwait and Qatar, where it is central to family lunches and celebrations.
