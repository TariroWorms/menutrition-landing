🐟 Pescado a la veracruzana

Origin: Mexico · Veracruz

Pescado a la veracruzana is a Mexican dish of white fish fillets simmered in a sauce of tomato, onion, green olives and capers cooked in olive oil. The sauce is briny, tangy and lightly sweet, and the fish stays tender and flaky. Red snapper is the classic choice, and the dish is served hot, often with white rice or warm tortillas on the side.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 4 white fish fillets (e.g. red snapper), about 600 g
  • 600 g ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 60 g green olives, sliced
  • 2 tbsp capers
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 bay leaf, oregano, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil and soften the onion and garlic for 5 minutes.
  2. Add the tomatoes, bay leaf and oregano and simmer for 10 minutes.
  3. Stir in the olives and capers and season lightly — they are already salty.
  4. Season the fish, lay it in the sauce, cover and simmer for 8–10 minutes until it flakes.
  5. Serve the fillets with plenty of sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 1 tbsp olive oil instead of 2 — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Olives add fat; a small handful is enough for flavour.
  • Pair with a vegetable side or a small portion of rice to keep the plate light.

History

The dish comes from the port of Veracruz on the Gulf of Mexico. Its olives, capers and olive oil are usually linked to Spanish influence, combined with local tomatoes and Gulf fish. It remains a signature dish of Veracruz and is cooked throughout Mexico, and it is a popular choice during Lent.
