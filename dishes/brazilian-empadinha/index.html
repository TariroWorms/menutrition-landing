Empadinha (Brazilian mini pie)

Origin: Brazil

Empadinha is a bite-sized pie made in small fluted tins, with a short, melt-in-the-mouth crust and a moist filling. The most common is chicken cooked with tomato, onion and olives, but shrimp and hearts of palm are also classic. The top is often brushed with egg yolk for a golden finish. It is a beloved salgado at parties, bakeries and snack bars.

Recipe

20 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 200 g cold butter
  • 1 egg + 1 yolk for brushing
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 400 g chicken breast, cooked and shredded
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 50 g green olives, sliced

Method

  1. Rub butter into flour and salt, add the egg and press into a dough; chill 30 minutes.
  2. Cook onion and tomato, add the chicken and olives and simmer until thick; cool.
  3. Line small tins with thin dough, pressing it up the sides.
  4. Fill with the chicken mixture and cover with a thin dough lid.
  5. Brush with yolk and bake at 190 °C for 25-30 minutes until golden.
  6. Cool slightly before unmoulding.

How to make it lighter

  • Make the crust thinner and the filling generous.
  • Replace part of the butter with yogurt in the dough for fewer calories.
  • Count them: two or three make a satisfying snack.

History

Empadinhas descend from Portuguese empadas, small covered pies brought to Brazil in colonial times. In Brazil they became smaller and more delicate and are now an essential part of the party salgadinho tray.
