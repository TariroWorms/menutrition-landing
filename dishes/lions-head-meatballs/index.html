Shanghai braised lion's head meatballs (hong shao shi zi tou)

Origin: China · Jiangsu · Shanghai
480kcal

These are fist-sized pork meatballs, hand-chopped for a tender, almost fluffy texture, then braised slowly with napa cabbage. The cabbage looks like a lion's mane around the meatball. The broth is savory and slightly sweet and the dish is eaten with rice.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork with some fat, hand-chopped or coarsely minced
  • 1 head napa cabbage, about 500 g
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine
  • 20 g ginger
  • 3 scallions
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch
  • 400 ml stock

Method

  1. Mix pork with minced ginger, scallion, wine, half the soy sauce and cornstarch until springy.
  2. Shape into four large balls with wet hands.
  3. Brown the balls lightly in a pan.
  4. Line a pot with half the cabbage, add the balls, stock and remaining soy sauce.
  5. Cover with remaining cabbage and braise gently for 90 minutes.
  6. Serve in the broth with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use leaner pork and skim the fat from the broth.
  • Add more cabbage for volume and fiber.
  • Skip browning to reduce oil.

History

The dish belongs to the Huaiyang cuisine of Jiangsu and is also a favorite in Shanghai home cooking. The name comes from the meatball resembling a lion's head, with the cabbage as its mane.
