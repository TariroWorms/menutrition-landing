Fried Beef Kibbeh

Origin: Levant · Middle East

Fried kibbeh made with beef follows the classic shape: a hollow oval shell of fine bulgur kneaded with lean minced beef and onion, filled with minced beef browned with onion, spices and pine nuts, then deep-fried. The shell turns crunchy and deep brown while the inside stays savory and juicy. It is a staple of mezze tables, restaurants and festive trays.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g fine bulgur
  • 500 g lean minced beef
  • 2 onions
  • 40 g pine nuts
  • 2 tsp seven-spice (baharat)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Soak bulgur 15 minutes and squeeze dry.
  2. Blend bulgur with 300 g beef, 1 onion, salt and half the spices into a smooth paste; chill.
  3. Brown the remaining beef with the chopped second onion, spices and toasted pine nuts.
  4. Shape walnut-sized pieces into hollow ovals, fill and seal.
  5. Deep-fry at 175 °C for 4–5 minutes until deep brown and drain on paper.

How to make it lighter

  • Air-fry or bake at 200 °C with a light brush of oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Choose 5% fat minced beef for both shell and filling.
  • Stop at 3 pieces and add a salad with lemon dressing.

History

Beef is widely used for kibbeh across the Levant and in Arab diaspora kitchens, where it is often easier to find than lamb; the dish itself is a pillar of Lebanese and Syrian cooking.
