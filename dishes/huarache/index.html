A huarache is a long, oval flatbread of corn masa, shaped like a sandal, cooked on a griddle and topped. Typical toppings are refried beans, grilled beef, salsa, crumbled cheese and chopped onion. It is a filling street food eaten with a knife and fork or by hand.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g masa harina (corn dough flour)
  • 350 ml warm water and salt
  • 200 g cooked pinto beans, mashed
  • 400 g thin beef steak (such as flank)
  • 1 onion, chopped, and 1 garlic clove
  • 100 g queso fresco, crumbled
  • Green or red salsa, oil for the griddle

Method

  1. Mix the masa harina with water and salt into a soft dough; rest 15 minutes.
  2. Season and sear the beef on a hot griddle for 3–4 minutes per side, then slice thinly.
  3. Shape the dough into 4 oval pieces, press flat to about 5 mm thick and fill each with a spoon of beans, folding the dough around it, then press flat again.
  4. Cook the huaraches on a lightly oiled griddle for 3–4 minutes per side until set and speckled.
  5. Spread with beans, top with beef, salsa, cheese and onion and serve at once.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook the huarache on a dry griddle without extra oil: the masa base is naturally low in fat.
  • Use lean beef and a modest sprinkle of cheese; the beef and beans give a good share of protein.
  • Add plenty of onion, cilantro and salsa for volume, and skip cream.

History

The huarache is a classic of Mexico City street food, named after the sandal it resembles. It belongs to the family of masa antojitos, along with sopes and gorditas, built on corn dough that goes back to pre-Hispanic cooking. Today it is sold at market stalls and street stands across the capital.
