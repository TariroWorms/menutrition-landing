Charquicán is a thick, comforting Chilean stew of mashed potato and pumpkin cooked with beef and onion. It is usually served with a fried egg on top. A homely everyday dish, it is eaten at lunch in homes and small restaurants.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g beef, cut into small cubes
  • 800 g potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 500 g pumpkin, peeled and cubed
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp ground cumin and 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 cup sweetcorn or green beans
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt and pepper
  • 4 eggs

Method

  1. Fry the onion and garlic in the oil until soft, add the beef with the cumin, paprika, salt and pepper and brown it.
  2. Add 500 ml of water and simmer for 25 minutes until the beef is nearly tender.
  3. Add the potato, pumpkin and corn or green beans, cover and cook 20 minutes more.
  4. Mash the vegetables partly into the stew until thick and creamy, with some small pieces left.
  5. Fry the eggs and serve one on each portion.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook the eggs in a non-stick pan with just a drop of oil, or poach them, to save fat.
  • Use lean beef and add more pumpkin or green beans: the stew stays filling with fewer calories.
  • Fry the onion in one tablespoon of oil rather than two; the stew is thick enough that it does not need more.

History

Charquicán is a traditional dish of central Chile, and its name comes from the Quechua language. It was originally made with dried, salted meat, and today fresh beef is more common. It is an everyday home-cooking dish that makes use of simple vegetables like potato and pumpkin. Cooks often add sweetcorn or green beans to it.
