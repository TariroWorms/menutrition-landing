Achu with yellow soup (Cameroonian pounded cocoyam)

Origin: Cameroon · Northwest

Achu is a smooth, soft dough of pounded cocoyam, shaped in a mound with a hollow filled by golden yellow soup. The soup is made from palm oil emulsified with limestone water and flavored with spices, meat and fish. It is eaten by hand, scooping the achu through the soup.

What it is made of

cocoyam (taro)red palm oillimestone waterfishbeefchili pepper

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g cocoyam
  • 200 ml palm oil
  • 1 tbsp limestone water (kanwa)
  • 300 g beef
  • 200 g fish
  • Achu spices, salt, 1 l water

Method

  1. Boil the cocoyam until soft.
  2. Pound it until perfectly smooth and shape it into mounds with a hollow in the centre.
  3. Boil the beef with the spices, salt and water until tender to make a stock.
  4. Whip the palm oil with the limestone water to a yellow emulsion.
  5. Pour the emulsion into the stock, add the fish and simmer 10 minutes, then pour into the hollows.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less palm oil.
  • Add vegetables.
  • Keep the achu portion small.

History

Achu is a signature dish of the Grassfields of the Northwest Region, and a feast food at weddings and funerals. The limestone water (kanwa) gives the soup its color and texture.
