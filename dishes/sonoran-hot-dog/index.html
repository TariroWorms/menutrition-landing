Sonoran hot dog (dogo sonorense)

Origin: Mexico · Sonora

The Sonoran hot dog, or dogo, starts with a frankfurter wrapped in bacon and cooked on a griddle until crisp. It goes into a soft, slightly sweet split-top bun and is loaded with pinto beans, chopped tomato and onion, mayonnaise, mustard and green salsa, often with a roasted chile on the side. It is a late-night street food eaten at carts across Hermosillo.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 4 frankfurters
  • 4 thin bacon slices
  • 4 soft hot dog buns
  • 150 g cooked pinto beans
  • 1 tomato (120 g)
  • 1/2 onion (60 g)
  • mayonnaise, mustard, jalapeño salsa

Method

  1. Wrap each frankfurter tightly in a slice of bacon.
  2. Cook on a griddle over medium heat, turning, until the bacon is crisp.
  3. Warm the buns on the griddle without toasting them hard.
  4. Put a sausage in each bun and add warm beans.
  5. Top with tomato, onion, a thin line of mayonnaise and mustard, and salsa.

How to make it lighter

  • Use chicken or turkey frankfurters and lean bacon.
  • Use light mayonnaise or skip it in favour of salsa.
  • Serve with a cucumber and lime salad rather than a second dog.

History

Bacon-wrapped hot dogs became a street food in Hermosillo, Sonora, in the late 20th century. From there they crossed the border and became famous in Tucson, Arizona.
