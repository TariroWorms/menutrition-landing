Buss up shut (Trinidadian paratha roti)

Origin: Trinidad and Tobago

Buss up shut is a flaky, buttery paratha roti that is clapped between two hands while still hot so it breaks into soft ragged shreds, like a torn-up shirt, which is where the name comes from. It is made from a layered dough rubbed with butter or ghee and is used to scoop up curried chicken, goat, channa and potato or vegetables.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 300 ml water
  • 100 g butter or ghee
  • Oil for the griddle

Method

  1. Mix flour, baking powder, salt, sugar and water into a soft dough and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Divide into 6 balls, roll thin, spread with butter and roll into a coil.
  3. Rest the coils 20 minutes.
  4. Roll out each coil into a thin round.
  5. Cook on a hot griddle, brushing with butter, until golden and flaky.
  6. Clap between your hands to tear into shreds and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less butter and brush lightly.
  • Pair with a vegetable and chickpea filling.
  • Choose whole-wheat flour.

History

The roti came to Trinidad with the indentured laborers from India in the 19th century and became a national staple. It is typically sold at roti shops across the island.
