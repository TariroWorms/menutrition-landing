Olluquito con charqui (ulluco and dried meat stew)

Origin: Peru

Olluquito con charqui is a simple, homely stew made with olluco, a small, colourful Andean tuber with a crisp, slightly slippery texture. It is cut into fine matchsticks and cooked with shredded charqui (dried meat), onion, garlic and ají panca until tender. It is usually served with white rice and is a weekday classic in Lima and the highlands.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg olluco, cut into matchsticks
  • 200 g charqui (dried beef) or 400 g fresh beef
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ají panca paste
  • 1/2 tsp cumin
  • 200 ml stock
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Parsley to finish

Method

  1. Soak the charqui in water for several hours, boil until soft and shred finely.
  2. Cut the olluco into thin strips without washing them after cutting.
  3. Fry onion, garlic, ají panca and cumin in oil.
  4. Add the charqui and stir for a few minutes.
  5. Add the olluco and stock, cover and cook gently for about 20 minutes without stirring too much.
  6. Sprinkle with parsley and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use fresh lean beef to reduce salt.
  • Serve with less rice since olluco is already starchy.
  • Add a side salad for freshness.

History

Both ulluco and charqui are ancient Andean foods: the tuber was domesticated in the Andes and charqui is a traditional way of preserving meat by drying it. Today many cooks replace charqui with fresh beef or chicken.
