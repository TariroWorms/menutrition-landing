🌮 Tacos al Pastor

Tacos al pastor are filled with thin slices of chili-marinated pork that is roasted on a vertical spit and shaved off to order. Each small tortilla is topped with the juicy, smoky-red pork, a sliver of roasted pineapple, chopped onion and cilantro. The meat is tangy and lightly spicy, and the pineapple adds a sweet contrast; lime and salsa are served on the side.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork shoulder, thinly sliced
  • 3 dried guajillo chilies, soaked
  • 2 tbsp achiote paste or paprika
  • 3 tbsp pineapple juice
  • 2 tbsp vinegar
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 200 g fresh pineapple, sliced
  • 12 small tortillas
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 bunch cilantro, chopped

Method

  1. Blend chilies, achiote, pineapple juice, vinegar, garlic and salt into a marinade.
  2. Coat the pork and marinate for at least 2 hours.
  3. Grill or pan-sear the pork in batches until charred, then chop finely; grill the pineapple slices.
  4. Warm the tortillas in a dry pan.
  5. Fill with pork, pineapple, onion and cilantro and serve with lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork loin and trim visible fat.
  • Count your tacos — two or three make a satisfying portion.
  • Add plenty of onion, cilantro and fresh salsa, which add volume for few calories.

History

Tacos al pastor are usually credited to Lebanese immigrants in Mexico, whose spit-roasted shawarma was adapted in the 20th century using pork and Mexican chili marinades. Today they are one of the signature street foods of Mexico City.
