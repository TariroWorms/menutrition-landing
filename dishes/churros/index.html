🥖 Churros

Origin: Spain

Churros are ridged sticks of fried choux-style dough made from flour, water, butter and egg, piped through a star nozzle and deep-fried until golden. They are crisp on the outside and soft and slightly doughy inside, rolled in cinnamon sugar while hot. They are served with a chocolate sauce for dipping, at breakfast or as a snack.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 250 ml water
  • 50 g butter
  • 1 tbsp sugar, a pinch of salt
  • 150 g plain flour
  • 2 eggs
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • For coating: 60 g sugar mixed with 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 150 ml chocolate sauce for dipping

Method

  1. Bring the water, butter, sugar and salt to the boil. Tip in the flour and beat until the dough forms a ball.
  2. Cool for 5 minutes, then beat in the eggs one at a time until smooth and glossy.
  3. Heat the oil to 180 °C. Pipe 10–12 cm lengths through a star nozzle straight into the oil.
  4. Fry for 2–3 minutes, turning, until golden. Drain on paper towels.
  5. Roll in cinnamon sugar while hot and serve with warm chocolate sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Have 3 churros with a spoonful of sauce rather than a full plate with a cup of dip.
  • Fry at a steady 180 °C so they absorb less oil, and drain them well.
  • Skip the sugar coating and get sweetness from the dip only.

History

Churros have been sold by street vendors and in churrerías in Spain for centuries; their exact origin is uncertain, and several stories link them to shepherds or to Portuguese sailors. Spanish colonists carried them to Latin America, where they are now just as popular.
