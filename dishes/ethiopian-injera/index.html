Injera (Ethiopian teff flatbread)

Origin: Ethiopia

Injera is a large, soft pancake with a bubbly surface and a mild sour tang from days of fermentation. It serves as plate, spoon and bread at once: stews are ladled on top and eaten by tearing off pieces. Its flavor is subtly tart and earthy.

Recipe

6 servings4320 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g teff flour
  • 600 ml water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Mix the teff flour with the water to a smooth batter.
  2. Cover and ferment at room temperature for 2-3 days until bubbly and sour.
  3. Stir in the salt and thin with a little water to a pourable batter.
  4. Pour a thin spiral onto a hot non-stick pan and cover for 2 minutes.
  5. Cook only one side; cool the injera on a cloth and stack.

How to make it lighter

  • Teff is rich in iron and fiber, and is naturally gluten-free when 100% teff is used.
  • Use a starter from a previous batch to speed up fermentation.
  • Pair with legume stews for a balanced meal.

History

Teff is a tiny grain native to the Horn of Africa and has been cultivated in Ethiopia for a very long time. Injera is the base of nearly every traditional meal there and in Eritrea.
