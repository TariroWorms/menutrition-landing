Chalop (cold katyk soup)

Origin: Uzbekistan

Chalop is Uzbekistan's answer to a hot afternoon. Thick katyk is thinned with icy water, salted and filled with chopped cucumber, radish and handfuls of dill, cilantro and basil. It is sour, cold and very refreshing, and is eaten with a spoon alongside flatbread or after a heavy plov.

What it is made of

katyk (fermented milk)cucumberdillcilantroradishgarlic

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g katyk or plain yogurt
  • 500 ml cold water
  • 2 cucumbers (300 g)
  • 4 radishes (80 g)
  • 20 g dill
  • 20 g cilantro
  • 1 garlic clove
  • salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Whisk the katyk with the cold water and salt until smooth.
  2. Dice the cucumbers and radishes finely.
  3. Chop the dill and cilantro and crush the garlic.
  4. Stir everything into the katyk.
  5. Chill for at least 30 minutes; add ice cubes on very hot days.
  6. Serve with a pinch of black pepper and flatbread.

How to make it lighter

  • Use low-fat yogurt for an even lighter soup.
  • Add more cucumber for extra volume with few calories.
  • Skip the bread to keep it a light starter.

History

Cold soups based on soured milk are common across Central Asia, and chalop is the Uzbek summer version. It is a simple home dish made in many families once the heat arrives.
