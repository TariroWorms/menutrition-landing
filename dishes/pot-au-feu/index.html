Pot-au-feu is the classic French boiled dinner: cuts of beef and marrow bones simmered slowly with carrots, leeks, turnips and cabbage until the meat is meltingly tender. The clear, savoury broth is often served first as a soup, followed by the sliced meat and vegetables, with coarse salt and mustard or pickles on the side.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg beef for braising (shin, chuck or short rib)
  • 4 marrow bones
  • 6 carrots, halved
  • 3 leeks, tied in bundles
  • 4 small turnips, halved
  • ½ small cabbage, in wedges
  • 1 onion studded with 2 cloves
  • 1 bouquet garni (thyme, bay leaf, parsley)
  • Coarse salt and peppercorns

Method

  1. Put the beef in a large pot, cover with cold water, bring to a simmer and skim off the foam.
  2. Add the onion, bouquet garni, peppercorns and salt; simmer very gently, partly covered, for 2.5 hours.
  3. Add the carrots, leeks and turnips and cook 30 minutes; wrap the marrow bones' ends in salt or foil and add them with the cabbage for the last 20–30 minutes.
  4. Lift out the meat and vegetables; strain and degrease the broth.
  5. Serve the broth as a first course, then the sliced meat and vegetables with coarse salt.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the broth and lift off the solid fat before reheating.
  • Fill the plate with vegetables and keep beef to about 150 g.
  • Enjoy the marrow as a taste, not a whole bone each; it is very rich.

History

Pot-au-feu, literally 'pot on the fire', is a traditional French family dish whose roots lie in long-simmered country stews. It is often regarded as one of France's national dishes and has many regional variations.
