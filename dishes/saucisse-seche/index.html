🥓 Saucisse sèche

Origin: France · Auvergne and the south

Saucisse sèche is a French dry-cured pork sausage, thin and often shaped in a horseshoe or long rope. It is firm and chewy with a concentrated savoury flavour and a pleasantly salty finish. It is sliced thinly and eaten as part of a charcuterie board or an apéritif with bread, pickles and cheese on the side.

What it is made of

dried pork sausage

Recipe

6 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g saucisse sèche
  • 1 small cucumber
  • 100 g cherry tomatoes
  • 50 g cornichons (small gherkins)
  • 1 small handful of radishes
  • Fresh parsley

Method

  1. Remove the casing from the sausage if it is thick.
  2. Slice the sausage thinly on a slight diagonal.
  3. Slice the cucumber and radishes and halve the tomatoes.
  4. Arrange the sausage and vegetables on a board with the cornichons and parsley and serve at room temperature.

How to make it lighter

  • Slice the sausage very thinly — it makes a small portion feel generous.
  • Balance the board with plenty of raw vegetables and pickles rather than adding more cured meat.
  • Weigh a portion once: 30–50 g is a typical apéritif serving for such a rich sausage.

History

Dry-cured sausages are an old tradition in mountainous regions of France such as the Auvergne, where air-drying preserved pork through the year; today saucisse sèche is sold across France at markets and charcuteries.
