Coq au vin is chicken braised slowly in red wine with bacon, mushrooms, onions and garlic. The long cooking makes the meat tender enough to fall off the bone, while the wine reduces into a dark, glossy, deeply savoury sauce. It is a classic French main course, often served with potatoes or crusty bread to soak up the sauce.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg chicken pieces, skin on
  • 150 g bacon lardons
  • 250 g mushrooms, quartered
  • 12 small onions or shallots, peeled
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 500 ml red wine
  • 2 sprigs thyme and 1 bay leaf
  • 1 tbsp oil, salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Fry the bacon in the oil until crisp, remove, then brown the seasoned chicken in the fat on all sides.
  2. Add the onions and garlic and cook for 3 minutes, then return the bacon.
  3. Pour in the red wine, add the herbs, cover and simmer gently for about 1¼ hours until the chicken is tender.
  4. Add the mushrooms for the last 20 minutes and simmer uncovered to reduce the sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the chicken skin before serving to save a good share of the fat.
  • Halve the bacon — it still gives plenty of smoky flavour.
  • Serve with steamed green beans or a salad instead of a large portion of potatoes.

History

Coq au vin is closely associated with Burgundy, where an old rooster was traditionally braised in the region's red wine to tenderise it. Similar dishes exist across France with local wines, and the dish became internationally famous in the 20th century as a symbol of French home cooking.
