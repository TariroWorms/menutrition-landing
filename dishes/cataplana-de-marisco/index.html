🦐 Cataplana de marisco

Origin: Portugal · Algarve

Cataplana de marisco is a Portuguese seafood stew of clams, shrimp and firm white fish steamed with tomato, onion and white wine. It is cooked and brought to the table in a cataplana, a hinged copper pan shaped like two shells, which traps the steam so the seafood stays tender and the broth becomes briny, herby and tomato-rich. It is served with the broth for spooning.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g clams, scrubbed
  • 400 g raw shrimp
  • 400 g firm white fish fillet, in chunks
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 150 ml dry white wine
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, chopped parsley and coriander

Method

  1. Soften the onions and garlic in the oil in a cataplana or wide lidded pan for 8 minutes.
  2. Add the tomatoes and wine and simmer for 10 minutes.
  3. Lay the fish on top, then the shrimp and clams. Close tightly and cook over medium heat for 8–10 minutes until the clams open.
  4. Discard any clams that stay shut, scatter with herbs and serve from the pan.

How to make it lighter

  • The stew is lean on its own; keep the oil to 1 tbsp (about 120 kcal) for the whole pan.
  • Spoon up the broth with extra vegetables instead of filling up on extras on the side.
  • Add more fish and shrimp rather than richer extras to make it a bigger main course.

History

The dish is named after the cataplana pan, which is strongly associated with the Algarve in southern Portugal. The clam-shaped pan is often said to reflect Moorish cooking traditions in the region, though its exact origins are unclear. Today seafood cataplana is a fixture of coastal restaurants across Portugal.
