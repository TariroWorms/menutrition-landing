🧀 Cheese-stuffed luqaimat

Origin: Gulf · Arabian Peninsula

Cheese luqaimat are a modern twist on the classic Gulf dumplings: balls of yeasted dough with a small piece of cheese hidden inside, deep-fried until golden so the centre melts. They are drizzled with date syrup and sprinkled with sesame seeds, giving a sweet-and-salty contrast of crisp shell, soft dough and stretchy cheese. They are popular at cafés and Ramadan gatherings.

Recipe

6 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 7 g dried yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 400 ml warm water
  • A pinch of salt
  • 200 g mild melting cheese (such as mozzarella), cut into small cubes
  • 120 ml date syrup
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
  • Oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, sugar, salt and warm water into a thick, sticky batter; cover and rest for 1 hour.
  2. Heat the oil to about 175 °C.
  3. Take a spoonful of batter with a wet spoon, press a cheese cube into it and cover it with a little more batter.
  4. Drop into the oil and fry, turning, until golden and crisp; drain on paper.
  5. Drizzle with date syrup, sprinkle with sesame and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • The cheese adds richness — four or five pieces make a fair portion.
  • Use a lighter cheese, such as reduced-fat mozzarella.
  • Serve the date syrup on the side for dipping instead of pouring it over.

History

Cheese-filled luqaimat are a recent variation of the traditional Gulf sweet, now common at cafés, food stalls and Ramadan tents in the UAE, Saudi Arabia and neighbouring countries.
