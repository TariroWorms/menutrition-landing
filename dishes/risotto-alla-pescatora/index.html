🦐 Risotto alla Pescatora

Risotto alla pescatora is the fisherman's risotto of the Italian coast: Arborio rice stirred slowly with clams, mussels and shrimp, garlic, a splash of white wine and tomato. The rice turns loose and brothy rather than stiff, soaking up the sweet, briny juices of the shellfish. It is served straight from the pan, usually with chopped parsley on top.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g Arborio rice
  • 500 g clams, purged
  • 500 g mussels, cleaned
  • 250 g shrimp, peeled
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 120 ml dry white wine
  • 200 g tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1.2 l hot water
  • Parsley, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Steam the clams and mussels in a covered pan with a little water until they open; strain and keep the liquid, discard any that stay closed.
  2. Warm the oil, fry the garlic briefly, add the rice and toast it for 2 minutes.
  3. Pour in the wine and let it evaporate, then add the tomatoes.
  4. Add the shellfish liquid and hot water a ladle at a time, stirring, for about 16 minutes.
  5. Stir in the shrimp for the last 3 minutes, then fold in the clams and mussels.
  6. Rest 1 minute, season and finish with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • A 350 g plate at about 150 kcal per 100 g is roughly 525 kcal — serve 250 g with a big salad to save around 150 kcal.
  • 1 tbsp olive oil ≈ 120 kcal: use 1 tbsp instead of 2 for the whole pan.
  • Ask for extra shellfish and less rice to raise the protein share of the plate.

History

Seafood risottos are cooked all along Italy's coastline, and pescatora ('fisherman's style') signals a mix of whatever shellfish the day brought in. The dish is a staple of trattorias in seaside towns today.
