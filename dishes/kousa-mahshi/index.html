🥒 Kousa mahshi (stuffed zucchini)

Origin: Levant · Middle East

Kousa mahshi is small zucchini cored and filled with rice, ground beef, tomato and mint, then braised in a light tomato broth until tender. The zucchini soaks up the tangy sauce while the filling stays savoury and soft. It is one of the most common family lunches across the Levant and Egypt, often cooked in the same pot as stuffed vine leaves.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 12 small zucchini
  • 150 g short-grain rice, rinsed
  • 250 g ground beef
  • 4 tomatoes: 1 finely chopped, 3 grated
  • 1 tbsp dried mint
  • 1 tsp seven-spice, salt and pepper
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Hollow out the zucchini with a corer, leaving thin walls.
  2. Mix the rice, beef, chopped tomato, half the mint, spices, salt and pepper; fill the zucchini three quarters full.
  3. Arrange them in a pot, add the grated tomatoes, tomato paste and water just to cover.
  4. Cover with a plate to keep them down and simmer for 45 minutes until the rice is cooked.
  5. Fry the garlic and remaining mint briefly in oil and stir into the broth for the last 5 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean ground beef and add more chopped tomato to the filling.
  • Serve it in its broth with a salad instead of starchy sides.
  • Eat the scooped-out zucchini flesh cooked into a side dish rather than throwing it away.

History

Stuffed vegetables, mahshi, are cooked across the eastern Mediterranean, and zucchini is one of the most popular. The tomato-braised version is a weekday staple in Lebanese, Syrian, Palestinian, Jordanian and Egyptian homes.
