🥑 Avocado Hosomaki

Origin: Japan

Hosomaki are thin sushi rolls with a single filling wrapped in seasoned rice and a sheet of nori. In this roll the filling is ripe avocado, which gives a soft, creamy, buttery-tasting centre against the mild vinegared rice. Each roll is cut into six bite-size pieces, served with pickled ginger and wasabi, and often ordered as a light starter or alongside other sushi.

What it is made of

sushi riceavocadonori

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g sushi rice
  • 240 ml water
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 2 sheets nori, halved
  • 1 ripe avocado

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then cook it with the water and rest it covered for 10 minutes.
  2. Dissolve the sugar and salt in the vinegar and fold into the warm rice; let it cool to room temperature.
  3. Halve the avocado, remove the stone and cut the flesh into long thin strips.
  4. Place a half nori sheet on a bamboo mat, spread a thin layer of rice over it, leaving 1 cm free at the top edge.
  5. Lay the filling in a line across the middle and roll tightly with the mat.
  6. Cut each roll into 6 pieces with a wet knife and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Keep the rice layer thin; the rice carries most of the calories in each piece.
  • Use thin avocado strips; avocado is rich in fat, so a little goes a long way.
  • Count pieces: one roll of 6 pieces is a light portion; pair it with a salad rather than fried sides.

History

Rolled sushi (makizushi) developed in Japan during the Edo period, and thin single-filling rolls became a standard of sushi counters. Today hosomaki are sold everywhere from Japanese restaurants to supermarket counters worldwide.
