🥢 Fresh spring rolls (gỏi cuốn)

Origin: Vietnam · southern Vietnam

Fresh spring rolls are soft, translucent rice paper wrappers rolled around poached shrimp, thin rice noodles, crisp lettuce and fragrant herbs such as mint and Thai basil. They are not fried, so the wrapper stays chewy and the filling cool and crunchy. They are served at room temperature with a thick peanut dipping sauce, usually as a starter or light lunch.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 12 sheets rice paper
  • 300 g raw shrimp, peeled
  • 100 g thin rice noodles
  • 1 small head lettuce, leaves separated
  • 1 bunch mint and Thai basil
  • 1 carrot, cut into thin sticks
  • 6 tbsp peanut dipping sauce

Method

  1. Cook the rice noodles according to the packet, rinse in cold water and drain.
  2. Poach the shrimp in simmering salted water for 2–3 minutes, cool and halve lengthways.
  3. Dip one rice paper sheet in warm water for a few seconds until just pliable and lay it flat.
  4. Place lettuce, noodles, carrot and herbs in the lower third, then three shrimp halves pink side down above them.
  5. Fold in the sides and roll up tightly; repeat with the remaining sheets.
  6. Serve straight away with the peanut sauce for dipping.

How to make it lighter

  • The rolls themselves are light — most of the calories are in the peanut sauce, so dip rather than pour.
  • Thin the sauce with a little warm water and lime juice to stretch it further.
  • Use more lettuce and herbs and fewer noodles for a lower-carb roll.

History

Gỏi cuốn, often called summer rolls in English, are associated with southern Vietnam, where rice paper wrapping is an everyday technique. They spread internationally with the Vietnamese diaspora and are now a staple of Vietnamese restaurants worldwide.
