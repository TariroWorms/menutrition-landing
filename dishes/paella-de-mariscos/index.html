🦐 Seafood Paella

Paella de mariscos is the all-seafood version of Spain's famous rice dish: short-grain rice cooked in fish stock with saffron, then topped with shrimp, mussels, clams and squid. The rice turns golden and briny, soaking up the flavour of the shellfish, and forms a toasted crust on the bottom of the pan. It is served straight from the pan, often with lemon wedges.

Recipe

4 servings55 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g short-grain rice (such as bomba)
  • 250 g raw shrimp
  • 300 g mussels, cleaned
  • 250 g clams, cleaned
  • 200 g squid, cut into rings
  • 1 pinch saffron
  • 1 l fish stock
  • 1 tomato, grated
  • 2 cloves garlic, chopped
  • 3 tbsp olive oil, salt

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a paella pan and sear the squid for 2 minutes; add the garlic and grated tomato and cook for 5 minutes.
  2. Pour in the hot fish stock with the saffron, season with salt and bring to a boil.
  3. Scatter the rice evenly and simmer for 10 minutes without stirring.
  4. Arrange the shrimp, mussels and clams on top, pressing them in slightly.
  5. Cook for 8–10 minutes until the liquid is absorbed and the shells open, then rest for 5 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Seafood is lean, so most calories come from rice and oil; keep the oil to 2 tbsp (≈ 240 kcal) per pan.
  • Fill half the plate with salad or grilled vegetables.
  • A portion of about 300 g fits most calorie budgets better than a heaped plate.

History

Paella comes from the rice-growing area around the Albufera lagoon near Valencia, where farm workers cooked rice in a wide, shallow pan over an open fire; the word 'paella' is Valencian for that pan. Seafood versions became common along the Mediterranean coast, where fish stock replaced the meat of the inland original.
