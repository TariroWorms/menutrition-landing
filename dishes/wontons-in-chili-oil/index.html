🥟 Sichuan wontons in red chili oil

Origin: China · Sichuan

Wontons in chili oil, known in Sichuan as hong you chao shou, are small pork dumplings in thin wrappers, boiled and tossed in a sauce of chili oil, soy sauce, vinegar and garlic. The silky wrappers soak up a sauce that is spicy, tangy, savoury and slightly sweet at once. They are served hot in a bowl as a snack or light meal, topped with spring onion.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g minced pork
  • 30 wonton wrappers
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely grated (plus 3 for the sauce)
  • 1 tsp grated ginger and 2 spring onions, chopped
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp black or rice vinegar
  • 3 tbsp chili oil
  • 1 tsp sugar, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Mix the pork with 2 grated garlic cloves, ginger, half the spring onion, 1 tbsp soy sauce, salt and pepper.
  2. Put a teaspoon of filling in the centre of each wrapper, moisten the edges with water, fold into a triangle and bring the two corners together.
  3. For the sauce, stir together the remaining garlic, 2 tbsp soy sauce, vinegar, chili oil and sugar.
  4. Boil the wontons in plenty of water for 4–5 minutes until they float and the filling is cooked.
  5. Drain, toss with the sauce and finish with the remaining spring onion.

How to make it lighter

  • Chili oil is the richest part of the dish: spoon it on sparingly — 1 tbsp of oil is about 120 kcal.
  • Use lean minced pork to cut the fat in the filling.
  • Serve a smaller portion of wontons alongside a large plate of blanched greens.

History

The dish comes from Sichuan province, where wontons are called chao shou, often explained as "folded hands" after their shape. It is a classic of Chengdu street food and is now served in Sichuan restaurants around the world.
