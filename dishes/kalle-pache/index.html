Kalle pache (Persian sheep head and trotter soup)

Origin: Iran

Kalle pache is a rich, gelatinous broth of sheep head and trotters simmered for many hours. It is eaten at dawn with fresh sangak bread, lemon juice, and cinnamon or turmeric. The tongue, cheeks and brain are served separately in traditional shops.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 sheep head, split and cleaned (about 1.5 kg)
  • 4 sheep trotters, cleaned
  • 2 onions, halved
  • 1 head of garlic
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • salt and black pepper
  • lemon wedges and bread to serve

Method

  1. Soak and carefully clean the head and trotters; blanch for 10 minutes and rinse.
  2. Place in a large pot with onions, garlic, cinnamon, turmeric and salt.
  3. Cover with water and simmer very gently for 6-8 hours.
  4. Skim the foam regularly and top up with water when needed.
  5. Serve the broth with pieces of meat, lemon juice, cinnamon and fresh bread.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat from the top of the cooled broth for a lighter dish.
  • Eat in small portions as it is rich in fat and cholesterol.
  • Serve with plenty of lemon and fresh herbs.

History

Specialist kalle pazi shops open before sunrise across Iran and sell the dish to early risers. It follows the nose-to-tail tradition common across the Middle East and Caucasus.
