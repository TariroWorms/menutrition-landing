🍰 Honey cake (medovik)

Origin: Russia · Eastern Europe

Medovik is a layered honey cake: eight or more thin, crisp-baked honey sponge layers stacked with a tangy sour-cream frosting. After resting overnight the layers soak up the cream and turn soft and moist, with a caramel-honey flavour. A slice is served with tea, with the sides and top covered in fine cake crumbs.

Recipe

12 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 450 g plain flour
  • 100 g honey
  • 150 g sugar plus 100 g for the cream
  • 2 eggs
  • 60 g butter
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 700 g sour cream (20%)

Method

  1. Melt the butter, honey and 150 g sugar in a bowl over simmering water, stir in the soda until foamy, then beat in the eggs.
  2. Off the heat, mix in the flour to a soft dough and divide it into 8 pieces.
  3. Roll each piece very thin, cut a 24 cm circle and bake at 180 °C for 4–5 minutes. Bake the trimmings too.
  4. Whisk the sour cream with 100 g sugar.
  5. Stack the layers, spreading cream on each, cover the sides and top, and coat with crushed trimmings. Chill for at least 8 hours.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thinner slices: a 70–80 g slice satisfies the craving.
  • Use 15% sour cream and sweeten the cream lightly.
  • Treat it as the dessert of the day and pair it with unsweetened tea.

History

The honey cake is a favourite of Russian and Soviet home baking and is eaten across Eastern Europe. A popular story credits its creation to a cook at the court of Alexander I in the early 19th century, but there is no reliable evidence for it.
