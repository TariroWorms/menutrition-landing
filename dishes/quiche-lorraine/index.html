Quiche Lorraine is the classic French savoury tart: a crisp pastry shell filled with a rich custard of eggs and cream, studded with smoky bacon lardons and topped with Gruyère. It bakes to a wobbly, golden filling with a salty, savoury flavour. It is served warm in slices with a green salad.

Recipe

6 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 1 pastry sheet (about 230 g)
  • 150 g smoked bacon lardons
  • 3 eggs
  • 250 ml cream
  • 80 g Gruyère, grated
  • Nutmeg, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Line a 24 cm tin with the pastry and blind-bake at 190 °C for 12 minutes.
  2. Fry the lardons in a dry pan until lightly browned; drain on paper.
  3. Whisk eggs, cream, nutmeg and pepper; add only a little salt.
  4. Scatter the bacon in the shell, pour in the custard and sprinkle with Gruyère.
  5. Bake 30–35 minutes until just set; rest 10 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean bacon and drain off the rendered fat.
  • Replace part of the cream with water.
  • Cut 8 slices instead of 6 and serve with salad.

History

The dish comes from Lorraine in north-eastern France, where the original version used only eggs, cream and smoked bacon; cheese was added later. The name quiche is usually linked to the German word Kuchen, meaning cake.
