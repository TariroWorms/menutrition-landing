Arepa catira

Origin: Venezuela

Catira is the Venezuelan word for a blonde, and this arepa is named for its golden filling of shredded chicken and yellow cheese. The chicken is stewed in a light sofrito of tomato, onion, pepper and garlic until soft and juicy, then pulled apart. Packed into a hot arepa with grated cheese, it is mild, savoury and comforting. Children and adults alike order it at areperas.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g precooked white corn flour
  • 380 ml warm water
  • 400 g chicken breast
  • 1 tomato (120 g), 1 onion (120 g), ½ red pepper (70 g), 2 garlic cloves
  • 150 g yellow cheese (gouda), grated
  • 1 tbsp oil, cumin, salt

Method

  1. Poach the chicken in salted water for 20 minutes, cool and shred.
  2. Fry the onion, pepper and garlic in oil, add grated tomato and cumin and cook 10 minutes.
  3. Stir in the chicken with a splash of broth and simmer until juicy.
  4. Make the arepa dough, shape 6 discs and cook on a griddle 6–7 minutes per side.
  5. Split the arepas, fill with chicken and top with grated cheese.

How to make it lighter

  • Chicken breast keeps this lean; skip the skin.
  • Use 15 g cheese per arepa or a light cheese.
  • Add extra peppers and tomato to the stew for more vegetables.

History

The catira is a close cousin of the pelúa, swapping beef for chicken. Its name follows the Venezuelan habit of giving arepas nicknames drawn from everyday speech.
