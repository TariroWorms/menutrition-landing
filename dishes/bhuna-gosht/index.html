Bhuna gosht (dry-fried mutton curry)

Origin: Pakistan · Punjab

Bhuna gosht is a thick, nearly dry curry in which mutton is repeatedly stirred and fried with onion, tomato and spices until the gravy clings to the meat in a dark glossy coating. The flavour is concentrated, rich and deeply savoury. It is eaten with roti or naan and a salad of onion and lemon.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g mutton, in pieces
  • 3 onions (400 g), sliced
  • 3 tomatoes (300 g)
  • 40 g ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tsp ground coriander, 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 80 ml oil, salt

Method

  1. Fry onions in oil until deep brown.
  2. Add the paste and spices and cook for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the meat and fry on high heat until browned.
  4. Add the tomatoes and a little water, cover and cook on low for 60 minutes.
  5. Uncover and stir-fry until the oil separates and the gravy is thick.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less oil and lean cuts.
  • Pair with roti and a large salad.
  • Skim extra oil from the pan before serving.

History

The bhuna technique, frying spices and meat over high heat until the oil separates, is central to northern South Asian cooking. Bhuna gosht is a classic at Punjabi family meals and festive tables.
