🥧 Engadine nut tart (Bündner Nusstorte)

Origin: Switzerland · Graubünden

The Engadine nut tart is a Swiss pastry of buttery shortcrust filled with chopped walnuts bound in a thick caramel made with sugar and cream, then closed with a pastry lid. It is dense, chewy and very rich, with a deep caramel flavour. It is cut into thin slices and served with coffee, and it keeps well for weeks.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 150 g cold butter, plus 30 g for the filling
  • 80 g sugar for the dough
  • 1 egg
  • 4–5 tbsp cold water
  • 250 g sugar for the caramel
  • 200 ml cream
  • 300 g walnuts, roughly chopped

Method

  1. Rub the flour, 150 g butter and 80 g sugar together, add the egg and water and gather into a dough; chill 30 minutes.
  2. Melt 250 g sugar in a pan to a light amber caramel, carefully stir in the cream and 30 g butter, then the walnuts; cool.
  3. Roll out two-thirds of the dough to line a 24 cm tin, fill with the walnut mixture and cover with the rest of the dough.
  4. Prick the top and bake at 180 °C for 35–40 minutes until golden; cool completely before slicing.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thin slices — the tart is very dense, and a small piece is satisfying.
  • Share one slice and pair it with black coffee or tea.
  • When baking, reduce the caramel sugar slightly; the walnuts carry plenty of flavour.

History

The tart is closely associated with the Engadine valley and the canton of Graubünden, though its exact origin is not well documented. Today it is a well-known speciality and souvenir of the region.
