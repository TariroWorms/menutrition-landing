🍝 Farfalle with Genovese pesto

Farfalle al pesto genovese is bow-tie pasta tossed with raw basil pesto: fresh basil, pine nuts, garlic, parmesan and olive oil pounded or blended into a bright green sauce. The pesto is never cooked, so it stays fragrant, herby and slightly nutty. A spoonful of pasta water helps it coat every piece evenly.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 350 g farfalle
  • 50 g fresh basil leaves
  • 30 g pine nuts
  • 50 g parmesan, grated
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 80 ml extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Blend or pound the basil, pine nuts, garlic and a pinch of salt to a paste.
  2. Mix in the parmesan, then stir in the olive oil to make a loose pesto.
  3. Cook the farfalle in salted boiling water until al dente; keep a cup of pasta water.
  4. Off the heat, toss the pasta with the pesto, adding pasta water to loosen.
  5. Serve immediately, without reheating the sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Pesto is oil- and nut-rich: use about 1 heaped tablespoon per portion.
  • Loosen the pesto with pasta water instead of extra oil.
  • Add green beans or courgette to the pasta to make a bigger, lighter plate.

History

Pesto alla genovese comes from Genoa and the Liguria region, where small-leaf basil grows well. Written recipes for the basil sauce appear in the 19th century, and it has since become one of the best-known Italian pasta sauces worldwide.
