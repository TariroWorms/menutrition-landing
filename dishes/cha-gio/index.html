🥢 Chả giò (Vietnamese fried spring rolls)

Origin: Vietnam · South

Chả giò are small Vietnamese spring rolls wrapped in rice paper and deep-fried until blistered and crisp. The filling is minced pork and chopped shrimp mixed with wood ear mushrooms and soaked vermicelli, seasoned with pepper and garlic. They are eaten hot, wrapped in lettuce with fresh herbs, as a starter or on top of noodle bowls.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g minced pork
  • 150 g shrimp, finely chopped
  • 10 g dried wood ear mushrooms, soaked and chopped
  • 40 g vermicelli, soaked and cut short
  • 2 garlic cloves and 1 shallot, minced
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper, salt
  • 16 rice paper sheets
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • Lettuce, mint and cilantro to serve

Method

  1. Mix the pork, shrimp, mushrooms, vermicelli, garlic, shallot, salt and pepper.
  2. Briefly dip each rice paper sheet in water and lay it flat.
  3. Place a spoonful of filling near the edge, fold in the sides and roll up tightly.
  4. Deep-fry at 170 °C in batches for 8–10 minutes until golden and crisp.
  5. Drain on paper towels and serve with lettuce and herbs for wrapping.

How to make it lighter

  • Deep-frying is where most fat comes from: bake the rolls at 200 °C, brushed lightly with oil, for a lighter version.
  • Wrap each roll in lettuce with plenty of herbs so two or three rolls feel like a full portion.
  • Use lean pork mince and a larger share of shrimp and mushrooms.

History

Chả giò is the southern Vietnamese name for these rolls; in the north they are called nem rán. They are a fixture of family feasts and the Lunar New Year (Tết) table across Vietnam.
