Parvarda (Uzbek pulled sugar candy)

Origin: Uzbekistan
114kcal

Parvarda is made by boiling sugar syrup to a thick stage, then pulling and stretching it by hand until it turns white and airy. The rope is cut into small pillows and rolled in toasted flour so they do not stick. The candies are melt-in-the-mouth sweet and are traditionally eaten with green tea instead of sugar.

Recipe

10 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g sugar
  • 200 ml water
  • 1 tsp vinegar or lemon juice
  • 100 g flour, toasted

Method

  1. Boil sugar, water and vinegar to the hard-ball stage (about 125 °C).
  2. Pour onto an oiled surface and let it cool slightly.
  3. Pull and fold the warm mass repeatedly until it turns white.
  4. Stretch into a thin rope and snip into small pieces.
  5. Roll the pieces in toasted flour and let them harden.

How to make it lighter

  • Treat it as a tea sweet: one or two pieces only.
  • Drink it with unsweetened green tea.
  • Brush teeth after eating sticky sugar candy.

History

Parvarda is one of the oldest Central Asian sweets and is a familiar sight on dastarkhan tables in Uzbekistan. Pulling the hot syrup is skilled work, and the candy is usually bought from confectioners at the bazaar.
