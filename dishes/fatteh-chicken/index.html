🍗 Chicken fatteh

Origin: Levant · Middle East

Chicken fatteh layers toasted bread and spiced rice with tender shredded chicken, then covers everything with a garlicky yogurt and tahini sauce and a handful of toasted almonds. It combines crunchy, soft and creamy textures in one dish. Served warm on a large platter, it is a popular choice for Ramadan iftars and family lunches.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g chicken pieces
  • 250 g rice
  • 1 large pita, cut into squares and toasted
  • 400 g plain yogurt
  • 2 tbsp tahini
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 30 g almonds, slivered and toasted
  • Allspice, cinnamon, bay leaf, salt

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken with bay leaf, allspice, cinnamon and salt for 40 minutes; shred the meat and keep the broth.
  2. Cook the rice in 500 ml of the broth.
  3. Whisk the yogurt with tahini, garlic and salt.
  4. Layer the toasted bread, moisten with a little broth, add the rice and chicken.
  5. Spoon over the yogurt sauce and top with the almonds.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skinless chicken breast and remove the fat from the broth.
  • Swap part of the rice for extra chicken to raise the protein share.
  • Keep the almonds to a 10 g sprinkle per plate.

History

Fatteh dishes are made across Syria, Lebanon, Palestine and Jordan, and the chicken version is a common way to turn a boiled chicken and its broth into a festive meal. It appears on home and restaurant tables throughout the Levant and the Gulf.
