🐟 Sarde in saor

Origin: Italy · Venice, Veneto

Sarde in saor is a Venetian sweet-and-sour appetiser of fried sardines layered with soft onions cooked in vinegar, raisins and pine nuts. It is left to marinate for a day or more, so the fish absorbs the tangy, slightly sweet dressing. It is served at room temperature as cicchetti in Venetian bars or as a starter.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g fresh sardines, cleaned and butterflied
  • 3 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 150 ml white vinegar
  • 40 g raisins, soaked in warm water
  • 30 g pine nuts
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Pat the sardines dry and season with salt.
  2. Fry them in 2 tbsp oil for 1–2 minutes per side until golden, then drain.
  3. In the same pan soften the onions in the remaining oil over low heat for 15 minutes without browning.
  4. Add the vinegar, drained raisins and pine nuts and simmer for 3 minutes.
  5. Layer sardines and onion mixture in a dish, cover and refrigerate for at least 24 hours. Serve at room temperature.

How to make it lighter

  • Grill or bake the sardines instead of frying to use less oil — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Go light on the pine nuts; a small scattering keeps the flavour.
  • Serve a small portion as a starter with a large green salad.

History

The dish is usually traced to Venetian fishermen and sailors, for whom the vinegar marinade (saor means 'flavour' in Venetian) kept fish edible on long voyages. Raisins and pine nuts are often linked to the city's trade with the eastern Mediterranean. It is traditionally eaten for the Festa del Redentore in July.
