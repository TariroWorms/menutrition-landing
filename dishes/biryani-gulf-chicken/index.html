🍛 Gulf-style chicken biryani

Origin: Gulf states · Arabian Peninsula

Gulf chicken biryani layers long-grain basmati rice with chicken marinated in yogurt, fried onions and a warm biryani spice mix, then steams it so the rice absorbs the juices. Saffron streaks the top grains gold and fresh mint lifts the aroma. It is milder and more fragrant than many South Asian biryanis and is served on a large platter, often with daqqus tomato sauce and salad.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g chicken pieces, skinless
  • 400 g basmati rice
  • 200 g plain yogurt
  • 3 onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 tbsp biryani spice mix
  • 3 garlic cloves and 2 cm ginger, grated
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • A pinch of saffron in 3 tbsp warm water
  • A handful of mint leaves
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil, salt

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken with the yogurt, garlic, ginger, half the spice mix and salt for at least 30 minutes.
  2. Rinse the rice and soak it for 20 minutes. Fry the onions in the oil until deep golden and set half aside.
  3. Add the tomatoes and remaining spices to the onions in the pot, then the chicken with its marinade; cover and cook for 25 minutes.
  4. Boil the rice in salted water for 6–7 minutes until three-quarters cooked and drain.
  5. Spread the rice over the chicken, scatter the reserved onions and mint, and drizzle with saffron water.
  6. Cover tightly and steam on very low heat for 20 minutes, then gently mix and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Rice is the biggest share of calories: ask for a half portion of rice with extra chicken and salad.
  • Fry the onions in 1–2 tablespoons of oil instead of deep-frying them — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Use skinless chicken and low-fat yogurt for the marinade.

History

Biryani comes from the Indian subcontinent and reached the Gulf through centuries of trade and migration across the Arabian Sea. Gulf cooks adapted it to local taste, usually with less chilli and more saffron and whole spices. Today it is a staple of restaurants and family gatherings in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman.
