🍮 Île flottante

Île flottante, or 'floating island', is a cloud of soft poached meringue resting on a pool of chilled vanilla custard (crème anglaise), finished with a drizzle of caramel. The meringue is light and airy, the custard silky and cold, and the caramel adds a crisp, slightly bitter sweetness. It is served in shallow bowls as a classic bistro dessert.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs, separated
  • 500 ml milk
  • 1 vanilla pod or 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 100 g sugar for the custard and meringue
  • 80 g sugar for the caramel
  • A pinch of salt

Method

  1. Heat the milk with the vanilla. Whisk the yolks with 50 g sugar, pour in the hot milk, return to the pan and stir over low heat until it coats a spoon; chill.
  2. Whisk the whites with a pinch of salt to soft peaks, then add 50 g sugar and whisk until glossy.
  3. Poach large spoonfuls of meringue in barely simmering water for 1 minute per side, or microwave in a bowl for 30–40 seconds; drain.
  4. Melt 80 g sugar with 2 tbsp water to an amber caramel.
  5. Pour the custard into bowls, set the meringue on top and drizzle with caramel.

How to make it lighter

  • Make the custard with semi-skimmed milk.
  • Just a thin thread of caramel is enough — 1 tbsp sugar is about 50 kcal.
  • Serve a smaller ladle of custard with a big meringue: the meringue is mostly air.

History

Île flottante is a traditional French dessert found on bistro menus across the country; it is closely related to œufs à la neige, where the meringue is shaped into small poached quenelles.
