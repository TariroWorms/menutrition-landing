🐔 Phoenix Claws (Dim Sum Chicken Feet)

Phoenix claws are a Cantonese dim sum classic: chicken feet that are fried or blanched, then braised and steamed in a sauce of fermented black beans, garlic, chili and soy sauce. The skin turns soft, puffy and gelatinous and soaks up the savory, slightly spicy sauce. They are served hot in small bamboo steamers and eaten by nibbling the meat and skin off the bones.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken feet, nails trimmed
  • 2 tbsp fermented black beans, rinsed
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1–2 red chilies, sliced
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 500 ml water

Method

  1. Blanch the feet for 5 minutes, drain and dry well.
  2. Fry in hot oil until blistered (or skip for a lighter version), then soak in cold water for 1 hour so the skin puffs.
  3. Fry the garlic, black beans and chili in 2 tbsp oil, add soy sauce, sugar and water.
  4. Braise the feet in the sauce for 30–40 minutes until tender.
  5. Transfer to small dishes and steam for 15 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the deep-frying step and simply blanch, braise and steam — it saves a lot of absorbed oil.
  • Use reduced-salt soy sauce and taste before adding more.
  • Pair a portion with steamed greens instead of fried dim sum.

History

The dish belongs to the Cantonese yum cha tradition of Guangdong and Hong Kong, where the poetic name 'phoenix claws' is used for chicken feet on menus. Today it is a staple of dim sum restaurants across southern China and Chinatowns worldwide.
