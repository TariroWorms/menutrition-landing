Marqooq is a comforting Gulf stew of lamb, pumpkin, eggplant, tomato and onion in which thin sheets of wheat dough are simmered until soft and silky. The dough thickens the baharat-spiced broth into a rich, spoonable dish where the vegetables almost melt. It is served hot in deep plates, often on cold evenings and in Ramadan.

Recipe

5 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g lamb, cubed
  • 250 g plain flour, ½ tsp salt and about 130 ml water for the dough
  • 300 g pumpkin, cubed
  • 1 eggplant, cubed
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped, and 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tsp baharat
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1.5 litres water

Method

  1. Make a firm dough from the flour, salt and water and rest it for 20 minutes.
  2. Brown the onion and lamb with the baharat in the oil, add the tomatoes, paste and water and simmer for 45 minutes.
  3. Add the pumpkin and eggplant and cook for 10 minutes.
  4. Roll the dough into very thin sheets, tear or cut into pieces and add to the stew.
  5. Simmer for 15 minutes, stirring gently, until the dough is soft and the stew thick.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean lamb and skim fat off the surface of the broth.
  • Increase the vegetables and use less dough to lower the carbs per bowl.
  • It is a full meal in itself — serve with a fresh salad, not bread or rice.

History

Marqooq — also spelled margoog — is a traditional dish of Saudi Arabia, especially Najd, and is also cooked in Kuwait, Qatar and the Emirates, with vegetables varying by season and family.
