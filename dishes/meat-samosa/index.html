Meat samosas are crisp, triangular pastries filled with minced beef, onion and green peas spiced with garam masala. The thin wheat shell blisters and crackles in hot oil, while the filling stays moist and fragrant. They are served hot as a snack or starter, usually with a tangy chutney or a squeeze of lemon.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g wheat flour
  • 3 tbsp oil for the dough, plus oil for deep-frying
  • 300 g minced beef
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 100 g green peas
  • 1½ tsp garam masala
  • salt, about 80 ml water

Method

  1. Rub the flour, salt and 3 tbsp oil together, add water and knead a firm dough; rest 20 minutes.
  2. Fry the onion, add the beef and cook until browned, then stir in the peas, garam masala and salt and cook 5 minutes; cool.
  3. Divide the dough into 6 balls, roll into ovals, cut in half and fold each half into a cone.
  4. Fill the cones, seal the edges with a little water and press closed.
  5. Deep-fry at about 170 °C for 6–8 minutes, turning, until golden and crisp; drain on paper.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the samosas at 200 °C brushed lightly with oil instead of deep-frying to cut a lot of fat.
  • Use lean minced beef and add more peas to the filling.
  • Count every samosa: one medium samosa is a snack, three are closer to a meal.

History

Filled fried pastries known as sambusak were described in medieval Persian and Arab cookery and are thought to have reached South Asia with traders and cooks from the Middle East and Central Asia. In India the samosa became a staple street food, with meat versions especially common in Muslim cooking traditions. Similar pastries are eaten today from East Africa to the Gulf.
