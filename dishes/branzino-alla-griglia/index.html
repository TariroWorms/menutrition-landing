🐟 Grilled sea bass (branzino alla griglia)

Branzino alla griglia is sea bass cooked whole or butterflied over hot coals or a grill pan, brushed with olive oil and finished with lemon and fresh herbs. The skin chars and crisps, while the white flesh stays moist with a light smoky flavour. It is a seaside restaurant classic, often served simply with a herb and lemon dressing called salmoriglio.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 2 whole sea bass (about 400 g each), cleaned and scaled
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 lemon (juice and wedges)
  • 1 tbsp chopped parsley and 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 sprig of rosemary
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Heat a grill or grill pan until very hot and oil the grate lightly.
  2. Pat the fish dry, make 2 shallow cuts on each side, put rosemary inside and season with salt and pepper.
  3. Brush the fish with 1 tsp of the oil and grill for 5–7 minutes per side without moving it, until the skin is charred and the flesh flakes.
  4. Whisk the remaining oil with lemon juice, parsley and oregano.
  5. Spoon the dressing over the fish and serve with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Grilling needs very little oil — brush it on rather than pouring.
  • Make the dressing with more lemon than oil for the same bright flavour with fewer calories.
  • Serve with grilled vegetables or a tomato salad instead of fries.

History

Grilling whole fish over wood or charcoal is one of the oldest cooking methods on the Italian coast, and sea bass is among the most popular fish for it. Many coastal regions have their own version of the oil, lemon and herb dressing poured over the fish. Today grilled branzino is found on menus across Italy and well beyond.
