🌼 Carciofi alla giudia (Jewish-style artichokes)

Origin: Italy · Rome

Carciofi alla giudia are whole artichokes trimmed, flattened open like a flower and deep-fried twice in olive oil. The outer leaves turn shatteringly crisp and golden, like chips, while the heart stays soft and nutty. They are seasoned only with salt and served hot with lemon wedges as an antipasto.

What it is made of

artichokeolive oillemonsalt

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 4 large round artichokes (Roman type)
  • 1 lemon
  • Olive oil for deep-frying
  • Salt

Method

  1. Remove the tough outer leaves, trim the stem and the tips, and rub the cut surfaces with lemon.
  2. Press each artichoke head-down on the board to open the leaves; season inside with salt.
  3. Fry in olive oil at about 140 °C for 10–15 minutes until tender, then drain.
  4. Raise the oil to about 180 °C and fry again for 1–2 minutes, head-down, until the leaves are crisp and golden.
  5. Drain on paper, salt and serve hot with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Drain the artichokes well on paper towels to remove excess oil.
  • Share one artichoke as a starter rather than eating two.
  • For a lighter version, braise the artichokes instead, Roman-style, with herbs and a little oil.

History

The dish is a specialty of the Roman Jewish community and is closely associated with the restaurants of Rome's old Jewish Ghetto, where it is eaten especially in spring, at the height of the artichoke season.
