Trinidad corn soup

Origin: Trinidad and Tobago

Trinidad corn soup is a thick, golden soup of split peas simmered with pieces of corn on the cob, carrots, potato and small flour dumplings. It tastes hearty and mildly sweet, lifted with garlic, thyme and a touch of coconut milk. It is a popular late-night street food, sold from carts and eaten hot from a cup.

Recipe

6 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g yellow split peas
  • 3 ears of corn, cut in rounds
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 1 potato, diced
  • 150 ml coconut milk
  • 100 g flour for dumplings
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 3 sprigs thyme
  • 1.5 l water, salt

Method

  1. Boil the split peas with garlic and thyme until very soft, about 40 minutes.
  2. Blend part of the peas to thicken the broth.
  3. Add the corn pieces, carrot and potato and simmer 15 minutes.
  4. Mix flour with water and a pinch of salt and roll into small dumplings.
  5. Drop the dumplings into the soup and cook 10 minutes.
  6. Stir in the coconut milk and season to taste.

How to make it lighter

  • Use whole wheat flour for the dumplings.
  • Light coconut milk keeps the soup lower in fat.
  • Add extra vegetables for more fiber.

History

The soup is a staple of Trinidadian street food, especially after parties and carnival nights. It reflects the mix of South Asian and African influences in local cooking, with split peas and dumplings playing a central role.
