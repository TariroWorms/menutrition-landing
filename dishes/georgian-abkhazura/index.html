Abkhazura (meat rolls in caul fat)

Origin: Georgia · Abkhazia

Abkhazura are oblong patties of minced pork and beef seasoned with onion, garlic, coriander, chili and other spices, each wrapped in a thin sheet of caul fat. During grilling the caul melts into a crisp, lacy coating and keeps the meat very juicy. They are served with pomegranate seeds, onion rings, herbs and a sour sauce such as tkemali.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g pork shoulder
  • 500 g beef
  • 300 g caul fat
  • 2 onions (200 g)
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp chili flakes
  • seeds of 1 pomegranate

Method

  1. Mince the pork and beef together with the onion.
  2. Add crushed garlic, salt, coriander, chili and barberry if you have it, and knead until sticky.
  3. Soak the caul fat in warm water and cut it into squares.
  4. Shape the meat into small oblong patties and wrap each one in caul fat.
  5. Grill over embers or in a hot pan for 10–12 minutes, turning, and serve with pomegranate seeds.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef for most of the mix to lower the fat.
  • Grill on a rack so the melted fat drains away.
  • Serve with fresh herbs, cucumbers and tomatoes instead of fries.

History

The name points to Abkhazia, though the dish is widely cooked across Georgia. Wrapping minced meat in caul fat is an old technique found in many cuisines of the Caucasus and beyond.
