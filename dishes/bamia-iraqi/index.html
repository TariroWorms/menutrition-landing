🍲 Iraqi bamia

Iraqi bamia is a stew of lamb and okra in a tangy tomato sauce sharpened with dried lime (noomi basra) and plenty of garlic. The okra stays soft and whole, and the sauce is thick, sour and savoury. It is almost always served with white rice, often as part of a family lunch.

Recipe

4 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g lamb on the bone, cut into pieces
  • 500 g small okra, trimmed
  • 3 tomatoes, grated, and 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 8 garlic cloves, whole
  • 2 dried limes, pierced
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Brown the lamb in the oil, cover with water and simmer for 1 hour, skimming the surface.
  2. Add the tomato paste, tomatoes, dried limes, salt and pepper.
  3. Add the okra and whole garlic and simmer for 25–30 minutes until the sauce thickens.
  4. Taste and adjust the sourness, then serve with white rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean cuts and skim the fat during simmering.
  • Keep the oil to one tablespoon — about 120 kcal for the whole pot.
  • Serve a smaller rice portion and a bigger ladle of stew.

History

Bamia is one of the best-loved everyday stews (maraq) of Iraqi cooking, traditionally eaten with timman, Iraqi white rice.
