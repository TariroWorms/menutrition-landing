Chiu chow fun guo (Teochew steamed dumplings)

Origin: China · Guangdong
285kcal

Fun guo are small crescent dumplings with a glassy, slightly chewy skin made from starch. The filling is savory and crunchy at once, with minced pork, roasted peanuts, chives and bits of dried radish. They are served warm with a dab of chili oil or sweet soy, often at dim sum or as a snack.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g tapioca starch
  • 100 g wheat starch
  • 300 g ground pork
  • 60 g roasted peanuts, chopped
  • 50 g garlic chives
  • 40 g dried radish, minced
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp white pepper

Method

  1. Stir the pork with soy sauce, pepper and a splash of water until sticky.
  2. Mix in the chopped peanuts, chives and dried radish to make the filling.
  3. Pour boiling water over the two starches and knead into a smooth, elastic dough.
  4. Roll the dough into thin circles, spoon in filling and fold into crescents.
  5. Steam on oiled parchment over high heat for 8 to 10 minutes until the skins turn clear.
  6. Serve hot with chili oil or sweet soy sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork and extra chives to lower the fat.
  • Skip the oil brush and steam on parchment for fewer calories.
  • Choose a light soy dip instead of chili oil to cut salt and fat.

History

They belong to the Teochew cooking of eastern Guangdong, where starch-skinned dumplings have long been a teahouse staple. The dish also travelled with Teochew emigrants to Hong Kong and Southeast Asia, where similar dumplings are still made.
