🥖 Shrimp po' boy

Origin: United States · New Orleans

A shrimp po' boy is the classic New Orleans sandwich: a long, crisp-crusted French bread roll filled with deep-fried shrimp, shredded lettuce, sliced tomato and a tangy, spicy remoulade sauce. The contrast of crunchy shrimp, cool vegetables and soft bread is the point, and it is usually eaten "dressed" with all the toppings.

Recipe

2 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g peeled raw shrimp
  • 60 g plain flour mixed with 1 tsp Cajun-style spice
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • Oil for frying
  • 1 French bread loaf (about 30 cm), split
  • 60 g shredded lettuce
  • 1 tomato, sliced
  • 4 tbsp remoulade sauce

Method

  1. Dip the shrimp in beaten egg, then toss in the seasoned flour.
  2. Fry in oil at 180 °C for 2–3 minutes until golden; drain on paper.
  3. Split and lightly toast the bread.
  4. Spread remoulade on both sides, add lettuce and tomato.
  5. Pile on the hot shrimp, close and cut in half to serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the coated shrimp instead of deep-frying to save a lot of oil.
  • Use half the remoulade, or thin it with lemon juice; it is mostly mayonnaise.
  • Hollow out some of the soft bread inside and add more lettuce and tomato.

History

The po' boy (short for "poor boy") is usually credited to Bennie and Clovis Martin, former streetcar conductors who ran a New Orleans sandwich shop; the story goes that during the 1929 streetcar strike they gave free sandwiches to striking workers they called "poor boys". Fried shrimp and fried oyster versions are now among the most popular in Louisiana.
