🍜 Cao lầu

Origin: Vietnam · Hội An

Cao lầu is a noodle dish from Hội An made with thick, chewy yellow noodles topped with slices of marinated roast pork, fresh herbs, bean sprouts and crisp croutons of fried noodle dough. Only a few spoonfuls of rich pork gravy are added, so it is eaten as a dressed noodle bowl rather than a soup. Greens and herbs are mixed in at the table.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g thick cao lầu or udon-style wheat noodles
  • 500 g pork shoulder
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp five-spice, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 200 g bean sprouts
  • Lettuce, mint, Thai basil, coriander
  • A handful of croutons (fried squares of noodle dough or bread)
  • Lime wedges and chilli

Method

  1. Marinate the pork in the soy sauce, five-spice, garlic and sugar for at least 1 hour.
  2. Sear the pork in the oil, add 300 ml water, cover and simmer for 40 minutes until tender. Slice thinly and reduce the liquid to a thin gravy.
  3. Blanch the noodles and bean sprouts briefly and drain.
  4. Put herbs and lettuce in bowls, add the noodles, top with pork, a few spoonfuls of gravy and the croutons. Serve with lime and chilli.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork loin instead of shoulder and trim visible fat.
  • Halve the croutons and add extra herbs and bean sprouts for crunch.
  • Keep the noodle portion modest and pile on the greens.

History

Cao lầu is the signature dish of the old trading port of Hội An in central Vietnam. Local tradition holds that authentic noodles must be made with water from a particular old well and ash-treated water, which gives them their firm bite; such claims are part of local lore. The dish is often said to show influences from the Chinese and Japanese merchants who traded in the town.
