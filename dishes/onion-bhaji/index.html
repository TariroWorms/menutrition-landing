🧅 Onion bhaji

Origin: India · Maharashtra

Onion bhaji are crisp fritters of thinly sliced onion bound in a spiced batter of besan (ground chickpeas) and deep-fried. Green chilli, cumin and fresh coriander season the batter, and the onion strands turn sweet inside a lacy, crunchy shell. They are served hot as a snack or starter, often with chutney.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 3 onions, thinly sliced
  • 120 g besan (ground chickpeas)
  • 1 green chilli, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • Handful of fresh coriander, chopped
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • About 80 ml water
  • Vegetable oil for frying

Method

  1. Mix the onions with the salt and leave for 10 minutes to soften.
  2. Add the besan, chilli, cumin and coriander and just enough water to coat the onions in a thick batter.
  3. Heat the oil to about 175 °C.
  4. Drop in small spoonfuls of the mixture and fry for 3–4 minutes, turning, until deep golden.
  5. Drain on paper towels and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake them on a lined tray at 200 °C, brushed with 1 tbsp oil, instead of deep-frying.
  • Drain well on paper towels to remove surface oil.
  • Count your bhaji and pair two or three with a green salad rather than a full plate.

History

Onion bhaji, also called kanda bhaji or pakora, are a common street and teatime snack across India, especially in Maharashtra and the north. They became a fixture of Indian restaurant menus in the United Kingdom in the second half of the twentieth century.
