Norin (cold noodles with horse meat)

Origin: Uzbekistan

Norin is a cold dish of hand-rolled noodles cut into thin threads and mixed with boiled horse meat or kazy sausage sliced just as finely. The taste is clean and meaty, sharpened by raw onion and black pepper. It is served in small portions on festive tables, often with a cup of hot broth on the side.

What it is made of

horse meathorse-meat sausage (kazy)egg noodlesonionblack pepper

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g horse meat or kazy
  • 300 g flour
  • 1 egg
  • 100 ml water
  • 1 onion
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • salt

Method

  1. Boil the meat in salted water for about 2 hours, then cool and keep the broth.
  2. Knead a stiff dough from flour, egg, water and salt; rest 30 minutes.
  3. Roll thin sheets, boil them briefly, then oil and cool.
  4. Cut the noodle sheets and the meat into very thin strips.
  5. Mix with finely sliced onion, pepper and a little broth; serve cold with hot broth.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef if you want less fat than kazy.
  • Keep the portion small and add a side salad.
  • Cut the onion in advance and rinse it to make it milder.

History

Norin is shared by Uzbek and Kazakh cooking and is closely tied to the steppe tradition of eating horse meat. Preparing it is labour-intensive, so it is usually made for weddings and family gatherings.
