Fettuccine alla panna is one of the simplest Italian cream pastas: ribbon pasta simmered briefly in cream, then finished with grated parmesan and a little nutmeg. The cream reduces into a silky, pale sauce that coats the pasta, with a gentle savoury note from the cheese and warmth from the nutmeg. It is served hot as a first course or light main.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g dried fettuccine
  • 250 ml cooking cream
  • 60 g parmesan, grated
  • a pinch of grated nutmeg
  • salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Cook the fettuccine in salted boiling water until just al dente; keep some pasta water.
  2. Warm the cream in a wide pan with the nutmeg and let it reduce slightly for 2–3 minutes.
  3. Add the pasta and toss for a minute so it absorbs the sauce.
  4. Stir in the parmesan, loosen with pasta water if needed, season and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light cooking cream or replace part of the cream with pasta water.
  • Add steamed peas or spinach to the sauce for more volume on the plate.
  • Keep the parmesan to about 15 g per plate for flavour without a heavy finish.

History

Pasta alla panna is a simple dish of Italian home and trattoria cooking. It is a close relative of fettuccine Alfredo. Today it is a quick family dish in Italy and a common base for variations with mushrooms or peas.
