🍤 Prawn Crackers

Origin: Southeast Asia

Prawn crackers are light, airy crisps made from a dough of ground prawns and tapioca starch that is steamed, sliced thin, dried and then deep-fried. In hot oil the hard discs puff up within seconds into crunchy, melt-in-the-mouth crackers with a gentle seafood flavour. They are served as a snack or alongside rice dishes and curries.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g dried prawn cracker discs (prawn and tapioca starch)
  • 500 ml oil for deep-frying
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a wok or deep pan to about 180 °C.
  2. Drop in a few discs at a time; they will puff up within 3–5 seconds.
  3. Lift them out immediately with a slotted spoon before they brown.
  4. Drain on paper towels, salt lightly and serve once cool and crisp.

How to make it lighter

  • Puff the discs in the microwave for 30–60 seconds without oil for a much lighter cracker.
  • Count out a portion into a small bowl instead of eating from the bag.
  • Drain well on paper towels to remove surface oil.

History

Prawn crackers are widely eaten across Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam and China, where they go by names such as krupuk udang and bánh phồng tôm. They are a standard accompaniment in Southeast Asian restaurants around the world today.
