🧅 French Onion Soup

Soupe à l'oignon is the classic French onion soup: onions are cooked slowly in butter until deep brown and sweet, then simmered in beef broth. The soup is ladled into bowls, topped with a slice of toasted bread and a thick layer of grated Gruyère, and baked until the cheese melts and blisters. The result is sweet, savoury and cheesy, with a soft bread layer soaking up the broth.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg onions, thinly sliced
  • 40 g butter
  • 1.2 l beef broth
  • 1 tsp sugar, 1 tbsp plain flour
  • 2 bay leaves, a few thyme sprigs
  • 4 slices baguette, toasted
  • 120 g Gruyère, grated
  • Salt, pepper

Method

  1. Melt the butter and cook the onions over low to medium heat for 40–50 minutes, stirring often, until deep golden; add the sugar halfway.
  2. Stir in the flour for 1 minute, then add the broth, bay and thyme and simmer 20 minutes; season.
  3. Heat the grill. Ladle the soup into ovenproof bowls.
  4. Top each with a slice of toast and a quarter of the cheese.
  5. Grill 3–5 minutes until the cheese is bubbling and browned.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter and a splash of water to stop the onions from catching.
  • Use a smaller toast and 20 g of cheese per bowl.
  • Choose a fat-skimmed broth — most of the flavour comes from the onions.

History

Onion soups are old peasant food in France, but the gratinéed version with bread and melted cheese is closely associated with the Paris markets of Les Halles, where it was eaten late at night by porters and revellers. The story that King Louis XV invented it is a popular legend rather than established fact.
