Cajuzinho (peanut cashew-shaped sweets)

Origin: Brazil

Cajuzinho means little cashew, and these sweets are moulded to look like the cashew fruit with its nut on top. Ground roasted peanuts are mixed with condensed milk, a little cocoa and butter, shaped into teardrops, rolled in sugar and crowned with half a peanut. They are a staple of Brazilian party tables alongside brigadeiros and beijinhos.

Recipe

25 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g roasted unsalted peanuts, finely ground
  • 395 g sweetened condensed milk
  • 15 g cocoa powder
  • 15 g butter
  • Sugar for rolling
  • 25 peanut halves to decorate

Method

  1. Combine the ground peanuts, condensed milk, cocoa and butter in a pan.
  2. Cook over low heat, stirring, for 8 to 10 minutes until thick.
  3. Spread on a buttered plate and leave to cool.
  4. Shape small pieces into teardrops resembling cashew fruits.
  5. Roll in sugar and press a peanut half into the wide end.
  6. Place in paper cases and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the sugar coating; the sweets hold their shape without it.
  • Use natural peanut butter for part of the peanuts to save time.
  • Keep portions to one or two pieces since they are energy-dense.

History

The cashew is native to northeastern Brazil, but peanuts are cheaper and more widely available, so the sweet borrowed the cashew's shape rather than its nut. It became a common party sweet in the second half of the twentieth century.
