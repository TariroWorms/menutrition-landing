Picaña (grilled rump cap)

Origin: Uruguay

Picaña is the triangular cap of the beef rump, covered by a thick layer of fat. In Uruguay it is grilled whole or in thick steaks with the fat facing the embers, so it bastes the meat as it melts. The result is tender, juicy beef with a crisp, salty edge. It is sliced thin and served with chimichurri or a simple salad.

What it is made of

beef rump capcoarse saltchimichurri

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg beef rump cap with fat
  • Coarse salt
  • Chimichurri to serve

Method

  1. Score the fat in a criss-cross pattern without cutting into the meat.
  2. Rub coarse salt all over.
  3. Grill fat side down over medium embers for 25–30 minutes.
  4. Turn and grill the meat side 15–20 minutes to medium.
  5. Rest 10 minutes, slice thinly against the grain and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook with the fat cap on, then cut it away before eating to save many calories.
  • Serve with a tomato and onion salad rather than potatoes.
  • Thin slices make a 120–150 g portion feel generous.

History

The cut is famous across South America and is known as picanha in Brazil. In Uruguay, a country with a strong cattle and grilling culture, it is a regular at both home asados and parrilla restaurants.
