Colombian mondongo (tripe soup)

Origin: Colombia

Mondongo is a slow-cooked soup of beef tripe cut into strips, simmered until very tender with pork, potato, yuca and carrot in a broth flavoured with hogao, cumin and cilantro. The tripe gives it a soft, gelatinous body and a deep savoury taste. In Antioquia and elsewhere it is a Sunday favourite, served with white rice, avocado, ají and a slice of ripe banana.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g beef tripe, cleaned
  • 400 g pork ribs or belly
  • 500 g potatoes
  • 300 g yuca
  • 1 carrot
  • 1 onion, 1 tomato, 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • Cilantro and lime

Method

  1. Wash the tripe well with lime and salt, then boil it in water for about 2 hours until tender; cut into strips.
  2. In a separate pot simmer the pork in 3 l water for 40 minutes.
  3. Sauté onion, tomato, garlic and cumin into hogao and add to the pork with the tripe.
  4. Add the potatoes, yuca and carrot in chunks and cook 30 minutes more.
  5. Season, add cilantro and serve with rice, avocado and ají.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a pressure cooker to soften the tripe in 40 minutes.
  • Skip the pork belly — tripe alone is lean and protein-rich.
  • Leave out the side of rice to keep the meal light.

History

Tripe soups exist across Spain and Latin America, and Colombia has developed its own style with local roots and tubers. It is a typical dish in traditional restaurants, especially at weekends.
