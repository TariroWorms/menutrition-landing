🥬 Kimchi

Kimchi is fermented napa cabbage seasoned with gochugaru chili flakes, garlic, ginger, scallions and fish sauce. After a few days of fermentation it turns crunchy, sour and spicy, with a deep savoury flavour. In Korea it appears at almost every meal as a side dish, and it is also cooked into stews, fried rice and pancakes.

Recipe

10 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 napa cabbage (about 1.5 kg)
  • 80 g coarse salt
  • 40 g gochugaru
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 20 g fresh ginger
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 4 scallions, sliced

Method

  1. Quarter the cabbage, rub salt between the leaves and leave for 2 hours, turning once.
  2. Rinse the cabbage three times and drain well.
  3. Blend the garlic, ginger, fish sauce and sugar, then mix with the gochugaru and scallions.
  4. Rub the paste between every leaf, wearing gloves.
  5. Pack tightly into a jar, leave at room temperature for 1–2 days, then refrigerate.

How to make it lighter

  • Kimchi is very low in calories, so a generous spoonful adds flavour to plain rice or soup at little cost.
  • Rinse or drain it lightly if you are watching salt.
  • Use it in place of rich sauces to season plain grilled meat or vegetables.

History

Salted and fermented vegetables have been eaten in Korea for many centuries, but red chili, which arrived from the Americas around the 17th century, gave kimchi its familiar colour and heat. The autumn making of kimchi, kimjang, is recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.
