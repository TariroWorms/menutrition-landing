🥩 Milanesa a la napolitana

Origin: Argentina · Buenos Aires

Milanesa a la napolitana is a thin beef cutlet coated in egg and breadcrumbs, fried until crisp, then topped with tomato sauce, a slice of ham and mozzarella and grilled until the cheese melts. It combines a crunchy crust with a soft, cheesy, tangy top. In Argentina and Uruguay it is a classic of neighbourhood restaurants, usually served with French fries or mashed potato.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thin beef cutlets (about 600 g in total)
  • 2 eggs, beaten with a pinch of salt
  • 150 g breadcrumbs
  • 200 ml tomato sauce
  • 4 slices ham
  • 150 g mozzarella, sliced
  • Vegetable oil for frying
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Season the beef, dip each cutlet in the beaten egg, then press firmly into the breadcrumbs.
  2. Fry in hot oil for 2–3 minutes per side until golden; drain on paper towels.
  3. Lay the cutlets on a baking tray, spread with tomato sauce and top with a slice of ham and the mozzarella.
  4. Grill or bake at 220 °C for 5–8 minutes until the cheese melts and bubbles.
  5. Sprinkle with oregano and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the breaded cutlets at 200 °C on a lightly oiled tray instead of frying them.
  • Use less mozzarella or a thin layer of tomato sauce only — cheese and frying oil carry most of the calories.
  • Serve with a green salad instead of fries.

History

The breaded milanesa arrived in the River Plate region with Italian immigrants in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The napolitana topping is usually credited to a Buenos Aires restaurant called Nápoli, the story goes, rather than to Naples itself, though the details are not well documented. Today it is one of the most popular dishes in Argentina.
