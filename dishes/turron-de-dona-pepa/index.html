Turrón de Doña Pepa (anise layer bar)

Origin: Peru

Turrón de Doña Pepa is Lima's best-known October sweet. Sticks of crumbly dough flavoured with anise seed are baked and stacked in layers, each one soaked with a dark, sticky syrup of chancaca cooked with fruit such as quince, pineapple and orange. The top is covered with candy sprinkles and little sweets. It is very sweet and dense, and is cut into small squares to eat with coffee.

Recipe

12 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 200 g butter, 4 egg yolks
  • 2 tbsp anise seeds, boiled in 150 ml water
  • For the syrup: 500 g chancaca, 1 quince, 1 orange and some pineapple, 1 cinnamon stick, cloves
  • colourful sprinkles and small sweets

Method

  1. Strain the anise water and knead it with flour, butter and yolks into a soft dough.
  2. Roll into finger-thick sticks, place them side by side and bake at 180 °C for 20 minutes.
  3. Simmer chancaca with the fruit, cinnamon and cloves until it forms a thick syrup; strain.
  4. Stack three layers of baked sticks in a tray, pouring syrup over each layer.
  5. Finish with syrup and sprinkles, let set for a few hours and cut into squares.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut very small squares; a little goes a long way.
  • Use less syrup on the inner layers.
  • Enjoy it as an occasional festive treat rather than a daily sweet.

History

The sweet is tied to the October processions of the Señor de los Milagros in Lima, when it is sold everywhere. Popular tradition credits its creation to a woman called Josefa, or Doña Pepa, though the details of the story are not documented.
