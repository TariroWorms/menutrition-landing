🍲 Paila marina

Paila marina is a Chilean seafood stew of fish, shrimp, clams and mussels simmered in a flavourful onion broth. It is served in the shallow pot it is cooked in, hot and brothy, with a squeeze of lemon and fresh herbs. It is light, high in protein and low in calories, and a favourite along the coast.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g firm white fish, cut into large chunks
  • 300 g peeled shrimp
  • 500 g clams, cleaned
  • 500 g mussels, cleaned
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1.2 litres fish stock or water
  • Chopped cilantro and parsley, lemon wedges, salt and pepper

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a wide pot and cook the onion and garlic over medium heat for 6–8 minutes until soft.
  2. Add the stock, bring to the boil, season with salt and pepper and simmer for 10 minutes.
  3. Add the fish chunks and the clams and cook, covered, for 4 minutes.
  4. Add the mussels and shrimp, cover and cook 3–4 more minutes until the shells open and the shrimp turn pink; discard any shells that stay closed.
  5. Stir in the cilantro and parsley and serve in deep bowls with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • At about 65 kcal per 100 g this is a very light, protein-rich dish; keep the oil to one spoonful and enjoy the broth.
  • Serve with a slice of bread or a small boiled potato instead of fries or heavy sides.
  • Add extra onion, herbs and lemon for flavour rather than cream or butter.

History

A paila is a shallow pot, and the dish takes its name from the vessel it is cooked and served in. Chile’s long Pacific coastline provides a rich variety of fish and shellfish, and this stew is a staple of coastal kitchens and seafood restaurants. Cooks vary the shellfish with the catch of the day, and the broth is the heart of the dish.
