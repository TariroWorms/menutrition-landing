🥟 Custard bun (nai wong bao)

Origin: China · Hong Kong and Guangdong

Custard buns are soft, fluffy steamed buns filled with a smooth, sweet custard of egg, milk, butter and sugar. The white dough is pillowy and slightly chewy, while the golden filling is rich and gently set. They are a classic dim sum item, served warm in bamboo steamers with tea.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g plain flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 30 g sugar for the dough
  • 160 ml warm milk for the dough
  • 3 eggs
  • 80 g sugar for the filling
  • 150 ml milk for the filling
  • 30 g butter
  • 2 tbsp flour for the filling

Method

  1. Mix the 300 g flour, yeast, 30 g sugar and warm milk into a smooth dough and leave to rise for 1 hour.
  2. Whisk the eggs, sugar, milk and 2 tbsp flour, then steam or stir over low heat until set into a thick custard.
  3. Beat in the butter while warm, chill until firm and divide into 12 balls.
  4. Divide the dough into 12, flatten each piece, wrap around a custard ball and pinch closed.
  5. Place on squares of baking paper, rest for 15 minutes, then steam over high heat for 10–12 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • One bun is a natural portion; pair it with unsweetened tea rather than a sweet drink.
  • Cut the filling sugar to 60 g; the custard still tastes sweet.
  • Make smaller buns from the same dough to get 16 portions instead of 12.

History

Nai wong bao belongs to the Cantonese dim sum tradition of Guangdong and Hong Kong, where steamed sweet buns are a teahouse staple. Today they are found on dim sum menus across China and in Chinese restaurants worldwide.
