🍮 Banana pudding

Origin: United States · the South

Banana pudding is a chilled layered dessert of sliced bananas, vanilla custard and soft sponge biscuits, finished with whipped cream. As it rests in the fridge, the biscuits soak up the custard and turn cake-like, while the bananas stay soft and sweet. It is spooned out of a big dish or served in individual glasses, and is a fixture at potlucks, barbecues and family gatherings.

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 4 egg yolks
  • 100 g sugar
  • 30 g cornstarch
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 4 ripe bananas, sliced
  • 200 g ladyfingers
  • 250 ml whipping cream, whipped with 1 tbsp sugar

Method

  1. Whisk the yolks, sugar and cornstarch in a bowl. Heat the milk until steaming, then whisk it slowly into the yolks.
  2. Return everything to the pan and stir over medium heat until the custard thickens and bubbles, 3–5 minutes. Stir in the vanilla and cool for 15 minutes.
  3. In a dish, layer ladyfingers, banana slices and custard; repeat twice, ending with custard.
  4. Spread the whipped cream on top, cover and chill for at least 4 hours before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve it in small glasses: a 100 g portion instead of a large scoop makes a real difference.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk for the custard and a thinner layer of whipped cream on top.
  • Add more banana and fewer ladyfingers per layer to keep the sweetness while lowering the calories.

History

Banana pudding is closely associated with the American South, where recipes began appearing in cookbooks and magazines in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as bananas became cheap and widely available. Today it is a classic of Southern home cooking and barbecue restaurants across the United States.
