Zhoug, or sahawiq, is a fiery Yemeni green relish of fresh cilantro, green chilies and garlic, ground with cumin and cardamom and loosened with olive oil. It is bright, herby and very hot. A spoonful is served with almost everything: bread, eggs, grilled meat, soups and rice dishes.

What it is made of

cilantrogreen chiligarliccumincardamomolive oil

Recipe

8 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 1 large bunch cilantro (about 60 g)
  • 4–6 green chilies
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt
  • 1–2 tbsp water

Method

  1. Roughly chop the cilantro and chilies; remove seeds for a milder zhoug.
  2. Blend or pound with the garlic, cumin, cardamom and salt.
  3. Add the olive oil and a little water and pulse into a coarse paste.
  4. Taste for salt and heat and store in a jar in the fridge for up to a week.

How to make it lighter

  • Use it in place of creamy sauces: a spoonful adds a lot of flavour.
  • Reduce the oil and add more water or lemon juice for a lighter relish; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Stick to 1–2 tbsp (about 20 g) per serving, the standard condiment amount.

History

Zhoug comes from Yemen, where it is traditionally pounded on a grinding stone and eaten daily. Yemeni Jewish communities brought it to Israel, where it is now a common condiment at falafel and shawarma stands.
