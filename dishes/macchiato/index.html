A macchiato, or caffè macchiato, is a single shot of espresso 'stained' with a small spoonful of foamed milk. It keeps the strong, bitter intensity of espresso while the foam softens the first sip. Served in a small espresso cup, it is a quick coffee taken at the bar, especially in the afternoon when Italians usually skip larger milky drinks.

Recipe

1 servings5 min

Ingredients

  • 1 shot espresso (about 30 ml)
  • 1–2 tbsp milk, foamed
  • Sugar to taste (optional)

Method

  1. Brew a single shot of espresso into a small warmed cup.
  2. Steam or froth the milk until it has a fine foam.
  3. Spoon a little foam onto the centre of the espresso and serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • A plain macchiato is one of the lightest coffee drinks; most calories come from added sugar, about 4 kcal per gram.
  • Skip syrups and flavoured toppings to keep it light.
  • If you want a bigger drink, add another shot of espresso rather than more milk.

History

Caffè macchiato comes from Italian espresso bar culture; macchiato means 'stained' or 'marked', after the dot of milk on top. The name is said to have helped baristas tell it apart from a plain espresso. It should not be confused with the much larger layered latte macchiato.
