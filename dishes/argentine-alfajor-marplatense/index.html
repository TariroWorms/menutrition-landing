Alfajor marplatense (chocolate alfajor)

Origin: Argentina

The alfajor marplatense is the chocolate-coated alfajor linked to the seaside city of Mar del Plata. Two soft, cake-like cookies flavoured with honey and cocoa enclose a thick layer of dulce de leche, and the whole sweet is dipped in dark or milk chocolate. It is dense, rich and very sweet, and boxes of them are a classic gift brought back from holidays on the coast.

Recipe

12 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g flour
  • 30 g cocoa powder
  • 100 g butter
  • 100 g sugar
  • 80 g honey
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 400 g dulce de leche
  • 300 g dark chocolate for coating

Method

  1. Cream the butter, sugar and honey, then beat in the egg.
  2. Sift in the flour, cocoa and baking soda and mix to a soft dough.
  3. Roll to 6 mm, cut 6 cm rounds and bake at 180 °C for 8–10 minutes.
  4. Cool, then sandwich the rounds in pairs with a thick layer of dulce de leche.
  5. Melt the chocolate and dip each alfajor to cover completely.
  6. Let them set on a rack until the chocolate is firm.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose dark chocolate with at least 60% cocoa.
  • Cut each alfajor in half and share it.
  • Make mini versions or coat only the top in chocolate.

History

Mar del Plata became a major beach resort in the twentieth century, and local confectioners turned chocolate alfajores into a souvenir that holidaymakers took home. The style spread across Argentina and is now produced by large brands and small shops alike. Argentines eat alfajores as an everyday snack at kiosks.
