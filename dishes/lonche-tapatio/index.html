Lonche tapatío (Guadalajara pork sandwich)

Origin: Mexico · Jalisco

A lonche is Guadalajara's everyday sandwich, built on the birote, a crusty, slightly sour local roll. It is filled with sliced roast pork leg, refried beans, avocado, onion and pickled jalapeños, and often finished with a spoonful of mild tomato sauce. It is a quick lunch or snack sold at corner stands across the city.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 4 birote or crusty bread rolls (80 g each)
  • 400 g roast pork leg, sliced
  • 200 g refried beans
  • 1 avocado
  • 1/2 onion (60 g)
  • pickled jalapeños, salt

Method

  1. Split the rolls and lightly toast the cut sides.
  2. Spread the bottoms with warm refried beans.
  3. Layer on the sliced pork leg.
  4. Add avocado slices, thin onion rings and pickled jalapeños.
  5. Close the rolls, press gently and serve straight away.

How to make it lighter

  • Use beans cooked without lard.
  • Scoop out some of the soft crumb to cut bread calories.
  • Add lettuce and tomato for extra freshness.

History

The name comes from the English word 'lunch'. The birote roll is said to have arrived with French bakers in the 19th century and is considered hard to reproduce outside Guadalajara, which gives the lonche its local character.
