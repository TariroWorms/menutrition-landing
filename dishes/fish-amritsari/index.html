🐟 Amritsari fish

Origin: India · Punjab, Amritsar

Amritsari fish is a Punjabi street snack of firm white fish pieces coated in a spiced besan (chickpea) batter and deep-fried until crisp. Carom seeds (ajwain), red chili and lemon give it a sharp, peppery, tangy flavour, while the fish inside stays moist. It is served hot with lemon wedges, sliced onion and green chutney.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g firm white fish fillets, cut into pieces
  • 80 g besan (ground chickpeas)
  • 1 tsp carom seeds (ajwain)
  • 1–2 tsp red chili powder
  • 2 lemons
  • 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • Salt, oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Rub the fish with the juice of 1 lemon and a little salt and leave for 15 minutes.
  2. Mix the besan, carom seeds, chili powder, ginger-garlic paste and salt with a few spoons of water into a thick batter.
  3. Coat the fish pieces in the batter.
  4. Fry in oil at 180 °C for 4–5 minutes until crisp and golden; drain on paper.
  5. Serve with the remaining lemon in wedges and sliced onion.

How to make it lighter

  • Shallow-fry or air-fry the coated fish instead of deep-frying.
  • Keep the batter thin so less oil clings to it.
  • Fill half the plate with onion, cucumber and salad.

History

The dish takes its name from Amritsar in Punjab, where fried fish stalls are a well-known part of the city's food streets. It is especially popular in the winter months across North India.
