Jangjorim (soy-braised beef)

Origin: South Korea

Jangjorim is a classic banchan of lean beef simmered slowly in sweet soy sauce until it can be torn into strips. Hard-boiled eggs and sometimes garlic or chili peppers soak in the same sauce. It is salty-sweet and keeps for days, so it is a lunchbox favorite eaten with rice.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g beef shank or brisket
  • 6 hard-boiled eggs
  • 100 ml soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp sugar (25 g)
  • 8 garlic cloves
  • 20 g ginger
  • 1 onion (150 g)

Method

  1. Soak the beef in cold water for 30 minutes to draw out blood.
  2. Boil it with onion and ginger in 1 litre of water for 40 minutes.
  3. Remove the beef and keep 400 ml of broth.
  4. Simmer the broth with soy sauce, sugar, garlic and the beef for 20 minutes.
  5. Add the peeled eggs and cook 10 minutes, then tear the beef into strips.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less sugar and low-sodium soy sauce.
  • Choose lean shank and skim any fat off the broth.
  • Serve small portions as a flavorful side, with extra vegetables.

History

The word jorim means to braise down in seasoning, and this is one of the best-known examples. Because the strong sauce preserves the meat, it was practical for Korean households before refrigeration.
