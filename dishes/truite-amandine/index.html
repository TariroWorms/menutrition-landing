Trout Amandine

Origin: France

Trout amandine is a classic French fish dish: trout fillets pan-fried in butter until golden, then topped with flaked almonds toasted in the same butter, a squeeze of lemon and chopped parsley. The fish is delicate and flaky, while the almonds add crunch and a nutty, browned-butter flavour. It is usually served hot with boiled potatoes or green vegetables.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 2 trout fillets (about 300 g)
  • 30 g butter
  • 30 g flaked almonds
  • 1/2 lemon, juiced
  • 1 tbsp chopped parsley
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Pat the trout dry and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Melt half the butter in a pan and cook the fillets skin side down for 3–4 minutes, then turn for 1–2 minutes; transfer to plates.
  3. Add the remaining butter and the almonds to the pan and cook until golden.
  4. Stir in the lemon juice and parsley and spoon over the fish.

How to make it lighter

  • Use about 10 g butter per portion; most of the fat in the dish comes from butter and almonds.
  • Keep the almonds to about 10–15 g per plate.
  • Serve with steamed green beans or a salad instead of fried potatoes.

History

Fish served 'amandine', with almonds browned in butter, is a technique of classic French cuisine. Trout amandine also became a signature dish of New Orleans restaurants in the 20th century.
