🍵 Lebanese mint tea

Origin: Lebanon · Levant

Lebanese mint tea is black tea brewed strong and steeped with a generous handful of fresh mint sprigs, then sweetened with sugar. It is hot, sweet and fragrant, with the freshness of mint softening the tea's bitterness. It is poured into small glass cups and served to guests, after meals and through long evenings of conversation.

What it is made of

black teamintsugarwater

Recipe

4 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 1 litre water
  • 3 tsp loose black tea or 4 tea bags
  • 1 large handful fresh mint sprigs
  • 3–4 tbsp sugar, to taste

Method

  1. Bring the water to the boil and add the tea; simmer gently for 2 minutes or steep for 4 minutes.
  2. Add the mint sprigs and sugar, stir and leave to steep for 3–4 minutes off the heat.
  3. Strain into small glass cups and serve hot with a fresh mint leaf in each glass.

How to make it lighter

  • Sweeten with less sugar — 1 tsp is about 16 kcal — or serve the sugar on the side.
  • Add more mint for flavour so less sweetness is needed.
  • Drink it unsweetened between meals as a calorie-free option.

History

Black tea with mint is drunk across the Levant, and in Lebanon it is a common gesture of hospitality at home and in cafés.
