🥘 Red-braised pork belly (hong shao rou)

Origin: China · Hunan / Shanghai

Hong shao rou is Chinese red-braised pork belly: cubes of pork are browned with caramelised sugar, then slowly braised with soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, ginger and star anise until the fat melts and the sauce turns glossy and dark. The meat is meltingly soft, sweet and savoury. It is served with plain steamed rice and a green vegetable.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g skin-on pork belly, in 3 cm cubes
  • 30 g rock sugar or white sugar
  • 3 tbsp Shaoxing wine
  • 2 tbsp light soy sauce and 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
  • 4 slices ginger
  • 2 star anise
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 500 ml hot water

Method

  1. Blanch the pork in boiling water for 3 minutes, drain and pat dry.
  2. Heat the oil with the sugar over low heat until it melts into an amber caramel.
  3. Add the pork and stir to coat, then add the ginger, star anise, wine and both soy sauces.
  4. Pour in the hot water, cover and simmer gently for 60–90 minutes until very tender.
  5. Uncover and boil, stirring, until the sauce reduces to a sticky glaze.

How to make it lighter

  • Pork belly is very fatty: serve 3–4 pieces per person with lots of greens.
  • Chill the braise and lift off the solid fat before reheating.
  • Choose a leaner piece of belly or mix in some pork shoulder.

History

Red braising is an old Chinese technique, and hong shao rou is cooked in many regional styles, notably in Shanghai and Hunan. The Hunan version is widely associated with Mao Zedong, who is said to have loved the dish, which is why it is sometimes called Chairman Mao's pork.
