🦆 Duck confit with lentils

Duck confit with lentils is a hearty French bistro dish: duck legs slowly cooked in their own fat until tender, then crisped and served on green lentils braised with carrot, onion and thyme. The rich, salty meat and the earthy, soft lentils balance each other. It is a classic cold-weather main course served straight from the pan.

Recipe

4 servings70 min

Ingredients

  • 4 duck confit legs
  • 300 g green lentils, rinsed
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 sprigs thyme and 1 bay leaf
  • 750 ml water or light stock
  • Salt, pepper, chopped parsley

Method

  1. Put the lentils, carrot, onion, thyme and bay leaf in a pan, cover with the water and simmer for 25–30 minutes until tender but whole. Season at the end.
  2. Meanwhile warm the duck legs skin side down in a dry pan for 10 minutes, then roast at 200 °C for 15–20 minutes until the skin is crisp.
  3. Spoon the lentils onto plates, set a duck leg on top and finish with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook the lentils without adding any duck fat — they are full of flavour already.
  • Remove the skin from the leg to cut a large share of the fat.
  • Make the lentils the bigger part of the plate and add a green salad.

History

Confit comes from southwest France, especially Gascony, where salting meat and cooking it slowly in fat was a traditional way to preserve it through the winter. Pairing confit with lentils or beans is common in the region's farmhouse cooking. Today duck confit is a staple of bistros across France.
