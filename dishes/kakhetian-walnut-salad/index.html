Kakhetian tomato and cucumber salad with walnut paste

Origin: Georgia · Kakheti

This richer cousin of the everyday Georgian salad comes from Kakheti, in the east of the country. Instead of a few chopped nuts, the vegetables are tossed with a smooth paste of ground walnuts, garlic, herbs and a little vinegar, then drizzled with unrefined Kakhetian sunflower oil, known for its deep, roasted-seed aroma. The result is creamy, nutty and tangy, and it is often served with mtsvadi or fresh bread.

What it is made of

tomatocucumberwalnutsKakhetian sunflower oilgarliccilantro

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g tomatoes
  • 300 g cucumbers
  • 80 g walnuts
  • 1-2 garlic cloves
  • 1 small bunch cilantro
  • 1 tbsp wine vinegar
  • 1 tbsp unrefined Kakhetian sunflower oil, salt, a little blue fenugreek

Method

  1. Grind the walnuts with garlic, salt and fenugreek into a paste.
  2. Loosen the paste with vinegar and 2-3 tablespoons of water until creamy.
  3. Cut the tomatoes and cucumbers into chunks.
  4. Toss the vegetables with the walnut paste and chopped cilantro.
  5. Drizzle with Kakhetian oil just before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the walnuts and more water for a lighter dressing.
  • Skip the extra oil – the walnut paste is already rich.
  • Serve as a side for grilled lean meat or fish instead of bread.

History

Kakheti is famous for its unrefined sunflower oil, pressed from roasted seeds, which many Georgians consider essential for a proper tomato salad. Walnut paste is one of the cornerstones of Georgian cooking and is used in sauces, vegetable dishes and salads alike.
