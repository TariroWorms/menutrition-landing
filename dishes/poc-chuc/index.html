Poc chuc (Yucatecan citrus-grilled pork)

Origin: Mexico · Yucatán

Poc chuc is thinly sliced pork leg or loin marinated in sour orange juice with garlic and salt, then grilled quickly over hot charcoal. The citrus tenderises the meat and gives it a bright, tangy edge against the smoke. It comes with charred red onion, a roasted tomato salsa called chiltomate, black beans, avocado and warm tortillas.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork leg or loin, sliced 5 mm thick
  • 250 ml sour orange juice (or 2:1 orange and lime juice)
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 red onions, halved
  • 4 tomatoes
  • 1 habanero (optional)
  • cilantro and tortillas to serve

Method

  1. Season the pork with salt and garlic and cover with the citrus juice.
  2. Marinate for 30 minutes to 2 hours in the fridge.
  3. Grill the onions and tomatoes until blackened; chop the tomatoes with cilantro and salt for chiltomate.
  4. Grill the pork over very hot coals for 2–3 minutes per side.
  5. Slice into strips.
  6. Serve with charred onion, chiltomate, beans and tortillas.

How to make it lighter

  • Use loin and trim all visible fat.
  • Serve with a big salad and fewer tortillas.
  • Skip the lard often added to the beans.

History

The name comes from Maya words describing meat toasted over coals. The citrus marinade is widely linked to the restaurant Los Almendros in Ticul, which popularised the dish in the twentieth century, though grilling salted pork over coals is older.
