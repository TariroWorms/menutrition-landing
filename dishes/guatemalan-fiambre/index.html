Fiambre

Origin: Guatemala

Fiambre is a huge cold salad made with dozens of ingredients, including cold cuts, sausages, cheese, pickled vegetables, beets, olives and sometimes chicken, all tossed in a sweet-and-sour dressing. It is colorful, tangy and rich. Families prepare it for All Saints' Day on 1 November and share it at the cemetery or at home.

Recipe

12 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g beets, cooked
  • 200 g ham and cold cuts
  • 200 g sausage
  • 150 g cheese
  • 200 g mixed pickles
  • 150 g green beans
  • 2 carrots, cooked
  • 100 g olives
  • vinegar, oil, sugar, mustard, salt

Method

  1. Cook the beets, carrots and green beans and cut into pieces.
  2. Slice the meats and cheese.
  3. Make a dressing of vinegar, oil, sugar, mustard and salt.
  4. Marinate the cooked vegetables in the dressing for several hours.
  5. Layer the vegetables, meats, pickles and olives on a platter.
  6. Top with cheese and serve cold.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean meats and less sausage.
  • Increase the vegetable share.
  • Use less oil and sugar in the dressing.

History

Fiambre is tied to Guatemala's Día de los Muertos and All Saints' traditions, when families remember the dead with food. Every household has its own version, and the number of ingredients is part of its pride.
