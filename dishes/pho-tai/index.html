🍜 Pho tai (rare beef pho)

Origin: Vietnam · Hanoi

Pho tai is beef pho topped with paper-thin slices of raw beef that cook in the bowl when the boiling broth is poured over them, staying pink and tender. The broth is simmered from beef bones with star anise and cinnamon and seasoned with fish sauce. It is served over flat rice noodles, usually with herbs, bean sprouts and lime on the side.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg beef bones and 300 g beef brisket
  • 300 g beef eye of round or sirloin, partly frozen and sliced paper-thin
  • 400 g flat rice noodles
  • 1 onion and a 5 cm piece of ginger, charred
  • 3 star anise and 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce, salt and a little sugar
  • Herbs and lime wedges to serve

Method

  1. Blanch and rinse the bones, then simmer with the brisket, charred onion and ginger, star anise and cinnamon in 3 litres of water for 3 hours, skimming often.
  2. Strain the broth, slice the brisket and season the broth with fish sauce, salt and a little sugar.
  3. Cook the noodles and divide into bowls with the brisket.
  4. Lay the raw beef slices on top.
  5. Bring the broth to a rolling boil and pour it straight over the raw beef so it cooks; serve at once with herbs and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Lean eye of round keeps this one of the lighter pho choices.
  • Leave out the brisket and use only the rare beef.
  • Swap some noodles for herbs and extra broth.

History

Tái means 'rare' in Vietnamese, and this is one of the most popular ways to order pho in Hanoi. Pho itself emerged in northern Vietnam in the early 20th century and spread worldwide with the Vietnamese diaspora.
