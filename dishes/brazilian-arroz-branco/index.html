Arroz branco (Brazilian white rice)

Origin: Brazil

Arroz branco is the plain white rice served with almost every Brazilian lunch. The raw grains are first fried briefly in oil with garlic and onion, then simmered in hot water until the water is absorbed. This gives separate, fluffy grains with a gentle savoury aroma. It is the base of the classic plate of rice and beans, eaten with meat, salad and farofa.

What it is made of

ricegarliconionoilsalt

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g long-grain white rice
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 onion, chopped
  • 600 ml boiling water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs nearly clear and drain well.
  2. Heat oil and soften the onion and garlic for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the rice and stir for 2 minutes until the grains look opaque.
  4. Pour in boiling water, add salt and bring back to a boil.
  5. Cover, lower the heat and cook 15 minutes until the water is absorbed.
  6. Rest 5 minutes covered, then fluff with a fork.

How to make it lighter

  • Use one tablespoon of oil instead of two; the flavour stays.
  • Mix in some brown rice for more fibre.
  • Keep the portion to a small scoop and fill the plate with beans and salad.

History

Rice and beans together form the backbone of the Brazilian daily diet across all regions and social groups. The technique of sautéing rice before boiling it is inherited from Portuguese cooking.
