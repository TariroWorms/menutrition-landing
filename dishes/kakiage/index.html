🍤 Kakiage

Kakiage is a type of Japanese tempura in which thin strips of vegetables such as onion, carrot and burdock are mixed with small shrimp, bound with a light flour-and-egg batter and fried into crisp, lacy fritters. It is crunchy outside and tender inside. Kakiage is served on its own with salt, on top of rice as kakiage-don, or over udon and soba.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 carrot, cut into matchsticks
  • 100 g small peeled shrimp
  • 70 g flour
  • 1 egg
  • 120 ml ice-cold water
  • Oil for deep-frying, salt

Method

  1. Mix the onion, carrot and shrimp in a bowl and dust with 1 tbsp of the flour.
  2. Whisk the egg with the ice-cold water, then briefly stir in the remaining flour; lumps are fine.
  3. Pour the batter over the vegetables and toss just to coat.
  4. Heat the oil to 170 °C. Slide in spoonfuls of the mixture, flattening them slightly, and fry for 2–3 minutes per side until crisp.
  5. Drain on a rack and serve hot with a pinch of salt.

How to make it lighter

  • Deep-fried batter absorbs oil: drain fritters well on a rack and keep them thin and small.
  • Use more vegetables and shrimp and less batter for a lighter fritter.
  • Have one kakiage with a big bowl of plain broth or salad instead of over rice.

History

Kakiage developed alongside tempura, which grew popular in Edo-period Japan as street food. It is a thrifty way to use small pieces of vegetables and seafood and remains a standard topping in noodle shops today.
