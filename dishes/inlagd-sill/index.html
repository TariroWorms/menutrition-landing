Pickled herring (Inlagd sill)

Origin: Sweden

Inlagd sill is herring cured in a sweet-and-sour brine of vinegar, sugar and spices such as allspice and bay. The fish is tender and tangy with a gentle sweetness. It is eaten cold on rye bread or with boiled new potatoes, sour cream and chives.

What it is made of

herringvinegarsugaronionallspicepotato

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g herring in vinegar brine
  • 100 ml white vinegar
  • 80 g sugar
  • 100 ml water
  • 1 red onion
  • 2 g allspice, 1 bay leaf
  • 1 carrot
  • 500 g potatoes
  • 10 g fresh dill and chives, sour cream to serve

Method

  1. Rinse the herring fillets and cut them into pieces.
  2. Heat vinegar, water, sugar, allspice and bay leaf until the sugar dissolves, then cool.
  3. Layer the herring with sliced onion and carrot in a jar.
  4. Cover with the cooled brine and refrigerate for at least 24 hours.
  5. Serve with boiled potatoes, sour cream, chives and dill.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less sugar in the brine for a lower-sugar version.
  • Serve with plenty of vegetables and a small portion of potato.
  • Choose a small portion if you are watching salt intake.

History

Pickled herring is the centrepiece of Swedish celebration tables at Midsummer and Christmas, where several flavours are served together. Pickling was a practical way to preserve the abundant Baltic herring.
