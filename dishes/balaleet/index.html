Balaleet is a Gulf breakfast of thin vermicelli cooked with sugar and perfumed with cardamom, saffron and rosewater, served under a thin, savoury omelette. The noodles are soft and fragrant, and the egg on top balances their sweetness. It is popular for weekend breakfasts, Eid mornings and during Ramadan.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g thin vermicelli
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 80 g sugar
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • A pinch of saffron soaked in 2 tbsp warm water
  • 1 tbsp rosewater
  • 400 ml water
  • 3 eggs, beaten with a pinch of salt

Method

  1. Toast the vermicelli in 1 tbsp oil until lightly golden.
  2. Add the water and cook until absorbed and the noodles are soft.
  3. Stir in the sugar, cardamom, saffron water and rosewater; cover and keep warm on low heat.
  4. Cook the eggs in the remaining oil as a thin omelette.
  5. Serve the vermicelli topped with the omelette.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut the sugar to 40–50 g; the saffron and rosewater still carry the flavour.
  • Add an extra egg white to the omelette for more protein in the same portion.
  • Toast the vermicelli in one tablespoon of oil (about 120 kcal) rather than more.

History

Balaleet is a traditional dish of the Emirates and the wider Gulf, including Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait and Oman, where it remains a classic festive breakfast.
