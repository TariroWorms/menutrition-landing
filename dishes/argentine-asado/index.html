Asado (Argentine mixed grill)

Asado is the Argentine way of grilling, and also the name of the long gathering around it. Chorizo and morcilla come off the grill first, followed by offal and then the big beef cuts that cook slowly over low embers. The meat tastes deeply of smoke and beef, with little more than salt. Chimichurri, bread and a simple salad are passed around the table.

What it is made of

beef short ribsbeef flankchorizo sausageblood sausagecoarse saltchimichurri

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef short ribs (tira de asado)
  • 800 g beef flank (vacío)
  • 4 chorizo sausages
  • 4 morcilla (blood sausages)
  • Coarse salt
  • 150 ml chimichurri

Method

  1. Burn hardwood or charcoal to embers and spread them in an even layer under the grill.
  2. Salt the beef cuts generously with coarse salt shortly before grilling.
  3. Put the beef on the grill bone or fat side down over gentle heat and cook for 1.5–2 hours, turning once.
  4. Add chorizo and morcilla for the last 30–40 minutes and serve them first as a starter.
  5. Rest the beef for a few minutes, slice and serve with chimichurri.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose leaner cuts such as flank or tenderloin and skip the sausages to cut fat sharply.
  • Fill half the plate with a tomato and onion salad or grilled vegetables.
  • Keep portions to about 200 g of meat and go easy on bread.

History

Grilling over wood embers grew out of the cattle culture of the Pampas and the gauchos who cooked beef in the open. Today the Sunday asado with family or friends is one of the strongest social traditions in Argentina, and the person tending the fire is called the asador.
