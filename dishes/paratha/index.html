🫓 Paratha

Origin: South Asia · India and Pakistan

Paratha is an unleavened whole wheat flatbread that is folded with ghee and rolled again, so it puffs into flaky, layered sheets on a hot griddle. It is crisp at the edges, soft and chewy inside, with a buttery aroma. Parathas are eaten at breakfast with tea or alongside curries and dals for lunch and dinner.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g whole wheat flour (atta)
  • 180 ml water
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 3 tbsp ghee, melted

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt and water into a soft dough, knead 5 minutes and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Divide into 4 balls, roll each into a thin circle, brush with ghee and fold into a triangle or spiral.
  3. Roll the folded dough out again to about 15 cm.
  4. Cook on a hot griddle 1–2 minutes per side, brushing lightly with ghee until golden and flaky.

How to make it lighter

  • Brush with a thin film of ghee instead of spooning it on: 1 tbsp ghee ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Roll the parathas smaller and count one as a portion next to a vegetable curry.
  • Pair with a protein-rich dal rather than another starch to keep the meal balanced.

History

Paratha belongs to the wheat-growing north of the Indian subcontinent, where layered griddle breads have been made for centuries. The name is usually explained as a combination of words for 'layers' and 'flour'. Today it is a daily bread across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and in South Asian communities worldwide.
