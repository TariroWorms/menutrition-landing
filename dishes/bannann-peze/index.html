Bannann peze (twice-fried green plantain)

Origin: Haiti

Bannann peze are thick discs of green plantain that are fried once to soften, pressed flat, briefly dipped in salted garlic water and fried again. They come out golden, crunchy outside and slightly starchy inside. They are the standard side for griot, fried fish and pikliz.

What it is made of

Green plantainoilsaltgarliclime

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 3 green plantains
  • 500 ml oil for frying
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 250 ml warm water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 lime

Method

  1. Peel the plantains and cut into thick rounds.
  2. Fry in medium-hot oil for 3 to 4 minutes until pale gold.
  3. Drain and press each piece flat with a plate or tortilla press.
  4. Dip briefly in the garlic and salt water.
  5. Fry again in hotter oil until golden and crisp.
  6. Drain, sprinkle with salt and serve with lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Air-fry or bake the pressed pieces with a spray of oil.
  • Drain well on paper to remove surface oil.
  • Share a portion and add a salad.

History

Bannann peze is a fixture of Haitian street food and family meals. Similar twice-fried plantains exist across Latin America and the Caribbean under other names.
