Saltah is Yemen’s national dish: a bubbling stew of lamb broth with potato, tomato and rice, served in a scorching stone pot and crowned with a frothy, slightly bitter fenugreek foam called hulba. An egg is often cracked into the stew and a spicy green chili and cilantro relish is stirred in. It is thick, savoury, aromatic and eaten piping hot at lunchtime.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 litre lamb broth
  • 2 tbsp ground fenugreek, soaked in water for 2 hours
  • 2 potatoes, cubed
  • 3 tomatoes, grated
  • 60 g cooked rice
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 green chilies
  • 1 bunch cilantro
  • 1 onion, chopped, 1 tbsp oil, salt

Method

  1. Whip the soaked fenugreek with a little cold water until it turns into a pale, airy foam; set aside.
  2. For the relish, blend 1 chili and half the cilantro with a pinch of salt.
  3. Fry the onion in the oil, add the tomatoes and cook for 5 minutes, then add the broth and potatoes and simmer for 20 minutes.
  4. Stir in the rice and the remaining chopped chili and cilantro and bring to a fierce boil in a heavy pot.
  5. Crack in the eggs and stir lightly until just set, spoon the fenugreek foam on top and serve bubbling with the relish.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat from the lamb broth after chilling it — the stew becomes much lighter.
  • Use one egg instead of two and add extra tomato and potato for volume.
  • Keep the rice amount small; the broth and fenugreek foam are filling on their own.

History

Saltah is closely associated with Sana’a and the northern highlands, where it is the classic midday meal, traditionally served in a heavy stone pot (maqla). The name is often linked to the Turkish word for salad, a trace of Ottoman presence in Yemen, though this origin is not certain.
