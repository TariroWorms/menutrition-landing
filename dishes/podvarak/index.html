🥬 Podvarak

Podvarak is a Serbian winter dish of shredded sauerkraut baked slowly with pork, sweet paprika and bay leaf. The long time in the oven softens the cabbage and mellows its sourness, while the pork releases its juices into it, giving a tangy, smoky and deeply savoury result. It is served hot straight from the baking dish, often as a main course in cold months.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg sauerkraut, shredded
  • 600 g pork shoulder or ribs, in pieces
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tbsp sweet paprika
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Black pepper and 250 ml water

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180 °C. Rinse the sauerkraut briefly if very salty and squeeze it.
  2. Brown the pork in the oil, then add the onion and cook 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in the paprika off the heat, then mix with the sauerkraut, bay leaves and pepper.
  4. Spread in a baking dish, pour in the water and cover with foil.
  5. Bake 1½–2 hours, removing the foil for the last 20 minutes to brown the top.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the pork or use lean pork loin.
  • Use a larger share of sauerkraut and less meat per plate; the cabbage is very low in calories.
  • Measure the oil: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Podvarak draws on the Balkan tradition of fermenting whole cabbages in autumn to last through the winter. Today it is a common home and tavern dish in Serbia and is also cooked in neighbouring countries.
