Patarashca (fish grilled in leaves)

Origin: Peru · Amazon

Patarashca is a whole or filleted river fish seasoned with garlic, cumin, sweet ají, onion, tomato and sacha culantro, then wrapped in bijao leaves and placed over hot coals. The leaves char on the outside while the fish steams in its own juices and the vegetables, keeping it moist and fragrant. It is served straight from the opened leaf with boiled yuca or plantain.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g firm white fish fillets or 4 small whole fish
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed, 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 red onion, sliced
  • 2 tomatoes, sliced
  • 1 sweet pepper, in strips
  • 1 bunch sacha culantro or cilantro
  • 8 bijao or banana leaves
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt, lime

Method

  1. Season the fish with garlic, cumin, salt and lime and leave 15 minutes.
  2. Soften the leaves over a flame.
  3. Lay fish on two crossed leaves and cover with onion, tomato, pepper and herbs.
  4. Drizzle with a little oil and fold into a sealed parcel; tie with string.
  5. Cook over hot coals or on a griddle 20–25 minutes, turning once.
  6. Open at the table and serve with yuca.

How to make it lighter

  • It is already low in fat; skip the oil to make it even lighter.
  • Serve with boiled yuca rather than fried plantain.
  • Add extra vegetables inside the parcel.

History

Wrapping fish in leaves to cook over fire is an old technique of the peoples of the Amazon basin. Today patarashca is common in Iquitos and Tarapoto, often made with paiche, doncella or tilapia.
