Pan de yuca (Colombian cassava cheese bread)

Origin: Colombia

Pan de yuca is a gluten-free bread made from cassava starch kneaded with fresh cheese, egg and a little butter. It is usually shaped into small crescents or horseshoes and baked until lightly golden, with a stretchy, almost elastic chew. Colombians eat it warm with coffee, often with a glass of yogurt or kumis.

Recipe

12 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g sweet cassava starch
  • 250 g fresh white cheese, finely grated
  • 1 egg
  • 20 g butter, softened
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 2-4 tbsp milk
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 210 °C.
  2. Mix the cassava starch, baking powder and salt.
  3. Add the cheese, butter and egg and knead well.
  4. Add milk little by little until the dough is smooth and does not crack.
  5. Shape into 12 small crescents and place on a lined tray.
  6. Bake 15-18 minutes until puffed and just golden; eat warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Leave out the butter and use a light fresh cheese for fewer calories.
  • Pair with plain yogurt and fruit rather than sweet drinks.
  • Freeze shaped raw dough and bake only what you need each time.

History

Breads based on cassava starch are found across South America, from Brazilian pão de queijo to Paraguayan chipa. In Colombia, pan de yuca is sold in bakeries and roadside shops nationwide, especially in the southwest.
