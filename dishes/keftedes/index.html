🧆 Keftedes

Origin: Cyprus · Greece

Keftedes are Greek and Cypriot meatballs of minced beef mixed with grated onion, soaked bread, egg and a generous amount of fresh mint. They are shaped into small flattened balls and fried until crisp and deeply browned outside while staying soft within. Keftedes are a favourite meze, served warm with lemon, salad and dips.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g minced beef
  • 2 slices stale bread, soaked in water and squeezed
  • 1 egg
  • 1 onion, grated
  • A handful of fresh mint, chopped
  • Salt, pepper, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Combine the beef, squeezed bread, egg, onion, mint and spices and knead until smooth.
  2. Rest the mixture in the fridge for 30 minutes.
  3. Shape into small flattened balls.
  4. Fry in a little oil over medium heat for 3–4 minutes per side until crisp and cooked through. Serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Oven-bake or air-fry the keftedes and brush with just a little oil.
  • Use lean minced beef.
  • Serve with a big salad and lemon rather than bread and fries.

History

The name comes from the Turkish köfte and ultimately the Persian kofta, and meatballs of this kind are shared across the eastern Mediterranean. Mint is especially characteristic of the Cypriot version. Today keftedes appear on almost every meze spread in Cyprus and Greece.
