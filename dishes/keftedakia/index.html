🧆 Keftedakia

Keftedakia are small Greek meatballs of minced beef mixed with breadcrumbs, grated onion, plenty of parsley and spices, then pan-fried until crisp and brown. They are bite-sized, juicy inside and fragrant with herbs. Keftedakia are a classic meze, served warm or at room temperature with lemon wedges and salad.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g minced beef
  • 60 g breadcrumbs
  • 1 egg
  • 1 onion, grated
  • A large handful of parsley, chopped
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, ½ tsp ground cumin, salt, pepper
  • Oil for frying
  • Lemon wedges to serve

Method

  1. Mix the beef, breadcrumbs, egg, onion, parsley and spices; knead well and chill for 30 minutes.
  2. Roll into balls about 3 cm across with wet hands.
  3. Shallow-fry in batches over medium heat for 6–8 minutes, turning, until browned and cooked through.
  4. Drain on paper and serve with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the meatballs at 200 °C for 15–20 minutes instead of frying.
  • Use lean minced beef (5–10% fat).
  • Pair them with a large Greek-style salad instead of fries or bread.

History

Keftedes, from the Turkish köfte and ultimately the Persian kofta, have long been part of Greek home cooking; the diminutive keftedakia simply means 'little meatballs'. Today they are a fixture of Greek tavernas, family gatherings and meze tables.
