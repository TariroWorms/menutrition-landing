Mondongo venezolano (Venezuelan tripe soup)

Origin: Venezuela

Venezuelan mondongo is a thick, comforting soup of beef tripe cooked slowly until soft, then simmered with chickpeas, potato, carrot, pumpkin, corn and a sofrito of onion, pepper and garlic, finished with cilantro. The tripe becomes tender and slightly chewy, and the vegetables thicken the broth into something between a soup and a stew. It is a weekend lunch in many homes and is often served with avocado and arepas.

Recipe

8 servings210 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef tripe, well cleaned
  • 2 limes for washing
  • 250 g cooked chickpeas
  • 400 g potatoes, 2 carrots, 300 g pumpkin
  • 2 corn cobs in chunks
  • 1 onion, 1 red pepper, 4 garlic cloves, 2 tomatoes
  • 30 g cilantro, cumin, salt

Method

  1. Rub the tripe with lime and salt, rinse well and boil in fresh water for 2–3 hours (or 45 minutes in a pressure cooker) until tender.
  2. Drain, cut into small strips and return to a pot with 2.5 l of fresh water.
  3. Fry onion, pepper, garlic and tomato into a sofrito and add it to the pot with cumin.
  4. Add the corn, potatoes, carrots and pumpkin and simmer 25 minutes.
  5. Add the chickpeas and cook 10 minutes more, then season and finish with cilantro.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim all visible fat from the tripe before cooking.
  • Add more pumpkin and carrot and fewer potatoes.
  • Tripe is low in fat but high in cholesterol – keep portions moderate.

History

Tripe soups came to Latin America from Spanish cooking and took different forms in each country. In Venezuela mondongo is a classic of popular restaurants and family Sundays, and is traditionally credited with reviving people after late nights.
