Megrelian khachapuri

Origin: Georgia · Samegrelo

Megrelian khachapuri starts like the Imeretian round but gets a second layer of grated sulguni on top before baking. The cheese melts into a golden, slightly blistered crust over the stuffed dough. It is richer and more indulgent than the plain version. Georgians serve it in wedges, often as a centrepiece at a meal.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 250 ml warm matsoni or yogurt-milk mix
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 1 egg for dough + 1 for brushing
  • 350 g Imeruli cheese, grated
  • 250 g sulguni, grated
  • 20 g butter
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Make a soft dough with flour, yeast, warm liquid, one egg and salt; let it rise for an hour.
  2. Mix the Imeruli cheese with half the sulguni.
  3. Divide the dough into four, wrap each around a ball of filling and roll flat.
  4. Place on a lined tray and brush with beaten egg.
  5. Scatter the remaining sulguni over the tops.
  6. Bake at 230 °C for 12–15 minutes until the cheese is golden.
  7. Rub with a little butter and cut into wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the topping cheese for a lighter version that still browns well.
  • Use part wholemeal flour for more fibre.
  • Pair it with a herb salad rather than other heavy dishes.

History

It comes from Samegrelo (Mingrelia) in western Georgia, a region known for its cheese and bold cooking. Covering the bread with cheese is the feature that sets it apart from other regional khachapuri.
