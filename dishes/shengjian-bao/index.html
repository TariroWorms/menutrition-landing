Shengjian bao (pan-fried pork buns)

Origin: China · Shanghai
352kcal

Shengjian bao are small yeast-dough buns filled with seasoned pork and a savory aspic that melts into soup. They are fried bottom-down until crisp and golden, then steamed in the same pan. A sprinkle of sesame and scallion finishes them, and they are best eaten hot with a bite of vinegar.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 5 g instant yeast
  • 170 ml warm water
  • 300 g minced pork
  • 100 g pork aspic or jellied stock
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce and 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2 scallions, chopped
  • 1 tbsp sesame seeds
  • oil for frying

Method

  1. Knead flour, yeast and water to a smooth dough and let it rise for 1 hour.
  2. Mix pork with soy sauce, ginger and aspic until sticky.
  3. Divide the dough into 12 balls, flatten, fill and pleat the tops closed.
  4. Rest the buns 15 minutes, then fry bottoms in oil until golden.
  5. Add a little water, cover and steam for 8 minutes.
  6. Sprinkle with sesame and scallion and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less oil and more water when steaming to lighten the buns.
  • Choose lean pork and add finely chopped greens to the filling.
  • Dip in vinegar rather than chili oil.

History

The buns are a Shanghai street breakfast classic, sold from tea houses and street stalls for generations. Unlike steamed soup dumplings they use a fluffier raised dough that crisps underneath.
