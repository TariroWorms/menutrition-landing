Ranfañote (Peruvian bread dessert with syrup)

Origin: Peru

Ranfañote is a humble but rich dessert made from day-old bread cut into cubes and toasted. The pieces are drenched in a thick syrup of chancaca (raw cane sugar) with cinnamon, cloves and orange peel, then mixed with walnuts, peanuts, coconut, raisins and crumbled fresh cheese. It is eaten at room temperature in small portions, usually as an afternoon sweet.

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g day-old bread
  • 250 g chancaca or dark panela
  • 300 ml water
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 cloves
  • 1 strip orange peel
  • 60 g walnuts
  • 50 g roasted peanuts
  • 40 g grated coconut
  • 40 g raisins
  • 80 g fresh cheese

Method

  1. Cut the bread into small cubes and toast in the oven at 180 °C for 10–12 minutes until dry and crisp.
  2. Simmer the chancaca with the water, cinnamon, cloves and orange peel for about 15 minutes into a thick syrup.
  3. Strain out the spices.
  4. Roughly chop the walnuts and peanuts and crumble the cheese.
  5. Toss the bread with the nuts, coconut and raisins in a serving dish.
  6. Pour the hot syrup over, mix gently, then top with the cheese and let it soak for 10 minutes before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use whole-grain bread to add fibre and slow down the sugar spike.
  • Make a thinner syrup with less chancaca and add more orange peel for flavour.
  • Serve small portions of about 100 g, as it is very energy-dense.

History

Ranfañote is linked to the Afro-Peruvian cooking of colonial Lima, where cooks turned leftover bread into a festive sweet. It was once common in street sales in the capital and is now made mostly at home and at food fairs.
