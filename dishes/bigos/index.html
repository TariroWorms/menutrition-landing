Bigos is a slow-cooked Polish stew of sauerkraut and fresh cabbage with chunks of pork, smoked sausage, dried mushrooms and prunes. Long simmering turns it dark, soft and deeply savoury, with a sour-smoky taste and a hint of sweetness from the prunes. It is served hot in a bowl, and many cooks say it tastes best reheated on the second or third day.

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g sauerkraut, drained and chopped
  • 500 g fresh white cabbage, shredded
  • 500 g pork shoulder, cut into 3 cm cubes
  • 300 g smoked sausage, sliced
  • 20 g dried mushrooms, soaked in 300 ml hot water
  • 8 prunes, chopped
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 bay leaves, 5 allspice berries, 1 tsp black pepper
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt

Method

  1. Put the sauerkraut in a large pot with 300 ml water and simmer for 30 minutes.
  2. Meanwhile brown the pork in the oil, then add the onion and sausage and fry for 5 minutes more.
  3. Add the fresh cabbage, the browned meat, the mushrooms with their strained soaking water, the bay leaves and allspice to the sauerkraut.
  4. Cover and simmer on low heat for 1.5–2 hours, stirring now and then and adding a splash of water if it gets dry.
  5. Stir in the prunes and tomato paste, season with salt and pepper and cook for 20 minutes more.
  6. Rest overnight if possible and reheat gently before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork shoulder trimmed of visible fat and skim the fat that rises to the top after resting.
  • Raise the share of cabbage and sauerkraut and cut the sausage by half — the stew stays full-flavoured.
  • Serve one generous bowl without extra sides; the cabbage is filling on its own.

History

Bigos is often called Poland's national dish and has been cooked in the region for centuries, traditionally as a way to use up meat and preserved cabbage through the winter. It is famously praised in Adam Mickiewicz's 19th-century epic poem Pan Tadeusz. Today it is a fixture of Christmas, Easter and winter family tables in Poland and Lithuania.
