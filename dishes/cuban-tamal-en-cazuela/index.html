Tamal en cazuela (Cuban corn porridge with pork)

Origin: Cuba

Tamal en cazuela is a thick, creamy pot of ground fresh corn stirred with pork and a sofrito of onion, pepper and garlic. It has the flavor of a tamal without the wrapping, soft and spoonable with a golden color from annatto. It is eaten warm as a main dish with a sprinkle of lime.

Recipe

6 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 8 ears fresh corn, kernels grated or blended (about 800 g)
  • 250 g pork, diced
  • 1 onion and 1 pepper, chopped (220 g)
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp oil (30 ml)
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1/2 tsp annatto
  • 400 ml water or broth

Method

  1. Grate or blend the corn kernels to a coarse puree.
  2. Brown the pork in oil with salt.
  3. Add onion, pepper, garlic, cumin and annatto and cook until soft.
  4. Stir in the corn puree and the liquid.
  5. Cook on low, stirring often, for 35-40 minutes until thick.
  6. Taste for salt and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork or chicken to lighten.
  • Stir constantly near the end to prevent sticking.
  • Serve with a tomato salad to balance richness.

History

Corn was already central to the diet of the island's indigenous peoples. The pot version is a practical home adaptation of the wrapped tamal, easier to make for a family. It is common in central and western Cuba.
