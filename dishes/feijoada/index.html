🍲 Feijoada

Feijoada is Brazil's famous black bean stew, slow-cooked with several cuts of pork and smoked sausage until the beans turn thick, dark and smoky. It is served with white rice, sautéed collard greens, toasted cassava meal called farofa and orange slices that cut through the richness. It is a long, sociable meal traditionally eaten on Wednesdays or Saturdays.

Recipe

6 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g dried black beans, soaked overnight
  • 400 g pork shoulder or ribs, cut into pieces
  • 250 g smoked pork sausage, sliced
  • 1 onion and 4 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 2 bay leaves, salt, pepper
  • 300 g rice, cooked
  • 1 bunch collard greens, shredded and sautéed in 1 tbsp oil
  • 150 g farofa (cassava meal toasted in oil), 2 oranges, sliced

Method

  1. Simmer the beans with bay leaves in plenty of water for 1 hour.
  2. Brown the pork and sausage in a heavy pot, add the onion and garlic and cook until soft.
  3. Add the beans with their liquid and simmer for 1½–2 hours until the meat is tender and the stew thick.
  4. Season and serve with rice, collard greens, farofa and orange slices.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean pork loin and fewer sausage slices, and skim fat from the stew.
  • Fill half the plate with beans and greens and keep rice and farofa to a few spoonfuls.
  • Toast the farofa with just 1 tbsp oil — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Feijoada is often called Brazil's national dish. A popular story says it was created by enslaved people from leftover pork cuts, but food historians generally trace it to Portuguese meat-and-bean stews adapted with Brazilian black beans. Today it is a weekend tradition across the country, especially in Rio de Janeiro.
