🍡 Black sesame tangyuan

Origin: China · Zhejiang, Ningbo

Black sesame tangyuan are soft, chewy balls of glutinous rice dough filled with a sweet paste of toasted black sesame and sugar, boiled and served in a warm ginger syrup. When bitten, the molten nutty filling flows out against the smooth, springy skin. They are eaten hot from a bowl, a few at a time, as a dessert or festive snack.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 80 g black sesame seeds
  • 60 g sugar for the filling
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 250 g glutinous rice powder (sticky rice, finely ground)
  • About 220 ml warm water
  • 30 g fresh ginger, sliced, and 50 g brown sugar
  • 800 ml water for the syrup

Method

  1. Toast the sesame seeds in a dry pan for 3–4 minutes, cool and grind finely with the sugar; mix in the oil to a firm paste and chill for 20 minutes.
  2. Mix the glutinous rice powder with warm water little by little and knead into a smooth, soft dough.
  3. Divide the dough into 20 pieces, flatten each, add a small ball of filling and roll closed into a smooth ball.
  4. Simmer the ginger and brown sugar in 800 ml water for 15 minutes to make the syrup.
  5. Boil the tangyuan in a separate pot of water until they float, then 2 minutes more.
  6. Lift them into bowls and ladle the hot ginger syrup over them.

How to make it lighter

  • Each ball is small but dense: 4–5 balls make a full dessert portion.
  • Halve the brown sugar in the ginger syrup — the ginger still gives plenty of flavour.
  • Make the balls smaller with less filling to keep the sesame and sugar in check.

History

Tangyuan are eaten across China at the Lantern Festival and the winter solstice, and their round shape is seen as a symbol of family reunion. The city of Ningbo in Zhejiang is especially famous for black sesame tangyuan. Today they are also a common frozen dessert in Chinese supermarkets worldwide.
