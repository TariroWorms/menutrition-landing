🍆 Babaganoush

Babaganoush, as it is often spelled on menus, is a creamy eggplant dip from the Levant. In this lighter, everyday version the eggplants are roasted in the oven until soft and smoky at the edges, then mashed with a modest spoonful of tahini, lemon juice, garlic and a little olive oil. It tastes fresh and tangy rather than rich, and is eaten as a mezze or alongside grilled meats and vegetables.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 3 medium eggplants (about 900 g)
  • 2 tbsp tahini
  • Juice of 1 lemon (about 40 ml)
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt, ground cumin, chopped parsley

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 230 °C. Prick the eggplants, halve them lengthwise, brush the cut sides very lightly with the oil and roast cut side down for 30–35 minutes until very soft.
  2. Scoop out the flesh, drain it for 10 minutes and chop it finely.
  3. Mix with the tahini, lemon juice, garlic, a pinch of cumin and salt. Taste and add more lemon if needed.
  4. Serve cool, sprinkled with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • More eggplant and lemon and less tahini keep the dip light without losing its creaminess.
  • A typical mezze portion is 3–4 tbsp — spoon it onto your plate rather than dipping from the shared bowl.
  • Pair it with grilled chicken and salad for a filling, balanced plate.

History

Roasted eggplant dips are part of everyday eating across the Levant, Turkey and Egypt, and babaganoush has become one of the best-known Middle Eastern mezze around the world.
