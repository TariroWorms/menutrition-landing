🍜 Tonkotsu Ramen

Origin: Japan · Fukuoka (Kyushu)

Tonkotsu ramen is a noodle soup made with a cloudy, milky broth from pork bones boiled hard for many hours. Thin wheat noodles go into the bowl with slices of tender pork belly, a soft-boiled egg, sheets of nori and sliced scallions. The broth is rich and savoury, and the noodles are served firm.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg pork bones
  • 2 l water
  • 500 g pork belly
  • 4 eggs
  • 400 g fresh ramen noodles
  • 4 scallions, sliced
  • 4 sheets nori
  • 3 garlic cloves and 1 piece ginger
  • Salt

Method

  1. Blanch the bones, rinse, then boil hard in fresh water with garlic and ginger for at least 3 hours, topping up, until milky.
  2. Simmer the pork belly in the broth for about 1.5 hours, then cool and slice.
  3. Boil the eggs for 6.5 minutes, cool in cold water and peel.
  4. Strain the broth and season with salt.
  5. Cook the noodles briefly, divide into bowls, ladle over the broth and top with pork, halved egg, nori and scallions.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the broth and lift off the solid fat before reheating.
  • Use fewer slices of pork belly and more scallions.
  • Leave some of the broth in the bowl: it holds much of the fat and salt.

History

Tonkotsu ramen is associated with Kyushu, especially Fukuoka and Kurume, where pork-bone broths became popular in the mid-20th century. It has since spread throughout Japan and to ramen shops worldwide.
