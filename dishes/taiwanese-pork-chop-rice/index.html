Taiwanese pork chop rice (paigu fan)

Origin: Taiwan

This is a set meal of a thin pork chop, marinated and fried until crisp, served over steamed rice with a braised egg and greens. The chop is savory, fragrant with five-spice and a little sweet. It is a common lunch at small restaurants and railway stations in Taiwan.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thin pork chops
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp five-spice powder
  • 60 g sweet potato starch
  • 4 braised eggs
  • 400 g rice and 300 g bok choy

Method

  1. Pound the chops thin and marinate in soy sauce, five-spice and garlic for 1 hour.
  2. Coat the chops in starch.
  3. Fry in hot oil until crisp and cooked through, about 5 minutes.
  4. Blanch the bok choy and season lightly.
  5. Serve the chops over rice with the egg and greens.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the chops instead of frying.
  • Use brown rice and more greens.
  • Choose lean cuts and trim the fat.

History

The dish is closely linked with the lunchboxes sold at Taiwan's railway stations, which helped to popularize it island-wide. The chop is traditionally pounded thin and coated with sweet potato starch.
