🥒 Kabees (Levantine pickles)

Origin: Levant · Middle East

Kabees is the Arabic word for pickles, and the classic Levantine jar mixes crunchy cucumbers, turnips dyed bright pink by a slice of beetroot, and hot green chillies. They are sour, salty and very crisp. A small plate of kabees sits on almost every table next to falafel, shawarma, grills and rice dishes.

Recipe

10 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g small cucumbers
  • 500 g turnips, peeled and cut into wedges
  • 1 small beetroot, peeled and sliced
  • 4 green chillies
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 500 ml water
  • 250 ml white vinegar
  • 2 tbsp salt

Method

  1. Pack the vegetables, chillies and garlic into clean jars, tucking beetroot slices between the turnips.
  2. Bring the water, vinegar and salt to the boil until the salt dissolves; cool slightly.
  3. Pour the brine over the vegetables to cover them completely and seal.
  4. Leave at room temperature 5–7 days until sour and pink, then keep in the fridge.

How to make it lighter

  • Pickles are very low in calories and add crunch and flavour to a lighter plate.
  • They are high in salt, so keep the portion to a small side plate.
  • Use them instead of rich sauces with grilled meat or falafel.

History

Preserving vegetables in vinegar and salt is an old practice across the Middle East, keeping summer produce for the winter. Today pink turnips and pickled cucumbers are made at home and sold in jars and from barrels throughout Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Palestine and the Gulf.
