🐟 Fritto misto di pesce

Fritto misto di pesce is the seaside version of Italy’s mixed fry: shrimp, squid rings, whole small sardines and batons of zucchini, all dusted in flour and fried until crisp. The zucchini adds a soft, sweet contrast to the seafood. It arrives at the table hot, heaped on a plate with lemon wedges, and is eaten as a main course in trattorias along the coast.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g raw shrimp, shell on or peeled
  • 250 g cleaned squid, cut into rings
  • 300 g fresh sardines, cleaned
  • 2 zucchini, cut into thin batons
  • 150 g plain flour (or semolina flour)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Oil for frying
  • 1 lemon, in wedges

Method

  1. Pat the seafood and zucchini dry.
  2. Toss everything in the flour seasoned with salt and shake off the excess.
  3. Heat the oil to 180 °C. Fry the zucchini first (2 minutes), then sardines (3 minutes), shrimp (2 minutes) and squid (1–2 minutes), in small batches.
  4. Drain, salt lightly and serve at once with lemon.

How to make it lighter

  • Ask for extra zucchini and fewer sardines and shrimp to lower the energy density of the plate.
  • Pair it with a simple tomato or green salad instead of bread.
  • Sardines are oily fish — keep them and cut back on the fried shrimp if you want more nutrients per calorie.

History

Frying the day’s small catch in flour is a long-standing habit of Italian fishing towns, and each coast has its own mix; adding vegetables such as zucchini is common in many places. Today fritto misto di pesce is a staple of trattorias from Liguria to Sicily.
