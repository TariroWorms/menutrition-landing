🍲 Albóndigas en salsa

Albóndigas en salsa are tender Spanish meatballs of minced beef and pork bound with breadcrumbs and egg, browned and then simmered in a sauce of tomato and slowly softened onion. The egg and soaked crumbs keep them light and moist inside, while the sauce turns thick and savoury. They are a classic family lunch in Spain, served with rice, potatoes or simply with the sauce.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g minced beef
  • 250 g minced pork
  • 40 g breadcrumbs
  • 1 egg
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed, 2 tbsp chopped parsley, salt, pepper
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 400 g tomato passata and 150 ml water

Method

  1. Mix the beef, pork, breadcrumbs, egg, garlic, parsley, salt and pepper and shape into about 20 balls of 3 cm.
  2. Brown the meatballs in the oil and set aside.
  3. In the same pan cook the onions over low heat for 15 minutes until very soft.
  4. Add the passata and water and simmer for 5 minutes; blend the sauce smooth if you like.
  5. Return the meatballs, cover and simmer for 20 minutes until tender.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean minced meat — the fat in the mince matters more than the sauce.
  • Bake the meatballs at 200 °C for 15 minutes instead of frying to save most of the oil.
  • Serve a portion of 5–6 meatballs with plenty of vegetables and a small portion of rice.

History

Meatballs simmered in sauce have long been part of Spanish home cooking, and every region and family has its own version of the sauce, from tomato-based to onion and stock. The name albóndiga is usually traced to the Arabic al-bunduqa, “a small ball”, from the time of al-Andalus. Today albóndigas en salsa are a staple of Spanish home menus and daily-set-menu restaurants.
