Majarete (Dominican corn pudding)

Origin: Dominican Republic

Majarete is a thick, silky pudding made from fresh corn blended with milk and cooked until it sets. It tastes gently sweet, with cinnamon, vanilla and a faint corn flavor. Dominicans serve it warm or chilled in small bowls, often as a comforting home dessert.

What it is made of

cornmilksugarcinnamonsaltvanilla

Recipe

6 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 4 ears fresh corn (about 500 g kernels)
  • 750 ml whole milk
  • 120 g sugar
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1 pinch salt
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • ground cinnamon for dusting

Method

  1. Blend the corn kernels with half the milk until smooth and strain through a sieve.
  2. Pour into a pot with the remaining milk, sugar, cinnamon stick and salt.
  3. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, for 25-30 minutes until thick.
  4. Remove the cinnamon stick and stir in the vanilla.
  5. Pour into bowls and cool, then chill if you like.
  6. Dust with ground cinnamon before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use low-fat milk and cut the sugar by a third for a lighter dessert.
  • Strain the corn well for a smoother texture.
  • Keep portions small and top with fresh fruit instead of extra sugar.

History

Corn puddings of this kind are found across Latin America and the Caribbean, where maize has been a staple since long before colonial times. In the Dominican Republic majarete is a familiar everyday sweet, often made for family gatherings.
