Tlacoyos (stuffed blue masa cakes)

Origin: Mexico · Mexico City

Tlacoyos are thick oval cakes of blue corn masa with a filling of mashed beans, fava beans or cheese sealed inside. They are cooked on a dry comal until the outside is lightly crisp and the inside stays soft and earthy. Street vendors top them with nopal salad, crumbled fresh cheese, onion and salsa, and they are eaten by hand as a snack or light lunch.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g blue corn masa
  • 250 g refried black beans
  • 150 g cooked nopales, diced
  • 80 g queso fresco
  • 1/2 white onion, chopped
  • 150 ml green or red salsa
  • Salt

Method

  1. Knead the masa with a pinch of salt and a little water until smooth and pliable.
  2. Divide into 8 balls, flatten each into a disc and spoon beans into the centre.
  3. Fold the edges over the filling and shape into a flat oval about 1 cm thick.
  4. Cook on a hot dry griddle for 4–5 minutes per side until spotted and firm.
  5. Top with nopales, onion, crumbled cheese and salsa and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Cook on a dry griddle instead of frying to keep fat low.
  • Use plenty of nopales on top for fibre and volume.
  • Choose beans mashed without lard for a vegan version.

History

Tlacoyos belong to the family of masa snacks that predate the Spanish conquest in central Mexico. They remain a fixture of markets and street stalls in Mexico City, often sold by women cooking on clay or steel griddles.
