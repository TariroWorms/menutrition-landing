Xinjiang roast whole lamb (kao quan yang)

Origin: China · Xinjiang

A whole young lamb is rubbed with salt, cumin, chili and spices, then roasted for hours in a pit or tandoor-like oven. The skin turns crisp and amber while the meat stays soft and falls off the bone. It is carved at the table for a festive crowd.

What it is made of

Recipe

12 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1 young lamb (8 kg) or a 2 kg shoulder for a small version
  • 4 tbsp salt
  • 3 tbsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp chili powder
  • 2 tbsp ground Sichuan pepper
  • 1 head of garlic
  • 2 onions
  • 100 ml oil

Method

  1. Rub the lamb all over with salt, spices and garlic and rest overnight.
  2. Brush with a little oil.
  3. Roast in a hot pit oven at about 180°C, turning as needed, for 3–4 hours.
  4. Baste occasionally with its own fat.
  5. Rest 15 minutes.
  6. Carve and serve with onion, naan and tea.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove visible fat before eating.
  • Serve with plenty of raw onion and salad.
  • Keep portions to a palm-sized piece of meat.

History

Roast whole lamb is a celebratory dish of the nomadic and Uyghur tradition in Xinjiang and Central Asia. It appears at weddings and large gatherings.
