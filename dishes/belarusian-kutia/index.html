Kutia (Christmas wheat porridge)

Origin: Belarus

Kutia is a ritual sweet porridge of whole wheat or barley grains mixed with crushed poppy seeds, honey, nuts and dried fruit. It is chewy, nutty and honey-sweet, with the poppy giving it a grey-blue colour and a slightly oily richness. In Belarus it opens the meatless Christmas Eve supper and is also served at memorial meals.

What it is made of

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g wheat berries
  • 100 g poppy seeds
  • 100 g honey
  • 80 g walnuts
  • 60 g raisins
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Soak the wheat overnight, then boil in fresh water for 1-1.5 hours until tender, and drain.
  2. Pour boiling water over the poppy seeds, leave for 30 minutes, drain and grind or pound until milky.
  3. Soak the raisins in warm water for 10 minutes and drain.
  4. Toast and chop the walnuts.
  5. Dissolve the honey in 3 tbsp of warm water.
  6. Mix the wheat with poppy, honey, nuts and raisins and let stand for an hour before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Kutia is dense: a small bowl is a full dessert portion.
  • Use less honey and more dried fruit for natural sweetness.
  • Whole wheat grains give plenty of fibre; barley works as well.

History

Kutia is one of the oldest ritual dishes of the Eastern Slavs and is closely linked to winter holidays and the remembrance of ancestors. The Christmas Eve evening itself is often called kuccia in Belarusian, after the dish.
