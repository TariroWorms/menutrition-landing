Akara (black-eyed pea fritters)

Origin: Nigeria

Akara are golden fritters with a crisp crust and a light, fluffy inside, made from peeled black-eyed peas whipped into a paste with onion and hot pepper. They taste savory and slightly peppery. Nigerians eat them for breakfast with pap or bread, or as a snack from street vendors.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g black-eyed peas
  • 1 onion (120 g)
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper
  • 1 tsp salt
  • about 100 ml water
  • 500 ml oil for frying

Method

  1. Soak the beans, rub off skins and rinse until the water runs clear.
  2. Blend with onion, pepper and a little water into a thick paste.
  3. Whip the paste by hand or with a mixer for several minutes until airy.
  4. Season with salt.
  5. Heat oil to medium-high and drop in spoonfuls of paste.
  6. Fry, turning, until deep golden, then drain on paper.

How to make it lighter

  • Drain well on paper to remove excess oil.
  • Bake spoonfuls at 220 C for a lighter version.
  • Serve with tomatoes and cucumber rather than fried bread.

History

Akara is an old West African bean fritter, popular among the Yoruba and carried across the Atlantic to Brazil, where a related version is called acarajé. It is usually fried in hot oil in the morning and eaten while still warm.
