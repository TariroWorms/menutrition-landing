🌯 Lamb shawarma

Lamb shawarma is spiced lamb roasted on a vertical spit and carved into thin, crisp-edged slices. It is rolled in pita with tahini sauce, garlic, a squeeze of lemon and pickles, giving a richer, more intense flavour than chicken shawarma. It is served as a wrap or on a plate with the same sauce and pickles.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g boneless lamb leg, thinly sliced
  • Juice of 1 lemon, 3 garlic cloves, 1 tbsp oil, salt
  • 2 tsp seven-spice mix (allspice, cinnamon, cumin, black pepper, nutmeg, cloves, coriander)
  • 4 tbsp tahini whisked with lemon juice and water
  • 4 pita breads
  • Pickles, onion and parsley

Method

  1. Marinate the lamb with lemon, garlic, oil, spices and salt for at least 3 hours.
  2. Sear in batches in a very hot pan for 2–3 minutes until browned and slightly crisp.
  3. Whisk the tahini with lemon juice and water into a pourable sauce.
  4. Fill each pita with lamb, tahini sauce, pickles, onion and parsley and roll tightly.

How to make it lighter

  • Lamb leg is leaner than shoulder; trim any visible fat before slicing.
  • Use a thin drizzle of tahini sauce rather than a thick layer.
  • Pair a smaller wrap with a big parsley-and-onion salad.

History

Vertical-spit roasting developed in Ottoman lands, probably in the 19th century, and lamb and mutton were among the traditional meats used. The Arabic name comes from the Turkish çevirme, “turning”. Lamb or mixed lamb-and-beef shawarma with tahini remains a classic across Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Palestine.
