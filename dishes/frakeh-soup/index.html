🍲 Freekeh Soup

Freekeh soup, shorbat freekeh, is a hearty broth of cracked freekeh — young green wheat that is roasted while still unripe — simmered in chicken broth with onion, cumin and shredded chicken. The grain stays slightly chewy and gives the soup its distinctive smoky flavour. It is served hot with a squeeze of lemon and is a familiar dish during Ramadan in Palestine, Jordan, Syria and Egypt.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g cracked freekeh, rinsed
  • 1.5 l chicken broth
  • 300 g chicken breast or thighs, skinless
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in the broth for 20 minutes, then lift it out and shred it.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion in the olive oil for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the freekeh and cumin to the onion and stir for 1 minute.
  4. Pour in the hot broth and simmer for 20–25 minutes until the freekeh is tender.
  5. Return the shredded chicken, season and serve with lemon wedges.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim the fat from the broth after simmering the chicken to keep the soup lighter.
  • Use skinless chicken breast instead of thighs for more protein per calorie.
  • A big bowl of this soup before the main course makes a filling start to the meal for relatively few calories.

History

Freekeh is made by harvesting durum wheat while green and burning off the straw and chaff, a method long practised in the Levant and Egypt. Freekeh soup is eaten today across the region, and is especially popular as an iftar starter during Ramadan.
