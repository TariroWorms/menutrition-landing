Majarete (corn and coconut pudding)

Origin: Venezuela

Majarete is a soft, sliceable pudding made by cooking corn (fresh kernels or cornflour) with coconut milk, sugar and cinnamon until very thick. It is poured into a dish, left to set and sprinkled with ground cinnamon. The texture is silky and the flavour gently sweet, with coconut in the foreground. It is eaten cold as a dessert, especially during Easter week.

What it is made of

corncoconut milksugarcinnamoncornstarch

Recipe

8 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 300 ml water
  • 120 g fine white cornflour or blended fresh corn
  • 150 g sugar or papelón
  • 1 cinnamon stick plus ground cinnamon
  • pinch of salt

Method

  1. Whisk the cornflour into the coconut milk and water until smooth.
  2. Add sugar, salt and the cinnamon stick.
  3. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, for 15–20 minutes until very thick.
  4. Remove the cinnamon and pour into a wet mould.
  5. Cool, chill for 2 hours and dust with cinnamon before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk.
  • Cut the sugar by a third and add vanilla for aroma.
  • Serve with fresh fruit instead of extra syrup.

History

Corn and coconut desserts of this kind are found across the Caribbean, and majarete is a well-known member of the family in Venezuela. It is closely tied to coastal cooking, where coconut is plentiful.
