Brazilian churrasco (gaúcho barbecue)

Origin: Brazil · Rio Grande do Sul

Brazilian churrasco is the barbecue tradition of the south, where large cuts of beef, pork ribs, chicken and sausages are threaded on long skewers and roasted slowly over charcoal or wood embers. The only seasoning is usually coarse salt, so the flavour comes from the meat, its fat and the smoke. Meat is carved straight from the skewer in thin slices as each piece is ready. It is served with farofa, vinaigrette salsa, rice and salads.

What it is made of

beefporkchickensausagesalt

Recipe

8 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg picanha or other beef cuts
  • 600 g pork ribs
  • 600 g chicken thighs
  • 500 g fresh pork sausage
  • Coarse salt
  • For the salsa: tomatoes, onion, green pepper, vinegar, oil

Method

  1. Light a charcoal fire and let it burn down to glowing embers.
  2. Cut beef into thick pieces and thread all meats on long skewers.
  3. Rub with coarse salt just before grilling.
  4. Put the chicken and sausages on first, at a moderate distance from the coals, as they take longest.
  5. Add beef and pork, turning skewers now and then.
  6. Carve thin slices from the outside as they are done.
  7. Serve with salsa, farofa and salad.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose lean cuts and trim the fat cap after cooking.
  • Fill half the plate with salad and vinaigrette.
  • Limit sausages, the fattiest and saltiest part.

History

Churrasco grew out of the cattle-ranching culture of the gaúchos on the pampas of Rio Grande do Sul. From there it spread across Brazil and abroad, notably through the rodízio-style churrascarias where waiters bring skewers to the table.
