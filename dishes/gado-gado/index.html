🥗 Gado-gado

Gado-gado is an Indonesian salad of lightly boiled and fresh vegetables served with a thick peanut sauce. This version combines fried tofu, blanched bean sprouts and cabbage, boiled potato and hard-boiled egg. The sauce, made from ground peanuts with chili, garlic, palm sugar and lime, is poured over everything, making it a filling meal rather than a side.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g firm tofu, cubed
  • 200 g bean sprouts
  • 250 g white cabbage, shredded
  • 400 g potatoes
  • 4 eggs
  • 150 g roasted peanuts
  • 2 garlic cloves and 1–2 red chilies
  • 2 tbsp palm or brown sugar, juice of 1 lime
  • 1 tbsp oil, salt, water

Method

  1. Boil the potatoes and eggs; peel and slice them.
  2. Blanch the cabbage and bean sprouts in boiling water for 1 minute and drain.
  3. Fry the tofu in the oil until golden on all sides.
  4. Blend the peanuts with garlic, chili, sugar, lime juice, salt and enough warm water to make a pourable sauce.
  5. Arrange the vegetables, tofu, potato and egg on plates and spoon the sauce over.

How to make it lighter

  • The peanut sauce carries most of the energy — serve it on the side and use 2–3 tbsp per plate.
  • Bake or pan-sear the tofu with a brush of oil instead of deep-frying.
  • Increase the cabbage and bean sprouts and use less potato for a lighter plate.

History

Gado-gado is eaten all over Indonesia and is especially associated with Jakarta and Java, where it is sold at street stalls and markets. The name is usually explained as "mix-mix", describing the jumble of ingredients.
