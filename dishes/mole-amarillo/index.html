Mole amarillo (Oaxacan yellow mole)

Origin: Mexico · Oaxaca

Mole amarillo is one of the lighter, brothier moles of Oaxaca, coloured orange-yellow by chilhuacle amarillo or guajillo chiles. Instead of nuts and chocolate, it is thickened with fresh corn masa, which gives it a smooth, almost velvety body. Chicken or beef simmers in it with green beans and chayote, and a leaf of hierba santa adds an anise note.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken pieces
  • 4 guajillo chiles
  • 2 ancho or chilhuacle chiles
  • 300 g tomatillos
  • 150 g fresh corn masa
  • 200 g green beans
  • 2 chayotes
  • 2 hierba santa leaves (or basil)
  • garlic, cumin, clove

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in salted water with garlic for 35 minutes; keep the broth.
  2. Toast and soak the chiles, then blend with roasted tomatillos, garlic and spices.
  3. Fry the purée in a little oil for 5 minutes.
  4. Dissolve the masa in 500 ml broth and stir it into the sauce until it thickens.
  5. Add green beans, diced chayote and the chicken; simmer 15 minutes.
  6. Finish with hierba santa and salt, and serve with tortillas.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove chicken skin before cooking to keep it lean.
  • Load up on chayote and green beans to add volume for few calories.
  • Seed the chiles and remove the veins for a milder sauce.

History

Oaxaca is often called the land of seven moles, and amarillo is counted among them. It is also the sauce used to fill the region's well-known empanadas de amarillo.
