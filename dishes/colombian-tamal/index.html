Colombian tamal

Origin: Colombia

Across Colombia, tamales are a weekend ritual: a soft, savoury corn dough seasoned with hogao is wrapped around pieces of pork and chicken, potato, carrot and peas, folded inside plantain leaves and cooked slowly in a big pot. When opened, the leaf releases a green, slightly smoky aroma and the dough is moist and tender. They are eaten at breakfast or for supper with hot chocolate, coffee and bread or arepa.

Recipe

10 servings210 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g precooked cornmeal or fresh corn dough
  • 1.2 l pork or chicken broth
  • 500 g pork shoulder
  • 500 g chicken thighs
  • 3 potatoes, 2 carrots, 150 g peas
  • Hogao with achiote and cumin
  • Plantain leaves and string

Method

  1. Season and pre-cook the pork and chicken briefly with hogao.
  2. Cook the cornmeal with broth and some hogao, stirring, into a thick, smooth dough.
  3. Lay dough on softened plantain leaves and add meat, potato and carrot slices and peas.
  4. Fold into neat parcels and tie with string.
  5. Steam or simmer 3 hours, then rest a few minutes before unwrapping.

How to make it lighter

  • Use chicken breast and lean pork and skim fat from the broth.
  • Add more vegetables to the filling to stretch each portion.
  • Enjoy one tamal without extra bread.

History

Corn tamales were part of Indigenous cooking long before Spanish meats and spices were added. Today almost every region — from Santander to Valle del Cauca — has a variant, differing in fillings and the colour of the dough.
