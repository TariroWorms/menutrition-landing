🧀 Pan-fried sérac with salad

Origin: France · Savoie; Switzerland

Sérac poêlé is slices of sérac, a fresh, firm whey cheese from the Alps, seared in a hot pan until a golden crust forms while the inside stays soft and mild. The cheese has a delicate, milky flavour and holds its shape when cooked. It is served warm on a dressed green salad, making a light lunch or starter.

Recipe

2 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g sérac cheese, cut in 1.5 cm slices
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • 150 g mixed salad leaves
  • 1 tbsp olive oil and 2 tsp cider vinegar for dressing
  • A few cherry tomatoes
  • Salt, pepper and fresh thyme

Method

  1. Pat the cheese slices dry and season with pepper and thyme.
  2. Heat a non-stick pan with 1 tsp oil over medium-high heat.
  3. Sear the slices for 2 minutes per side until golden, without moving them.
  4. Toss the salad and tomatoes with the oil, vinegar and salt and lay the warm cheese on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a non-stick pan so a teaspoon of oil is enough.
  • Make the salad the bigger part of the plate and the cheese the topping.
  • Dress the leaves lightly; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Sérac is a traditional whey cheese of the Savoie mountains and western Switzerland, made from the whey left over after making hard cheeses such as Beaufort. Pan-frying it is a common way to serve it in Alpine homes and restaurants.
