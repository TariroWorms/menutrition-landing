🫘 Iraqi white bean stew (fasolia)

Margat fasolia is a thick Iraqi stew of white beans and lamb cooked in tomato sauce with onion and garlic. The beans turn creamy, the lamb tender and the sauce mild and comforting. Like most Iraqi margas, it is served over white rice, often with a squeeze of lemon and pickles on the side.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g dried white beans, soaked overnight
  • 400 g lamb, cubed
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, salt and pepper
  • 1 litre water

Method

  1. Boil the soaked beans in fresh water for 45 minutes until almost tender; drain.
  2. Fry the onion in the oil, add the lamb and brown it, then add the garlic and cumin.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste, add the water and simmer the lamb for 40 minutes.
  4. Add the beans and cook another 25–30 minutes until the sauce thickens.
  5. Season and serve over rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb, or swap half the meat for extra beans.
  • Measure the oil — 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal is enough for the whole pot.
  • Beans already give plenty of fibre and starch, so keep the rice portion modest.

History

Fasolia (beans) is one of the everyday stews of Iraqi home cooking, part of the large family of tomato-based margas eaten with rice for lunch across the country.
