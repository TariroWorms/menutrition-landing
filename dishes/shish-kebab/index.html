🍢 Shish Kebab

Origin: Turkey · Anatolia

Shish kebab is cubes of lamb threaded onto skewers, usually alternating with chunks of bell pepper, onion and tomato, and grilled over hot coals. The meat is marinated with onion juice, oil and spices so it stays juicy inside while the edges char. It is served straight from the skewer with grilled vegetables, herbs and a squeeze of lemon.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g boneless lamb leg, in 3 cm cubes
  • 1 onion, grated, plus 1 onion in chunks
  • 2 bell peppers, in chunks
  • 8 cherry tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Mix the lamb with the grated onion, oil, paprika, cumin, salt and pepper; marinate at least 1 hour.
  2. Thread the meat onto skewers, alternating with pepper, onion chunks and tomatoes.
  3. Grill over high heat for 8–10 minutes, turning, until charred outside and juicy inside.
  4. Rest 2 minutes and serve with herbs and lemon.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before cubing.
  • Load the skewers with more vegetables and less meat.
  • Brush the marinade on lightly: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

The name comes from Turkish şiş, meaning skewer, and kebap, grilled meat. Grilling meat on skewers is ancient across Anatolia, Persia and the Caucasus, and the Turkish shish kebab spread worldwide with Turkish and Middle Eastern restaurants in the 20th century.
