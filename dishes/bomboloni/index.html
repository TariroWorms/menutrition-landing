🍩 Bomboloni

Bomboloni are Italian filled doughnuts: a soft, buttery yeast dough enriched with egg, fried until puffed and golden, rolled in sugar and piped full of vanilla custard or jam. Unlike ring doughnuts they have no hole, so each one is a round, pillowy bun with a creamy or fruity centre. They are eaten warm at breakfast, at cafés or on the beach in summer.

Recipe

12 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 7 g dried yeast
  • 200 ml lukewarm milk
  • 2 eggs
  • 60 g butter, softened
  • 70 g sugar, plus extra for rolling
  • A pinch of salt and grated lemon zest
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • 400 g vanilla custard or jam for filling

Method

  1. Mix the flour, yeast, sugar and lemon zest, add the milk and eggs and knead for 5 minutes, then work in the butter and salt until smooth and elastic.
  2. Cover and leave to rise for about 1.5 hours, until doubled.
  3. Roll out 1.5 cm thick, cut 8 cm rounds, set them on floured baking paper and leave to rise for 45 minutes.
  4. Fry in oil at 170 °C for about 2 minutes per side until golden, then drain on paper.
  5. Roll in sugar while warm, make a small hole in the side and pipe in custard or jam.

How to make it lighter

  • One filled bombolone is a full dessert in itself — log it as one, not as a small snack.
  • Jam filling is usually a little lighter than custard, and skipping the sugar coating saves more.
  • Keep the oil at 170–175 °C: cooler oil soaks into the dough and adds calories.

History

Bomboloni are especially associated with Tuscany, where they are sold in bakeries, cafés and by beach vendors. They belong to the wider European family of filled yeast doughnuts, close relatives of the German Berliner and the Krapfen eaten in Austria and northern Italy.
