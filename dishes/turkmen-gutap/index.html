Gutap (Turkmen fried pastries)

Origin: Turkmenistan

Gutap are paper-thin half-moon pastries cooked in a pan with a little oil until crisp and lightly blistered. The filling can be minced mutton with onion, or a mix of spring greens, pumpkin, or even camel meat in some regions. They are folded large and flat, cut in pieces and eaten hot with tea.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour
  • 220 ml water
  • 400 g minced mutton or lamb
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 200 g spinach or mixed greens (optional)
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Knead flour, water and salt into a smooth dough; rest 20 minutes.
  2. Mix the meat with onion, salt and pepper (or prepare the chopped greens).
  3. Roll the dough very thin into circles about 20 cm across.
  4. Spread filling on one half, fold over and press the edges firmly.
  5. Cook in a lightly oiled pan for 3–4 minutes per side until crisp.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose the greens filling for a lighter, vegetarian option.
  • Brush the pan with oil rather than pouring it.
  • Use lean mince and add chopped herbs for flavour.

History

Gutap are among the most common home snacks in Turkmenistan and are also sold at bazaars. Greens-filled versions are especially popular in spring.
