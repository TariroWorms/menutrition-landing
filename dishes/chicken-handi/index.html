Chicken handi is boneless chicken slowly cooked in a handi, a deep clay or metal pot, in a rich gravy of tomato, onion, ginger and garlic finished with yogurt, cream and butter. The sauce is thick, creamy and mild, with gentle warmth from the spices rather than strong heat. It is usually brought to the table in the pot itself and scooped up with naan or rice.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g boneless chicken, in chunks
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, puréed
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 150 g plain yogurt, whisked
  • 60 ml cream
  • 1 tbsp butter and 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp each ground cumin, coriander and chili, 1 tsp garam masala, salt

Method

  1. Heat the oil and butter in a heavy pot and fry the onions until golden.
  2. Add the ginger-garlic paste and ground spices, then the tomato purée, and cook until the oil separates, about 8 minutes.
  3. Add the chicken and salt and cook for 5 minutes, stirring.
  4. Lower the heat, stir in the yogurt a spoonful at a time, cover and simmer for 20 minutes.
  5. Stir in the cream and garam masala, warm through and serve in the pot.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the cream and butter: the yogurt alone keeps the sauce thick and tangy.
  • Eat it with one piece of naan or a small portion of rice rather than both.
  • Fill half the plate with salad or plain vegetables to balance the rich gravy.

History

The name comes from the handi, the narrow-necked cooking pot used across North India and Pakistan for slow, sealed cooking. Chicken handi is a restaurant favourite in both countries, and the creamy versions served today are a relatively modern style.
