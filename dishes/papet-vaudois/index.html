🥘 Papet vaudois

Origin: Switzerland · Vaud

Papet vaudois is a hearty dish from the Swiss canton of Vaud: leeks and potatoes stewed slowly until soft, finished with a little cream and topped with a smoky cabbage sausage (saucisse aux choux). The stew is mellow and creamy, the sausage rich and savoury. It is a winter comfort meal, served straight from the pot.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 1 cabbage sausage (about 500 g)
  • 800 g leeks, sliced
  • 600 g potatoes, diced
  • 100 ml cream
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 300 ml water
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Soften the leeks in the oil over low heat for 10 minutes.
  2. Add the potatoes and water, season and bring to a simmer.
  3. Lay the sausage on top, cover and cook gently for about 45 minutes.
  4. Remove the sausage, stir the cream into the vegetables and adjust seasoning.
  5. Slice the sausage and serve on the leek and potato stew.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the cream — the potatoes already make the stew creamy.
  • Serve more vegetables and a smaller slice of sausage per plate.
  • Skip extra oil: the sausage releases its own fat.

History

Papet vaudois is the signature dish of the canton of Vaud, where the cabbage sausage is a protected regional speciality. The word papet refers to the thick, porridge-like texture of the leek and potato mixture. It is traditionally eaten in the cold months and on the cantonal holiday.
