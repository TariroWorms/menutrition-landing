Locro (hominy and squash stew)

Origin: Argentina

Locro is a slow-cooked stew in which white corn and squash break down into a creamy, filling base studded with beef, pork and chorizo. It tastes deep and mellow, and is served steaming with a spoonful of red oil spiced with paprika and chili on top. Families make huge pots to share on patriotic holidays.

Recipe

8 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g dried white hominy, soaked overnight
  • 150 g dried white beans, soaked
  • 1 kg butternut squash
  • 500 g beef brisket
  • 300 g pork shoulder
  • 200 g chorizo
  • 2 onions (250 g)
  • 2.5 l water
  • For the oil: 30 ml oil, 2 spring onions, 10 g sweet paprika, chili flakes

Method

  1. Drain the hominy and beans and simmer them in the water for 1 hour.
  2. Add the diced meats and onions and continue cooking gently.
  3. After another hour add the cubed squash and the sliced chorizo.
  4. Simmer, stirring often so it does not stick, until the squash dissolves and the stew is thick, about 1–1.5 hours.
  5. For the topping, warm the oil with chopped spring onion, then stir in paprika and chili off the heat.
  6. Season the locro and serve in bowls with the red oil spooned over.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and skip the pork belly or bacon some recipes add.
  • Halve the chorizo and add more beans for protein and fiber.
  • Go easy on the red oil, or replace it with fresh chopped spring onion.

History

Locro has roots in Andean cooking, where corn and squash stews were eaten long before the Spanish arrived. Today it is closely tied to 25 May and 9 July, Argentina's main patriotic dates.
