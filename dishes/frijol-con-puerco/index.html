Frijol con puerco (Yucatecan black beans with pork)

Origin: Mexico · Yucatán

Frijol con puerco is a hearty Yucatecan stew of pork pieces cooked slowly with black beans until the meat is tender and the broth is dark and silky. Epazote gives it a distinctive herbal note. Each diner adds rice, chopped radish, onion and cilantro with lime, and a charred tomato salsa, and it is eaten with tortillas.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork shoulder or loin, cubed
  • 400 g dried black beans, soaked overnight
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, salt
  • 1 sprig epazote
  • rice to serve
  • radish, onion, cilantro and lime relish
  • charred tomato salsa

Method

  1. Cover the beans with water, add onion and garlic and simmer 1 hour.
  2. Add the pork, epazote and salt and simmer another hour until both are tender.
  3. Chop radish, onion and cilantro and dress with lime and salt.
  4. Char tomatoes and blend or chop into a salsa.
  5. Cook the rice.
  6. Serve the pork and beans in bowls with rice, relish and salsa.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean loin and skim the fat from the broth.
  • Serve more beans and relish and less meat per bowl.
  • Swap white rice for brown rice or skip it and eat with a tortilla.

History

In many Yucatecan homes the dish is traditionally served on Mondays, a custom often explained by laundry day, when a pot could simmer unattended. It reflects the region’s long reliance on black beans and pork.
