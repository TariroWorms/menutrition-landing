🧀 Cheese fatayer

Cheese fatayer are small baked pastries of soft yeast dough wrapped around a filling of salty white cheese such as akkawi and feta, mixed with chopped parsley and a sprinkle of nigella seeds. The dough bakes golden and soft while the cheese melts and turns slightly stretchy. They are eaten warm at breakfast, packed for school or served on a mezze table.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 1 tsp dried yeast, 1 tsp sugar
  • 180 ml warm water
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, ½ tsp salt
  • 150 g akkawi cheese, soaked to reduce salt and grated
  • 100 g feta, crumbled
  • 1 small bunch parsley, chopped
  • 1 tsp nigella seeds

Method

  1. Mix flour, yeast, sugar, salt, oil and water into a soft dough, knead 5 minutes and leave to rise for 40 minutes.
  2. Mix the akkawi, feta, parsley and nigella seeds.
  3. Divide the dough into 12 balls and roll each into an oval.
  4. Spoon filling into the middle and pinch the ends to form open boats.
  5. Bake at 220 °C for 12–15 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the cheese and add more parsley to keep the filling generous with fewer calories.
  • Make the boats small: two mini fatayer satisfy as much as one large one.
  • Skip brushing the baked pastries with oil — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Fatayer belong to the wider family of filled breads baked in Levantine bakeries, sold alongside manakish from the same ovens. Today cheese fatayer are a standard item in bakeries across Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Palestine and in Arab bakeries abroad.
