Regag is an Emirati paper-thin bread made from a simple batter of flour, water and salt, spread by hand in a thin film over a hot domed griddle. It cooks in seconds into a crisp, almost translucent sheet that shatters when broken. It is eaten for breakfast and as a street snack, plain or with toppings added on the griddle.

Recipe

6 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g plain flour
  • About 400 ml water
  • 1/2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, salt and water into a smooth, very soft dough-batter and rest it for 20 minutes.
  2. Heat a large griddle or upturned pan until very hot.
  3. With a wet hand, grab a little batter and quickly wipe it across the griddle in a thin layer.
  4. Cook for 30–60 seconds until crisp and lifting at the edges, then peel off.
  5. Repeat with the remaining batter.

How to make it lighter

  • Plain regag is light per sheet — keep track of how many sheets you eat.
  • Add toppings like tomato, cucumber or chilli rather than rich spreads.
  • Cook it on a dry griddle with no oil.

History

Regag (raqaq) is a traditional bread of the UAE and the wider Gulf, still made by hand at heritage festivals and in small shops and cafeterias.
