🍢 Sate Padang

Origin: Indonesia · West Sumatra

Sate Padang is a West Sumatran satay of beef, first simmered with turmeric, lemongrass and chilli until tender, then grilled on skewers. Instead of a sweet sauce, it is drenched in a thick, spicy yellow gravy thickened with ground peanuts and rice. It is served with ketupat, compressed rice cakes, which make it a filling meal.

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g beef (brisket or topside)
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 2 lemongrass stalks, bruised
  • 3 red chillies and 4 garlic cloves, blended
  • 1 litre water, salt
  • 100 g ready peanut curry sauce
  • 2 tbsp ground rice mixed with 100 ml water
  • 300 g ketupat (compressed rice cakes), cubed
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Simmer the beef in the water with turmeric, lemongrass, half the chilli paste and salt for about 1.5 hours until tender.
  2. Lift out the beef, cut it into small cubes and thread it onto skewers; keep 500 ml of the broth.
  3. Fry the remaining chilli paste in oil, add the broth and peanut curry sauce, then stir in the ground rice mixture and cook until thick.
  4. Grill the skewers for 3–4 minutes until lightly charred.
  5. Lay the ketupat on plates, add the skewers and pour the hot gravy over.

How to make it lighter

  • The gravy and rice cakes carry much of the energy: ask for the sauce on the side and use it sparingly.
  • Choose a lean cut of beef and trim visible fat before cooking.
  • Add a plate of sliced cucumber or steamed greens to bulk up the meal with few calories.

History

Sate Padang comes from the Minangkabau people of West Sumatra and is named after the regional capital, Padang. It is sold from street carts across Indonesia today, and local variants such as sate Padang Panjang differ mainly in the colour and spice of the gravy.
