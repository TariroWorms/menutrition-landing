🍩 Pączki

Origin: Poland

Pączki are Polish doughnuts: rich, round yeast-dough buns deep-fried until deep golden, filled with jam such as rose petal or plum, and topped with icing or sugar. A small splash of spirit is traditionally added to the dough so it absorbs less fat while frying. They are denser and richer than many doughnuts, with a soft, slightly chewy crumb.

Recipe

12 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 25 g fresh yeast or 7 g dried yeast
  • 250 ml warm water
  • 4 egg yolks and 1 whole egg
  • 80 g sugar
  • 1 tbsp vodka
  • A pinch of salt
  • Oil for deep-frying
  • 200 g plum or rose jam; icing made from 150 g icing sugar and 2 tbsp water

Method

  1. Mix the yeast with a little warm water, a spoon of sugar and flour and leave for 15 minutes until foamy.
  2. Beat the yolks, egg and sugar, then knead with the flour, starter, remaining water, vodka and salt into a smooth soft dough; let it rise for 1 hour.
  3. Roll to 1.5 cm, cut 7 cm rounds and let them rise for 30 minutes.
  4. Fry at 170 °C for about 2 minutes per side until deep golden and drain.
  5. Fill with jam using a piping bag and spread with the icing.

How to make it lighter

  • Make them smaller — a mini doughnut is a satisfying portion with far fewer calories.
  • Skip the icing and dust lightly with sugar instead.
  • Treat it as a once-a-year festive dessert rather than an everyday snack.

History

Pączki are especially linked to Fat Thursday (Tłusty Czwartek), the last Thursday before Lent, when Poles traditionally eat them in large numbers. The tradition has travelled with Polish communities abroad, where Pączki Day is celebrated just before Lent.
