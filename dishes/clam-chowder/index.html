New England clam chowder is a thick, creamy soup of tender clams, diced potatoes, onion and smoky bacon simmered in a cream-enriched broth. The potatoes soften and thicken the soup naturally while the clams add a briny sweetness. It is served hot in bowls, a classic of seaside restaurants along the US Northeast coast.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg fresh clams, or 300 g cooked clam meat plus 300 ml clam juice
  • 100 g bacon, diced
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 500 g potatoes, peeled and diced
  • 250 ml cream
  • 1 bay leaf, fresh thyme
  • Salt, black pepper, chopped parsley

Method

  1. Steam the clams in 500 ml water until they open, then remove the meat and chop it; strain and keep the liquid.
  2. Fry the bacon until crisp, add the onion and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the potatoes, clam liquid, bay leaf and thyme and simmer for 15 minutes until the potatoes are tender.
  4. Mash a few potato cubes against the side of the pot to thicken, then stir in the cream and clams.
  5. Heat gently without boiling for 3–4 minutes, season and serve with parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the bacon and pour off its fat before adding the onion.
  • Replace part of the cream with clam broth — mashed potato keeps the soup thick.
  • A cup-sized portion with a green salad is a lighter meal than a large bowl.

History

Chowder has been made in New England since at least the 18th century, and the name is usually linked to the French chaudière, a cooking pot. Cream-based clam chowder became the signature style of Boston and the New England coast, distinct from the tomato-based Manhattan version.
