Cheese empanada

Origin: Chile · Central Chile

The Chilean cheese empanada is a half-moon of thin, crisp pastry filled with melted cheese. It is usually fried and eaten hot, straight from the pan, while the cheese is stretchy. It is a simple, popular snack and a favourite vegetarian option.

What it is made of

pastrycheese

Recipe

10 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 100 g butter or lard, melted
  • 1 egg
  • about 150 ml warm water
  • 400 g melting cheese, grated or cubed (mozzarella or similar)
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Make a smooth dough from the flour, salt, butter, egg and water; rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Roll thinly and cut into 12–14 cm circles.
  3. Place about 40 g of cheese in the centre of each circle.
  4. Fold over and seal the edges tightly with a fork so the cheese cannot escape.
  5. Fry in hot oil for 3–4 minutes per side until golden, drain, and eat while hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake instead of frying (200 °C, brushed with beaten egg): the empanada absorbs far less oil.
  • Choose a lower-fat melting cheese or mix cheese with a little spinach for fewer calories.
  • Pair it with a salad and tomato, and eat one rather than two.

History

The cheese empanada is one of the most common empanadas in Chile and is sold at street stalls, bakeries and festivals. Its filling is simple: a soft cheese that melts well. Some cooks sprinkle it with a little sugar, a habit in some regions. It is a popular snack at national celebrations alongside the empanada de pino.
