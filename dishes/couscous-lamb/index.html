Couscous with lamb is a North African dish of steamed semolina grains served under a slow-cooked stew of lamb, chickpeas, carrots and onions flavoured with ras el hanout. The couscous is light and fluffy, the lamb tender, and the broth fragrant with warm spices. It is served on a large shared dish with extra broth poured over at the table.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g lamb shoulder, in large pieces
  • 300 g couscous
  • 1 can chickpeas (400 g), drained
  • 3 carrots, halved
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 tsp ras el hanout
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1.5 l water, salt

Method

  1. Brown the lamb and onions in olive oil with the ras el hanout and salt.
  2. Add the water, cover and simmer for 1 hour.
  3. Add the carrots and chickpeas and cook 30 minutes more until the lamb is very tender.
  4. Pour 300 ml hot broth over the couscous, cover for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork.
  5. Mound the couscous on a platter, top with lamb and vegetables and serve with broth.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the lamb and skim fat from the broth.
  • Take more vegetables and broth and a smaller mound of couscous.
  • Fluff the couscous with broth instead of extra oil — 1 tbsp oil is about 120 kcal.

History

Couscous is a staple of the Berber (Amazigh) peoples of the Maghreb and has been eaten across Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia for many centuries. In 2020 the knowledge and practices of couscous were added to UNESCO's intangible heritage list as a joint nomination by four Maghreb countries.
