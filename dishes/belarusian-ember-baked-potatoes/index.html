Potatoes baked in embers

Origin: Belarus

Pechanaya bulba are whole unpeeled potatoes buried in the glowing embers of a fire until the skins char and the inside turns fluffy and smoky. They are broken open by hand, sprinkled with coarse salt and eaten with thin slices of salo and rye bread. The dish is tied to evenings outdoors by a fire.

What it is made of

potatosalo (cured pork fat)salt

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 1000 g potatoes
  • 120 g salo (cured pork fatback)
  • salt and black pepper — to taste
  • rye bread

Method

  1. Build a fire and let it burn down to a thick bed of glowing embers.
  2. Choose medium potatoes of similar size and wash them; do not peel.
  3. Bury the potatoes in the embers and cover them with hot ash.
  4. Bake for 40–50 minutes, until a skewer slides in easily.
  5. Pull them out, brush off the ash and break them open.
  6. Season with coarse salt and serve with thinly sliced salo and rye bread.

How to make it lighter

  • Eat the potatoes with herbs and a drizzle of oil instead of salo for a vegan option.
  • Wrap them in foil for a cleaner result with less charring.
  • Serve with fresh tomatoes, cucumbers and green onion.

History

Baking potatoes in the ashes after the autumn harvest has long been a rural custom in Belarus, especially among children watching the herds. Today it remains a favorite at camping trips and outdoor gatherings.
