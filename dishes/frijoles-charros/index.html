🫘 Charro beans (Frijoles charros)

Origin: Mexico · Northern Mexico

Frijoles charros are pinto beans simmered with bacon, sausage, tomato, onion and jalapeño into a hearty, brothy stew. The name refers to the charro, the Mexican horseman. It is eaten as a side with grilled meat or as a filling main with tortillas.

Recipe

6 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g dried pinto beans
  • 150 g bacon, diced
  • 150 g sausage (chorizo or smoked), sliced
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion and 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 jalapeños, chopped
  • Fresh cilantro, salt

Method

  1. Rinse the beans and simmer them in plenty of water with half the onion for 1.5–2 hours until nearly tender.
  2. Fry the bacon until the fat renders, add the sausage and brown lightly.
  3. Add the remaining onion, garlic, jalapeño and tomato and cook for 8 minutes.
  4. Tip the mixture into the beans, season with salt and simmer 30 minutes more until creamy and brothy.
  5. Finish with chopped cilantro and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Beans bring good protein and fibre (about 6 g protein per 100 g here); the bacon and sausage supply most of the fat.
  • Halve the bacon and sausage, or use lean smoked sausage, to cut calories while keeping the flavour.
  • Serve with a fresh tomato salsa and a single tortilla rather than a big pile of rice and bread.

History

This dish is tied to the ranch and cowboy cooking of northern Mexico, where beans are a daily staple and cured pork adds flavour. It is a common companion to carne asada at family barbecues. Recipes vary by household in the choice of sausage and chiles.
