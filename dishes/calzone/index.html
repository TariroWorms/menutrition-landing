🥟 Calzone

A calzone is pizza dough folded into a half-moon around a filling, sealed and baked until the crust is crisp and golden. The classic filling combines mozzarella, ricotta, ham and a little tomato sauce, which melt together into a creamy, savoury centre. It is eaten hot, often with extra tomato sauce on top or on the side.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g pizza dough (flour, water, yeast, salt, 1 tbsp olive oil)
  • 250 g ricotta
  • 200 g mozzarella, diced and drained
  • 150 g cooked ham, diced
  • 150 ml tomato sauce
  • Black pepper, dried oregano

Method

  1. Let the dough rise until doubled, then divide it into 4 balls.
  2. Mix the ricotta, mozzarella and ham with pepper and oregano.
  3. Roll each ball into a 25 cm round, spread a spoonful of tomato sauce over one half and top with the filling.
  4. Fold over, press the edges firmly to seal and cut a small vent on top.
  5. Bake at 240 °C for 15–18 minutes until golden; serve with the remaining sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • A whole calzone is a large meal: share one between two with a big green salad.
  • Use light mozzarella and ricotta and fill with half cheese, half grilled vegetables.
  • Skip brushing the crust with oil before baking — the dough browns well without it.

History

The calzone comes from Naples, the home of pizza, where folding the dough made a pizza easier to eat on the street; the name means “trouser leg” in Italian. Fried folded pizzas are still sold as street food in Naples today. Baked calzoni are now served in pizzerias around the world.
