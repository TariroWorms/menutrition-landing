🍖 Coda alla vaccinara (Roman oxtail stew)

Origin: Italy · Rome, Lazio

Coda alla vaccinara is a Roman stew of oxtail braised for hours in tomato sauce until the meat slips off the bone. Near the end, celery is added along with a sweet-and-sour touch of raisins, pine nuts and a little cocoa, which gives the sauce depth and a glossy dark colour. It is eaten as a main course, and the sauce is often mopped up or served over pasta separately.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg oxtail, cut into pieces
  • 800 g canned chopped tomatoes
  • 4 celery stalks, cut into 5 cm pieces
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, finely chopped
  • 30 g raisins, soaked
  • 20 g pine nuts
  • 1 tsp unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, salt, pepper, 1 clove

Method

  1. Blanch the oxtail in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain and pat dry.
  2. Brown the oxtail in the oil, then add the onion and carrot and cook until soft.
  3. Add the tomatoes, clove, salt and a glass of water; cover and simmer gently for 3–3½ hours, adding water if needed.
  4. Boil the celery for 5 minutes and add it to the pot with the raisins, pine nuts and cocoa; cook 15 minutes more.
  5. Rest briefly and serve the oxtail in its thick sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Oxtail is fatty: chill the stew overnight and lift off the fat before reheating.
  • Use 1 tbsp oil for browning instead of a generous pour — 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve with plenty of extra celery or a green salad alongside the stew.

History

The dish belongs to Rome's quinto quarto tradition, the “fifth quarter” of offal and cheaper cuts left after the prime meat was sold; vaccinari were the butchers who worked with cattle. Today it is a staple of Roman trattorias.
