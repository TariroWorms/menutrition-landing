🥩 Vitello Tonnato

Origin: Italy · Piedmont

Vitello tonnato is a cold Piedmontese dish of lean veal, cooked until just tender, chilled and sliced very thin, then covered with a smooth, creamy tuna sauce. The sauce is made from tuna blended with an egg-based emulsion and lemon, and is dotted with salty capers. It is served cool as a starter or summer main, and is a traditional festive dish in northern Italy.

What it is made of

vealtuna saucecaperslemon

Recipe

6 servings100 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g veal topside
  • 150 g canned tuna, drained
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 100 ml oil
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 2 tbsp capers
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Simmer or roast the veal until just cooked through, about 45–60 minutes, then cool completely.
  2. Whisk the egg yolks with lemon juice and slowly drizzle in the oil to make a thick emulsion.
  3. Blend in the tuna and half the capers until smooth, and season.
  4. Slice the cold veal very thinly, cover with the sauce and top with the remaining capers. Chill for 1 hour before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Spread the sauce thinly: the sauce carries most of the fat.
  • Use tuna in water rather than oil for the sauce.
  • Serve with a large salad to make a lighter summer main.

History

Vitello tonnato comes from Piedmont, where it has been made since at least the nineteenth century; early versions used a sauce of tuna without egg, while the creamier emulsion-based sauce became common later.
