Tamales de elote (Salvadoran sweet corn tamales)

Origin: El Salvador

Tamales de elote are made from ground fresh corn, a little sugar and butter, wrapped in husks and steamed until set. The result is tender, lightly sweet and tastes strongly of corn. They are eaten for breakfast or as a snack, often with sour cream or fresh cheese.

Recipe

8 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 8 fresh corn cobs, kernels cut off (about 800 g)
  • 60 g butter, melted
  • 80 g sugar
  • 100 ml milk
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 12 corn husks

Method

  1. Soak the husks in warm water until pliable.
  2. Grind the kernels with milk in a blender to a coarse paste.
  3. Mix in butter, sugar and salt.
  4. Spoon about 3 tablespoons onto each husk and fold.
  5. Steam in a covered pot for 60 minutes.
  6. Rest 10 minutes and serve warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Reduce sugar for a more savoury tamal.
  • Replace butter with a little oil for less saturated fat.
  • Serve with fresh cheese rather than cream.

History

Corn tamales are among the oldest foods of Mesoamerica. In El Salvador, sweet elote versions are made in the corn season and are a favourite afternoon treat.
