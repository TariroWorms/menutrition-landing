Arishta (Armenian homemade noodles)

Origin: Armenia

Arishta are thin egg noodles rolled and cut by hand, then dried so they keep for months. Before cooking they are toasted in butter until golden, which gives them a nutty taste, and then boiled or steamed until tender. They are served hot with cool garlic matsun, and sometimes with fried onion.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 60 ml water
  • salt
  • 40 g butter
  • 300 g matsun or yogurt
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 onion (optional)

Method

  1. Knead the flour, eggs, water and salt into a stiff dough and rest 20 minutes.
  2. Roll very thin, dust with flour and cut into thin strips.
  3. Let the noodles dry for a few hours or overnight.
  4. Toast the noodles in the butter, stirring, until golden.
  5. Boil them in salted water for 5–7 minutes until tender and drain.
  6. Serve hot topped with matsun mixed with crushed garlic, and fried onion if you like.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the butter and toast the noodles in a non-stick pan.
  • Use low-fat yogurt for the topping.
  • Mix part wholemeal flour into the dough for more fibre.

History

Making and drying noodles at the end of summer was a common household task in Armenian villages. The word arishta is related to similar noodle names used across Iran and the Caucasus.
