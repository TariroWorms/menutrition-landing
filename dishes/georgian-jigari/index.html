Jigari (fried liver, heart and lungs)

Origin: Georgia

Jigari is a quick pan dish of diced liver, heart and lungs, usually from beef or lamb, fried with lots of onion. It is seasoned with salt, black pepper, chili, coriander and sometimes a splash of wine vinegar or pomegranate juice at the end. The result is tender and deeply savoury, and it is eaten hot with bread and fresh herbs.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g beef liver
  • 300 g beef heart
  • 200 g beef lungs
  • 2 onions (200 g)
  • 2 tbsp oil (25 ml)
  • 1 tsp chili, 1 tsp ground coriander
  • pomegranate seeds and cilantro

Method

  1. Clean the offal and cut it into small cubes, keeping the liver separate.
  2. Fry the heart and lungs in oil over high heat for 10 minutes.
  3. Add sliced onion and cook until golden.
  4. Add the liver and cook for 5 minutes more so it stays tender inside.
  5. Season with salt, pepper, chili and coriander and finish with pomegranate seeds and herbs.

How to make it lighter

  • Liver is very rich in vitamin A and iron, so keep portions moderate.
  • Use a non-stick pan and just one spoon of oil.
  • Serve with a vegetable salad and skip extra bread.

History

The name comes from a Persian and Turkic word for liver, and similar fried offal dishes are common throughout the Caucasus. In Georgia it is a typical dish after the slaughter of an animal for a celebration.
