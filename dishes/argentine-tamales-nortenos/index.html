Tamales norteños (Argentine beef tamales)

Origin: Argentina · Northwest

Argentine tamales are made from ground corn mixed with squash and fat, filled with finely chopped beef cooked with onion, paprika and cumin. Each one is folded into dried corn husks, tied with a strip of husk and boiled until firm. The result is soft, earthy and gently spiced, eaten hot from the husk at fairs, markets and home gatherings.

Recipe

8 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g fine cornmeal or masa flour
  • 300 g cooked mashed squash
  • 80 g lard or oil
  • 600 ml hot beef broth
  • 400 g beef, boiled and finely chopped
  • 1 large onion (200 g)
  • 10 g paprika, 3 g cumin, chili to taste
  • 16–20 dried corn husks, soaked

Method

  1. Cook the onion in a little fat, add the beef, paprika, cumin and chili and cook until fairly dry.
  2. Mix the cornmeal with squash, the remaining fat, salt and enough broth for a soft dough.
  3. Lay two overlapping husks, spread a layer of dough and add a spoonful of filling.
  4. Fold the husks to enclose the filling and tie with a thin strip of husk.
  5. Stand the tamales in a pot, cover with salted boiling water and simmer for about 1 hour.
  6. Drain briefly and serve hot in the husk.

How to make it lighter

  • Replace lard with a smaller amount of vegetable oil.
  • Use lean beef and add more squash to the dough.
  • Pair one tamal with a fresh tomato and onion salad instead of eating several.

History

Tamales are an ancient food of the Americas, and the version from Salta, Jujuy and Tucumán reflects Andean corn traditions. They are typically larger and tied differently from Mexican tamales.
