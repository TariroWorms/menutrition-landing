Swedish pancakes (Pannkakor)

Origin: Sweden

Pannkakor are thin, delicate pancakes made from a runny milk-and-egg batter and fried until golden at the edges. They are soft, lightly buttery and only mildly sweet. Rolled or folded, they are eaten warm with lingonberry jam and a spoonful of whipped cream.

What it is made of

milkeggflourbutter

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml milk
  • 3 eggs
  • 150 g wheat flour
  • 40 g butter
  • ¼ tsp salt
  • 10 g sugar
  • 100 g lingonberry jam
  • 150 ml cream

Method

  1. Whisk the flour with half the milk until smooth, then beat in the eggs.
  2. Stir in the remaining milk, sugar, salt and melted butter and let the batter rest for 20 minutes.
  3. Heat a pan with a little butter and pour in a thin layer of batter.
  4. Fry about a minute per side until golden, and repeat with the rest.
  5. Serve warm with lingonberry jam and whipped cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half wholemeal flour for more fibre.
  • Replace the cream with plain yogurt to cut fat.
  • Fry in a non-stick pan with very little butter.

History

Thin pancakes are an everyday home dish in Sweden and are closely linked to Thursday pea soup, which they traditionally follow as dessert. The batter is much thinner than in American pancakes, so they are closer to French crêpes.
