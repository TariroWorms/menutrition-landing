🍳 Oyakodon

Oyakodon is a Japanese rice bowl of chicken and onion simmered in a sweet-savoury broth of dashi, soy sauce and mirin, then bound with lightly beaten egg that is left soft and silky. Everything is slid over a bowl of hot steamed rice so the sauce soaks in. The name means “parent-and-child bowl”, because both chicken and egg go into it.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g chicken thigh, bite-sized pieces
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 4 eggs, lightly beaten
  • 150 ml dashi
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp mirin
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 400 g hot cooked Japanese rice
  • Spring onion to garnish

Method

  1. Bring the dashi, soy sauce, mirin and sugar to a simmer in a small frying pan.
  2. Add the onion and chicken and cook for 6–7 minutes until the chicken is done.
  3. Pour in the beaten egg in a circle, cover and cook for about 1 minute until just set but still soft.
  4. Divide the rice between bowls, slide the chicken and egg on top with the sauce and garnish with spring onion.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a smaller portion of rice — it makes up most of the bowl's carbohydrate.
  • Choose chicken breast instead of thigh for a leaner bowl.
  • Add extra onion or spinach to the simmering broth for more volume.

History

Oyakodon is usually credited to Tamahide, a Tokyo restaurant specialising in chicken, in the late 19th century, where it is said to have started from a dish of chicken simmered with egg. It has since become one of Japan's most common home and lunch-counter donburi.
