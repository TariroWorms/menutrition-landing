Potjiekos (South African pot stew)

Origin: South Africa

Potjiekos is a stew cooked in a three-legged cast-iron pot over open coals. Meat is browned first, then vegetables are layered on top and left to steam without stirring. The result is tender, mellow and savory, shared with rice or bread.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g beef chuck, cubed
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 4 carrots, sliced
  • 600 g potatoes, halved
  • 300 g cabbage, wedges
  • 400 g tomatoes
  • 250 ml beef stock
  • 2 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot and brown the beef.
  2. Add the onions and cook until soft.
  3. Layer tomatoes, carrots, potatoes and cabbage on top, seasoning each layer.
  4. Pour in the stock and cover tightly.
  5. Simmer gently for 2 hours without stirring.
  6. Rest 10 minutes and serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and trim the fat before browning.
  • Add extra vegetables such as pumpkin or butternut.
  • Serve with brown rice instead of white.

History

The pot descends from the cast-iron cooking pots carried by Dutch settlers and later by Voortrekkers. Cooking a potjie outdoors with friends is a social weekend tradition. Old rules say the lid stays on and no stirring is allowed.
