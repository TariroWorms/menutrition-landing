🌭 Alexandrian sausage (sogo’ eskandarani)

Sogo’ eskandarani is spicy Egyptian beef sausage, cut into pieces and sautéed with onion, garlic, tomato and hot chili until it sits in a thick, glossy, peppery sauce. The sausage itself is heavily spiced, so the dish is bold, juicy and quite hot. In Egypt it is a street-food classic, most often stuffed into baladi bread, but it is also eaten straight from the pan with salad and pickles.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g spicy beef sausage, cut into 2 cm pieces
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 green pepper, sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped, and 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1–2 green chilies, chopped
  • ½ tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp coriander, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Brown the sausage pieces in the oil over high heat for 5 minutes, then lift them out.
  2. In the same pan cook the onion and green pepper for 5 minutes, add the garlic and chilies and cook for 1 minute.
  3. Add the tomatoes, tomato paste, cumin and coriander and a splash of water and cook for 5 minutes until saucy.
  4. Return the sausage and cook for 5 more minutes. Season and serve hot with salad and pickles.

How to make it lighter

  • The sausage releases plenty of its own fat — you can skip the oil and drain the fat after browning.
  • Bulk it up with extra peppers, onion and tomato and use less sausage per person.
  • Serve it with a large salad and count each extra portion of sausage separately.

History

As the name says, the dish is associated with Alexandria, Egypt’s Mediterranean port city, where sausage sandwich shops are a familiar sight. Its precise origins are not well documented. Today Alexandrian sausage is sold by street vendors and sandwich shops across Egypt and is a common quick meal at home.
