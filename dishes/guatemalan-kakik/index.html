Kak'ik

Origin: Guatemala · Alta Verapaz

Kak'ik is a vivid red turkey soup flavored with dried chiles, tomatoes, achiote and plenty of fresh coriander. The broth is warming and peppery, with tender pieces of turkey. In Alta Verapaz it is served in a bowl with rice or tamalitos.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg turkey pieces
  • 5 tomatoes
  • 3 guajillo chiles
  • 1 onion
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp achiote
  • 1 bunch cilantro
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 l water

Method

  1. Boil the turkey in water with onion and salt until tender, about 60 minutes.
  2. Roast tomatoes, chiles and garlic, then blend with achiote.
  3. Strain the sauce into the broth.
  4. Simmer 15 minutes.
  5. Add chopped cilantro stems and leaves.
  6. Serve hot with rice or tamalitos.

How to make it lighter

  • Use turkey breast to keep it lean.
  • Skim the fat from the broth.
  • Control heat by removing chile seeds.

History

Kak'ik comes from the Q'eqchi' Maya people of Alta Verapaz, and its name means "red chili" in their language. It remains a festive dish served at celebrations and recognized as part of Guatemala's cultural heritage.
