🐟 Salt cod croquetas

Croquetas de bacalao are creamy Spanish croquettes made with flaked salt cod folded into a thick béchamel, then rolled in egg and breadcrumbs and fried. The fish gives a gentle salty, savoury flavour against the smooth, rich filling and the crisp coating. They are a classic tapa in bars across Spain, especially in the north and during Lent.

Recipe

6 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g salt cod, soaked for 24–48 hours with several water changes
  • Half a small onion, very finely chopped
  • 60 g butter
  • 70 g flour
  • 600 ml milk
  • Pinch of nutmeg, black pepper
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 100 g fine breadcrumbs
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Poach the soaked cod in the milk for 5 minutes, lift it out, remove skin and bones and flake it finely. Keep the milk.
  2. Melt the butter, cook the onion for 2 minutes, stir in the flour and cook for 1 minute.
  3. Gradually whisk in the warm milk and cook, stirring, for 8–10 minutes until very thick. Fold in the cod and season with nutmeg and pepper.
  4. Spread in a dish, cover the surface and chill for at least 4 hours.
  5. Shape into small logs, roll in beaten egg and then breadcrumbs, and fry in 175 °C oil for 2–3 minutes until golden. Drain and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake instead of frying: brush with a little oil and bake at 200 °C for 15–20 minutes, turning once.
  • Count them before you start — each croqueta is small but rich, so three or four with a salad make a portion.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk for the béchamel; the texture stays creamy.

History

Salt cod has been a staple in Spain and Portugal for centuries because it keeps without refrigeration, and it was traditionally eaten on meat-free days. Combining it with French-style béchamel croquettes produced one of the most popular croqueta fillings in Spanish bars.
