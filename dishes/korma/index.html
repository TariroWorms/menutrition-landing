Chicken korma

Origin: India · Mughlai cuisine

Korma is a mild, creamy curry of chicken gently simmered in yogurt, cream and ground almonds, scented with cardamom and coriander. It has little chili heat, so the flavour is rich, nutty and slightly sweet. It is usually served with rice or flatbread and is a favourite for those who prefer gentle spicing.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g boneless chicken thighs, cubed
  • 150 g plain yogurt
  • 100 ml cream
  • 50 g ground almonds
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves and 15 g ginger, grated
  • 6 cardamom pods, lightly crushed
  • 2 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, 150 ml water, salt
  • Fresh coriander to serve

Method

  1. Mix the chicken with the yogurt and a pinch of salt and leave for 20 minutes.
  2. Fry the onion in oil until soft and pale golden, then add garlic, ginger, cardamom and ground coriander for 1 minute.
  3. Add the chicken with its yogurt and cook gently for 5 minutes.
  4. Stir in the almonds and 150 ml water, cover and simmer for 20 minutes.
  5. Stir in the cream, warm through without boiling and scatter with coriander.

How to make it lighter

  • Replace half the cream with extra yogurt for a lighter sauce.
  • Use chicken breast and measure the oil: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve with a measured portion of rice and a large salad or plain vegetables.

History

Korma is associated with the Mughal court kitchens of northern India, where meat braised in yogurt and nuts was a refined dish. The name is usually linked to an Urdu word for braising. A milder, creamier style later became one of the best-known curries in British Indian restaurants.
