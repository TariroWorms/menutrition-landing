🍢 Mici (mititei)

Mici, or mititei, are small skinless Romanian grilled sausages made from a mix of minced beef, pork and lamb, seasoned with plenty of garlic, thyme, cumin and black pepper. A little baking soda makes them light and springy. Charred outside and juicy inside, they are served hot from the grill with pickles and salad, often at barbecues.

Recipe

6 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g minced beef
  • 250 g minced pork
  • 250 g minced lamb
  • 6 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 2 tsp dried thyme, 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp salt, 1 tsp black pepper
  • 150 ml cold water

Method

  1. Mix the meats with the garlic, spices, salt and baking soda.
  2. Knead well, adding the cold water a little at a time, until sticky and smooth.
  3. Cover and chill for at least 12 hours to let the flavours develop.
  4. Shape into finger-thick rolls about 10 cm long.
  5. Grill over hot coals or a griddle for 8–10 minutes, turning, until browned and cooked through.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef for most of the mix and less pork.
  • Count 3–4 mici as a portion and fill the rest of the plate with salad.
  • Grill rather than pan-fry so fat drips away.

History

Mici are usually said to have been invented in a Bucharest inn in the late 19th century, when a cook ran out of sausage casings and grilled the meat without them — a story that cannot be verified. Today they are Romania's favourite grill food, especially for the 1 May holiday.
