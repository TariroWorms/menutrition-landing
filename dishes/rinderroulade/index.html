🥩 Rinderrouladen

Rinderrouladen are thin slices of beef spread with mustard, rolled around bacon, onion and pickled gherkin, then browned and braised until tender in a dark gravy. The meat is soft, the filling tangy and smoky, and the gravy rich. They are a classic German Sunday or holiday dish, traditionally served with braised red cabbage and potatoes or dumplings.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thin slices beef topside (about 150 g each)
  • 4 tsp mustard
  • 4 slices bacon
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 pickled gherkins, cut into strips
  • 400 ml beef gravy
  • 400 g red cabbage, shredded
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Season the beef, spread with mustard and top with bacon, onion and gherkin.
  2. Roll up tightly and secure with toothpicks or string.
  3. Brown the rolls in the oil, pour in the gravy, cover and braise on low heat for 1.5 hours.
  4. Meanwhile, cook the red cabbage with a splash of water and vinegar until soft.
  5. Slice or serve the rolls whole with the gravy and red cabbage.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the beef and use thin bacon slices.
  • Skim fat from the gravy before serving.
  • Fill half the plate with red cabbage and keep potatoes to a small side.

History

Beef roulades are eaten across Germany and are especially associated with family Sunday lunches and Christmas. Similar rolled, braised meat dishes exist throughout Central Europe, and the exact origin of the German version is not documented.
