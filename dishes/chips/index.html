Potato chips (crisps in the UK) are wafer-thin slices of potato fried in oil until crunchy and seasoned simply with salt. They are light, salty and crackly, and very calorie-dense for their size. Usually eaten as a snack from a bag, they also appear as a side with sandwiches.

What it is made of

potatooilsalt

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 2 medium potatoes (about 400 g)
  • Vegetable oil for frying
  • Salt

Method

  1. Slice the potatoes as thinly as possible, ideally on a mandoline.
  2. Rinse the slices in cold water to remove the starch and dry them very well.
  3. Fry in small batches in oil at 170–180 °C for 2–3 minutes until golden and crisp.
  4. Drain on paper and salt immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • Portion a small bowl instead of eating from the bag — 30 g is a typical serving.
  • Bake thin slices at 180 °C on a lined tray with a light brush of oil instead of frying.
  • Pair chips with fresh vegetables like cucumber or carrot sticks to fill up.

History

Thinly fried potato slices appear in English cookbooks from the early 19th century. A popular American story credits the chef George Crum of Saratoga Springs with inventing them in 1853, but this legend is disputed. Packaged chips became a mass snack in the 20th century.
