Kotlet schabowy is Poland's classic breaded pork cutlet: a slice of pork loin pounded thin, coated in flour, beaten egg and breadcrumbs, and pan-fried until golden and crisp. It is the traditional Sunday lunch, served with boiled potatoes and a cabbage or cucumber salad.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 4 slices boneless pork loin, about 150 g each
  • 50 g wheat flour
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 100 g breadcrumbs
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 4 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Pound the pork slices to about 5 mm thick and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Dip each one in flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs, pressing to coat.
  3. Heat the oil in a large pan over medium heat.
  4. Fry the cutlets for 3–4 minutes per side until golden and cooked through.
  5. Drain on paper and serve with potatoes and salad.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat from the loin before pounding.
  • Fry in a non-stick pan with less oil, or bake at 200 °C on a rack.
  • Pair it with a large cucumber or cabbage salad instead of extra potatoes.

History

Breaded cutlets spread through Central Europe in the 19th century, in the same family as the Viennese schnitzel, and a Polish recipe for a breaded pork cutlet is often cited from a cookbook of the 1860s. In the 20th century kotlet schabowy became the most typical Polish home dinner.
