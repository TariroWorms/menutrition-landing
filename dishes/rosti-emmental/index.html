🧀 Rösti with Emmental

Origin: Switzerland · Bern

Rösti with Emmental combines the classic Swiss potato cake with softened onion and a blanket of mild, nutty Emmental melted over the top. The crust is crisp and golden, the cheese stretchy and gentle in flavour. It is served as a filling main course, often with a green salad.

What it is made of

Recipe

2 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g waxy potatoes
  • 100 g Emmental, grated
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 30 g butter
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Black pepper to taste

Method

  1. Boil the potatoes in their skins for 10 minutes, cool completely (ideally overnight), then peel and coarsely grate.
  2. Season the grated potato with salt and pepper.
  3. Soften the onion in a little of the butter for 5 minutes and mix it into the potato.
  4. Melt half the butter in a 24 cm non-stick pan over medium heat, add the potato and press into a flat cake.
  5. Cook 12–15 minutes until golden underneath, flip with the help of a plate, add the remaining butter and cook another 10–12 minutes.
  6. Scatter the cheese over the top, cover and leave 2–3 minutes until melted.

How to make it lighter

  • 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal — use a non-stick pan and brush on a thin layer instead of a generous knob.
  • Use 50 g of a well-aged Emmental instead of 100 g — a stronger cheese gives the same flavour with less.
  • Pair it with a big green salad and keep the rösti portion to about 200 g.

History

Rösti is traditionally linked to the canton of Bern, where it is often said to have been a farmers' breakfast of grated potatoes fried in butter. It later became a Swiss national dish, and the 'Röstigraben' (rösti ditch) is a popular nickname for the cultural line between German- and French-speaking Switzerland.
