🥯 Simit

Origin: Turkey · Istanbul

Simit is a Turkish bread ring dipped in grape molasses and thickly coated with sesame seeds before baking. The crust is crunchy and nutty, the inside chewy and lightly sweet. Sold from street carts and bakeries, it is eaten for breakfast or as a snack, often with tea.

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g bread flour
  • 7 g dried yeast
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 300 ml warm water
  • 120 ml grape molasses
  • 200 g sesame seeds

Method

  1. Mix flour, yeast, salt, sugar and water and knead into a smooth dough; let rise for 1 hour.
  2. Divide into 8 pieces, roll each into a long rope, fold in half, twist and join into a ring.
  3. Dilute the molasses with 120 ml water; dip each ring in it, then press into the sesame seeds on all sides.
  4. Let rest for 15 minutes on a lined tray.
  5. Bake at 220 °C for 18–22 minutes until deep golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Make smaller rings — the same dough gives 10–12 lighter pieces.
  • Eat half a simit with vegetables like tomato and cucumber instead of a whole one.
  • Shake off loose sesame seeds before baking, since the seeds are energy-dense.

History

Ring-shaped breads are recorded in Istanbul from Ottoman times, and simit has long been sold by street vendors in the city. Today it is one of the most common everyday breads in Turkey.
