Griot (Haitian fried pork)

Origin: Haiti

Griot is the dish Haitians cook for celebrations and Sunday tables. Pork shoulder is soaked in sour orange juice and a green seasoning called epis, simmered until soft, then fried so the outside turns deeply browned and crackly. It is eaten with pikliz, fried plantains and rice.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork shoulder, cubed
  • 150 ml sour orange juice (or lime plus orange)
  • 4 tbsp epis seasoning
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper, whole
  • 3 sprigs thyme
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Toss the pork with the sour orange juice, epis, onion, salt and thyme and marinate for at least 4 hours.
  2. Put the meat and marinade in a pot with the whole pepper and a glass of water.
  3. Simmer covered for about 60 minutes until tender and the liquid has reduced.
  4. Lift the pieces out and pat them dry.
  5. Fry in hot oil until deep golden and crisp on the edges.
  6. Serve hot with pikliz and plantains.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim visible fat before cooking.
  • Bake the cooked cubes at 220 C instead of deep frying.
  • Serve with plenty of pikliz and a vegetable side.

History

Griot is considered one of the national dishes of Haiti and appears at family gatherings and holidays across the country. The long marinade and the two-step cooking are typical of Haitian home cooking.
