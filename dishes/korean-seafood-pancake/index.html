🥞 Haemul pajeon (Korean seafood pancake)

Origin: South Korea

Haemul pajeon is a savoury Korean pancake packed with long scallion stalks, squid and shrimp, held together by a thin flour-and-egg batter. It is pan-fried in a wide skillet until the edges are lacy and crisp while the seafood stays tender. It is cut into wedges and shared, often on rainy days, with a tangy dipping sauce.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g wheat flour
  • 1 egg
  • 200 ml cold water
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 bunch scallions, cut into 10 cm lengths
  • 100 g squid, sliced
  • 100 g peeled shrimp
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Dip: 2 tbsp rice vinegar, 1 tsp chili flakes, pinch of sugar

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, egg, water and salt into a thin batter.
  2. Heat oil in a large pan and lay the scallions in a flat layer.
  3. Scatter the squid and shrimp over and pour the batter evenly on top.
  4. Fry over medium heat for 4–5 minutes, flip and cook 4 minutes more until crisp.
  5. Mix the dip, cut the pancake into wedges and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a non-stick pan and 1 tbsp oil instead of 3 (1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal).
  • Add more scallions and seafood and less batter.
  • Share one pancake between two as a starter, not a main.

History

Pajeon, the scallion pancake, is a long-standing Korean dish, and the seafood version is especially associated with Busan and the coast, where Dongnae pajeon is a well-known local speciality. Koreans traditionally eat jeon when it rains, a custom often explained by the sizzle of the pan sounding like rainfall.
