🍤 Shrimp machboos

Origin: Arabian Gulf · Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar

Shrimp machboos, known locally as machboos rubyan, is a Gulf rice dish cooked in a spiced tomato-and-onion broth with dried lime and baharat, finished with juicy shrimp laid on top. The rice is fragrant and tangy, and the shrimp stay tender because they cook only briefly. It is a favourite in coastal towns and is served with salad and daqoos.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g raw shrimp, peeled
  • 400 g basmati rice, soaked 30 minutes
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 dried limes (loomi), pierced
  • 2 tsp baharat and ½ tsp turmeric
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt
  • Chopped coriander to serve

Method

  1. Season the shrimp with half the baharat and turmeric, sear in 1 tbsp oil for 2 minutes and set aside.
  2. Fry the onions in the remaining oil until golden, add the garlic, tomatoes, loomi and the rest of the spices and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Add 750 ml water and salt, bring to the boil and simmer for 10 minutes.
  4. Add the drained rice, cover and cook on low heat for 15 minutes.
  5. Lay the shrimp on top, cover for another 5 minutes, then fluff and serve with coriander.

How to make it lighter

  • Keep the rice to about one cup cooked per plate and add extra salad or daqoos.
  • Measure the oil: 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Choose a larger share of shrimp and a smaller share of rice for a more filling plate.

History

Machboos is one of the best-known rice dishes of the Gulf, and the shrimp version is popular along the coasts of Bahrain, Kuwait and Qatar, where seafood has long been part of everyday cooking.
