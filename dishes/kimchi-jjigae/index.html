Kimchi jjigae is a bubbling Korean stew of sour kimchi, pork and soft tofu, seasoned with gochujang and scattered with scallions. The broth is spicy, tangy and deeply savoury from the fat of the pork and the fermented kimchi. It is served straight from the pot, usually with a bowl of steamed rice and side dishes.

Recipe

3 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g ripe kimchi, chopped, plus 100 ml kimchi juice
  • 200 g pork shoulder, sliced
  • 300 g firm or soft tofu, sliced
  • 1 tbsp gochujang
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 scallions, sliced
  • 1 tsp neutral oil
  • 600 ml water

Method

  1. Brown the pork in the oil in a heavy pot.
  2. Add the kimchi and onion and cook 5 minutes until the kimchi softens.
  3. Stir in the gochujang, kimchi juice and water, bring to the boil and simmer 20 minutes.
  4. Lay the tofu on top and simmer 5 more minutes.
  5. Scatter with scallions and serve bubbling hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use leaner pork or trim visible fat before cooking.
  • Add extra tofu and less pork to keep protein high with less fat.
  • Watch the rice served alongside: a 150 g bowl of cooked rice is a sensible single portion.

History

Kimchi jjigae is one of the most common stews in Korean home cooking and has been made for generations as a way to use kimchi that has grown very sour. It remains a daily staple in Korean households and casual restaurants.
