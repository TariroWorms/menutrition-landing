Sumalak (sprouted wheat Navruz pudding)

Origin: Uzbekistan
160kcal

Sumalak is made from the juice of young wheat sprouts mixed with flour and oil and stirred in a huge kazan for many hours until it turns into a thick, dark brown paste. No sugar is added: the sweetness comes from the sprouted grain itself. It has a malty, slightly caramel taste and is eaten with a spoon or with bread.

What it is made of

Recipe

20 servings1440 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg wheat grains (to sprout)
  • 1 kg flour
  • 200 ml cotton or sunflower oil
  • 6 l water
  • a few clean pebbles (traditional, optional)

Method

  1. Soak the wheat and sprout it for 3–4 days until the shoots are 2–3 cm.
  2. Grind the sprouts, cover with water, squeeze out the juice; repeat 2–3 times.
  3. Heat the oil in a large pot, add the flour and the sprout juice and stir well.
  4. Cook over low heat for 12–20 hours, stirring constantly so it doesn't burn.
  5. Rest covered until morning, then serve the thick brown paste.

How to make it lighter

  • It is naturally sweet; never add sugar.
  • Eat a small bowl: it is still energy-dense.
  • Use less oil at the start; the sprouts carry the flavour.

History

Sumalak is the symbolic dish of Navruz, the spring new year, and is prepared by groups of women who stir it through the night while singing. Its preparation is part of the Navruz traditions recognised by UNESCO, and it is shared among neighbours as a sign of abundance.
