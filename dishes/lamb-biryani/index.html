Lamb biryani is a layered dish of long-grain basmati rice and lamb marinated in yogurt and spices, steamed together in a sealed pot. Saffron streaks the rice gold, fried onions add sweetness and ghee gives richness. The result is fluffy, fragrant rice over tender, spiced meat, served from the pot with raita or salad.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g lamb, bone-in pieces
  • 400 g basmati rice
  • 3 onions, thinly sliced
  • 250 g plain yogurt
  • 1 tbsp garam masala
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste, 1 tsp chili powder, salt
  • A pinch of saffron in 3 tbsp warm water
  • 3 tbsp ghee
  • Fresh mint and coriander

Method

  1. Marinate the lamb with yogurt, garam masala, ginger-garlic paste, chili and salt for at least 1 hour.
  2. Fry the onions in the ghee until deep golden; set half aside.
  3. Add the lamb to the pot and cook covered on low heat for 45–60 minutes until nearly tender.
  4. Meanwhile boil the soaked rice in salted water for 5 minutes until 70% cooked, then drain.
  5. Layer the rice over the lamb, scatter the reserved onions, herbs and saffron water.
  6. Seal the lid tightly and steam on very low heat for 25 minutes. Fluff gently and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean lamb and trim visible fat before marinating.
  • Cut the ghee to 1 tbsp and fry the onions in a non-stick pan.
  • Serve a smaller rice portion with a large cucumber-and-tomato salad.

History

Biryani developed in the royal kitchens of Mughal-era India, drawing on Persian rice cooking; the word is usually traced to Persian birian, 'fried' or 'roasted'. Distinct regional schools grew up, notably the Hyderabadi and Lucknowi (Awadhi) styles. Today it is a celebratory dish across South Asia and the Gulf.
