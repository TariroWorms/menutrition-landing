Rabo de Toro

Origin: Spain · Córdoba, Andalusia

Rabo de toro is a Spanish oxtail stew braised for hours in red wine with onion, carrot, garlic and bay leaf until the meat slides off the bone. The sauce reduces to a deep, glossy gravy rich with gelatine. It is served hot, usually with potatoes, and is a staple of Andalusian taverns.

What it is made of

oxtailwineonioncarrotgarlicbay leaf

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1.5 kg oxtail, in pieces
  • 300 ml red wine
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2 carrots, chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt, pepper, water

Method

  1. Season the oxtail and brown it well in the oil; set aside.
  2. Soften onion, carrot and garlic in the same pot for 10 minutes.
  3. Return the meat, pour in the wine and let it bubble for 5 minutes.
  4. Add bay leaves and water to just cover; simmer covered for 3–3.5 hours.
  5. Remove the meat, blend or strain the sauce, reduce if thin, and pour back over.

How to make it lighter

  • Chill the stew and lift off the solidified fat before reheating.
  • Serve with vegetables instead of fried potatoes.
  • Brown the oxtail with only 1 tbsp oil — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

The dish is strongly associated with Córdoba and Andalusia, where it was traditionally made with the tails of bulls from the bullring. Today it is usually made with ordinary oxtail.
