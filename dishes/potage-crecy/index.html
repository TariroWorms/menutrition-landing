🥕 Potage Crécy

Origin: France · Île-de-France

Potage Crécy is a French cream of carrot soup: carrots and onion are softened in butter, simmered with thyme, pureed until velvety and finished with a little cream. It is naturally sweet from the carrots, smooth and bright orange. It is served hot as a starter, sometimes with a few thyme leaves or a swirl of cream on top.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g carrots, sliced
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 25 g butter
  • 900 ml water or vegetable stock
  • 2 sprigs thyme
  • 60 ml cream
  • Salt and white pepper

Method

  1. Melt the butter in a pot and soften the onion for 5 minutes without browning.
  2. Add the carrots and thyme and cook for 5 more minutes, stirring.
  3. Pour in the water or stock, season and simmer for 20–25 minutes until the carrots are very soft.
  4. Remove the thyme stems and blend until completely smooth.
  5. Stir in the cream, adjust the seasoning and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the butter and soften the onion in a covered pot with a splash of water.
  • Swirl a teaspoon of cream on each bowl instead of stirring it all in.
  • Make the soup thicker with more carrot rather than more cream.

History

In French cuisine, dishes 'à la Crécy' are made with carrots; the name is usually linked to Crécy-la-Chapelle near Paris, a town once known for its carrots. Potage Crécy has long been a standard of classic French cookery.
