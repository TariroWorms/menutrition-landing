🍚 Turkish rice pilaf

Pilav is the Turkish way of cooking rice: grains are first coated in butter, then simmered in stock until every grain is separate, fluffy and glossy. It has a mild, buttery, savoury taste. Pilav is served as a side with stews, kebabs and beans, and is a constant on Turkish home and restaurant tables alike.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g long-grain or baldo rice
  • 30 g butter
  • 600 ml hot chicken stock
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Rinse the rice and soak it in warm salted water for 20 minutes, then drain.
  2. Melt the butter in a pot and stir the rice in it for 2–3 minutes until glossy.
  3. Pour in the hot stock and salt, bring to a boil, then cover and simmer on low for 15 minutes.
  4. Take off the heat, lay a clean towel under the lid and rest for 10 minutes before fluffing.

How to make it lighter

  • Halve the butter — 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal.
  • Keep the rice to about 150 g cooked per serving and fill the plate with salad or vegetables.
  • Use a skimmed, low-fat stock.

History

Pilaf-style rice is cooked from Central Asia to the Balkans, and in Ottoman palace kitchens it was a prized dish with many variations. In Turkey today plain butter pilav is an everyday side dish, especially alongside kuru fasulye (bean stew).
