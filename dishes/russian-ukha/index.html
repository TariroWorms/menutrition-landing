Ukha (Russian clear fish soup)

Origin: Russia

Ukha is a transparent fish soup made by gently simmering freshwater or sea fish with a few root vegetables and spices. The broth is delicate and clean-tasting, and the fish stays in tender pieces. It is finished with dill and black pepper and eaten with bread, sometimes outdoors by the river.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g fish (pike-perch, perch or salmon)
  • 3 potatoes
  • 1 onion
  • 1 carrot
  • 2 bay leaves
  • black peppercorns
  • dill
  • salt

Method

  1. Clean the fish and cut it into large pieces.
  2. Bring 2 l water to a boil with the whole onion and peppercorns.
  3. Add diced potatoes and sliced carrot and cook 10 minutes.
  4. Add the fish and bay leaves and simmer gently for 10-12 minutes.
  5. Remove the onion and season with salt.
  6. Serve with chopped dill.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean white fish for the lightest soup.
  • Skim carefully to keep the broth clear without extra fat.
  • Add more vegetables and fewer potatoes.

History

In old Russian the word ukha could mean any broth, and only later came to mean fish soup specifically. It is closely linked to fishing traditions, and cooking ukha over a campfire is still a popular custom.
