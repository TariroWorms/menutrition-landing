Biscoito de polvilho (cassava starch rings)

Origin: Brazil

Biscoito de polvilho are airy baked snacks made from cassava starch, milk, oil and egg, piped into rings or sticks. They puff up in the oven and become so light and crisp that they almost dissolve in the mouth. In Rio de Janeiro they are sold in bags by beach vendors, and they are naturally gluten-free.

What it is made of

cassava starchmilkoileggsalt

Recipe

6 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g cassava starch (polvilho)
  • 250 ml milk
  • 150 ml vegetable oil
  • 2 eggs
  • 10 g salt

Method

  1. Bring the milk, oil and salt to the boil.
  2. Pour over the cassava starch and mix well with a spoon until crumbly.
  3. Let cool slightly, then add the eggs one at a time and knead to a sticky, smooth paste.
  4. Spoon into a piping bag with a wide nozzle.
  5. Pipe rings or sticks onto a tray, leaving space between them.
  6. Bake at 200 °C for 25 to 30 minutes without opening the oven, until dry and lightly golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less oil and add a little water to keep the dough pipeable.
  • Eat a small handful as a gluten-free alternative to crisps.
  • Store in an airtight jar so you are not tempted to finish a whole bag.

History

Cassava starch has been used in Brazil since long before European arrival, and baked starch snacks developed in the colonial kitchens of Minas Gerais and São Paulo. The bagged rings became an emblem of Rio's beaches in the second half of the twentieth century.
