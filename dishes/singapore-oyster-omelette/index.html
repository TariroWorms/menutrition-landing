Oyster omelette (orh luak)

Origin: Singapore

Orh luak is a chewy-crisp omelette made from a batter of starch and eggs fried with plump small oysters. The edges are crunchy while the centre is soft and sticky. It is served with a sharp chili-lime dip and coriander. It is a classic hawker-centre snack.

What it is made of

oysterseggtapioca starchchili saucecoriander

Recipe

2 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 8 fresh oysters (100 g)
  • 2 eggs
  • 3 tbsp tapioca or potato starch, 100 ml water
  • 1 tbsp chili sauce with lime
  • 1 garlic clove, coriander, 2 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Whisk the starch with the water into a thin batter.
  2. Fry the garlic and oysters in the oil briefly.
  3. Pour in the batter and let it set.
  4. Add the beaten eggs and fry until crisp at the edges.
  5. Serve with chili dip and coriander.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a non-stick pan and less oil.
  • Add bean sprouts for extra vegetables.
  • Share one omelette as a snack.

History

The dish is Hokkien in origin and is common across the Hokkien-influenced coast of Southeast Asia. The starch gives the typical gelatinous texture.
