Arroz tapado (Peruvian rice mould with beef)

Arroz tapado means 'covered rice': a layer of garlicky white rice, a layer of picadillo, and more rice on top, all pressed into a bowl and turned out onto the plate. The beef filling is cooked with onion, ají panca and cumin, then studded with raisins, black olives and chopped hard-boiled egg. Sweet, salty and savoury notes meet in each bite. It is a favourite home lunch, often served with a fried egg or plantain.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g rice
  • 400 g lean ground beef
  • 1 onion (150 g), finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ají panca paste
  • 40 g raisins
  • 50 g black olives, chopped
  • 2 hard-boiled eggs
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 2 tbsp oil, salt, parsley

Method

  1. Cook the rice with a little garlic and salt until fluffy.
  2. Fry onion, garlic, ají panca and cumin in oil for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the beef and cook until browned and dry; stir in raisins and olives.
  4. Chop the eggs and fold them into the filling with parsley.
  5. Press a layer of rice into an oiled bowl, add filling, then more rice.
  6. Turn out onto a plate and serve with salad.

How to make it lighter

  • Use extra-lean beef and drain any fat after browning.
  • Swap half the rice for brown rice for more fibre.
  • Serve with a large salad instead of a fried egg.

History

Arroz tapado is a thrifty Lima home dish, a way to stretch a little meat over many plates. The combination of raisins, olives and egg in the filling is shared with Peruvian empanadas and tamales.
