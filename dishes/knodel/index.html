🥟 Bread Dumplings (Semmelknödel)

Origin: Germany · Bavaria and Austria

Semmelknödel are bread dumplings from southern Germany and Austria, made from cubes of stale white rolls soaked in warm milk and bound with egg, onion softened in butter and chopped parsley. They are shaped into balls and simmered in salted water until light and fluffy. Soft and savoury, they are served as a side to roasts and gravies or mushroom sauce.

What it is made of

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g stale white rolls, cut into small cubes
  • 250 ml milk, warmed
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 15 g butter
  • 3 tbsp chopped parsley
  • Salt, pepper and nutmeg

Method

  1. Pour the warm milk over the bread cubes and leave 15 minutes.
  2. Soften the onion in the butter and add it to the bread with the eggs, parsley and seasoning.
  3. Mix gently with wet hands and shape 8 dumplings.
  4. Simmer in barely bubbling salted water for 20 minutes and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve two dumplings rather than three alongside plenty of vegetables.
  • Choose a light mushroom or vegetable sauce instead of a rich roast gravy.
  • Use low-fat milk; the texture stays almost the same.

History

Bread dumplings have long been a way to use up stale bread in Bavaria, Austria and Bohemia. Today they are a standard side dish in Bavarian and Austrian inns.
