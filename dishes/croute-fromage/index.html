Croûte au fromage is a Swiss Alpine classic: thick slices of bread moistened with white wine, covered with plenty of melting cheese and baked until golden and bubbling, often finished with a fried egg. The bread becomes soft underneath and crisp at the edges, with a rich, tangy cheese crust. It is a hearty mountain-restaurant meal, served with pickles or a salad.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thick slices of day-old bread
  • 200 g Gruyère or Vacherin cheese, grated
  • 100 ml dry white wine
  • 2 eggs
  • Black pepper, pinch of nutmeg

Method

  1. Place the bread in an ovenproof dish and drizzle each slice with the wine.
  2. Mix the cheese with one egg, pepper and nutmeg and spread it thickly over the bread.
  3. Bake at 220 °C for 10–15 minutes until the cheese is melted and golden.
  4. Meanwhile fry the remaining egg (or one per person) and place it on top before serving, if you like.

How to make it lighter

  • Use 120 g of a strongly flavoured cheese instead of 200 g — the taste stays bold.
  • Skip the fried egg on top, or poach it instead of frying.
  • Serve one slice with a large salad of crisp leaves and pickles rather than two.

History

Cheese croûtes come from the Swiss and French Alps, where they were a thrifty way to use stale bread and local cheese. Today the dish is a fixture on menus in Swiss ski resorts and mountain restaurants.
