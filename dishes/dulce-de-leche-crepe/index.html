🥞 Dulce de leche crepes

Origin: Argentina · Río de la Plata

Dulce de leche crepes are thin, tender pancakes spread with dulce de leche, the thick milk caramel of South America, then folded and warmed in a pan until the filling softens and the edges caramelise. They are topped with chopped walnuts and a spoonful of cream, giving a rich, very sweet dessert with a gentle crunch.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 120 g flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 300 ml milk
  • 15 g butter for the pan
  • 200 g dulce de leche
  • 40 g walnuts, chopped
  • 100 ml whipping cream

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, eggs and milk into a smooth batter and rest 15 minutes.
  2. Lightly butter a hot pan, pour in a thin layer of batter and cook about 1 minute per side; repeat to make 8 crepes.
  3. Spread each crepe with dulce de leche and fold into quarters.
  4. Warm the folded crepes in the pan for 1 minute per side.
  5. Serve topped with walnuts and lightly whipped cream.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a thin layer of dulce de leche: 1 tbsp ≈ 60 kcal.
  • Skip the cream or swap it for fresh fruit.
  • Have one crepe instead of two as a dessert portion.

History

Panqueques con dulce de leche are a classic home and restaurant dessert in Argentina and Uruguay, where dulce de leche is a national passion. Crepes arrived with European immigrants, and filling them with milk caramel became one of the most typical sweets of the Río de la Plata region.
