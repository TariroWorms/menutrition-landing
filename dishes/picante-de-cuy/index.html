Picante de cuy (guinea pig in peanut-chili sauce)

Origin: Peru · Andes

Picante de cuy combines crisp pan-fried guinea pig with a thick, brick-red sauce of ají panca, garlic, onion and ground roasted peanuts. The meat is rich and slightly gamey, and the sauce is earthy, nutty and pleasantly hot. It is served with boiled potatoes, often yellow or native varieties, and sometimes a little rice.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 2 guinea pigs, cleaned and quartered (about 1.4 kg)
  • 80 g roasted peanuts, ground
  • 3 tbsp ají panca paste
  • 1 onion, 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 300 ml broth
  • 800 g potatoes
  • 3 tbsp oil, salt

Method

  1. Season the cuy with salt, garlic and cumin and leave for 30 minutes.
  2. Pan-fry the pieces in hot oil until golden on both sides; set aside.
  3. In the same pan sauté onion, garlic and ají panca for 8 minutes.
  4. Add the peanuts and broth and simmer until the sauce thickens.
  5. Return the cuy to the sauce and cook 10 minutes more.
  6. Serve with boiled potatoes.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake the cuy instead of frying to save a lot of fat.
  • Use half the peanuts and thicken with a little potato instead.
  • Serve a vegetable salad alongside to balance the rich sauce.

History

Guinea pigs were domesticated in the Andes thousands of years ago and are still raised in many highland homes. Cuy is a festive food in regions such as Cusco, Arequipa, Ayacucho and Cajamarca, and picante versions are especially loved in Arequipa.
