🥬 Capuns

Origin: Switzerland · Graubünden

Capuns are parcels of chard leaves wrapped around a soft spaetzle-style dough studded with diced Bündnerfleisch, the air-dried beef of Graubünden. The rolls are simmered and then baked in cream, usually under a layer of melted Gruyère, which gives a rich, savoury, alpine dish. They are served hot as a hearty main course.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 16 large chard leaves
  • 250 g plain flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 120 ml milk
  • 80 g Bündnerfleisch (air-dried beef), finely diced
  • 200 ml cream
  • 60 g Gruyère, grated
  • Salt, pepper, nutmeg, a little chopped parsley

Method

  1. Mix the flour, eggs, milk, salt, pepper and nutmeg into a thick, sticky spaetzle dough; stir in the diced dried beef and parsley.
  2. Blanch the chard leaves for 30 seconds and lay them flat.
  3. Put a spoonful of dough on each leaf and roll it up into a small parcel.
  4. Simmer the parcels in lightly salted water for about 10 minutes, then transfer to a baking dish.
  5. Pour over the cream, sprinkle with Gruyère and bake at 200 °C for 15–20 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half the cream and add a little of the cooking water so the parcels stay moist.
  • Reduce the Gruyère to a light sprinkle on top: the dried beef already gives plenty of flavour.
  • Serve two or three parcels with a large green salad instead of a full plate.

History

Capuns come from Graubünden in eastern Switzerland, where they are considered a signature dish of the Romansh-speaking valleys. Recipes vary from village to village and family to family, and the dish is now a fixture of mountain restaurants across the canton.
