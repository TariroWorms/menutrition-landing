Num banh chok (Khmer rice noodles with fish gravy)

Origin: Cambodia
368kcal

Fresh, soft rice noodles are topped with a thin green gravy of freshwater fish, lemongrass, turmeric and coconut milk. Heaps of raw vegetables and herbs, such as cucumber, beans and mint, go on top. It tastes light, herbal and slightly sweet, and is mostly eaten for breakfast.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g fresh rice noodles
  • 400 g white fish fillet
  • 2 stalks lemongrass
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 200 ml coconut milk
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • cucumber, green beans and herbs to serve

Method

  1. Poach the fish with lemongrass and turmeric until cooked, then flake it.
  2. Pound the fish with the aromatics into a paste.
  3. Simmer the paste with coconut milk, fish sauce and about 400 ml of the poaching liquid for 15 minutes into a thin gravy.
  4. Put noodles in bowls and ladle over the gravy.
  5. Pile on sliced raw vegetables and herbs.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk.
  • Pile on extra vegetables for fibre.
  • Keep the gravy portion modest to limit fat and salt.

History

This is one of the most typical Khmer dishes, served at breakfast, at weddings and at festivals. The noodles were traditionally made by pressing fermented rice dough through a mould, though many cooks now buy them fresh.
