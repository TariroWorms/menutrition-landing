Ajika (Megrelian chili paste)

Origin: Georgia · Samegrelo

Ajika is the hot, aromatic paste of Samegrelo in western Georgia. Red chili peppers are ground with plenty of garlic, salt, coriander seed, blue fenugreek, dill and cilantro into a thick, rust-red paste. Used by the spoonful, it seasons meat, beans and corn porridge, or is spread thinly on bread for those who love heat.

What it is made of

chili peppergarliccilantrocorianderfenugreeksalt

Recipe

20 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g fresh red chili peppers, seeds partly removed
  • 50 g dried chili peppers, soaked
  • 2 heads garlic
  • 1 bunch cilantro
  • ½ bunch dill
  • 2 tsp ground coriander, 1 tsp blue fenugreek
  • 2 tbsp salt

Method

  1. Wear gloves and remove stems and some seeds from the chilies.
  2. Soak the dried chilies in warm water for 20 minutes and drain.
  3. Grind fresh and dried chilies with the garlic into a coarse paste.
  4. Add chopped herbs, spices and salt and grind again until thick and even.
  5. Pack into clean jars and keep in the fridge.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a teaspoon to season food instead of adding oil or rich sauces.
  • Removing more seeds makes ajika milder for sensitive stomachs.
  • Mix a little with yogurt for a lighter dip.

History

Ajika is a cornerstone of Megrelian cooking, a regional cuisine known across Georgia for its bold spice. Similar pastes are also made in neighbouring Abkhazia, and every household has its own balance of chili, garlic and herbs.
