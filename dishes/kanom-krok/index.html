🥥 Khanom krok (Thai coconut pancakes)

Origin: Thailand

Khanom krok are small Thai coconut-rice cakes cooked in a dimpled cast-iron pan. A ground rice and coconut milk batter is poured into the cups, topped with a sweeter, richer coconut cream layer and sprinkled with chopped scallion. They come out crisp at the edges and custardy in the middle, sweet and slightly salty. Two halves are often pressed together and sold hot as street food.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g ground rice
  • 400 ml coconut milk
  • 60 g sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 200 ml water
  • 2 scallions, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp oil for the pan

Method

  1. Whisk 120 g ground rice with 200 ml coconut milk, the water and a pinch of salt to make the base batter.
  2. Whisk the remaining coconut milk with the remaining ground rice, the sugar and the rest of the salt to make the topping.
  3. Heat a dimpled pan and brush the cups lightly with oil.
  4. Fill each cup two-thirds with base batter, then top up with the coconut topping and sprinkle with scallion.
  5. Cover and cook over medium heat for 6–8 minutes until the edges are crisp and the centres set; lift out with a spoon.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk for the base batter to cut fat.
  • Reduce the sugar in the topping by a third; the coconut keeps it sweet.
  • Eat a few pieces as a snack rather than a full tray.

History

Khanom krok is a long-standing Thai street snack, sold from market stalls and carts throughout Thailand. Similar dimpled-pan cakes are made in neighbouring countries.
