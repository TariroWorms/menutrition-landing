🍱 Unadon

Origin: Japan · Tokyo (Edo)

Unadon is a bowl of hot steamed rice topped with charcoal-grilled eel glazed in tare, a sweet, glossy sauce of soy, mirin and sugar. The eel is tender and rich, with caramelized edges, and the sauce soaks into the rice. It is finished with a pinch of sansho pepper, whose citrusy tingle cuts the richness.

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g eel fillets
  • 600 g cooked short-grain rice
  • 60 ml soy sauce
  • 60 ml mirin
  • 30 g sugar
  • Sansho pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Simmer soy sauce, mirin and sugar for 8–10 minutes until syrupy to make the tare.
  2. Grill the eel skin-side down over high heat for 4–5 minutes, then flip and grill 2–3 minutes more.
  3. Brush with tare and grill for 1 minute; repeat the glazing two or three times.
  4. Spoon a little tare over hot rice in bowls, top with the eel and sprinkle with sansho.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a smaller rice portion (about 120 g) and add a side of cucumber or pickled vegetables.
  • Brush on the tare instead of pouring it — the sauce is mostly sugar and soy.
  • Eel is rich in fat; a 70–80 g piece is a satisfying single serving.

History

Grilling eel over charcoal and glazing it with a sweet soy-based sauce became popular in Edo (today's Tokyo) in the 18th–19th centuries. Eating eel on the midsummer Day of the Ox is a custom still widely kept in Japan. Serving the eel over rice in a bowl is usually traced to the early 19th century.
