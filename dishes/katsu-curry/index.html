🍛 Katsu curry

Katsu curry pairs a crisp, breaded pork cutlet with a thick, mildly sweet Japanese curry sauce and a mound of steamed short-grain rice. The cutlet is sliced into strips and laid across the rice, with the glossy brown curry poured alongside. The contrast of crunchy crumb, soft rice and rich, gently spiced sauce makes it a favourite of Japanese diners and canteens.

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 2 pork loin cutlets, about 150 g each
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 60 g panko breadcrumbs
  • 300 g cooked short-grain rice
  • 400 ml prepared Japanese curry sauce
  • 1 onion and 1 carrot, diced
  • Oil for frying, salt, pepper

Method

  1. Fry the onion and carrot in a little oil, add the curry sauce and simmer for 15 minutes.
  2. Season the pork, dip it in the beaten egg and press it into the breadcrumbs.
  3. Fry the cutlets in oil at about 170 °C for 3–4 minutes per side until golden; drain on paper.
  4. Slice the cutlets, set them on the rice and pour the curry alongside.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake or air-fry the breaded cutlet instead of deep-frying to cut the absorbed oil.
  • Serve a smaller scoop of rice and add more carrot and onion to the curry.
  • Choose a lean loin cutlet and trim the fat before breading.

History

Curry reached Japan in the late 19th century through British naval and trading contacts, and pork cutlets (tonkatsu) developed in Tokyo's Western-style restaurants around the same period. The combination became popular during the 20th century; a restaurant in Tokyo's Ginza district is often said to have served it first. Today katsu curry is a staple of Japanese curry chains, school canteens and home cooking.
