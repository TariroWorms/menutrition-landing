Ttvash (Armenian mixed pickles)

Origin: Armenia

Ttvash is the Armenian word for pickles, and a jar usually holds a colourful mix of cabbage, carrot, cauliflower, peppers and green beans. The vegetables stay crunchy and taste sour, salty and garlicky, often with a kick of hot pepper. A plate of ttvash sits on almost every Armenian table next to grilled meat, cheese and lavash.

Recipe

10 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g white cabbage
  • 200 g carrots
  • 300 g cauliflower
  • 2 bell peppers (250 g)
  • 100 g green beans
  • 1 head garlic
  • 1–2 hot peppers
  • a bunch of dill and celery leaves
  • 1.5 l water
  • 60 g coarse salt

Method

  1. Cut the cabbage into wedges, the carrots into sticks and the cauliflower into florets.
  2. Briefly blanch the green beans and cauliflower for one minute, then cool.
  3. Dissolve the salt in the water by bringing it to a boil, then let it cool.
  4. Layer the vegetables in a large jar with garlic, hot pepper, dill and celery leaves.
  5. Pour over the cool brine to cover, weigh down and cover loosely.
  6. Leave at room temperature for 4–6 days until sour, then move to the fridge.

How to make it lighter

  • Ttvash is very low in calories, so it is a great crunchy side for a diet.
  • It is salty, so rinse the vegetables briefly if you watch your sodium.
  • Naturally fermented pickles (no vinegar) also bring beneficial lactic bacteria.

History

Preserving the autumn harvest in salt brine has long been part of household life in Armenia, where winters in the highlands are cold. Families still put up jars of ttvash every autumn.
