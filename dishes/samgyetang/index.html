🍲 Samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup)

Origin: South Korea

Samgyetang is a whole young chicken stuffed with glutinous rice, garlic, jujubes and a ginseng root, then simmered until the meat falls off the bone. The broth is milky, mild and gently earthy from the ginseng, and the rice inside turns soft and sticky. It is served bubbling in an earthenware pot, one small chicken per person, with salt and pepper to season at the table.

Recipe

2 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 2 small chickens (about 500 g each), cleaned
  • 100 g glutinous rice, soaked for 1 hour
  • 2 fresh or dried ginseng roots
  • 12 garlic cloves
  • 4 dried jujubes
  • 2 litres water
  • Salt, black pepper and sliced spring onion to serve

Method

  1. Stuff each chicken with soaked rice, a few garlic cloves, a jujube and a piece of ginseng; cross the legs or tie them to close the cavity.
  2. Place in a pot with the remaining garlic, ginseng and jujubes and cover with water.
  3. Bring to the boil, skim, then simmer covered for about 60–70 minutes until very tender.
  4. Serve each chicken in a bowl of broth with salt, pepper and spring onion on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin before eating — most of the fat sits there.
  • Chill the broth and lift off the layer of fat before reheating.
  • Share one chicken between two with a large side of vegetables if you want a smaller portion.

History

Chicken and ginseng soups have long been made in Korea, and samgyetang in its present restaurant form became widespread in the second half of the 20th century. It is traditionally eaten on the hottest days of summer, the boknal, following the idea of “fighting heat with heat”.
