🐟 Masgouf

Masgouf is Iraq's national fish dish: a whole carp split open along the back, seasoned with salt, tamarind and turmeric, and grilled slowly beside an open wood fire. The flesh turns flaky and smoky with a gentle sour edge. It is served with sliced onion, tomato and lemon, usually shared straight from the platter.

Recipe

4 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 1 whole carp, about 1.5 kg, butterflied and cleaned
  • 2 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 3 tomatoes, sliced
  • 1 lemon, in wedges

Method

  1. Rinse the carp, pat dry and open it flat along the backbone.
  2. Mix tamarind, turmeric, salt and oil; rub over the flesh side.
  3. Grill skin side out beside a hot wood fire or under a high oven grill for 40–50 minutes until the flesh flakes.
  4. Briefly place over the coals skin side down to crisp the skin.
  5. Top with onion and tomato for the last 5 minutes and serve with lemon.

How to make it lighter

  • Fish is the bulk of the plate; pair it with salad and extra vegetables to keep the meal light.
  • Brush on just 1 tbsp oil (≈ 120 kcal) instead of basting repeatedly.
  • A 200–250 g portion of flesh is a satisfying, high-protein serving.

History

Masgouf is strongly associated with the banks of the Tigris in Baghdad, where riverside restaurants on Abu Nuwas Street grilled freshly caught carp for generations. Its roots are usually traced to the long tradition of river fishing in Mesopotamia, though no exact origin is documented.
