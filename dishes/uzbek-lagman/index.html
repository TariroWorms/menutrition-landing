Uzbek lagman

Origin: Uzbekistan

Lagman is a bowl of long, chewy hand-pulled noodles topped with a stew of beef, peppers, tomatoes, carrots and radish. In Uzbekistan it is usually served with plenty of savoury broth, somewhere between a soup and a sauced noodle dish. Garlic, cumin and a spoon of chilli vinegar at the table brighten it up. It is warming, filling and eaten year-round.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g flour, 200 ml water, salt (or 400 g thick fresh noodles)
  • 400 g beef
  • 2 bell peppers
  • 2 tomatoes
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 small radish
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 40 ml oil, cumin
  • 1 l water or broth

Method

  1. Make a soft dough, rest it oiled for 1 hour, then pull into long thin noodles.
  2. Fry diced beef in oil, add onion, carrot and radish and cook 10 minutes.
  3. Add peppers, tomatoes, garlic and cumin, then the broth; simmer 40 minutes.
  4. Boil the noodles 2-3 minutes and rinse.
  5. Place noodles in deep bowls and ladle the beef and vegetable sauce over.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and skim the fat from the broth.
  • Load extra vegetables into the sauce.
  • Serve a smaller noodle portion with more broth.

History

Lagman came to Central Asia with the Uyghur and Dungan communities and is widely eaten across Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan. The noodles are pulled by hand from a single piece of dough, a skill passed down in families and teahouses.
