Panta bhat (water-soaked fermented rice)

Origin: Bangladesh
213kcal

Panta bhat is leftover cooked rice soaked in water overnight until it turns slightly sour and cool. It is eaten in the morning with raw onion, green chilli, salt, a drizzle of mustard oil and often fried hilsa or mashed potato. The taste is mild, tangy and very refreshing, especially in hot weather.

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g cooked rice
  • 300 ml water
  • 1 small onion (60 g), sliced
  • 2 green chillies
  • 1 tsp mustard oil, salt

Method

  1. Place cooked rice in a bowl and cover with room-temperature water.
  2. Leave covered overnight at room temperature.
  3. In the morning add salt and gently stir.
  4. Top with sliced onion, chillies and mustard oil.
  5. Serve with fried fish, mashed potato or dried fish bhorta.

How to make it lighter

  • Do not leave it for more than 12 hours in hot weather.
  • Use brown rice for extra fibre.
  • Add cucumber and lime to boost freshness without extra salt.

History

Panta bhat is a traditional farmers' meal in rural Bengal and a humble way to use leftover rice. On Pohela Boishakh, the Bengali New Year, it became a symbolic dish that many people eat in the morning with fried fish.
