🍝 Tagliatelle Bolognese

Origin: Italy · Emilia-Romagna · Bologna

Tagliatelle alla bolognese are wide ribbon pasta served with ragù, a slow-cooked sauce of minced beef and pork with a base of carrot, celery and onion and a modest amount of tomato. The sauce is thick and meaty rather than tomato-heavy, and the porous ribbons hold it well. In Bologna this, not spaghetti, is the classic way to serve the ragù.

Recipe

4 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g dried tagliatelle
  • 250 g minced beef
  • 150 g minced pork
  • 1 carrot, 1 celery stalk and 1 onion, finely diced
  • 200 g tomato passata
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Gently cook the diced carrot, celery and onion in olive oil for 10 minutes until soft.
  2. Add the beef and pork and brown well, breaking up the meat.
  3. Stir in the passata and a ladle of water, cover partly and simmer for 2–2½ hours, adding water if it gets dry.
  4. Season the ragù; it should be thick and glossy.
  5. Cook the tagliatelle until al dente and toss with the sauce.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef and drain the fat after browning.
  • Double the carrot, celery and onion to stretch the sauce with vegetables.
  • Serve 80 g dry pasta per person with plenty of sauce and a green salad.

History

Meat ragù has been made in Bologna since at least the nineteenth century, and Pellegrino Artusi included a Bolognese-style sauce in his 1891 cookbook. In 1982 the Bologna delegation of the Italian Academy of Cuisine registered a reference recipe for ragù, traditionally served with tagliatelle.
