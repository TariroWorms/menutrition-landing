Venezuelan empanadas

Origin: Venezuela

Venezuelan empanadas are made with corn dough rather than wheat, which gives them a thin, bubbly, very crisp shell once fried. They are shaped as half-moons and filled with shredded beef, chicken, white cheese, black beans or, on the coast, cazón, a small shark stewed with spices. Eaten hot for breakfast from street stalls and beach kiosks, they are often splashed with a green garlic-and-herb sauce.

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g precooked corn flour
  • 500 ml warm water, 15 g sugar, salt
  • 400 g filling: shredded beef, shredded chicken or grated white cheese
  • 1 onion and ½ pepper for the stew
  • oil for frying

Method

  1. Knead the corn flour with water, sugar and salt into a soft, smooth dough.
  2. Cook the beef or chicken filling with onion and pepper until fairly dry, or grate the cheese.
  3. Place a ball of dough on a plastic sheet, press into a thin 14 cm round and add a spoonful of filling.
  4. Fold the plastic over to close the half-moon and cut the edge with a bowl or cutter.
  5. Fry at 180 °C for 3–4 minutes until blistered and golden; drain and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake brushed with oil at 220 °C or use an air fryer for a lighter version.
  • Choose chicken or bean fillings over cheese for more protein and less salt.
  • Keep the dough thin – less dough means fewer carbs per empanada.

History

Corn empanadas are a daily breakfast across Venezuela and change from region to region, with cazón fillings typical of the eastern coast and Margarita Island. Their corn shell links them to the arepa tradition rather than to the wheat empanadas of the Southern Cone.
