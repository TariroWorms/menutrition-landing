Morcipán

Morcipán is the sibling of choripán: a grilled morcilla, the soft Argentine blood sausage, spread or sliced into a crusty roll. Its texture is creamy and its flavor deep, slightly sweet and spiced. It is eaten at the start of an asado, often with chimichurri or salsa criolla.

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 4 morcillas (400 g)
  • 4 crusty rolls (320 g)
  • 4 tbsp salsa criolla (60 g)
  • Chili flakes to taste

Method

  1. Heat the grill to low–medium heat.
  2. Grill the morcillas gently for 15–20 minutes, turning carefully so the casing does not split.
  3. Toast the rolls cut side down.
  4. Open the morcilla and spread or lay it on the bread.
  5. Top with salsa criolla and chili flakes and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • One morcipán is a full meal; pair it with salad, not fries.
  • Use a wholegrain roll for more fibre.
  • Low heat keeps the sausage intact without extra oil.

History

The name combines morcilla and pan. Blood sausage arrived with Spanish settlers, and grilled morcilla is a standard part of the Argentine parrillada.
