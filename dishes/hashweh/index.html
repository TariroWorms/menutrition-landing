🍚 Hashweh (spiced rice with meat)

Origin: Lebanon · Levant

Hashweh is Levantine rice cooked with browned minced beef, warm spices such as cinnamon and allspice, and butter, finished with toasted pine nuts. The name means "stuffing", and the same mixture fills roast chicken, lamb and vegetables. Served on its own, it is fragrant, buttery and nutty, often with a fresh salad.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g minced beef
  • 300 g long-grain rice, rinsed
  • 30 g butter
  • 40 g pine nuts
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tsp ground allspice
  • 600 ml water
  • Salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Toast the pine nuts in a dry pan until golden and set aside.
  2. Melt the butter in a pot and brown the beef, breaking it up, for 6–8 minutes.
  3. Add the cinnamon, allspice, salt and pepper, then the rice, and stir for 1 minute.
  4. Pour in the water, bring to the boil, cover and cook on low heat for 18–20 minutes.
  5. Rest for 5 minutes, fluff and scatter the pine nuts on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean minced beef and halve the butter — 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal.
  • Pine nuts are calorie-dense: a small sprinkle on top gives the flavour.
  • Make hashweh a side rather than the main plate, next to a large salad.

History

Hashweh belongs to the shared cooking of Lebanon, Syria, Palestine and Jordan, where it is a fixture of festive tables and holiday meals. It is commonly served under roast chicken or lamb, a dish sometimes called riz a djeij.
