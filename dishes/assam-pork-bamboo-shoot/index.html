Assamese pork with fermented bamboo shoot (gahori mangxo)

Origin: India · Assam

This Assamese curry cooks pork with fermented bamboo shoot, known as khorisa, in a simple broth of ginger, garlic and green chilli. The bamboo shoot gives a distinctive tangy, funky sourness that cuts the richness of the fatty pork. The sauce is thin and peppery with no heavy spices. It is eaten with plain rice and is a favourite of the Bodo and other communities of Assam.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork belly or shoulder, cubed
  • 150 g fermented bamboo shoot, rinsed and sliced
  • 1 tbsp mustard oil (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger, 6 garlic cloves
  • 3 green chillies, 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • salt, 400 ml water

Method

  1. Heat the mustard oil until it smokes lightly, then add the pork and brown for 8 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic, ginger, chillies and turmeric and stir for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the bamboo shoot and salt and mix well.
  4. Pour in the water, cover and simmer for 40 minutes until the pork is tender.
  5. Cook uncovered a few minutes to reduce; serve with rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean shoulder and skim fat off the top.
  • Use just a teaspoon of oil as the pork renders its own fat.
  • Serve with extra leafy greens.

History

Fermented bamboo shoot is widely used across Northeast India and in Assam it is a traditional way of preserving the spring harvest. Pork dishes are especially important in the food of many hill and tribal communities.
