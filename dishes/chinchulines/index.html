Chinchulines (grilled beef intestines)

Origin: Argentina

Chinchulines are cleaned beef small intestines, grilled slowly until crisp and golden. They are rich and fatty, with a crunchy surface and a deeply savory taste. Some cooks braid them first, which is then called a trenza, and many grill them a long time until they are almost brittle. They are cut into short pieces, sprinkled with lemon and passed around as part of the achuras.

What it is made of

beef small intestineslemoncoarse salt

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g cleaned beef small intestines
  • 2 lemons
  • Coarse salt

Method

  1. Rinse the intestines well under running water and soak them in water with lemon for 30 minutes.
  2. Optionally boil them for 10 minutes and drain well.
  3. Coil or braid them and salt lightly.
  4. Grill over medium-low embers for 50–60 minutes, turning, until crisp all over.
  5. Cut into short pieces, squeeze over lemon and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Chinchulines are very fatty; a few pieces as a tasting starter are enough.
  • Grilling them longer renders out more fat.
  • Balance them with a large salad and skip other sausages that day.

History

The word chinchulín comes from the Quechua ch'unchul, meaning intestine. Grilled intestines have been part of the gaucho tradition of using the whole animal.
