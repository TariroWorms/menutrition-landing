🐦 Stuffed pigeon (hamam mahshi)

Origin: Egypt · Nile Delta

Hamam mahshi is young pigeon stuffed with spiced green freekeh or rice, gently boiled in a fragrant broth and then roasted or fried until the skin is crisp. The meat is dark, tender and richer than chicken, while the stuffing soaks up the juices and ghee. It is served whole, one bird per person, often with the cooking broth on the side.

Recipe

4 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 4 young pigeons, cleaned
  • 200 g green freekeh (or short-grain rice), rinsed
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 40 g ghee
  • 1 tsp each cumin, cinnamon and black pepper
  • 2 cardamom pods, 2 bay leaves
  • 1.5 litres water, salt

Method

  1. Soak the freekeh for 30 minutes and drain.
  2. Soften half the onion in 20 g ghee, add the freekeh, spices and salt and stir for 3 minutes.
  3. Stuff the pigeons loosely with the mixture and close the openings with a toothpick or thread.
  4. Simmer the birds in water with the remaining onion, cardamom and bay leaves for 40–50 minutes until tender.
  5. Brush with the remaining ghee and roast at 220 °C for 15–20 minutes until the skin is golden and crisp.

How to make it lighter

  • Roast rather than deep-fry the boiled pigeons to keep the fat down.
  • Use half the ghee — the broth already flavours the freekeh well.
  • Freekeh is filling; pair one bird with a large green salad instead of extra bread or rice.

History

Pigeon has been raised for food in Egypt for centuries, and the tall mud-brick dovecotes of the Delta countryside are still a familiar sight. Hamam mahshi is a festive and restaurant dish in Egypt today and is also popular in neighbouring Arab countries.
