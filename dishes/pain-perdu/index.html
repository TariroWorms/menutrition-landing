Pain Perdu (French Toast)

Origin: France

Pain perdu, the French original of French toast, is made by soaking thick slices of brioche in a mixture of egg, milk, sugar, vanilla and cinnamon, then frying them in butter until golden. The outside caramelises while the inside stays soft and custardy. It is served warm as a dessert or a weekend breakfast, often with a dusting of sugar or fresh fruit.

What it is made of

briocheeggmilksugarvanillacinnamonbutter

Recipe

4 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 4 thick slices brioche (about 240 g)
  • 2 eggs
  • 200 ml milk
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 20 g butter

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs, milk, sugar, vanilla and cinnamon in a shallow dish.
  2. Soak each brioche slice for 20–30 seconds per side so it absorbs the custard without falling apart.
  3. Melt the butter in a frying pan over medium heat.
  4. Fry the slices for 2–3 minutes per side until deep golden and caramelised.
  5. Serve warm, with fresh berries if you like.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a non-stick pan and just a thin film of butter: 10 g butter ≈ 75 kcal.
  • Top with fresh fruit instead of extra sugar.
  • One slice with a bowl of berries makes a satisfying portion.

History

The name means 'lost bread', because the dish was a way to use up stale bread that would otherwise be wasted. Similar recipes for soaking bread in egg and milk and frying it have appeared in European cookbooks since medieval times.
