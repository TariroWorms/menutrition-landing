Ojo de bife (grilled ribeye)

Origin: Argentina

Ojo de bife is the boneless ribeye, cut from the centre of the rib section. Its marbling and the soft fat pocket in the middle melt on the grill and keep the steak juicy. Argentine parrillas usually serve it thick, at least 3 cm, with a well-browned crust. It needs only salt, though chimichurri is always on the table.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 2 ribeye steaks, 400 g each, 3–4 cm thick
  • Coarse salt
  • Chimichurri to serve

Method

  1. Bring the steaks to room temperature.
  2. Prepare a hot bed of embers.
  3. Salt and grill 6–7 minutes on the first side until well browned.
  4. Turn and grill 5–6 minutes more for medium-rare.
  5. Rest 5 minutes, slice and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve a smaller steak of about 250 g per person.
  • Cut away the fat cap and outer fat at the table.
  • Pair with a big mixed salad instead of fries.

History

The name means "eye of the steak", after the round shape of the muscle. It is one of the premium cuts that made Argentine grass-fed beef known abroad.
