🥟 Shrimp Wonton Soup

Origin: China · Guangdong

Shrimp wonton soup is a bowl of silky, thin-skinned dumplings filled with chopped shrimp and fresh ginger, finished with a few drops of sesame oil and served in a clear, light broth. The wrappers turn soft and slippery while the filling stays plump and springy. It is eaten as a light meal or a comforting starter.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g raw peeled shrimp
  • 30 wonton wrappers
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2 spring onions, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1.2 l clear broth
  • Salt and white pepper to taste

Method

  1. Chop the shrimp coarsely and mix with ginger, half the spring onions, sesame oil, salt and white pepper.
  2. Place 1 tsp of filling in the centre of each wrapper, wet the edges and fold into a parcel, pressing to seal.
  3. Bring the broth to a simmer and season it lightly.
  4. Boil the wontons in a separate pot of water for 3–4 minutes until they float and the filling turns pink.
  5. Divide the wontons between bowls, ladle over the hot broth and top with the remaining spring onions.

How to make it lighter

  • Fill the bowl with extra greens such as bok choy and serve fewer wontons for a lighter meal.
  • Measure the sesame oil: 1 tsp is about 40 kcal, and a few drops are enough for aroma.
  • Choose a low-fat clear broth and skim any fat from the surface.

History

Wontons have been eaten in China for many centuries, and the shrimp-filled Cantonese style became a staple of Guangdong and Hong Kong noodle shops. Today shrimp wonton soup is served across China and in Chinese restaurants worldwide.
