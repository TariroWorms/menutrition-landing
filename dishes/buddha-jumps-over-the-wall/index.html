Buddha jumps over the wall

Origin: China · Fujian
380kcal

This is a deeply savoury, almost gelatinous soup built from many costly ingredients, such as dried abalone, sea cucumber, fish maw, chicken, pork and mushrooms. Everything is sealed in a pot and cooked for hours, so the flavours merge into one rich broth. It is served in small bowls at banquets.

Recipe

6 servings480 min

Ingredients

  • 100 g dried abalone, rehydrated
  • 150 g sea cucumber, rehydrated
  • 50 g fish maw, rehydrated
  • 400 g chicken pieces
  • 200 g pork shank
  • 60 g dried shiitake
  • 20 g ginger
  • 100 ml Shaoxing wine
  • 1.5 l chicken broth
  • salt

Method

  1. Rehydrate the dried abalone, sea cucumber and fish maw as directed and blanch all meats.
  2. Brown the ginger lightly and place everything in a heavy crock or pot.
  3. Add broth and wine so the contents are covered.
  4. Seal with foil and a lid.
  5. Cook gently for 6–8 hours.
  6. Season with salt and serve in small bowls.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve small cups, as it is very rich in sodium and cholesterol.
  • Skim the fat after chilling the soup.
  • Use less pork shank and more mushrooms for a lighter version.

History

The dish comes from Fuzhou, in Fujian, and is usually dated to the 19th century. The name says the smell is so tempting that a vegetarian monk would leap a wall to taste it, a popular saying rather than a recorded event.
