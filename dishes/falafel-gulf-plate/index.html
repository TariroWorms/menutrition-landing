🧆 Falafel plate

Origin: Gulf · Arabian Peninsula

The falafel plate is a cheap, filling breakfast across the Gulf: hot fried patties of chickpeas and fava beans with parsley and garlic, served with a bowl of tahini sauce, sharp pickles and warm khubz flatbread. Diners tear the bread, scoop up the falafel and dip it in tahini, alternating with crunchy pickles.

What it is made of

Recipe

2 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g dried chickpeas, soaked overnight
  • 100 g dried split fava beans, soaked overnight
  • 1 bunch parsley
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin, salt
  • Vegetable oil for frying
  • 4 tbsp tahini, juice of 1 lemon, water
  • Mixed pickles
  • 2 khubz flatbreads

Method

  1. Drain the chickpeas and fava beans well and pulse with parsley, garlic, cumin and salt to a coarse paste.
  2. Rest the mixture 20 minutes, then shape into small patties.
  3. Deep-fry at 175 °C for 3–4 minutes until browned; drain.
  4. Whisk tahini with lemon juice, salt and enough water to make a pourable sauce.
  5. Serve the falafel with the tahini, pickles and warm khubz.

How to make it lighter

  • Tahini is calorie-dense — 1 tbsp is about 90 kcal, so thin the sauce with lemon and water.
  • Have one flatbread instead of two and add extra pickles and fresh vegetables.
  • Count your patties: each fried falafel adds up quickly, so plate a fixed number.

History

Falafel arrived in the Gulf with cooks and workers from Egypt and the Levant, and small falafel and foul shops became a fixture of Gulf city neighbourhoods. Today a falafel plate is one of the most common budget breakfasts in the region.
