🥬 Lamb Saag

Lamb saag is a North Indian curry of tender lamb braised in a thick, silky spinach sauce. Onion, garlic and garam masala give it depth, and a splash of cream at the end rounds out the earthy greens. The result is rich, mildly spiced and deep green, usually served with basmati rice or flatbread.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 700 g boneless lamb shoulder, cubed
  • 500 g fresh spinach
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, grated
  • 2 tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 60 ml cream
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt, 1 fresh green chili (optional)

Method

  1. Wilt the spinach in a little boiling water, drain and blend into a coarse purée.
  2. Heat the oil and fry the onions until golden, about 10 minutes; add garlic, chili and the ground spices and cook 1 minute.
  3. Add the lamb and brown on all sides, then add 300 ml water and salt, cover and simmer 60 minutes until tender.
  4. Stir in the spinach purée and garam masala and cook 10 minutes uncovered until thick.
  5. Turn off the heat, stir in the cream and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use only 2–3 tbsp cream, or leave it out; the spinach alone makes the sauce thick.
  • Trim visible fat from the lamb before cooking — shoulder carries a lot of it.
  • Serve with a measured 150 g of cooked rice instead of buttery flatbread; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal, so measure the frying oil too.

History

Saag dishes, built on cooked leafy greens, are a staple of Punjabi home cooking in northern India and Pakistan. Pairing the greens with lamb or mutton is a common restaurant and home variation, and saag gosht is widely served in Indian restaurants abroad.
