Bingfen (Sichuan ice jelly)

Origin: China · Sichuan

Bingfen is a clear, wobbly jelly made by rubbing nicandra (shoo-fly plant) seeds in water so that their natural pectin sets into a gel, without any starch or gelatin. It is served in a bowl with brown sugar syrup, crushed ice, raisins, hawthorn pieces, sesame and peanuts. It is sweet, lightly fruity and very refreshing on a hot day.

Recipe

2 servings20 min

Ingredients

  • 10 g nicandra (ice jelly) seeds in a cloth bag
  • 1000 ml cold water
  • 60 g brown sugar syrup
  • 20 g raisins
  • 20 g hawthorn pieces
  • 15 g peanuts and sesame

Method

  1. Stir a pinch of food-grade lime water (calcium hydroxide) into the water if your seeds need it to set.
  2. Rub the seed bag in cold water for several minutes until the water thickens.
  3. Leave the liquid for 20-30 minutes at room temperature until it sets.
  4. Spoon the jelly into bowls.
  5. Top with syrup, ice, raisins, hawthorn, peanuts and sesame.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less syrup or a sugar-free sweetener to cut calories.
  • Add fresh fruit instead of sweet toppings.
  • The jelly itself is very light, so topping choices decide the calories.

History

The jelly is a street dessert across Sichuan, sold in summer in cups or bowls with a choice of toppings. It is associated with the region's tea-house and street-snack culture.
