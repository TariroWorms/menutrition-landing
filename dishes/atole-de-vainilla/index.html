Atole de vainilla (vanilla atole)

Origin: Mexico

Atole is a hot drink thickened with corn masa, and the vanilla version is one of the simplest and gentlest. It is smooth and lightly sweet, with the soft taste of corn under the vanilla and cinnamon. It is drunk from a mug at breakfast, at street stalls on chilly mornings, and alongside tamales.

What it is made of

corn masamilksugarvanillacinnamon

Recipe

6 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 70 g corn masa
  • 1 l milk
  • 250 ml water
  • 80 g sugar
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1 vanilla pod, split (or 2 tsp extract)

Method

  1. Simmer the milk with the cinnamon and vanilla pod for 5 minutes.
  2. Blend the masa with the water until completely smooth.
  3. Pour the masa through a sieve into the milk, whisking.
  4. Add the sugar and cook, stirring constantly, for 10 minutes until thickened.
  5. Remove the cinnamon and vanilla pod and serve hot.

How to make it lighter

  • Use skimmed or plant milk to make it lighter.
  • Cut the sugar to 50 g; vanilla makes it taste sweeter.
  • A mug of atole is filling on its own, so it can replace a sweet snack.

History

Atole is one of the oldest foods of Mexico, drunk long before the Spanish conquest as a plain corn gruel. Milk, sugar and cinnamon arrived later, and vanilla, a plant native to Mexico, became a favorite flavoring.
