🧀 Malakoff

Origin: Switzerland · Vaud

Malakoff are Swiss cheese fritters: a mound of grated Gruyère bound with egg, flour and a splash of kirsch, set on a slice of bread and deep-fried until puffed and golden. The outside is crisp while the inside stays soft and molten. They are eaten hot, usually with pickles and a green salad, as a starter or a light meal.

What it is made of

gruyère cheeseeggflourkirschbread

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g Gruyère, grated
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 tbsp flour
  • 1 tbsp kirsch
  • 8 small slices of bread
  • Pepper and a pinch of nutmeg
  • Oil for deep-frying

Method

  1. Mix the cheese, eggs, flour, kirsch, pepper and nutmeg into a thick paste.
  2. Pile it in a dome on each slice of bread, pressing it to the edges.
  3. Deep-fry, cheese side down first, at 180 °C for 2–3 minutes until puffed and golden.
  4. Drain and serve at once with pickles and salad.

How to make it lighter

  • One or two fritters with a big green salad makes a reasonable meal.
  • Drain them well on paper to remove surface oil.
  • Use thin slices of bread and a slightly smaller mound of cheese.

History

Malakoff is a speciality of the shores of Lake Geneva in the canton of Vaud. Its name is usually linked to the Crimean War siege of the Malakoff fort, and the story goes that Swiss soldiers brought the idea of cheese fritters home from there.
