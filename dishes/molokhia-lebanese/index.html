Lebanese molokhia

Origin: Lebanon · Levant

Lebanese molokhia is a stew of whole or roughly chopped jute leaves cooked with chicken and lamb in a garlicky, coriander-scented broth. Unlike the Egyptian soup, the leaves keep their shape, giving a thick, leafy, savoury dish. It is spooned over rice and topped with onions soaked in vinegar, which add a sharp contrast.

Recipe

6 servings120 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g dried or 600 g fresh molokhia leaves
  • 600 g chicken pieces
  • 400 g lamb, cubed
  • 8 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 bunch fresh coriander, chopped
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, salt, pepper
  • Cooked rice to serve
  • 1 onion, chopped and soaked in 100 ml vinegar

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken and lamb in salted water for about 1 hour; keep the broth.
  2. Wash the leaves (soak dried ones for 15 minutes) and squeeze dry.
  3. Fry the garlic and coriander in oil, add the leaves and stir for 5 minutes.
  4. Add the meat and enough broth to cover and simmer for 20 minutes.
  5. Serve over rice with the vinegar onions on top.

How to make it lighter

  • Use only chicken, or lean lamb, and skim the broth.
  • Use less oil when frying the garlic; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Serve a larger portion of the stew with a smaller portion of rice.

History

Molokhia is eaten across the Levant and Egypt, and the Lebanese style with whole leaves is a classic family lunch in Lebanon and Syria, especially in summer when fresh leaves are in season and dried leaves are stored for winter.
