🧀 Cheese Soufflé

A cheese soufflé is a light, puffed bake made from a butter-and-flour white sauce with milk, egg yolks and grated Gruyère, folded with stiffly whipped egg whites. In the oven it climbs above the rim of its dish and develops a golden crust over an airy, creamy interior. It is served straight from the oven, as a starter or a light lunch with salad.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 4 eggs, separated
  • 100 g Gruyère, grated
  • 40 g butter, plus extra for the dish
  • 40 g plain flour
  • 300 ml milk
  • Salt, pepper, pinch of nutmeg

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200 °C and butter a 1.5 l dish.
  2. Make a thick sauce: melt the butter, cook the flour in it for a minute, then whisk in the milk until smooth and thick.
  3. Take off the heat and beat in the yolks, cheese and seasoning.
  4. Whisk the whites to firm peaks and gently fold them in.
  5. Bake at 190 °C for 25–30 minutes until tall and golden; serve immediately.

How to make it lighter

  • Bake in four individual ramekins so each portion is clearly measured.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk and butter the dish lightly.
  • Pair it with a plain green salad rather than bread or fries.

History

The soufflé is generally traced to 18th-century France, and its name comes from the verb souffler, 'to puff up'. The cheese version spread from French cookery into British and American home kitchens and is now a standard of classic European cooking.
