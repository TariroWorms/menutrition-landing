Ajiaco cubano (Cuban root vegetable stew)

Origin: Cuba

Ajiaco is a thick, slow-cooked Cuban stew of mixed meats and a big pile of starchy roots and vegetables. Corn, cassava, malanga, sweet potato, pumpkin and plantain cook down until the broth turns thick and sweet-savory. It is served hot in deep bowls with a squeeze of lime.

Recipe

8 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g pork, cubed
  • 300 g beef, cubed
  • 2 corn cobs, sliced (400 g)
  • 300 g cassava
  • 300 g sweet potato (boniato)
  • 1 green plantain (200 g)
  • 200 g pumpkin
  • 1 onion, 4 garlic cloves, salt

Method

  1. Simmer the meats with onion and garlic in 3 liters water for 60 minutes.
  2. Add corn and cassava and cook 20 minutes.
  3. Add malanga or sweet potato and green plantain.
  4. Add pumpkin last and cook until it dissolves into the broth.
  5. Mash some vegetables against the pot to thicken.
  6. Season and serve with lime juice.

How to make it lighter

  • Skim fat from the broth before adding roots.
  • Use more vegetables and less meat to cut calories.
  • The thick broth makes it a full meal on its own.

History

The dish draws on indigenous ingredients and the Spanish stew tradition, and is often described as a symbol of Cuban cultural blending. Many cooks build it in a large pot over hours and add vegetables in order of hardness. It is a Sunday and holiday dish.
