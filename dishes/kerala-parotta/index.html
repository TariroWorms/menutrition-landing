Kerala parotta (layered flatbread)

Origin: India · Kerala

Kerala parotta is a layered flatbread made from a soft maida dough that is stretched thin, coiled and flattened. Cooked on a hot griddle with oil, it comes out golden with flaky layers that pull apart. It is eaten with beef fry, vegetable kurma or egg curry, and is a staple of restaurants and roadside stalls.

Recipe

6 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g maida (refined flour)
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 150 ml milk
  • 100 ml water
  • 5 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Mix flour, egg, sugar, salt, milk and water into a soft dough and rest 2 hours.
  2. Divide into balls and oil them.
  3. Stretch each ball very thin on an oiled surface.
  4. Pleat and coil it into a spiral, then flatten gently.
  5. Cook on a hot griddle with oil until golden, then clap the bread between your hands to open the layers.

How to make it lighter

  • Use half whole wheat flour for extra fibre.
  • Cook with less oil on a nonstick griddle.
  • Eat one parotta with a large serving of vegetable curry.

History

Parotta is closely associated with Kerala and the wider Malabar coast, where it is a late-night favourite. Its layered style is related to flatbreads found across South India and Sri Lanka.
