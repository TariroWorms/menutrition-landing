🍗 Chicken schnitzel

Chicken schnitzel is a flattened chicken breast coated in flour, egg and breadcrumbs and fried until golden and crisp. It follows the Viennese schnitzel method but uses chicken instead of veal, giving a mild, juicy cutlet under a crunchy crust. It is served with lemon wedges and sides such as potato salad, cucumber salad, rice or vegetables.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 4 chicken breast cutlets, about 150 g each
  • 60 g plain flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 100 g fine breadcrumbs
  • Salt and white pepper
  • Vegetable oil for shallow frying (about 300 ml)
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges
  • Parsley to garnish

Method

  1. Slice each chicken breast horizontally if thick, then pound between cling film to about 5 mm. Season with salt and pepper.
  2. Set out three plates: flour, beaten eggs and breadcrumbs.
  3. Coat each cutlet in flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs, shaking off any excess.
  4. Heat about 2 cm of oil in a wide pan to 170 °C and fry the cutlets 3 minutes per side until golden and cooked through.
  5. Drain on paper towels and serve hot with lemon and parsley.

How to make it lighter

  • Baking the breaded cutlets at 220 °C with a light spray of oil saves most of the frying fat.
  • Pound the meat thin and cut smaller pieces: a 120 g cutlet looks generous on the plate.
  • Choose a green or cucumber salad as the side instead of fries.

History

Chicken schnitzel is a popular variation of the Viennese veal schnitzel and is eaten widely in Austria, Germany and across Central Europe. In Austria it may not be sold as "Wiener Schnitzel", a name reserved for veal, so menus list it as chicken schnitzel or "Wiener Art".
