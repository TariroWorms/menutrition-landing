🥛 Egyptian Sahlab

Origin: Egypt

Egyptian sahlab is a thick, warm milk drink thickened with starch and sweetened with sugar, served in a cup or bowl and topped with shredded coconut, chopped nuts, raisins and a dusting of cinnamon. It is silky and almost pudding-like, sipped slowly or eaten with a spoon, and is a winter favourite in Cairo cafés and homes.

Recipe

2 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 500 ml milk
  • 3 tbsp cornstarch
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp shredded coconut
  • 2 tbsp chopped mixed nuts
  • 1 tbsp raisins
  • Ground cinnamon

Method

  1. Whisk the cornstarch into 100 ml cold milk until smooth.
  2. Heat the rest of the milk with the sugar.
  3. Pour in the cornstarch mix and stir constantly for 5 minutes until thick.
  4. Pour into cups and top with coconut, nuts, raisins and cinnamon.

How to make it lighter

  • Use semi-skimmed milk and 2 tbsp sugar for a lighter cup.
  • Top with cinnamon and a few raisins and go easy on coconut and nuts.
  • A 200 ml cup is plenty; it is filling.

History

Sahlab takes its name from the ground orchid root (salep) that once thickened it in Ottoman times; today most cafés and homes use cornstarch instead. It is especially popular in Egypt and the Levant in winter.
