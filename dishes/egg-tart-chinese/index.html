🥧 Hong Kong Egg Tart

Origin: Hong Kong · Guangdong

The Chinese egg tart is a small baked tart with a crisp, flaky pastry shell and a smooth, glossy egg custard filling lightly sweetened and scented with vanilla. It is best eaten warm, when the custard is just set and wobbly. Egg tarts are sold in bakeries and served at dim sum.

Recipe

12 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g shortcrust or puff pastry
  • 3 eggs
  • 120 ml milk
  • 100 ml water
  • 70 g sugar
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200 °C and line 12 small tart tins with pastry.
  2. Dissolve the sugar in the warm water and let it cool.
  3. Whisk the eggs with the milk, sugar syrup and vanilla, then strain for a smooth custard.
  4. Fill the shells about 80% full.
  5. Bake 10 minutes, lower to 180 °C and bake 10–15 minutes until the custard just sets.

How to make it lighter

  • Treat one tart as a full dessert portion and pair it with tea rather than a second pastry.
  • Cut the sugar in the custard by about a quarter; the vanilla keeps it fragrant.
  • Make mini tarts in a mini-muffin tin for smaller, easy-to-count portions.

History

Egg tarts are usually traced to Guangzhou in the early 20th century, likely influenced by English custard tarts, and they became an emblem of Hong Kong bakeries and cha chaan teng cafés.
