🥘 Lamb kleftiko

Arnaki kleftiko is lamb slow-cooked in a sealed parcel of baking paper with potatoes, tomatoes, garlic, oregano and lemon, with chunks of feta tucked in among the meat. Sealed in, everything steams in its own juices for hours until the lamb falls off the bone and the potatoes soak up the flavour. The parcel is opened at the table, letting out the aroma of herbs.

Recipe

4 servings210 min

Ingredients

  • 1.2 kg lamb shoulder or leg on the bone, cut into 4 large pieces
  • 800 g potatoes, peeled and cut into wedges
  • 2 tomatoes, sliced
  • 150 g feta, cut into cubes
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 tsp dried oregano
  • Juice of 1 lemon and 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and pepper; baking paper and foil

Method

  1. Rub the lamb with the lemon juice, olive oil, garlic, oregano, salt and pepper. Leave for at least 1 hour, or overnight in the fridge.
  2. Heat the oven to 160 °C. Lay a large sheet of baking paper over a sheet of foil in a deep roasting pan.
  3. Put the potatoes in the centre, place the lamb on top, then the tomato slices and feta, and pour over any marinade.
  4. Fold the paper and foil over the meat and seal the edges tightly so no steam can escape.
  5. Bake for about 3 hours, then open the parcel, turn the heat to 200 °C and cook 15–20 minutes more to brown the top. Serve in the parcel.

How to make it lighter

  • A 400 g serving with potatoes is about 760 kcal; trim visible fat from the lamb before marinating.
  • Use half the feta and crumble it over at the end so the flavour is more noticeable with less cheese.
  • Swap some of the potatoes for courgette, peppers and onion, which cook down well in the sealed parcel.

History

The name comes from the Greek word for 'stolen', and the story goes that it was cooked by the klephts — mountain bandits and rebels of the Ottoman period — who roasted stolen lamb in sealed pits in the ground so that no smoke would give them away. Whatever the truth of the tale, the slow, sealed cooking survives in the modern dish, now made in home ovens across Greece and Cyprus.
