Queijo coalho assado (grilled coalho cheese)

Origin: Brazil · Northeast

Queijo coalho is a firm, salty cheese from Brazil's Northeast that holds its shape over heat. Cut into thick sticks and grilled on skewers over charcoal, it develops a golden, smoky crust while the inside turns soft and squeaky. Vendors sell it on beaches, often drizzled with sugarcane molasses or sprinkled with oregano. It is also a staple on the churrasco grill.

Recipe

4 servings15 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g queijo coalho (or halloumi)
  • 4 wooden skewers, soaked
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 2 tbsp sugarcane molasses (optional)

Method

  1. Cut the cheese into thick sticks, about 2 cm wide.
  2. Thread each stick onto a skewer.
  3. Grill over hot charcoal or in a grill pan 2-3 minutes per side until golden marks form.
  4. Sprinkle with oregano or drizzle with molasses.
  5. Serve immediately while soft inside.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the molasses to cut sugar.
  • Serve with grilled vegetables or tomato salad.
  • One skewer is a high-protein snack; keep portions small because of the fat and salt.

History

Coalho cheese has long been made on farms in the Northeastern backlands, where it was traditionally curdled with rennet from animal stomachs, which gave it its name. Beach vendors grilling it on small portable braziers are a familiar sight on the Northeastern coast.
