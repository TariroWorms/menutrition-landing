Hayashi rice is a Japanese yōshoku dish of thinly sliced beef, onion and mushrooms simmered in a rich demi-glace and tomato sauce, served over steamed rice. The sauce is glossy, slightly sweet and tangy, milder than Japanese curry. It is a home and diner classic, served on one plate with the rice on one side and the sauce on the other.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g thinly sliced beef
  • 2 onions, thinly sliced
  • 200 g mushrooms, sliced
  • 200 g canned chopped tomatoes
  • 250 ml demi-glace sauce
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 600 g cooked short-grain rice

Method

  1. Heat the oil and cook the onions over medium heat for 10 minutes until soft and golden.
  2. Add the beef and brown it briefly, then add the mushrooms and cook for 3 minutes.
  3. Stir in the tomatoes, tomato paste and demi-glace with 150 ml water.
  4. Simmer for 15–20 minutes until the sauce is thick; season with salt and pepper.
  5. Serve alongside hot rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use a lean cut of beef and trim visible fat.
  • Make the rice portion smaller and add more mushrooms and onion to the sauce.
  • Cook the onions in 1 tbsp of oil or less — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Hayashi rice appeared in Japan in the Meiji or Taishō era as part of yōshoku, Western-inspired cooking. Its name is often linked to Hayashi Yuteki, founder of the bookshop Maruzen, but this and other stories about its origin are not confirmed.
