🍜 Pork lo mein

Origin: China · Guangdong

Lo mein is a Chinese noodle dish of soft boiled egg noodles tossed in a wok with strips of pork, cabbage and carrot, seasoned with soy sauce and a little sesame oil. Unlike crispy chow mein, the noodles stay soft and slippery, coating evenly in the savoury sauce. It is served hot as a one-plate meal.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g fresh egg noodles
  • 300 g pork loin, cut into thin strips
  • 250 g cabbage, shredded
  • 2 carrots, cut into matchsticks
  • 3 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, 2 spring onions

Method

  1. Cook the noodles for 2–3 minutes, drain and rinse briefly.
  2. Stir-fry the pork in the vegetable oil over high heat for 3 minutes; add the garlic.
  3. Add the cabbage and carrot and stir-fry 2–3 minutes until just tender.
  4. Add the noodles, soy sauce and sesame oil and toss for 2 minutes. Finish with spring onions.

How to make it lighter

  • Double the cabbage and carrot and use fewer noodles for a lighter plate.
  • 1 tsp sesame oil is enough for flavour; 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Use lean pork loin rather than fattier cuts.

History

Lo mein, Cantonese for 'tossed noodles', comes from southern Chinese cooking. It became one of the best-known dishes of Chinese-American takeaway restaurants and is eaten in many variations worldwide.
