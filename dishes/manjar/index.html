Manjar is the Chilean name for dulce de leche, a thick, golden caramel made by slowly cooking milk with sugar until it reduces to a smooth spread. It is sweet, creamy and deeply milky. In Chile it is spread on bread and pancakes and used as a filling for biscuits, cakes and pastries.

Recipe

12 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 1 litre whole milk
  • 250 g sugar
  • ¼ tsp baking soda
  • 1 vanilla pod or 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • A pinch of salt

Method

  1. Pour the milk into a wide, heavy-bottomed pot and add the sugar, baking soda, salt and vanilla.
  2. Bring to the boil over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Lower the heat to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for about 2 to 2½ hours, stirring regularly and more often towards the end so it does not catch.
  4. When the mixture has turned a deep caramel colour and coats the back of a spoon, remove the vanilla pod and take off the heat.
  5. Beat for a minute to smooth it, then pour into clean jars; it thickens as it cools and keeps in the fridge.

How to make it lighter

  • Manjar is about 310 kcal per 100 g, so a spoonful is already around 60 kcal; use a thin layer instead of a thick one.
  • Spread it on whole-grain toast or fruit rather than layering it in several pastries.
  • Make it with semi-skimmed milk and a little less sugar for a lighter spread.

History

Slow-cooked sweet milk spreads are loved across Latin America, and in Chile this one is called manjar. It is a pantry staple in Chilean homes and a key filling in many classic sweets, such as the sandwich biscuits known as alfajores. It can be made at home with just milk and sugar, or bought ready-made in jars and tins.
