🍜 Dan dan noodles

Origin: China · Sichuan

Dan dan noodles are a Sichuan street dish of springy wheat-and-egg noodles topped with crisp-fried minced pork, a spicy sauce of chilli oil, soy sauce and numbing Sichuan pepper, and a sprinkle of crushed peanuts. Each bite is hot, savoury and tingling. The bowl is small and stirred together before eating.

Recipe

2 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 200 g fresh egg noodles
  • 150 g minced pork
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, divided
  • 2 tbsp chilli oil
  • 1 tsp ground Sichuan pepper
  • 1 tsp sugar, 1 tsp black vinegar
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 20 g roasted peanuts, crushed
  • 1 tsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Fry the pork in the oil over high heat until crisp and browned, then add 1 tbsp soy sauce and cook until dry.
  2. Mix the remaining soy sauce, chilli oil, Sichuan pepper, sugar and vinegar in two bowls.
  3. Boil the noodles according to the packet and drain, keeping a little cooking water.
  4. Add a spoonful of cooking water to each bowl, add the noodles and top with pork, peanuts and spring onion.
  5. Stir everything together before eating.

How to make it lighter

  • Chilli oil is mostly fat: 1 tsp per bowl still gives heat; each tablespoon of oil is about 120 kcal.
  • Use lean minced pork and drain off the rendered fat.
  • Add blanched greens such as pak choi to make the bowl bigger without many calories.

History

The name is usually explained as coming from the shoulder pole (dan) that Chengdu street vendors used to carry their noodles and sauce. The dish is one of the best-known Sichuan snacks, and versions with more sauce are common in restaurants abroad.
