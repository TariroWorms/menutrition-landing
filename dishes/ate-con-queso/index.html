Ate con queso (fruit paste with cheese)

Origin: Mexico

Ate con queso pairs thick slices of ate, a firm fruit paste usually made from quince or guava, with slices of mild white cheese. The dense, jammy sweetness of the fruit meets the soft saltiness of the cheese in each bite. It is a simple dessert or snack, served on a small plate or tucked into a bolillo.

What it is made of

quince pastecheese

Recipe

6 servings10 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g quince or guava paste
  • 250 g queso fresco or panela
  • a few walnuts (optional)

Method

  1. Chill the fruit paste so it slices cleanly.
  2. Cut the paste into slices about 1 cm thick.
  3. Slice the cheese to the same size.
  4. Stack or alternate the slices on a plate.
  5. Add a few walnuts if you like and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Use more cheese and less paste for extra protein and less sugar.
  • Choose panela or another low-fat fresh cheese.
  • Keep the portion to two thin slices of each.

History

Fruit pastes came to Mexico with Spanish membrillo-making traditions, and Morelia in Michoacán is especially known for its ates. Pairing them with cheese is a common habit in Mexico, much like membrillo with cheese in Spain.
