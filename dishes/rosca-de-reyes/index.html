Rosca de reyes (Three Kings bread)

Origin: Mexico

Rosca de reyes is a big oval ring of enriched yeast bread scented with orange, decorated with strips of candied fruit and sugary topping. Small figurines of the baby Jesus are hidden inside. It is sliced and shared on 6 January with hot chocolate, and whoever finds a figurine traditionally hosts tamales on Candlemas, 2 February.

Recipe

12 servings210 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g flour
  • 150 g sugar
  • 150 g butter
  • 4 eggs plus 2 yolks
  • 12 g dried yeast
  • 100 ml warm milk
  • zest of 1 orange
  • 150 g candied fruit strips; topping of 60 g flour, 60 g icing sugar, 50 g butter

Method

  1. Mix flour, sugar, yeast, zest, eggs, yolks and milk, then knead in the butter until silky.
  2. Let rise for about 2 hours.
  3. Shape into a long rope, join into a large ring on a tray and tuck in small heat-proof figurines.
  4. Let it rise for 45 minutes while you rub the topping ingredients into a paste.
  5. Brush with egg, lay strips of the paste and the candied fruit on top and bake at 180 °C for 25–30 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut thin slices; a whole ring feeds many people.
  • Use less candied fruit and add dried figs or nuts.
  • Pair with milk or coffee rather than thick sweet chocolate.

History

The ring cake came to Mexico from Spain, where the roscón de reyes is eaten for Epiphany. In Mexico it became tied to the custom of the figurine and the tamal gathering in February.
