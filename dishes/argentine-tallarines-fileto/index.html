Tallarines con salsa fileto (noodles in tomato sauce)

Origin: Argentina

Tallarines con fileto is everyday Argentine comfort food: long fresh egg noodles in a light tomato sauce. Unlike the slow tuco, fileto cooks in about twenty minutes and contains no meat, so it tastes fresh, sweet and gently garlicky. It is a staple at home and in neighbourhood pasta shops, usually finished with grated cheese and sometimes a drizzle of oil.

Recipe

4 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g fresh egg tallarines
  • 600 g ripe tomatoes, peeled and chopped (or canned)
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • a handful of basil
  • 1 tsp sugar, salt, oregano
  • 30 g grated cheese

Method

  1. Warm the oil and gently cook the onion for 8 minutes without browning.
  2. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute.
  3. Add the tomatoes, sugar, salt and oregano.
  4. Simmer for 15–20 minutes until saucy, then add torn basil.
  5. Boil the tallarines in salted water for 2–3 minutes.
  6. Toss the drained noodles with the sauce and serve with cheese.

How to make it lighter

  • Use whole-wheat noodles for more fibre.
  • Add grated carrot or zucchini to the sauce for extra vegetables.
  • Use a teaspoon of oil less and skip the cheese to cut fat.

History

Fresh pasta shops, called fábricas de pastas, became common in Buenos Aires during the era of Italian immigration. Fileto is the Argentine name for a simple tomato sauce and has nothing to do with a cut of meat. Many families still buy fresh tallarines on weekends from their local shop.
