🍜 Lanzhou Beef Noodles

Origin: China · Gansu, Lanzhou

Lanzhou beef noodles are hand-pulled wheat noodles served in a clear, fragrant beef broth with thin slices of beef and white radish. Bright red chili oil floats on top and fresh cilantro finishes the bowl. The noodles are springy and chewy, the broth light but deeply beefy, and a bowl is usually eaten for breakfast or lunch.

Recipe

4 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g fresh hand-pulled or thick wheat noodles
  • 500 g beef shank
  • 2 l water
  • 200 g white radish (daikon), sliced
  • 1 star anise, 1 small cinnamon stick, 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns
  • 3 slices ginger
  • 2 tbsp chili oil
  • Fresh cilantro and spring onion
  • Salt

Method

  1. Simmer the beef with the spices, ginger and salt in the water for 2–2.5 hours, skimming foam, until tender.
  2. Remove the beef, slice it thinly and strain the broth.
  3. Cook the radish in the broth for 10 minutes.
  4. Boil the noodles separately until just tender and drain.
  5. Divide noodles into bowls, add broth, radish and beef, and top with chili oil and cilantro.

How to make it lighter

  • Go easy on the chili oil — 1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal.
  • Ask for or make a smaller noodle portion and add extra radish.
  • Chill the broth and lift off the fat layer before reheating.

History

The dish comes from Lanzhou in Gansu province and is closely associated with the city's Hui Muslim cooks, which is why it is made with beef and no pork. Lanzhou noodle shops have since spread across China and abroad, where cooks pull the noodles to order.
