Pernil (Puerto Rican roast pork shoulder)

Origin: Puerto Rico

Pernil is a whole pork shoulder rubbed with a paste of garlic, oregano and vinegar and roasted slowly until the meat falls apart and the skin turns crisp and glassy. It tastes deeply garlicky and savory. It is served with arroz con gandules and is the centerpiece of holiday meals.

Recipe

8 servings360 min

Ingredients

  • 2.5 kg bone-in pork shoulder with skin
  • 10 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp dried oregano
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 tbsp vinegar
  • 2 tbsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Pound the garlic with salt, oregano, oil, vinegar and pepper into a paste.
  2. Score the skin and cut deep slits into the meat, pushing paste into them.
  3. Rub the rest over the pork and marinate overnight.
  4. Roast skin side up, covered with foil, at 160 °C for 3 hours, then uncover.
  5. Raise the heat to 220 °C for 30-40 minutes until the skin is crisp.
  6. Rest 20 minutes, then chop or pull the meat with some skin.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the fat and skin before eating to cut calories.
  • Roast on a rack so fat drips away.
  • Pair with beans, salad or vegetables instead of extra rice.

History

Slow-roasted pork is a cornerstone of Puerto Rican celebrations, especially at Christmas. The garlic and oregano seasoning reflects Spanish heritage on the island.
