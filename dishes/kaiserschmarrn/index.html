🥞 Kaiserschmarrn

Origin: Austria · Vienna

Kaiserschmarrn is an Austrian shredded pancake: a thick, fluffy batter of eggs, milk and flour with raisins is fried in butter, then torn into bite-sized pieces in the pan and caramelised with a little sugar. It is dusted with icing sugar and traditionally served with plum or apple compote, as a dessert or a sweet main course.

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 120 g flour
  • 4 eggs, separated
  • 250 ml milk
  • 30 g sugar, plus icing sugar to serve
  • 40 g raisins
  • 30 g butter
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, milk, egg yolks and a pinch of salt into a smooth batter; stir in the raisins.
  2. Beat the egg whites with half the sugar to stiff peaks and fold into the batter.
  3. Melt half the butter in a large pan, pour in the batter and cook on medium heat for 4–5 minutes until set underneath.
  4. Flip in sections, cook 2 minutes, then tear into pieces with two forks.
  5. Add the rest of the butter and sugar, toss until lightly caramelised and serve dusted with icing sugar.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the final caramelising butter and sugar.
  • Serve with fresh fruit instead of sweetened compote.
  • Use semi-skimmed milk and share one pan between two as a dessert.

History

The name means “Emperor's mess”, and it is usually linked to Emperor Franz Joseph I, though the stories of how it got its name vary and are best treated as legend. It is a classic of Austrian and Bavarian mountain huts and cafés.
