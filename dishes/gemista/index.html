🫑 Gemista

Gemista are Greek tomatoes and bell peppers hollowed out and stuffed with rice, onion, fresh herbs and the scooped-out tomato pulp, then baked in olive oil until soft and caramelised. The filling is fragrant with mint, parsley and dill. Gemista are a classic summer dish, served warm or at room temperature as a main.

Recipe

4 servings90 min

Ingredients

  • 4 large tomatoes
  • 4 bell peppers
  • 200 g short-grain rice
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 bunch parsley, mint and dill, chopped
  • 5 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt, pepper, 1 tsp sugar

Method

  1. Slice the tops off the tomatoes and peppers, scoop out the tomato pulp and seeds of the peppers. Blend the tomato pulp.
  2. Soften the onion in 2 tbsp oil, add the rice, half the tomato pulp, herbs, salt and pepper and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Sprinkle a little sugar and salt inside the tomatoes, fill the vegetables three-quarters full and replace the lids.
  4. Arrange in a baking dish, pour over the remaining pulp, the rest of the oil and 150 ml water.
  5. Bake covered at 190 °C for 45 minutes, then uncovered for 20–30 minutes until the rice is tender and the tops are browned.

How to make it lighter

  • Olive oil is generous in this dish — 2–3 tbsp instead of 5 still works (1 tbsp oil ≈ 120 kcal).
  • Use less rice and more herbs and onion in the filling.
  • Add courgettes or aubergines to the tray for more vegetables per serving.

History

Gemista, meaning "filled" in Greek, belong to the wide family of stuffed vegetables cooked around the eastern Mediterranean. The meatless rice version is a summer favourite in Greek homes and tavernas today.
