Bolinho de chuva (Brazilian rain fritters)

Origin: Brazil

Bolinhos de chuva are small, irregular fritters made from a simple batter of flour, eggs, milk and sugar, dropped by the spoonful into hot oil. They puff up into golden balls that are crisp outside and soft and cake-like inside, then are rolled in sugar and cinnamon. They are eaten warm with coffee in the afternoon. The name means rain fritters, because they are a typical treat for rainy days at home.

Recipe

6 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 250 g flour
  • 2 eggs
  • 150 ml milk
  • 80 g sugar
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • Pinch of salt
  • Oil for frying
  • 3 tbsp sugar + 1 tsp cinnamon for coating

Method

  1. Whisk eggs, sugar and milk together.
  2. Stir in flour, salt and baking powder to make a thick, spoonable batter.
  3. Heat oil to 170 °C.
  4. Drop small spoonfuls of batter into the oil and fry 3-4 minutes, turning, until golden and cooked through.
  5. Drain on paper and roll in cinnamon sugar while warm.

How to make it lighter

  • Make them small and share; three fritters are plenty.
  • Use half the sugar in the coating and add more cinnamon.
  • Keep the oil hot enough so they do not soak it up.

History

They are a homely recipe passed down in families, strongly associated with grandmothers' kitchens. Their style echoes Portuguese fried sweets such as sonhos.
