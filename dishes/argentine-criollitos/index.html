Criollitos (Argentine lard biscuits)

Origin: Argentina

Criollitos are small square or round biscuits made from a lard dough that is rolled and folded to create flaky layers. The top is usually pricked or scored in a grid, and they bake into crisp, savoury pieces with a tender middle. They are a basic of the Argentine bakery counter and are eaten for breakfast or merienda, plain or with butter, jam or cheese, and almost always with mate.

Recipe

12 servings150 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g flour
  • 120 g lard
  • 220 ml warm water
  • 10 g dry yeast
  • 10 g salt

Method

  1. Mix flour, half the lard, water, yeast and salt into a firm dough.
  2. Roll out, spread with the remaining lard and fold in three.
  3. Repeat the roll and fold twice, resting 15 minutes each time.
  4. Roll to 1 cm thick and cut into small squares.
  5. Score a grid on top and let rise for 30 minutes.
  6. Bake at 200 °C for 18–20 minutes until golden.

How to make it lighter

  • Replace part of the lard with butter or a vegetable fat low in trans fats.
  • Have two with a cheese slice rather than butter and jam.
  • Drink mate unsweetened to keep the snack light.

History

Criollitos belong to the family of bizcochos de grasa, simple fat-based biscuits that have long been common in rural Argentina and Uruguay. The name, meaning roughly little creole ones, reflects their status as a local, everyday staple. They are sold by the dozen in neighbourhood bakeries.
