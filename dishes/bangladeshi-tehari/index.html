Tehari (Bangladeshi spiced beef rice)

Origin: Bangladesh · Dhaka

Tehari is a one-pot rice dish where small pieces of beef are cooked together with short-grain rice. Mustard oil gives it a sharp, nutty edge and green chillies add heat. The grains stay separate and glossy, and the meat is soft. It is usually served with a salad of sliced onion, cucumber and lime, and often with borhani.

Recipe

6 servings75 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g beef, cubed
  • 400 g rice (kalijira or basmati)
  • 80 ml mustard oil
  • 2 onions (250 g), sliced
  • 6 green chillies
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste, garam masala, salt

Method

  1. Heat the mustard oil until it loses its raw smell and fry the sliced onions until golden.
  2. Add ginger-garlic paste, spices and the beef; fry until browned.
  3. Add a little water, cover and cook until the beef is nearly tender.
  4. Stir in the washed rice, green chillies and hot water to cover by 1 cm.
  5. Cook covered on low heat until the rice is done, rest 10 minutes and fluff before serving.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean beef and trim visible fat to lower the calories.
  • Reduce the oil to 3 tablespoons; the flavour remains strong.
  • Serve with a large cucumber and onion salad to add fibre.

History

Tehari is closely tied to Old Dhaka, where it is sold in small shops and eaten at weddings and family gatherings. It is related to the pilaf traditions of the Mughal period, adapted to local ingredients such as mustard oil.
