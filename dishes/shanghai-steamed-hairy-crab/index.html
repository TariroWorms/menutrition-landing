Shanghai hairy crab (da zha xie)

Origin: China · Shanghai · Jiangsu

Hairy crabs are small freshwater crabs prized in autumn for their sweet meat and rich roe or creamy fat. They are tied, steamed whole with ginger and picked apart by hand at the table. A dip of black vinegar, ginger and a little sugar brightens the sweet, rich flesh.

Recipe

2 servings40 min

Ingredients

  • 4 live hairy crabs, about 150 g each
  • 30 g ginger, sliced
  • Perilla leaves or scallions for the steamer
  • 4 tbsp black vinegar
  • 2 tbsp julienned ginger
  • 1 tsp sugar

Method

  1. Rinse the crabs and tie them to keep the legs still.
  2. Lay ginger and perilla in a steamer and set the crabs belly up on top.
  3. Steam over high heat for 18 to 20 minutes.
  4. Mix black vinegar, julienned ginger and sugar for the dip.
  5. Let the crabs cool slightly, then twist off legs and open the shell.
  6. Pick out the meat and roe and dip lightly.

How to make it lighter

  • Eat in moderation, as the roe is high in cholesterol.
  • Serve with ginger tea or a vegetable side.
  • Skip fatty sauces; the vinegar dip is enough.

History

Hairy crabs are most associated with the lakes of Jiangsu, and Shanghai markets sell them from roughly October to December. Eating them is a seasonal ritual, often paired with ginger tea to balance their cooling nature in Chinese food belief.
