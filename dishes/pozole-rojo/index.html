🍲 Pozole Rojo

Origin: Mexico · Jalisco

Pozole rojo is the red version of Mexico's famous hominy soup, its broth coloured and flavoured by dried guajillo chilies. Tender pork and chewy hominy sit in a brick-red, gently spicy broth, topped with sliced radish, shredded cabbage, oregano and lime. It is lighter-tasting than it looks and is traditionally served in large bowls for celebrations.

Recipe

4 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork shoulder, in large chunks
  • 800 g cooked hominy, drained
  • 5 guajillo chilies, seeded
  • 1 onion, halved
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 l water
  • 6 radishes, thinly sliced
  • 150 g cabbage, shredded
  • 2 limes, in wedges
  • 1 tbsp dried oregano

Method

  1. Simmer the pork with onion, garlic, salt and water for about 2 hours until tender.
  2. Toast the guajillo chilies briefly in a dry pan, soak in hot water for 15 minutes and blend until smooth.
  3. Strain the chili purée into the pot, add the hominy and simmer for 30 minutes.
  4. Shred the pork, season and serve with radish, cabbage, oregano and lime.

How to make it lighter

  • Use lean pork loin or trimmed shoulder for less fat per bowl.
  • Skim the fat off the chilled broth before serving.
  • Load up on radish and cabbage and keep the hominy to one ladle for a lighter portion.

History

Red pozole is most closely associated with Jalisco and the neighbouring western states, where guajillo and ancho chilies give the broth its colour. It descends from pre-Hispanic hominy dishes and is a standard of Mexican Independence Day menus today.
