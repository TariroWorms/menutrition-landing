Paella Mixta

Origin: Spain

Paella mixta, or mixed paella, combines land and sea in one pan: saffron rice cooked with chicken, shrimp, mussels and squid, plus red bell pepper and green peas. The result is colourful and varied, with tender meat, sweet shellfish and grains that soak up all the juices before forming a lightly crisp base. It is a favourite in Spanish restaurants and is served family-style from the pan.

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 320 g short-grain rice
  • 300 g chicken thighs, cut into pieces
  • 200 g raw shrimp
  • 300 g mussels, cleaned
  • 150 g squid, cut into rings
  • 1 red bell pepper, sliced
  • 100 g green peas
  • 1 pinch saffron
  • 1 l chicken stock
  • 3 tbsp olive oil, salt

Method

  1. Brown the chicken in the oil with salt in a paella pan for 8 minutes; add the squid for 2 minutes more.
  2. Add the bell pepper and cook for 3 minutes.
  3. Pour in the hot stock with the saffron, bring to a boil and scatter in the rice; do not stir.
  4. Simmer for 10 minutes, then add the peas, shrimp and mussels.
  5. Cook for 8–10 minutes more until the rice is tender and dry, then rest for 5 minutes.

How to make it lighter

  • Choose skinless chicken and trim visible fat.
  • Serve a smaller portion of rice with an extra helping of seafood.
  • Limit the oil to 2 tbsp for the pan; 1 tbsp ≈ 120 kcal.

History

Paella comes from the rice-growing area around the Albufera lagoon near Valencia, where farm workers cooked rice in a wide, shallow pan over an open fire; the word 'paella' is Valencian for that pan. The mixed meat-and-seafood version is a later style, popular in restaurants across Spain and abroad.
