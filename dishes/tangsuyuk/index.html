Tangsuyuk (Korean sweet and sour pork)

Origin: South Korea

Tangsuyuk is a Korean-Chinese dish of pork fried in a starch batter until crunchy, then served with a glossy sweet and sour sauce of vinegar, sugar and soy with onion, carrot and cucumber. The coating stays chewy-crisp and the flavor is bright, sweet and tangy. It is shared at the table, often as a companion to jjajangmyeon.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g pork loin, cut in strips
  • 100 g potato starch
  • 1 egg white (30 g)
  • 1 carrot (80 g), 1 onion (100 g), 1/2 cucumber
  • 3 tbsp sugar (40 g)
  • 3 tbsp vinegar (45 ml)
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (30 ml), 150 ml water, oil for frying, 1 tbsp cornstarch mixed with 2 tbsp water

Method

  1. Toss the pork with egg white and potato starch to form a sticky coating.
  2. Deep-fry the pork twice until golden and crisp.
  3. Simmer water with sugar, vinegar and soy and add the vegetables.
  4. Thicken the sauce with starch slurry until glossy.
  5. Pour the sauce over the pork just before serving, or serve on the side.

How to make it lighter

  • Serve the sauce on the side so the pork stays crisp.
  • Bake or air-fry the pork to cut oil.
  • Use less sugar in the sauce.

History

Tangsuyuk developed in Korean-Chinese restaurants, adapted from Chinese sweet and sour pork to local tastes. It is a long-standing favorite for family meals and celebrations.
