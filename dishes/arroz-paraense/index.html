Arroz paraense (rice with tucupi and jambu)

Origin: Brazil · Pará

Arroz paraense is rice cooked in tucupi instead of plain water, so it turns pale yellow and slightly sour. Chopped jambu leaves and dried shrimp are folded in, bringing a gentle tingle and a salty sea flavour. It tastes like a rice version of tacacá. It is served as a side to grilled fish or eaten on its own.

Recipe

4 servings35 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g rice
  • 600 ml tucupi
  • 1 bunch jambu, chopped
  • 80 g dried shrimp
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 onion
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Soak the shrimp briefly and drain.
  2. Blanch the jambu 3 minutes and chop it.
  3. Fry onion and garlic in the oil, add the rice and stir 1 minute.
  4. Pour in hot tucupi, season lightly and cover.
  5. Cook on low heat 15–18 minutes.
  6. Fold in jambu and shrimp and rest 5 minutes covered.
  7. Fluff and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Soak the shrimp longer to reduce salt.
  • Use brown rice and add a little more tucupi.
  • Add more jambu for extra greens.

History

The dish uses the same key ingredients as tacacá and pato no tucupi, the trio that defines the cooking of Pará. It is a modern home and restaurant favourite in Belém rather than an old named recipe.
