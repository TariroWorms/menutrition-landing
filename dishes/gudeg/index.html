🍛 Gudeg

Gudeg is a Javanese stew of young jackfruit slow-cooked for hours in coconut milk and palm sugar until it turns soft, sweet and deep brown. It is typically served with braised chicken, hard-boiled eggs simmered in the same sauce and pieces of tofu. The flavour is distinctly sweet and savoury, and it is usually eaten with steamed rice.

Recipe

4 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g young jackfruit, cut into chunks
  • 800 ml coconut milk
  • 80 g palm sugar
  • 4 chicken thighs
  • 4 eggs, hard-boiled and peeled
  • 200 g firm tofu, cut into pieces
  • 4 shallots and 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 bay leaves, 3 cm galangal, 1 tsp ground coriander, salt

Method

  1. Line a heavy pot with the bay leaves and add the jackfruit, chicken, eggs, shallots, garlic, galangal and coriander.
  2. Dissolve the palm sugar in the coconut milk with a pinch of salt and pour over.
  3. Bring to a simmer, then cook on the lowest heat for 3–4 hours, adding a little water if it gets dry, until the jackfruit is dark brown and very soft.
  4. Add the tofu for the last 30 minutes.
  5. Serve with steamed rice.

How to make it lighter

  • Use light coconut milk or replace part of it with water to cut the fat.
  • Reduce the palm sugar — the stew stays sweet but lighter.
  • Pick the chicken and egg for protein and keep the rice portion to about 150 g cooked.

History

Gudeg is the signature dish of Yogyakarta in Central Java, which is often nicknamed the city of gudeg. Its exact origins are not documented, but it has long been sold by street vendors and family-run eateries there, and a drier version that keeps for days is popular as a souvenir.
