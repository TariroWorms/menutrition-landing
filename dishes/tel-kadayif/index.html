🍯 Tel kadayıf

Origin: Turkey · Ottoman cuisine

Tel kadayıf is a Turkish dessert of fine, hair-like shredded pastry tossed with melted butter, layered with chopped pistachios and baked until crisp and golden. Cold sugar syrup is poured over the hot pastry so it soaks in while the top stays crunchy. It is cut into squares and served at room temperature, often for holidays.

What it is made of

Recipe

8 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 500 g kadayıf (shredded pastry)
  • 150 g butter, melted
  • 150 g pistachios, chopped
  • 400 g sugar
  • 350 ml water
  • 1 tsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Boil the sugar and water for 10 minutes, add lemon juice and let the syrup cool.
  2. Loosen the kadayıf strands and toss with the melted butter.
  3. Press half into a tray, scatter the pistachios, and cover with the rest.
  4. Bake at 180 °C for 40–45 minutes until golden.
  5. Pour the cold syrup over the hot pastry and leave to soak before cutting.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less syrup and let the pastry stay crisper — the syrup is a big share of the calories.
  • Cut small squares; one piece is a satisfying dessert.
  • Brush the butter on rather than pouring it to use less; 1 tbsp butter ≈ 100 kcal.

History

Shredded-pastry sweets have been made in the Middle East for centuries, and kadayıf was an established dessert in Ottoman cuisine. Today tel kadayıf is sold in baklava shops across Turkey, with a close relative, kunafa, popular in the Arab world.
