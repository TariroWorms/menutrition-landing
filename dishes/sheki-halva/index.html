Sheki halva (Azerbaijani rice-flour lattice sweet)

Origin: Azerbaijan · Sheki

Sheki halva, also called Sheki pakhlava, is built from a delicate lattice of rice-flour batter drizzled from a special funnel onto a round tray in many fine layers. Between them goes a thick layer of chopped hazelnuts with cardamom and coriander, and the top is decorated with saffron lines. Once baked it is drenched in sugar syrup, giving a sticky, crunchy and very sweet confection eaten in small pieces with tea.

Recipe

4 servings180 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g rice flour
  • 300 ml water
  • 500 g hazelnuts, roughly chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • A pinch of saffron steeped in water
  • Syrup: 600 g sugar, 300 ml water, 2 tbsp honey

Method

  1. Whisk rice flour and water into a smooth, runny batter and let it stand for 1 hour.
  2. Mix the chopped hazelnuts with cardamom and coriander.
  3. Using a squeeze bottle or funnel, drizzle a fine lattice of batter onto a greased round tray, building several thin layers.
  4. Spread the hazelnut mixture evenly, then cover with more lattice layers and decorate with saffron lines.
  5. Bake at 170 °C for about 40 minutes until set and crisp.
  6. Boil the sugar, water and honey into a thick syrup, pour it over the hot halva and leave for several hours before cutting into diamonds.

How to make it lighter

  • Cut tiny pieces: a 30–40 g serving is very filling.
  • Make the syrup thinner and use less of it for a lighter result.
  • Serve with unsweetened tea or lemon to cut the sweetness.

History

This sweet is the signature of the town of Sheki in north-western Azerbaijan, a stop on old caravan routes known for silk and its confectioners. Making the lattice by hand is a skilled craft, and many visitors buy it in Sheki's shops as a souvenir.
