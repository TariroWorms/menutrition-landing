Magret de canard is a French duck breast, taken from ducks raised for foie gras, pan-seared skin side down until the thick fat layer renders and turns crisp. The meat is then cooked briefly and served pink and juicy, rested and sliced. It is typically served with potatoes or vegetables and a fruit or pan sauce.

What it is made of

duck breast

Recipe

2 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 2 duck breasts (about 350 g each)
  • Salt
  • Black pepper

Method

  1. Score the skin in a crosshatch without cutting the meat and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Place skin side down in a cold pan and cook over medium heat for 10–12 minutes, pouring off fat as it renders.
  3. Turn and cook the flesh side for 3–4 minutes for pink meat.
  4. Rest for 5 minutes, then slice and serve.

How to make it lighter

  • Remove the skin before eating to cut a large share of the fat.
  • Half a breast, about 150–180 g, is a standard portion.
  • Serve with steamed or roasted vegetables instead of potatoes cooked in duck fat.

History

Magret comes from Gascony in southwest France, where duck breast was traditionally preserved as confit. Cooking it as a rare steak is usually credited to chef André Daguin in the 1950s–60s, and it has since become a bistro classic.
