Sudado de pescado (steamed fish in broth)

Origin: Peru

Sudado means 'sweated': thick fish fillets cook gently in the steam of their own juices over a bed of onion and tomato. Ají amarillo, a little garlic and a splash of chicha de jora or vinegar give the broth a tangy, gently spicy edge. The fish stays soft and moist and the light broth is spooned over rice or boiled cassava. It is everyday home cooking along the coast.

Recipe

4 servings30 min

Ingredients

  • 800 g thick white fish fillets
  • 2 tomatoes (250 g), in wedges
  • 1 large onion (200 g), in thick strips
  • 1 ají amarillo, in strips
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 100 ml chicha de jora or 2 tbsp vinegar
  • 150 ml fish stock
  • 1 tbsp oil, cilantro, salt

Method

  1. Warm the oil and soften garlic and half the onion for 3 minutes.
  2. Lay the fish on top and season with salt.
  3. Cover with the rest of the onion, tomato and ají strips.
  4. Pour in the chicha and stock around the fish.
  5. Cover tightly and cook on low heat for 10–12 minutes.
  6. Sprinkle with cilantro and serve with rice or cassava.

How to make it lighter

  • Steaming in its juices keeps the dish very low in fat.
  • Serve with a small portion of rice and plenty of vegetables.
  • Replace chicha with vinegar for an alcohol-free version.

History

Sudados are a standard technique of coastal Peruvian kitchens and are made with many kinds of fish, from corvina to cabrilla. Chicha de jora, a fermented corn drink of Andean origin, is a traditional flavouring for the broth; most of its mild alcohol cooks off.
