Cioppino is a San Francisco seafood stew in which white fish, shrimp, clams and mussels simmer in a garlicky tomato and white wine broth. The shellfish open in the pot and release their juices, giving a rich, briny, lightly spicy sauce. It is served in large bowls, straight from the pot, with plenty of broth.

Recipe

4 servings50 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g firm white fish fillet, in chunks
  • 250 g raw shrimp, peeled
  • 500 g clams, scrubbed
  • 500 g mussels, cleaned
  • 800 g canned chopped tomatoes
  • 150 ml white wine
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced, and 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, chilli flakes, parsley, salt

Method

  1. Soften the onion and garlic in the olive oil for 5 minutes, add a pinch of chilli flakes.
  2. Pour in the wine and let it bubble for 2 minutes, then add the tomatoes and 300 ml water and simmer for 20 minutes.
  3. Add the clams and mussels, cover and cook for 4–5 minutes until they start to open.
  4. Add the fish and shrimp and simmer gently for 3–4 minutes until just cooked; discard any shellfish that stay closed.
  5. Season, scatter with parsley and serve in deep bowls.

How to make it lighter

  • Cioppino is mostly lean seafood — keep the olive oil to one or two tablespoons.
  • Fill the bowl with extra broth and seafood for a bigger, still light portion.
  • Add more tomatoes, peppers or fennel to stretch the stew without much extra energy.

History

Cioppino is usually credited to Italian, largely Ligurian, fishermen who settled in San Francisco's North Beach in the late 19th century and cooked stews from the day's catch. The name is often linked to the Ligurian fish soup ciuppin. It remains a signature dish of San Francisco's seafood restaurants.
