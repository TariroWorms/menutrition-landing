Ewedu soup

Origin: Nigeria · Yoruba
66kcal

Ewedu is a light green soup made from jute leaves. The leaves are boiled until soft and whisked with a wooden broom-like tool until the soup becomes smooth and silky. It tastes mild, grassy and slightly savoury from locust beans. It is almost always poured alongside gbegiri and tomato stew and eaten with amala.

What it is made of

ayoyo (jute) leaves, or spinachfermented locust beansdried crayfishonion

Recipe

4 servings25 min

Ingredients

  • 400 g jute leaves (ewedu)
  • 20 g fermented locust beans (iru)
  • 20 g ground dried crayfish
  • 100 g onions
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper
  • salt

Method

  1. Pick the leaves from the stems and wash them.
  2. Boil in water with a pinch of salt until soft, about 8 minutes.
  3. Whisk or blend until smooth and viscous.
  4. Add crushed locust beans, crayfish, chopped onion and pepper.
  5. Simmer 5 minutes and adjust the salt.

How to make it lighter

  • Skip the stock cubes and rely on locust beans and crayfish for savoury depth.
  • Add a handful of extra leafy greens, such as spinach, for more vegetables.
  • Serve with a modest portion of amala and plenty of gbegiri.

History

Jute leaves are an old staple vegetable of the Yoruba and are valued for their silky texture. The soup is the green part of the classic abula plate.
