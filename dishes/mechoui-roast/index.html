This méchoui-style roast is lamb seasoned simply with cumin, paprika and salt and cooked low and slow until it is tender enough to pull apart. The outside browns and smells of toasted spices, while the inside stays juicy. It is served in large pieces to share, with extra cumin and salt on the side for dipping.

Recipe

6 servings240 min

Ingredients

  • 1 lamb shoulder on the bone (about 2 kg)
  • 2 tsp ground cumin, plus extra to serve
  • 2 tsp sweet paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 200 ml water

Method

  1. Mix the cumin, paprika and salt with the oil and rub over the lamb.
  2. Put the lamb in a roasting tin with the water, cover tightly with foil.
  3. Roast at 160 °C for about 3½ hours until the meat pulls easily from the bone.
  4. Uncover, raise the oven to 220 °C and roast 15–20 minutes to brown the surface.
  5. Rest 10 minutes, pull into pieces and serve with cumin and salt for dipping.

How to make it lighter

  • Trim the external fat before roasting and skim the fat from the pan juices.
  • Choose leg instead of shoulder for a leaner roast.
  • Pair a palm-sized portion of lamb with salads or roasted vegetables.

History

Méchoui is the festive roast lamb of the Maghreb, especially Morocco and Algeria, traditionally cooked whole on a spit or in a clay pit for Eid al-Adha and family celebrations. Home cooks today often make a smaller oven version with a shoulder or leg, keeping the same simple cumin seasoning. It is eaten across North Africa and in North African communities in France.
