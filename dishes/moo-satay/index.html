🍢 Pork satay (moo satay)

Origin: Thailand

Moo satay is the Thai version of satay: thin strips of pork marinated in turmeric, spices and coconut milk, threaded on skewers and grilled until lightly charred. It is served with a sweet, creamy peanut sauce for dipping and a small bowl of ajad, a sweet-sour cucumber relish with shallots and chili. It is a popular snack and appetiser.

Recipe

4 servings45 min

Ingredients

  • 600 g pork loin, cut into thin strips
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric, 1 tsp ground coriander, 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 150 ml coconut milk
  • 1 tbsp sugar, salt
  • 120 ml ready-made peanut sauce for dipping
  • 1 cucumber, 1 shallot, 1 red chili, 3 tbsp vinegar, 1 tbsp sugar for the relish
  • Bamboo skewers, soaked

Method

  1. Mix the spices, sugar, salt and 100 ml coconut milk and marinate the pork for at least 1 hour.
  2. For the relish, dissolve the sugar in the vinegar and pour over thinly sliced cucumber, shallot and chili.
  3. Thread the pork onto skewers.
  4. Grill for 6–8 minutes, turning and brushing with the remaining coconut milk.
  5. Serve with the peanut sauce and cucumber relish.

How to make it lighter

  • Limit the peanut sauce to about 1 tbsp per skewer; it is the most calorie-dense part.
  • Use light coconut milk in the marinade.
  • Fill up on the cucumber relish, which adds very few calories.

History

Satay spread through Southeast Asia from Indonesia and Malaysia, and Thailand adopted it with pork and its own peanut sauce and cucumber relish. Today moo satay is a common street food and appetiser across Thailand.
