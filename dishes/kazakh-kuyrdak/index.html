Kuyrdak (fried meat and offal)

Origin: Kazakhstan
690kcal

Kuyrdak is made right after an animal is slaughtered, from the freshest parts: liver, heart, kidneys and lungs along with some meat. Everything is chopped into small pieces and fried in a cauldron with tail fat and plenty of onion, sometimes with potatoes. It is rich, savoury and filling, eaten hot with bread and tea.

What it is made of

lamblamb offalonionpotatolamb fatblack pepper

Recipe

4 servings60 min

Ingredients

  • 300 g lamb meat
  • 200 g lamb liver
  • 150 g lamb heart
  • 100 g lamb kidneys
  • 50 g lamb tail fat
  • 2 onions (250 g)
  • 2 potatoes (250 g), optional
  • salt, black pepper

Method

  1. Clean the offal and cut all meat and offal into 1–2 cm pieces.
  2. Render the tail fat in a heavy pan or kazan.
  3. Fry the heart, kidneys and meat first until browned, about 15 minutes.
  4. Add the potatoes if using and cook until almost tender.
  5. Add the liver and onion and fry 8–10 minutes more.
  6. Season with salt and pepper and serve hot with bread.

How to make it lighter

  • Use less tail fat and a little oil instead.
  • Increase the liver and heart, which are lean, and reduce fatty meat.
  • Serve with a fresh tomato and onion salad instead of extra bread.

History

Kuyrdak is traditionally the first dish cooked after a sheep or horse is slaughtered, when the offal is at its freshest. Variations exist across Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and neighbouring regions, with lamb, beef or horse.
