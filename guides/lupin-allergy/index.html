🌼Lupin allergy

Lupin is a legume whose flour and seeds are used in some breads, pastries and pasta, especially in Europe. It is one of the 14 major food allergens in the EU and UK, and people with a peanut allergy are more likely to react to it, so it is managed by avoiding lupin and reading labels.

🔬What it means

In a lupin allergy the immune system mistakes lupin proteins for a threat and releases chemicals such as histamine. Reactions can be severe, including anaphylaxis.

Lupin is related to peanuts, and some people with a peanut allergy also react to lupin, sometimes the first time they eat it. Others become allergic to lupin on its own. An allergist can test which foods are safe for you.

✅What you can eat

Everything that does not contain lupin. Plain meat, fish, eggs, dairy, grains, fruit and vegetables are naturally lupin-free, and most breads, pastries and pasta are made without it — just check the label. Whether peanuts or other legumes are safe depends on your own tests — ask your allergist.

🚫What to avoid

  • 🌼Lupin in any form: lupin flour, lupin seeds, lupin protein, lupini beans (a pickled snack in Mediterranean countries).
  • 🍞Gluten-free products: some gluten-free breads, flour mixes and baked goods use lupin flour.
  • 🥐Bakery and pastries: some breads, pastries, pies, waffles and pancakes, especially in Europe.
  • 🍝Pasta and pizza: some pasta and pizza bases.
  • 🥩Meat-free and high-protein products: some vegan alternatives and protein foods use lupin protein.
  • 🏷️Labels: in the EU and UK lupin must be highlighted in the ingredients, sometimes spelled “lupine”. US rules do not require it, so check imported products carefully.
  • 🍽️In restaurants and bakeries: tell the staff about the allergy every time; lupin flour is not always visible.

⚠️What happens during a reaction

Symptoms usually start within minutes to two hours: itchy mouth, hives, redness, swelling of the lips or face, stomach pain, vomiting, sneezing or a runny nose.

A severe reaction — anaphylaxis — affects breathing or circulation: swelling of the tongue or throat, hoarse voice, wheezing, difficulty breathing, feeling faint, pale or floppy skin, collapse. It can develop fast and is life-threatening.

A mild reaction in the past does not guarantee the next one will be mild. If you have a peanut allergy, ask your allergist whether you should avoid lupin too.

🚑When to get help urgently

If there are any signs of anaphylaxis, use an adrenaline auto-injector if you have one and call emergency services immediately, even if symptoms improve. Lie down with legs raised (or sit up if it is hard to breathe) and do not stand up or walk suddenly. After any reaction, see a doctor about an allergy plan.
